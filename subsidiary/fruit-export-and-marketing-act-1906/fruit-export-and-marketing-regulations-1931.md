## PART 1 PRELIMINARY [regs 1–2A]

### reg 1 Short title

These Regulations may be cited as the Fruit Export and Marketing Regulations 1931.

### reg 2 Interpretation

In these Regulations, unless the context otherwise requires—

**agent** means any person, association or company which exports or attempts to export or negotiates for the export of produce on behalf of the owners thereof;

**banana buyer’s licence** and licence means a licence issued by the Permanent Secretary by virtue of section 12(2) of the Act;

**fruit exporter’s licence** means a licence issued by virtue of section 7 of the Act;

**germinable seeds** means viable seeds which under the proper conditions of temperature and moisture will germinate;

**inert matter** means dead, diseased, insect infested seeds and damaged seeds of the Mauritius bean, seeds other than Mauritius bean seeds, and dust, stones and other foreign matter;

**inspector** means a fruit inspector appointed by the Minister under the Act;

**licensed buyer** and licensee means the holder of a licence issued by virtue of section 12(2) of the Act;

**licensed exporter** means the holder of a fruit exporter’s licence issued by virtue of section 7 of the Act;

**Permanent Secretary** means the permanent secretary responsible for agriculture;

[def subst LN 99 of 2016 reg 93, effective 1 December 2016]

**produce inspector** means the person occupying that post from time to time in the Ministry for Agriculture; and

[def am LN 99 of 2016 reg 93, effective 1 December 2016]

**quota** means the number of cases or bunches of bananas which may be supplied from any area or by any holder of a fruit exporter’s licence for export to New Zealand or Australia at or during any specified time.

[def insrt Regulations of 14 July 1932 reg 3, effective 15 July 1932; am Regulations of 19 October 1932 reg 2, effective 21 October 1932]

## PART 2 GENERAL [regs 3–15]

### reg 3 Application of Part

This Part shall apply to all fruit exported from Fiji.

### reg 4 Fruit exporter’s licence

(1) A fruit exporter’s licence shall be valid for such period, not exceeding 12 months, as is specified in the licence, being a period ending on 31 December in the year of its commencement.

[subreg (1) subst LN 80 of 1983 reg 3, effective 7 October 1983]

(1A) The prescribed fee for the purposes of section 7(1) of the Act is $5 (VEP) and must be paid into the account of the Biosecurity Authority of Fiji.

[subreg (1A) insrt LN 80 of 1983 reg 3, effective 7 October 1983; am LN 58 of 2021 reg 2, effective 1 August 2021]

(1B) A fruit exporter’s licence shall be in the form contained in Schedule 8.

[subreg (1B) insrt LN 18 of 1987 reg 2, effective 6 February 1987]

(2) A licensed exporter must produce his or her licence when required so to do by an inspector who may retain it long enough to take any necessary and lawful action relating to it, provided that, in the case of a person exporting through an agent the licence shall be deposited with the agent who shall produce it when required by an inspector for the purposes of this regulation.

### reg 5 Certificate to be furnished by licensed exporter

All fruit to be exported must be accompanied by a certificate as set out in Schedule 1 signed by the licensed exporter, setting forth the shipping marks, the quantity, the date when the fruit was removed from the plants, the tikina and province in which the fruit was grown, and certifying that the fruit, or the plants on which it was grown, were previously treated in accordance with the provisions of any regulations made under the Biosecurity Act 2008 and notices issued thereunder. Such certificates shall be furnished to the inspector prior to or at the time the fruit is presented for inspection, provided that the Produce Inspector may accept a certificate in the form set out in Schedule 1 signed by the agent of a licensed exporter which agent shall be held responsible for the correctness of the particulars set out therein.

[reg 5 am LN 99 of 2016 reg 93, effective 1 December 2016]

### reg 6 Marks

All fruit and the cases, bags or other containers in which it is packed for export shall be branded in a manner approved by the produce inspector with a registered brand prior to submission for inspection.

### reg 7 Certificate to be given by inspector

On the shipping receipt for any shipment of fruit being presented to an inspector, he or she shall, if the provisions of these Regulations have been complied with and he or she is satisfied that the fruit is free from disease and properly packed and marked, issue a certificate as set out in Schedule 2 stating that to the best of his or her knowledge and belief it is free from disease and properly packed and marked.

### reg 8 Fumigation of fruit

An inspector may order the fumigation of any fruit and such fruit shall not be shipped until it has been fumigated to the satisfaction of an inspector.

### reg 9 Conditions to which fruit for export must conform

All fruit shipped by any licensed exporter must conform to the following conditions—

(a) it must be harvested from plants which have been treated in accordance with the law;

(b) every bunch of bananas shall consist of not less than 8 hands of well-developed fruit, and (in common with fruit of every kind) shall be clean and undamaged either by water or otherwise, and shall be in all other respects satisfactory to an inspector;

(c) bananas shall not be cut more than 6 days prior to the advertised sailing date of the vessel by which they are intended to be exported and shall not be packed more than 5 days prior to the advertised sailing date of such vessel;

(d) the Permanent Secretary or the produce inspector may require any fruit to be graded. Such fruit shall be graded to the satisfaction of an inspector. The licensed exporter shall provide all labour required for the grading of such fruit;

(e) all fruit exported in containers shall be packed in cases, crates or bags of a size, make and condition approved by the Permanent Secretary or by an inspector;

(f) all punts and other vessels conveying fruit for export shall be floored, properly dunnaged and provided with tarpaulins or other covers and ventilators or windsails to the satisfaction of an inspector. Tarpaulins should be so constructed as to allow of their adjustment in order to make the punts or other vessels airtight for the purpose of fumigation, provided that all fruit carried in auxiliary cutters must be so stacked as to allow a clear space of at least 6 inches between the cases and engine room bulk heads;

(g) all tomatoes and cucumbers intended for export shall be brought in a green and thoroughly unripe condition to a place directed by the produce inspector where they shall be packed under the supervision of an inspector. Such fruit shall be conveyed to the place selected for packing in such receptacles as the Produce Inspector shall direct;

(h) all coconuts intended for export shall be bagged in clean bags which have not been used previously for any purpose. Coconuts, with the exception of consignments to New Zealand which shall not be husked and shall have the calyx unbroken, shall be husked and shall indicate the presence of fluid when shaken;

(i) all kumalas, yams, taro, egg-fruit, pumpkins, watermelons, peanuts, pineapples, pawpaws, green ginger, arrowroot and other fruit intended for export shall be brought in bags or in cases which have not been previously used for any purpose, to a place directed by the produce inspector where they shall be examined by an inspector;

(j) all mandarins, oranges and grapefruit intended for export shall be treated in accordance with the provisions of Part 4 and shall be brought in sound and marketable condition satisfactory to an inspector to a place directed by the produce inspector where they shall be packed under supervision of an inspector. All other fresh fruits intended for export shall be conveyed to a place directed by the produce inspector in cases where they shall be examined by an inspector.

[reg 9 am Regulations of 19 October 1932 reg 6, effective 21 October 1932]

### reg 10 Powers of inspector

An inspector shall have power to control the berthing and unloading of all vessels carrying fruit to the port of shipment, the stowing of such fruit on any wharf in any shed, and to open or require to be opened for the purpose of examination any case, crate or bag of fruit intended for shipment, and any attempt to interfere with an inspector or his or her assistants in the exercise of this power or refusal to obey an order of an inspector shall be deemed to be an infringement of these Regulations.

[reg 10 subst Regulations of 19 October 1932 reg 7, effective 21 October 1932]

### reg 11 Rejected fruit

Fruit rejected by an inspector shall not be exported, and an inspector may order such fruit to be removed forthwith from the place of inspection.

### reg 12 Registration of brands

A licensed exporter shall register his or her brands with the Permanent Secretary and no fruit shall be exported unless marked with a registered brand. The registration of any brand may be cancelled by the Permanent Secretary at any time.

### reg 13 Notice of intention to ship

A licensed exporter may not commence shipping fruit until he or she has—

(a) presented the fruit for inspection at such place as an inspector shall direct, provided that fruit shall be inspected only between sunrise and sunset unless the produce inspector consents in writing to inspect such fruit at any other time;

(b) informed an inspector when he or she wishes to commence shipping, the quantity which he or she proposes to ship, the name of the steamer, and the place of the intended shipment, provided that in the case of a licensed exporter intending to ship fruit at a port other than Suva he or she shall notify the Permanent Secretary of his or her intention to ship at least 14 days prior to the advertised sailing date of the vessel by which it is intended to export the fruit;

(c) complied with the provisions of the Act and with these Regulations and with all instructions given to him or her by an inspector in accordance with these Regulations.

### reg 14 Rejection of fruit

An inspector may reject the whole or any portion of fruit intended for shipment which in his or her opinion contains an undue proportion of fruit, unsuitable for shipment and the Permanent Secretary or the produce inspector may in their discretion refuse to the exporter of such fruit permission to repack the same and again present it for shipment. A licensed exporter shall not ship or attempt to ship any fruit which has been rejected by an inspector.

### reg 15 Handling, loading and stowing of fruit

All fruit shall be handled, loaded into vessels and stowed therein to the satisfaction of an inspector.

## PART 3 EXPORT OF MAURITIUS BEANS [regs 16–20]

### reg 16 Definition of “seed”

For the purpose of this Part, “seed” means the seed of the Mauritius bean (Stizolobium aterrimum).

### reg 17 Export of seeds

No exporter shall be permitted to export seeds containing less than 60% of germinable seeds, nor more than 2% of inert matter, exclusive of the packages in which they are contained.

### reg 18 Notice of intention to export

Intending exporters of seeds shall deliver to the Permanent Secretary or an inspector, not less than 14 days prior to the advertised date of departure of the vessel by which it is intended to export the seeds, written notice of his or her intention to export, whereupon the Permanent Secretary or an inspector shall take or cause to be taken such samples as are necessary for examination of the said seeds.

### reg 19 Powers of Permanent Secretary

The Permanent Secretary or an inspector shall be and is hereby empowered—

(a) to take samples of any seeds intended for export;

(b) to require that seeds intended for export shall be stored or kept under such conditions as shall prevent damage or deterioration thereto;

(c) to require bags containing seeds intended for export to be marked and/or sealed in such manner as he or she shall direct.

### reg 20 Issue of certificate

The Permanent Secretary or an inspector shall issue a certificate in the form set out in Schedule 3 in respect of all shipments of seeds which have been examined and found to comply with the provisions of regulation 17.

## PART 4 EXPORT OF CITRUS FRUITS [regs 21–24]

### reg 21 Notice of intention to export

A licensed exporter shall give to the Permanent Secretary at least 21 days’ notice in the form set out in Schedule 4 of his or her intention to export oranges, mandarins and grapefruit.

### reg 22 Storage of fruit in fly-proof sheds

Oranges, mandarins and grapefruit shall be stored in fly-proof sheds, constructed to the satisfaction of an inspector, for such period as the Permanent Secretary shall direct. Such sheds shall, during the time oranges and mandarins and grapefruit are stored therein, be under the control of an inspector and shall not be opened without the consent of an inspector.

### reg 23 Wrapping and packing of fruit

All citrus fruits intended for export shall, if the Permanent Secretary so directs, be graded, wrapped and packed to the satisfaction of an inspector, and shall be otherwise treated in such manner as the Permanent Secretary by written notice may direct.

### reg 24 Instructions may be issued

The Permanent Secretary shall be empowered to issue instructions by notice in the Gazette providing for—

(a) the method of harvesting the fruit;

(b) the conditions under which the fruit may be handled and transported;

(c) the times during which the fruit may be received at an approved fly-proof store;

(d) the construction and management of fly-proof sheds;

(e) the grading, curing, washing, sizing, wrapping, packing, fumigation or other treatment of the fruit.

## PART 5 TERMS AND CONDITIONS OF BANANA BUYERS’ LICENCES [regs 25–38]

### reg 25 Application of Part

This Part refers only to the terms and conditions governing the issue of banana buyers’ licences under the provisions of section 12(2) of the Act.

### reg 26 Application for banana buyer’s licence

Any person desiring to obtain a banana buyer’s licence shall make written application to the Permanent Secretary for such licence. The Permanent Secretary shall have power to issue or refuse such licence. The fee for such licence shall be $10. The licence shall as nearly as circumstances permit be in the form set out in Schedule 5.

### reg 27 Licence valid in area specified in licence only

A banana buyer’s licence shall be valid only for the prescribed area specified in the licence.

### reg 28 Licence may not be transferred without permission

The holder of a banana buyer’s licence shall not assign, mortgage, sublet or otherwise dispose of the licence or of his or her interest under the licence without the consent in writing of the Permanent Secretary.

### reg 29 Expiry of licence

Each licence shall remain valid for such period not exceeding 2 years, as the Permanent Secretary shall determine.

### reg 30 Licensee to deposit $200

The licensee shall deposit with the Chief Accountant the sum of $200 or a bank guarantee therefor, which shall be held by the Chief Accountant until the expiration of one calendar month after the termination of the licence, provided that if the said sum or any part thereof is attached after judgment in which a Fijian banana grower has recovered any sum in respect of a sale or contract to sell bananas to the licensee the amount so attached shall be deemed to be immediately due and payable and shall be paid into court.

### reg 31 Licensee shall purchase all bananas

The licensee so long as he or she is the only person holding a banana buyer’s licence in the area shall purchase, at not less than the minimum price fixed in accordance with regulation 33, all Fijian grown bananas to the extent of the quota allotted to the area by notice in writing by the Permanent Secretary, of the standards of maturity and quality as defined in these Regulations produced in the area and delivered and offered for sale to him or her by a Fijian producer at a packing station declared in accordance with regulation 40. The bananas shall be purchased in the area at such times as the licensee with the approval of the Produce Inspector shall appoint, provided that the licensee shall not purchase any bananas which an inspector, on the matter being referred to him or her in accordance with regulation 47, may consider to be unfit for export.

[reg 31 subst Regulations of 14 July 1932 reg 4, effective 15 July 1932; am LN 126 of 1946 reg 2, effective 26 July 1946]

### reg 32 Licensee not obliged to purchase from Fijians who export

Notwithstanding anything in regulation 31 a licensee shall not be obliged to purchase bananas grown by any Fijian who has exported bananas during the preceding 12 months, provided that the export of bananas by a Fijian prior to the issue of a banana buyer’s licence in respect of the area in which they were grown shall not be deemed to be an export by a Fijian for the purposes of this regulation.

### reg 33 Banana Board to fix prices

The Minister may appoint a Board to be known as the Banana Board which shall fix, from time to time by notice in the Gazette, the minimum prices to be paid for bananas grown by Fijians and purchased by the licensee under the licence.

[reg 33 am Regulations of 14 March 1945 reg 2; LN 165 of 1965, effective 3 December 1965; LN 112 of 1970 O 261, effective 8 October 1970]

### reg 34 Shipping prevented by act of God etc

In the event of it being impossible for the licensee to ship bananas delivered to him or her in accordance with the provisions of regulation 31 through an act of God, strikes (local or foreign), breakdown of steamers, lack of cargo space or any other circumstances beyond the control of the licensee then the licensee shall, if, in the opinion of the Commissioner, he or she has not given the Fijian producers sufficient notice not to cut and send bananas to a prescribed packing station and if the Fijian producers or the Fijian agents so desire, purchase the bananas at a minimum rate equivalent to half the rates fixed under the provisions of regulation 33.

### reg 35 Payment for bananas

The licensee shall pay for all bananas purchased by him or her in cash at the time of delivery of the bananas to him or her at a prescribed packing station.

### reg 36 Bananas to be purchased from producer or Fijian representative

The licensee shall not purchase bananas grown by Fijians in a notified area from any person other than an actual producer or an accredited Fijian representative of a Fijian grower.

### reg 37 Disposal of bananas

The licensee shall dispose of all bananas purchased by him or her under his or her licence to such person or persons as the Minister shall, by notice in the Gazette, direct.

[reg 37 insrt Regulations of 23 August 1939 reg 2, effective 1 September 1969; am LN 112 of 1970 O 261, effective 8 October 1970]

### reg 38 Minister may cancel licence

Notwithstanding anything in regulation 29, the Minister may cancel any licence if the licensee—

(a) commits an offence against the Act or against any regulation made under the Act;

(b) commits any breach of any of the conditions under which the licence was issued;

(c) is reported by the Produce Inspector to have packed, shipped or attempted to ship fruit unsuitable for export.

[reg 38 am LN 112 of 1970 O 261, effective 8 October 1970]

## PART 6 PURCHASE AND PACKING OF BANANAS IN PROCLAIMED AREAS [regs 39–47]

### reg 39 Application of Part

This Part shall apply only to bananas produced by Fijians in areas in respect of which banana buyers’ licences have been issued.

### reg 40 Declaration of packing stations

The Permanent Secretary may, from time to time by notice in the Gazette, prescribe the stations in each area at which bananas grown by Fijians in areas notified under section 12 of the Act shall be packed. Such bananas shall not be packed elsewhere than at a prescribed station within the area in which they were grown, provided that the Permanent Secretary may permit by writing under his or her hand the packing of such bananas at a prescribed station or stations outside the area.

### reg 41 Buyer or agent to pack bananas at station in his or her area

A licensed buyer or any person acting as an agent for any grower shall pack bananas only at a prescribed station in an area for which a buyer’s licence has been issued, provided that if in the opinion of the Permanent Secretary there are not sufficient suitable places for packing within such area the Permanent Secretary may permit by writing under his or her hand the packing of bananas at a prescribed station or stations outside the area.

### reg 42 Notification of intention to pack

A licensed buyer or any person acting as an agent for any grower shall give in writing to the Permanent Secretary or to the produce inspector at least 10 days’ notice of the date on which he or she intends to pack fruit at any prescribed packing station.

### reg 43 Bananas to be brought to stations in bunches

All bananas grown by Fijians in notified areas intended for sale to a licensee or for export through an agent shall be brought to a prescribed station in bunches, provided that the produce inspector may, by writing under his or her hand, permit bananas from such areas as he or she shall deem advisable, to be brought to a prescribed station in his or her hands.

[reg 43 am Regulations of 14 July 1932 reg 5, effective 15 July 1932]

### reg 44 Bananas to be purchased and packed in the presence of an inspector

Bananas shall be purchased and packed at a prescribed packing station in the presence of an inspector, provided that the produce inspector may permit, by writing under his or her hand, the packing of bananas in the absence of an inspector.

### reg 45 Fijian growers to be notified of dates of packing

A licensed buyer shall notify the Fijian growers in the area in respect of which he or she holds a licence, at least 7 days prior, of the dates on which bananas shall be packed at prescribed packing stations and the number of cases to be packed. Written notice in the iTaukei language to the turaga-ni-koro of every village in such area shall be deemed to be sufficient notice to the growers.

[reg 45 am Regulations of 14 July 1932 reg 6, effective 15 July 1932; Decree 31 of 2010 s 4, effective 2 July 2010]

### reg 46 Buyer to be responsible for quality of fruit

A holder of a banana buyer’s licence or an agent shall assume the responsibility for seeing that all bananas packed by him or her or on his or her account in the notified area in which he or she is operating are of good quality and of uniform grade and size, free from disease and suitable for the market to which they are to be exported. He or she shall have at least one representative approved by the Permanent Secretary or the produce inspector in attendance at each packing station.

[reg 46 am LN 126 of 1946 reg 4, effective 26 July 1946]

### reg 47 Duties of inspectors

Under Parts 5 and 6 the duties of an inspector shall be—

(a) to act as a referee and when appealed to by the licensed buyer or the Fijian grower, in the event of any dispute, to give a decision which would be final as to the suitability of any fruit offered for sale at a prescribed station. An inspector shall have the power to reject any fruit offered for sale whether he or she be appealed to as provided for in this regulation or not. Any fruit rejected by an inspector shall not be purchased or exported;

(b) to make such reports as the Permanent Secretary may from time to time direct and otherwise to act under the instructions of the Permanent Secretary or the produce inspector in a manner consistent with the provisions of these Regulations.

[reg 47 am Regulations of 14 July 1932 reg 7, effective 15 July 1932]

## PART 7 AGENTS [regs 48–53]

### reg 48 Application of Part

This Part shall apply to all persons or undertakings acting as agents for the growers of fruit, and to all fruit exported or intended to be exported by growers through agents.

### reg 49 Agent’s permit

No person shall act as an agent for the export of fruit unless he or she has first obtained a permit in the form set out in Schedule 6 from the Permanent Secretary to do so, provided that if the applicant holds a commission agent’s licence the Permanent Secretary may not withhold the issue of such permit without the consent of the Minister but may cancel any permit issued under this regulation upon the conviction of the holder thereof of any breach of the Act or of the provisions of these Regulations. An agent’s permit shall be valid only for the area in respect of which it is issued and until 31 December of the year of its issue.

[reg 49 am Regulations of 14 July 1932 reg 8, effective 15 July 1932; LN 112 of 1970 O 261, effective 8 October 1970]

### reg 50 Deposit to be made

All persons acting as agents shall deposit with the Chief Accountant the sum of $200 in respect of each area for which they hold a permit, or a bank guarantee therefor, which shall be held by the Chief Accountant until the termination of one calendar month after the expiration of the permit, provided that if the said sum or any part thereof is attached after judgment in which a grower has recovered any sum in respect of non-fulfilment of the terms of any arrangement entered into for the shipment of fruit the said sum shall be deemed to be immediately due and payable and shall be paid into court.

[reg 50 am Regulations of 14 July 1932 reg 9, effective 15 July 1932]

### reg 51 Responsibility of agent

An agent shall assume the responsibility for seeing that all fruit packed is of good quality and of uniform grade and size, free from disease and suitable for the market to which it is to be exported. He or she shall have at least one representative approved by the Permanent Secretary or the produce inspector in attendance at the place where such fruit is packed and shall be responsible for seeing that the fruit is packed at such places as the Permanent Secretary or the produce inspector shall direct and that the provisions of regulation 9 are complied with.

### reg 52 Certificate to accompany fruit

The fruit intended to be exported through agents must be accompanied by a certificate as set out in Schedule 7 signed by the agent setting forth the names of the growers of the fruit, the marks of each individual’s fruit and the town or location at which the fruit was grown.

### reg 53 Production of documents

An agent may be required by the Permanent Secretary to produce for inspection all original documents relating to his or her transactions as an agent for Fijian growers and the Permanent Secretary shall be empowered to retain such documents for a sufficient time to enable lawful action to be taken in connection therewith.

## PART 9 PENALTIES [regs 55, 56]

### reg 55 Cancellation etc of licence

Without prejudice to regulation 56, if a licensed exporter or the holder of an agent’s permit fails to comply with—

(a) any of the provisions of the Act or these Regulations; or

(b) any condition of his or her licence or permit, the Permanent Secretary may—

(i) suspend the licence or permit for a period not exceeding 6 months; or

(ii) cancel the licence or permit.

[reg 55 subst LN 18 of 1987 reg 3, effective 6 February 1987]

### reg 56 Penalty

Any person who contravenes any of these Regulations shall be guilty of an offence and liable to a fine not exceeding $40 and to imprisonment for 3 months and to the forfeiture of any fruit in respect of which the offence has been committed.

[reg 56 subst LN 18 of 1987 reg 3, effective 6 February 1987]
