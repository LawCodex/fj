## r 1 Short title

These Rules may be cited as the Inquests Rules 1968.

## r 2 Interpretation

In these Rules, unless the context otherwise requires—

**Chief Registrar** means the Chief Registrar of the High Court; and

**clerk of the court** includes an assistant clerk of the court or other officer performing or assisting a clerk of the court in his or her duties.

## r 3 Filing of death reports

(1) The report of death to a Magistrate under section 3 of the Act shall be filed with the clerk of the appropriate Magistrates Court, who shall make the necessary entries in the register kept for that purpose.

(2) Where there are more Magistrates than one at the court at which a death report is registered the senior of such Magistrates shall arrange which Magistrate shall discharge the duties to be performed by a Magistrate under the Act.

## r 4 Register in Magistrates Court

(1) Every Magistrates Court shall keep an indexed register of all deaths reported to any Magistrate of that court, and the register shall contain the particulars specified in Schedule 1.

(2) Every Magistrates Court shall, in addition, keep an indexed register of all inquests held by any Magistrate of that court and the register shall contain the particulars specified in Schedule 2.

## r 5 Procedure generally

Subject to the provisions of the Act and of these Rules, the practice and procedure at inquests shall be governed mutatis mutandis by the provisions of the Criminal Procedure Act 2009 relating to trials in Magistrates Courts.

[r 5 am LN 99 of 2016 reg 115, effective 1 December 2016]

## r 6 Witnesses

Every witness at an inquest shall be examined upon oath or affirmation, provided that the Magistrate may at any time, if he or she thinks it just and expedient (for reasons to be recorded in the proceedings) take without oath the evidence of any person declaring that the taking of any oath whatever is according to his or her religious belief unlawful, or who by reason of immature age or want of religious belief ought not in the opinion of the Magistrate, to be admitted to give evidence on oath; the fact of the evidence having been so taken being also recorded in the proceedings.

## r 7 Notice of resumption of inquest adjourned sine die

Before resuming an inquest adjourned sine die under the provisions of section 7(4), of the Act, the Magistrate shall give at least 7 days’ notice of his or her intention to do so by publishing in the Gazette, and in the newspaper in which under the provisions of section 9(1) of the Act the notice of the intention to hold the inquest was inserted, a notice stating the date, time and place at which the adjourned inquest will be held.

## r 8 Applications to the High Court

(1) An application to the High Court by the Attorney-General under section 20(1) or (2) of the Act shall be by originating motion, made ex parte in the first instance, and may be heard by a Judge in chambers.

(2) The High Court may at any time after the motion has been filed, either of its own motion or upon application by the Attorney-General, order that any person be joined as a respondent in the proceedings, but no appearance need be entered by any respondent.

(3) The powers of the High Court under section 20(4) of the Act may be exercised by a Judge in chambers without formal application.

## r 9 Register to be kept by Chief Registrar

The Chief Registrar shall keep an indexed register of every inquest the evidence and findings in which are delivered to him or her under the provisions of section 18 of the Act and the said register shall contain the particulars specified in Schedule 3.

## r 10 Copy depositions etc

The Solicitor-General shall, on application and on payment of the prescribed fees, supply to any properly interested person a copy of any depositions or notes of evidence made by the Magistrate at an inquest, or of any report of a post-mortem examination or ancillary examination or of any document put in evidence at an inquest, or may, on application and without charge, permit such person to inspect such depositions, report, notes of evidence, or documents.

## r 11 Certificates of death

(1) Where a death has been reported to a Magistrate under the provisions of the Act, whether or not an inquest has been held, the clerk of the court of the Magistrates Court at which the report is registered shall, on application and on payment of the prescribed fees, supply to any properly interested person a certificate of the cause of death which shall be in the form specified in Schedule 4.

(2) For the purposes of this and rule 10, the prescribed fees shall be the fees for the time being prescribed under the Magistrates Court Act 1944 in respect of the supply of copies of documents and certification thereof.

## r 12 Forms

The forms set out in Schedule 5, with such modifications as circumstances may require, shall be used for the purposes for which they are to be applicable.
