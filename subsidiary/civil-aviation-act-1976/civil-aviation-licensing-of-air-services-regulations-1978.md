## PART 1 PRELIMINARY [regs 1, 2]

### reg 1 Short title

These Regulations may be cited as the Civil Aviation (Licensing of Air Services) Regulations 1978.

### reg 2 Interpretation

In these Regulations and the Schedules, unless the context otherwise requires—

**air service** means any service performed by an aircraft for hire or reward, or any flight performed by an aircraft carrying more than 40 passengers or 4 metric tons of freight or as the Minister may from time to time direct, provided that a member of a club carried in an aircraft belonging to the club for the purpose of instruction shall not, if the instructor is also a member of the club, be deemed to be carried for hire or reward, notwithstanding that payment is made for such instruction or carriage;

**Authority** means the Civil Aviation Authority of Fiji established in terms of the Civil Aviation Authority of Fiji Act 1979;

[def insrt LN 169 of 1979 reg 2, effective 1 October 1979]

**Convention on International Civil Aviation** and “International Air Services Transit Agreement” mean respectively the Convention and the Agreement so styled to which Fiji adhered to respectively on 5 March 1973 and 14 February 1973;

**international journey** means a journey between places at least one of which is outside Fiji;

**licence** means a licence granted under regulation 5;

**licensing authority** means, in relation to air services between 2 places in Fiji, the Licensing Board and, in relation to other air services, the Minister;

**Licensing Board** means the Air Transport Licensing Board constituted by regulation 18;

**Permanent Secretary** means the permanent secretary responsible for civil aviation or any person authorised by him or her in writing;

**permit** means a permit granted under regulation 23 and shall also be deemed to be a licence for the purposes of any other written law;

**provisional licence** means a licence granted under regulation 13 and includes, where appropriate, a licence with a provisional amendment made to it; and

**scheduled journey** means one of a series of journeys which are undertaken between the same 2 places and which together amount to a systematic service operated in such a manner that the benefits thereof are available to members of the public from time to time seeking to take advantage of them.

## PART 2 LICENCES FOR AIR SERVICES PERFORMED BY AIRCRAFT REGISTERED IN FIJI [regs 3–20]

### reg 3 Application of Part 2

This Part shall not apply to any air service performed by aircraft registered outside Fiji.

### reg 4 Licence required

Subject to the provisions of these Regulations, it shall not be lawful for any person to use any aircraft for the carriage in Fiji of passengers, mail or freight for hire or reward upon any journey between 2 places, of which at least one is in Fiji, except under, and in accordance with, the provisions of a licence or a provisional licence granted by the licensing authority or of a special licence granted by the Permanent Secretary in accordance with regulation 6.

### reg 5 Grant of licences

(1) The licensing authority may grant to any person applying therefor a licence to perform an air service between 2 places, at least one of which is in Fiji, for such period and on such conditions as may be specified in the licence and, if the holder of a licence applies for an amendment of his or her licence, it may vary or amend the licence.

(2) Subject to the provisions of subregulation (3), the licensing authority may attach such conditions to any licence as it may think fit and may, from time to time, vary any such conditions and any of the conditions set out in subregulation (3).

(3) It shall be a condition of every licence that—

(a) neither the holder of the licence nor any other person on his or her behalf for his or her benefit shall—

(i) refuse or cause any other person to refuse, booking facilities to the holder of any other licence; or

(ii) grant or cause any other person to grant such facilities to such other holder only on more onerous terms than those under which the holder's facilities are available;

(b) the holder of the licence shall perform all such reasonable services as the permanent secretary responsible for posts and telecommunications may from time to time require in regard to the conveyance of mails (and of any persons who may be in charge thereof) upon journeys made under the licence. The remuneration for any such services shall be such as may be from time to time determined by agreement between the permanent secretary responsible for posts and telecommunications and the holder of the licence. Any dispute, difference or question which may arise as to the remuneration to be paid to the holder of the licence in respect of such services or as to the rights, duties or liabilities of such holder or of the permanent secretary responsible for posts and telecommunications under or otherwise in relation to any of the matters aforesaid shall, in default of agreement, be referred to a single arbitrator to be appointed jointly by the holder of the licence and the permanent secretary responsible for posts and telecommunications and the provisions of the Arbitration Act 1965 shall apply; and

(c) the holder of the licence shall, before commencing to operate an air service under the licence, obtain a certificate from the Authority that he or she is competent to operate the service in accordance with all written laws regulating civil aviation matters and shall not thereafter continue to operate the service if the Authority at any time notifies the holder that it is no longer so satisfied.

[subreg (3) am LN 169 of 1979 reg 3, effective 1 October 1979]

### reg 6 Special licence

In an emergency, an application may be made to the Permanent Secretary for a special licence for a period not exceeding 14 days, and the Permanent Secretary may, if he or she is satisfied that it is a genuine case of emergency, issue such a special licence, and the provisions of this part as to the publication of particulars of applications, the making of objections and representations, and the holding of inquiries at the instance of the applicant or an objector shall not, in that case, apply.

### reg 7 Applications for licences

Applications for licences shall be made in the form and manner, and shall contain the particulars, prescribed in Schedule 1, provided that the licensing authority may, at any time, require further information to be supplied to it before granting any licence.

### reg 8 Notice of application

(1) An applicant for any licence shall cause to be published, in a newspaper circulating in Fiji, in the manner prescribed in Schedule 2, particulars of his or her application.

(2) An applicant for an amendment of a licence shall cause such particulars of his or her application as the licensing authority shall specify to be published in such manner and in such form as the licensing authority shall direct in each particular case.

### reg 9 Objections

Any person may, in the form and manner, and within the time, prescribed in the Schedule 3, make representations or objections with regard to any application for a licence or for an amendment thereof.

### reg 10 Inquiry

The licensing authority may, if it thinks fit, for the purpose of determining applications for licences or for amendments thereof hold inquiries in public or in private. Before holding any such inquiry the licensing authority shall give to the applicant, and to any person who has made any representations or objections with regard to the application, notice in the form prescribed in Schedule 4 and shall give the applicant and any such person an opportunity of being heard at the inquiry.

### reg 11 Matters to be considered by licensing authority when granting, etc, licences

(1) In exercising its discretion to grant, or to refuse, a licence or to attach conditions to any licence or to amend a licence, the licensing authority shall have regard to the coordination and development of air services generally with the object of ensuring the most effective service to the public while avoiding uneconomical overlapping, and generally to the interests of the public, including those of persons requiring or likely to require facilities for air transport, as well as those of persons providing such facilities. In particular, but without prejudice to the generality of the foregoing, the licensing authority shall have regard to the following matters—

(a) the existence of other air services in the area through which the proposed services are to be operated;

(b) the demand for air transport in that area;

(c) the degree of efficiency and regularity of the air services, if any, already provided in that area, whether by the applicant or by other persons;

(d) the period for which such services have been operated by the applicant or by other persons;

(e) the extent to which it is probable that the applicant will be able to provide a satisfactory service in respect to safety, continuity, regularity of operation, frequency, punctuality, reasonableness of charges, and general efficiency;

(f) the financial resources of the applicant;

(g) the type and number of aircraft proposed to be used;

(h) the remuneration and general conditions of employment of aircrew and other personnel employed by the applicant;

(i) the ground facilities available for the proposed service;

(j) in the case of the Licensing Board, any directions issued to it by the minister,

and the licensing authority shall take into consideration any objections or representations duly made in accordance with the provisions of these Regulations.

### reg 12 Duration of licence

The licensing authority may grant licences to remain in force for such period, not exceeding 5 years, as it may in each case determine, commencing on the date on which the licence is expressed to take effect, provided that, if, on the date of the expiration of a licence, an application to the licensing authority is pending for the grant of a new licence in substitution for an existing licence held by the applicant, the existing licence shall continue in force until such application has been determined.

### reg 13 Provisional licence

The licensing authority may, if it thinks fit, pending the determination of an application for a licence or for an amendment of a licence, grant to the applicant a provisional licence or make a provisional amendment to a licence which shall remain in force until the application is determined.

### reg 14 Power to revoke or suspend in particular circumstances

(1) Subject to the provisions of subregulation (2), the licensing authority may revoke or suspend a licence if—

(a) the holder of the licence has, since the licence was granted, been convicted of an offence against regulation 4; or

(b) where the holder of the licence is a body corporate, any officer of that body corporate has, since the licence was granted, been convicted, in his or her capacity as such officer, of an offence against regulation 4; or

(c) the holder of the licence has failed to comply with any condition subject to which the licence was granted.

(2) Before revoking or suspending any licence under subregulation(1)(c),the licensing authority shall give to the holder such notice as is prescribed in Form 1 of Schedule 5 specifying the grounds upon which it is proposed to revoke or suspend the licence, and it shall not revoke or suspend the licence without first holding a public inquiry if, within the period prescribed in the authority's notice for the submission of representations, the holder of the licence (by such notice, as is prescribed in Form 2 of Schedule 5) requires it so to do, and unless it is satisfied that the licence should be revoked or suspended.

(3) The expression “officer” in subregulation (1)(b) means a director, general manager, secretary or other responsible employee or servant of the body corporate and includes any person, who, with the authority of the body corporate, acts as such officer.

### reg 15 Surrender of licence

A licence may at any time be surrendered by the holder to the licensing authority for cancellation. If, during the currency of a licence, the holder applies to the licensing authority for a new licence in substitution for the current licence, he or she shall, if a new licence is granted, surrender the current licence for cancellation on the date from which the new licence is expressed to take effect.

### reg 16 Fees

(1) There shall be paid to the Permanent Secretary in respect of every licence authorising 5 or more flights a fee of $100 in respect of each year or part of year of the term for which the licence is expressed to remain in force; and in respect of every licence authorising less than 5 flights a fee of $20 in respect of each flight authorised by the licence.

(2) No refund of any fee paid in respect of the grant of a licence shall be made, whether on the surrender of the licence or otherwise, save where a licence is surrendered before its normal date of expiry upon the grant of a new licence authorising a service over the same route, in which case there shall be refunded the sum of $100 for each full year of the unexpired period of the licence but the holder shall in addition to the normal fee for the new licence pay the special additional fee of $20.

### reg 17 Returns by licensee

(1) Every holder of a licence shall make a monthly return in writing in duplicate to the appropriate licensing authority giving, in respect of the month to which the return relates, such of the particulars set out in Schedule 6 as are relevant with regard to all air services authorised by the licence.

(2) Such return shall be sent to the appropriate licensing authority not later than one calendar month after the expiration of the month to which the return relates.

(3) The Licensing Board shall make those returns submitted to it available to the Permanent Secretary.

### reg 18 Air Transport Licensing Board

(1) The Licensing Board shall consist of such number of members, not being less than 3 but not more than 7, as the minister may from time to time determine, and shall be known as the “Air Transport Licensing Board”.

(2) The chairperson and every other member of the Licensing Board shall be appointed by the minister and, unless he or she shall earlier resign his or her office by writing under his or her hand addressed to the minister, shall hold office for such term as the minister shall, at the time of making the appointment, determine but shall be eligible for reappointment from time to time on the expiration of his or her term of office.

(3) At all meetings of the Licensing Board, the chairperson, or, in his or her absence, such member as the other members present may select, shall preside.

(4) When the minister proposes to appoint a person to be a member of the Licensing Board, he or she shall, before making the appointment, require that person to declare whether he or she has any, and if so what, interest in any undertaking which provides transport for passengers or goods or which owns or operates airports, manufactures aircraft, aircraft engines or accessories, or supplies aircraft fuel or lubricants. If any member of the Licensing Board acquires any such interest, he or she shall, within 4 weeks after so doing, give notice thereof in writing to the minister specifying the interest so acquired, and the minister, after taking the matter into consideration, may, if he or she thinks fit, declare that the member has vacated his or her office and the seat of such member shall thereupon become vacant.

(5)

(a) The minister may appoint a person to be secretary to the Licensing Board.

(b) The Permanent Secretary may appoint such officers and servants as are, in the opinion of the minister, necessary to enable the Licensing Board to discharge its duties.

[subreg (5) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 30, effective 1 December 2016]

(6) The minister may issue to the Licensing Board such general or specific directions as he or she may think fit.

(7) The Licensing Authority shall regulate its procedure subject to any directions given to it in respect thereof under subregulation (6), provided that the quorum at meetings of the Licensing Board shall be 3.

### reg 19 Licensing Board to publish decisions

The Licensing Board shall cause to be published in the manner prescribed in Schedule 7 such particulars of its decisions on applications for licences and of its decisions to revoke or suspend licences, and to such extent as it shall think fit, of its decisions on applications for amendments of licences.

### reg 20 Annual report of Licensing Board

The Licensing Board shall make an annual report to the minister on the exercise of its functions during the year.

## PART 3 PERMITS FOR AIR SERVICES PERFORMED BY AIRCRAFT REGISTERED OUTSIDE FIJI [regs 21–25]

### reg 21 Application of Part 3

This Part shall not apply to air services performed by aircraft registered in Fiji.

### reg 22 Permit required

(1) Subject to the provisions of these Regulations, it shall not be lawful for any person to use any aircraft registered outside Fiji, for the provision of an air service between any 2 places, of which at least one is in Fiji, except under and in accordance with the conditions of a permit granted by the Minister.

(2) The provisions of this regulation shall not apply in respect of any journey performed on the authority of Article 5 of the Convention on International Civil Aviation if the aircraft performing the journey flies across Fiji without landing or lands in Fiji for non-traffic purposes only.

(3) The provisions of this regulation shall not apply in respect of the aircraft of any state or territory which is a party to the International Air Services Transit Agreement, which fly across Fiji without landing or land in Fiji in accordance with the provisions of that agreement.

### reg 23 Grant of permits

(1) The minister may grant to any person, or to any person applying therefor, a permit to use any aircraft for the provision of any air services to which this Part applies for such period and on such conditions as may be specified in the permit.

(2) The Minister may attach such conditions to any permit as he or she may think fit.

### reg 24 Particulars to be supplied to Minister

(1) Applications for permits shall be made in such form, and shall contain such information, as the minister may require in each particular case.

(2) Nothing in this regulation shall prevent the minister, if he or she thinks fit, from granting a permit to any person notwithstanding that no application therefore has been made.

### reg 25 Revocation, suspension and variation of permits

The minister may at any time revoke, suspend or vary a permit granted under this Part, if he or she considers it appropriate to do so, whether or not an application with respect to the permit has been made by the holder.

## PART 4 GENERAL PROVISIONS [regs 26–31]

### reg 26 All written laws to apply to licences etc

It shall be a condition of every licence or permit issued under these Regulations that the requirements of all written laws relating to civil aviation shall be complied with at all times during the currency of the licence or permit.

### reg 27 Transfer of licences etc

A licence or permit issued under these Regulations shall not be capable of being transferred or assigned, provided that, in the event of the death, incapacity, bankruptcy, sequestration or liquidation of the holder of a licence or permit, or of the appointment of a receiver or manager or trustee in relation to the business of the holder of a licence or permit, the person for the time being carrying on that business shall, if, within 14 days of his or her commencing so to do, he or she makes application to the Licensing Board or the minister, as the case may be, for a new licence or permit, be entitled to provide the air services authorised by the existing licence or permit subject to the conditions thereof, until such application is determined.

### reg 28 Disclosure of financial resources

Nothing in these Regulations shall require the disclosure by any applicant for a licence or permit to any person other than the Licensing Board, the minister or the Permanent Secretary, as the case may be, of information as to the financial resources of the applicant, and any such information received by the Licensing Board, the minister or the Permanent Secretary, from any such applicant shall be treated as confidential.

### reg 29 Application of certain provisions to provisional and special licences

Any reference to a licence in regulation 5(2) and (3), in regulation 14(1), in regulations 17, 26, 28 and 30, or in regulation 31(3) shall be construed as including reference, so far as is appropriate, to a provisional licence or a special licence and, in the regulations cited above, references to the licensing authority or the Licensing Board shall be construed as references to the Permanent Secretary in the case of special licences.

### reg 30 Licences etc not to create rights

Nothing in these Regulations shall confer upon the holder of a licence or permit or upon any other person any right to the continuance of any benefits arising from the provisions of these Regulations or from any licence or permit granted thereunder or from any conditions attached to any such licence or permit.

### reg 31 Penalties etc

(1) Any person who uses any aircraft in contravention of the provisions of regulation 4 or 22 shall be guilty of an offence and shall be liable, on conviction, in the case of a first offence, to a fine not exceeding $1,000 or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding 3 months or to both such fine and such imprisonment, and, in the case of a second or subsequent offence, to a fine not exceeding $10,000 or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding 2 years or to both such fine and such imprisonment.

(2) Proceedings for an offence against regulation 22 shall not be instituted without the written authority of the Director of Public Prosecutions.

(3) Any person who commits any offence against any of these Regulations other than regulations 4 and 22 or who fails to comply with any of the conditions of any licence or permit issued under these Regulations shall be guilty of an offence and shall be liable, on conviction, to a fine not exceeding $1,000 or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding 3 months or to both such fine and such imprisonment.

(4) Where an offence against these Regulations has been committed by a body corporate, every person who, at the time of the commission of the offence, was a director or general manager, or who was purporting to act in any such capacity, shall be deemed to be guilty of that offence, unless he or she proves that the offence was committed without his or her consent or connivance and that he or she exercised all such diligence to prevent the commission of the offence as he or she ought to have exercised having regard to the nature of his or her functions in that capacity and to all the circumstances.

NOTICE BY THE LICENSING BOARD OF ITS DECISION ON AN APPLICATION FOR A LICENCE AND/OR ITS DECISION TO REVOKE OR SUSPEND A LICENCE

1 The notice shall be inserted in a newspaper published and circulating in Fiji.

2 In the case of a decision to grant an application, the following particulars shall be inserted in the notice—

(a) name and address of applicant;

(b) date on which the application was made;

(c) places at which landings are to be made for the purpose of loading or landing passengers or goods;

(d) such other information as will enable the licensee to be clearly identified.

3 In the case of a decision to refuse an application, particulars (a) and (b) of paragraph 2 together with the fact that the application has been refused shall be inserted in the notice.

4 In the case of a decision to revoke or suspend a licence, the grounds on which the licence is suspended or revoked shall be inserted in the notice, in addition to the particulars specified in paragraph 2 and 3.
