## PART 1 PRELIMINARY [regs 1–4]

### reg 1 Short title

These Regulations may be cited as the Civil Service (General) Regulations 1999.

[reg 1 am Act 2 of 2016 s 20, effective 16 February 2016]

### reg 2 Commencement

These Regulations commence on the date on which Part 3 of the Act commences.

### reg 3 Interpretation

In these Regulations, unless the context otherwise requires—

**Act** means the Civil Service Act 1999;

[def am Act 2 of 2016 s 20, effective 16 February 2016]

**Commission** means the Public Service Commission;

[def am Act 2 of 2016 s 20, effective 16 February 2016]

**investigation** means an investigation conducted and documented in accordance with written guidelines or policies issued by the Commission;

[def insrt LN 34 of 2017 reg 2, effective 23 May 2017]

**local authority** means a council of a city, town or district;

**station** includes an area declared to be a station by the Commission; and

**transfer** does not include the movement of an employee between positions in the same department unless the movement requires the employee to change his or her residence from one station to another.

[reg 3 am Act 17 of 2004 Sch 2, effective 1 January 2005]

### reg 4 Interpretation Act 1967

The Interpretation Act 1967 applies to these Regulations in the same way as it applies to every other written law within the meaning of that Act.

## PART 2 APPOINTMENTS, PROMOTIONS AND TRANSFERS [regs 5–13]

### DIVISION 1 APPOINTMENT AND PROMOTION [regs 5–8]

#### reg 5 Appointment and promotion based on merit

(1) The appointment or promotion of a person to an office must be made on the basis of merit after an open, competitive selection process.

[subreg (1) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 34, effective 1 December 2016]

(2) An appointment or promotion may only be made if—

(a) the vacancy was openly advertised in accordance with such guidelines as may be issued from time to time;

(b) an assessment has been made of the relative suitability of the candidates for the duties, after an open and competitive selection process;

(c) the assessment was based on the relationship between the candidate's work-related qualities and the work-related qualities genuinely required for the duties; and

(d) the assessment focused on the relative capacity of the candidates to perform the duties.

[subreg (2) am Act 2 of 2016 s 20, effective 16 February 2016; LN 99 of 2016 reg 34, effective 1 December 2016]

(3) The following work-related qualities may be taken into account in making an assessment referred to in subregulation (2) —

(a) skills and abilities;

(b) qualifications, training and competencies;

(c) standard of work performance;

(d) capacity to perform at the level required;

(e) demonstrated potential for further development; or

(f) ability to contribute to team performance.

(4) Subregulation (3) does not prevent any other relevant matter being taken into account.

(5)

[subreg (5) rep LN 99 of 2016 reg 34, effective 1 December 2016]

(6) In this regulation, vacancy includes a vacancy that has not commenced and a prospective vacancy.

#### reg 6 Dual appointments

If a permanent secretary of a ministry (the first ministry) is also appointed as permanent secretary of another ministry, the permanent secretary does not merely because of the additional appointment cease to be the permanent secretary of the first ministry.

[reg 6 am Act 17 of 2004 Sch 2, effective 1 January 2005; LN 99 of 2016 reg 34, effective 1 December 2016]

#### reg 7 Civil service employment

(1) A permanent secretary may, with the agreement of the Minister responsible for the ministry, permanently employ a person to perform duties in the civil service until such time the person resigns, retires or is otherwise terminated.

(2) The continuation of employment of a person employed under subregulation (1) is subject to the performance of the person in accordance with any performance management system guideline issued by the permanent secretary.

[reg 7 subst LN 20 of 2023 reg 2, effective 1 January 2023]

#### reg 8 Temporary employment

(1) A permanent secretary, with the agreement of the Minister responsible for the ministry, may employ a person to perform duties temporarily in a department.

[subreg (1) am Act 17 of 2004 Sch 2, effective 1 January 2005; LN 99 of 2016 reg 34, effective 1 December 2016]

(2) A person may be employed under subregulation (1) only if the permanent secretary is satisfied that—

(a) the employment is necessary for the efficient operation of the department, and assistance in temporarily performing the duties cannot conveniently be given by another department; or

(b) the person should not be appointed to an office in the civil service because of a physical or mental incapacity or a medical condition.

[subreg (2) am Act 17 of 2004 Sch 2, effective 1 January 2005; Act 2 of 2016 s 20, effective 16 February 2016; LN 99 of 2016 reg 34, effective 1 December 2016]

(3) A person employed under this regulation is to be paid at a rate determined by the requirements of the position they occupy, as decided by the permanent secretary with the agreement of the Minister responsible for the ministry.

[subreg (3) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 34, effective 1 December 2016; LN 34 of 2017 reg 4, effective 23 May 2017]

### DIVISION 2 PROBATION [regs 9–12]

#### reg 9 Appointments to be on probation

(1) A person appointed to the civil service as an employee may be on probation at the commencement of his or her employment in the civil service.

[subreg (1) am Act 2 of 2016 s 20, effective 16 February 2016; subst LN 34 of 2017 reg 5, effective 23 May 2017]

(2) The period of probation must not exceed 6 months.

[subreg (2) subst LN 20 of 2023 reg 3, effective 1 January 2023]

(3) A permanent secretary may confirm the appointment of a person before the end of the period of probation.

[subreg (3) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 34, effective 1 December 2016]

### DIVISION 3 TRANSFERS [regs 13]

#### reg 13 Notice of transfer etc

The permanent secretary may transfer an employee without the employee's agreement only if the permanent secretary has—

(a) given the employee 28 days written notice of the transfer;

(b) given the employee an opportunity to state his or her views about the transfer; and

(c) considered any views stated by the employee.

[reg 13 am LN 99 of 2016 reg 34, effective 1 December 2016]

## PART 3 RETIREMENT, RETRENCHMENT AND TERMINATION [regs 14–17]

### reg 14 Retirement age

(1) An employee must be retired from the civil service on reaching 60 years, unless the Constitution or any other written law specifies a different age in respect of any employee.

[subreg (1) am LN 55 of 2001 s 2, effective 1 June 2001; LN 36 of 2007 s 2, effective 31 March 2007; Act 2 of 2016 s 20, effective 16 February 2016; LN 20 of 2023 reg 4, effective 1 January 2023]

(2) Subregulation (1) does not apply to an employee if the permanent secretary, in agreement with the Minister responsible for the ministry and with the approval of the Minister responsible for civil service, determines that the employee is to be retained in the interests of the civil service.

[subreg (2) am Act 2 of 2016 s 20, effective 16 February 2016; LN 99 of 2016 reg 34, effective 1 December 2016; subst LN 34 of 2017 reg 6, effective 23 May 2017]

(3) In determining whether approval may be given under subregulation (2), the Minister responsible for civil service must consider—

(a) the lack of suitably qualified candidates for the position where there is confirmation of the scarcity of the relevant skill;

(b) whether there are development and succession plans in place for the position to ensure future recruitment for the position; and

(c) whether there are clear plans in place to phase out the retention of the employee over a period not exceeding one year.

[subreg (3) subst LN 34 of 2017 reg 6, effective 23 May 2017; am LN 20 of 2023 reg 4, effective 1 January 2023]

(4) This regulation does not prevent an employee from retiring at the age when an employee can lawfully retire under any written law relating to the grant of pensions, gratuities or compensation.

[subreg (4) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 34, effective 1 December 2016]

(5)

[subreg (5) rep LN 36 of 2007 s 2, effective 31 March 2007]

### reg 15 Compulsory retirement or retrenchment of excess employees

A permanent secretary, with the agreement of the Minister responsible for the ministry, may retire or retrench an employee from the civil service if the permanent secretary is satisfied that the employee is excess to the requirements of the employee’s department because—

(a) the department employs more employees than it needs for the effective, efficient, economical and appropriate performance of its functions; or

(b) the duties performed by the employee are no longer required.

[reg 15 am Act 17 of 2004 Sch 2, effective 1 January 2005; Act 2 of 2016 s 20, effective 16 February 2016; LN 99 of 2016 reg 34, effective 1 December 2016]

### reg 16 Compulsory retirement for ill-health

(1) A permanent secretary, with the agreement of the Minister responsible for the ministry, may retire an employee from the civil service if the permanent secretary is satisfied that the employee is unable to perform his or her duties, or any other available duties appropriate to the employee's classification, because of physical or mental incapacity.

[subreg (1) am Act 2 of 2016 s 20, effective 16 February 2016; LN 99 of 2016 reg 34, effective 1 December 2016]

(2) For the purpose of subregulation (1), the permanent secretary may require an employee to undergo examination by a medical board consisting of 3 members each of whom is a qualified medical practitioner.

[subreg (2) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 34, effective 1 December 2016]

(3) Retirement of an employee under this regulation does not take effect until the day immediately after the day when the employee exhausts any sick leave credit to which the employee is entitled before retirement.

### reg 17 Compulsory retirement or termination in the public interest

(1) A permanent secretary, with the agreement of the Minister responsible for the ministry, may retire an employee from the civil service, or terminate an employee's employment, if the permanent secretary is satisfied that the retirement or termination is in the public interest.

[subreg (1) am Act 2 of 2016 s 20, effective 16 February 2016; LN 99 of 2016 reg 34, effective 1 December 2016]

## PART 4 CONDUCT [regs 18–21]

### reg 18 Engaging in another occupation for reward

An employee must not engage in any occupation for reward outside the civil service without the written agreement of the permanent secretary.

[reg 18 am Act 2 of 2016 s 20, effective 16 February 2016; LN 99 of 2016 reg 34, effective 1 December 2016]

### reg 19 Holding office in public authority

An employee must not accept or continue to hold an office in a local authority without the written agreement of the permanent secretary.

[reg 19 am LN 99 of 2016 reg 34, effective 1 December 2016]

### reg 20 Absence without leave

(1) This regulation applies to an employee who is absent from duty without leave and does not have a reasonable excuse for the absence.

(2) The employee is not entitled to remuneration for the period of the absence.

(3) If, within 7 days of the beginning of the absence, the employee does not tell the permanent secretary the reason for the absence and the expected date of his or her return to duty, the employee is taken to have resigned from the civil service and forfeits all rights and entitlements in relation to the employee's civil service employment.

[subreg (3) am Act 2 of 2016 s 20, effective 16 February 2016; LN 99 of 2016 reg 34, effective 1 December 2016]

(4) The permanent secretary may decide that—

(a) subregulation (3) does not apply to the employee; or

(b) subregulation (3) applies to the employee, but the employee does not forfeit all or particular rights and entitlements in relation to the employee's civil service employment.

[subreg (4) am Act 2 of 2016 s 20, effective 16 February 2016; LN 99 of 2016 reg 34, effective 1 December 2016]

(5) This regulation does not prevent the employee from being disciplined under these regulations for the absence.

### reg 21 Employee to report legal proceedings

An employee who becomes subject to criminal proceedings in Fiji or elsewhere for an offence that carries a penalty of imprisonment in Fiji must immediately and in writing inform the permanent secretary of his or her ministry.

[reg 21 am Act 17 of 2004 Sch 2, effective 1 January 2005; LN 99 of 2016 reg 34, effective 1 December 2016]

## PART 5 DISCIPLINE [regs 22, 23]

### reg 22 Disciplinary action

(1) If a disciplinary action is instituted against an employee, then the Public Service Disciplinary Tribunal upon being satisfied that the employee has breached the Civil Service Code of Conduct, may direct the permanent secretary to implement one or more of the following penalties—

(a) terminate the employee's employment;

(b) demote the employee, provided that the disciplinary charge is related to performance and the employee had a good performance record at a lower level prior to promotion;

(c) transfer or redeploy the employee to other duties, provided that the disciplinary charges relate to the specific location of the employee;

(d) defer a merit increase in remuneration for the employee for a specified period;

(e) reduce the level of the employee's remuneration, provided that the reduction is within the salary band or classification for the position occupied;

(f) impose a penalty of not more than 10% of the employee’s annual salary;

(g) reprimand the employee; or

(h) forfeit all or part of the employee's remuneration which was withheld during the period of suspension from duty.

[subreg (1) am LN 89 of 2008 reg 2, effective 25 July 2008; Act 2 of 2016 s 20, effective 16 February 2016; LN 99 of 2016 reg 34, effective 1 December 2016; LN 34 of 2017 reg 7, effective 23 May 2017]

(2) A permanent secretary must implement the penalty that the Public Service Disciplinary Tribunal directs the permanent secretary to implement under subregulation (1).

[subreg (2) insrt LN 34 of 2017 reg 7, effective 23 May 2017]

(3) In this Part, “employee” includes a wage earner.

[subreg (4) renum as subreg (3) LN 34 of 2017 reg 7, effective 23 May 2017]

### reg 23 Suspension

(1) The permanent secretary may suspend an employee at any stage during the disciplinary process.

(2) In determining whether suspension is warranted, the permanent secretary must consider whether—

(a) the presence of the employee could compromise safety in the workplace;

(b) the presence of the employee could compromise or hinder the investigation; or

(c) there are concerns about the preservation or integrity of the ministry’s information, property or ability to provide services to the public.

(3) If the permanent secretary decides to suspend an employee, the permanent secretary must confirm the decision to suspend to the employee in writing, including all relevant details of the suspension.

(4) A period of suspension while the matter is under investigation by the ministry is on full pay.

(5) Upon completing the investigation, the permanent secretary of the relevant ministry or the Commission may decide to institute disciplinary action by referring the case to the Public Service Disciplinary Tribunal.

(6) Subject to subregulations (7) and (8), a referral to the Public Service Disciplinary Tribunal has the effect of suspending the employee commencing from the date the case is referred and the suspension must initially be on no pay, provided however that where the employee occupies staff quarters or receives a housing allowance, then that employee continues to be entitled to occupy the staff quarters or receive the housing allowance until the determination of the disciplinary charge by the Public Service Disciplinary Tribunal.

(7) The Public Service Disciplinary Tribunal may, on the application of the employee, reinstitute the payment of an employee’s salary following a request by the employee to the Public Service Disciplinary Tribunal in accordance with the Public Service Disciplinary Tribunal’s procedures.

(8) Where an employee occupies staff quarters or receives a housing allowance, the Public Service Disciplinary Tribunal may, on the application of the employing authority, direct the employee to vacate the staff quarters or direct the suspension of the housing allowance.

[reg 23 subst LN 34 of 2017 reg 8, effective 23 May 2017]

## PART 6 ANNUAL REPORTS, CORPORATE PLANS AND PERFORMANCE IMPROVEMENT PROGRAMMES [regs 24–26]

### reg 24 Annual reports

(1) This regulation applies to an annual report that the permanent secretary of a ministry is required to prepare under the Financial Management Act 2004.

[subreg (1) subst Act 17 of 2004 Sch 2, effective 1 January 2005; am LN 99 of 2016 reg 34, effective 1 December 2016]

(2) A report referred to in subregulation (l) must include in respect of the relevant year—

(a) an evaluation of the extent to which the ministry has incorporated the Civil Service Values;

(b) an evaluation of the adequacy of systems and procedures in the ministry for ensuring compliance with the Civil Service Code of Conduct;

(c) an evaluation of the handling of grievances in the ministry during the year;

(d) details of initiatives in employment policies and practices in the ministry during the year; and

(e) an analysis of trends in the size, structure and composition of the ministry.

[subreg (2) am Act 17 of 2004 Sch 2, effective 1 January 2005; Act 2 of 2016 s 20, effective 16 February 2016; LN 99 of 2016 reg 34, effective 1 December 2016]

(3) Subregulation (2) does not limit the matters that must or may be included in a report under subregulation (1).

(4) This regulation is in addition to, and does not alter, any reporting requirements under the Financial Management Act 2004.

[subreg (4) am Act 17 of 2004 Sch 2, effective 1 January 2005]

### reg 25 Corporate plans

(1) This regulation applies to an annual corporate plan that the permanent secretary of a ministry is required to prepare under the Financial Management Act 2004.

[subreg (1) subst Act 17 of 2004 Sch 2, effective 1 January 2005; am LN 99 of 2016 reg 34, effective 1 December 2016]

(2) The annual corporate plan must include details of human resources and employee relations strategies.

[subreg (2) am Act 17 of 2004 Sch 2, effective 1 January 2005]

(3)

[subreg (3) rep Act 17 of 2004 Sch 2, effective 1 January 2005]

### reg 26 Performance improvement programmes

(1) The permanent secretary responsible for each ministry must establish a performance improvement programme for his or her ministry.

[subreg (1) subst Act 17 of 2004 Sch 2, effective 1 January 2005; am LN 99 of 2016 reg 34, effective 1 December 2016]

(2) The performance improvement programme established under subregulation (1) should aim to foster a high performance culture in the ministry by—

(a) improving communication between supervisors and employees;

(b) providing employees with regular feedback about their performance;

(c) providing a good understanding of work responsibilities and results expected to be achieved;

(d) identifying employees’ training and development needs and ways these can be met;

(e) providing a method for deciding performance pay in accordance with written guidelines issued by the Commission; and

(f) establishing effective processes for managing poor performance.

[subreg (2) am Act 17 of 2004 Sch 2, effective 1 January 2005; LN 99 of 2016 reg 34, effective 1 December 2016; LN 34 of 2017 reg 9, effective 23 May 2017]

(3) The performance improvement programme should provide for—

(a) the development of performance expectations (which may be in the form of a performance agreement) and appropriate performance assessment criteria;

(b) review of performance between an employee and his or her supervisor; and

(c) a regular assessment cycle that provides for—

i. review of an employee's overall performance during the cycle; and

ii. consideration of an employee's training and development needs.

(4) Processes for managing poor performance as mentioned in subregulation (2)(f) should—

(a) be fair, streamlined and efficient;

(b) ensure that an employee is given a reasonable opportunity to improve;

(c) balance the needs of the ministry and the employee;

(d) be consistent with any applicable laws about discrimination, record keeping and privacy;

(e) ensure that an employee is given full information about job requirements, performance expectations, deficiencies in his or her performance and possible consequences; and

(f) be in accordance with any further written guidelines and instructions issued by the Commission.

[subreg (4) am Act 17 of 2004 Sch 2, effective 1 January 2005; LN 99 of 2016 reg 34, effective 1 December 2016; LN 34 of 2017 reg 9, effective 23 May 2017]
