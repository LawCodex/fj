## PART 1 PRELIMINARY [regs 1–3]

### reg 1 Short title and commencement

These Regulations may be cited as the Civil Service (Diplomatic and Consular Service) Regulations 2006 and come into force on 1 January 2006.

[reg 1 am Act 2 of 2016 s 20, effective 16 February 2016]

### reg 2 Interpretation

(1) In these Regulations, unless the context otherwise requires—

**Chief Executive Officer** [def rep LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

**Commission** [def rep LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

**Constitution** means the Constitution of the Republic of Fiji;

[def am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

**cost of education** means that cost to the parent of basic tuition of his or her child in subjects forming an essential part of the Fijian School curriculum or the normal school curriculum in the State system of education of the country concerned, including—

(a) the cost of essential text books, materials and stationery where separately charged for; and

(b) the cost of related registration examination, compulsory subscriptions and school bus fees in excess of F$120 per dependant child per school year,

but does not include the cost of school clothing, school meals, games, equipment, special subjects, individual coaching, musical instruments, mileage allowance claim in respect of the private vehicles to transport child to and from school, special recreational activities, insurance (except where compulsory) and personal expenditure, and fees for testing, therapy and boarding;

**dependant** means an officer's spouse who must be in resident at the post and any dependant child;

**dependant child** means the child of or the legally adopted child of a Head of Mission or an officer or spouse, (where the child was adopted at least one year before posting) who is—

(a) wholly dependant upon the Head of Mission or an officer for support; and

(b) has not reached his or her 16th birthday; or

(c) wholly dependant upon the Head of Mission or an officer for support;

(d) is a full-time student of a primary or secondary school; and

(e) has not reached his or her 19th birthday; or

(f) is 16 or more years of age; and

(g) is wholly dependant upon the Head of Mission or an officer for support by reason of physical or mental infirmity, as evidenced by a certificate of a duly registered medical practitioner;

**Head of Mission** means a Fijian principal representative, High Commissioner, Permanent Representative or Ambassador in another country or international organisation who is responsible for the operation of an overseas mission;

**living expenses** means the cost of accommodation, 3 meals a day, and reasonable incidentals, excluding liquor or cigarettes. They also include the cost of essential transport between the hotel and the airport, including porterage charges;

**locally recruited** staff means a person other than the Head of Mission or officer employed in an overseas mission under regulation 6(5);

**the Minister** means the Minister responsible for foreign affairs;

[def am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

**near relative** means the spouse, a parent, or a child of a Head of Mission or an Officer;

**officer** means a person other than the Head of Mission serving in an overseas mission and occupying a position listed under regulation 4(1);

**overseas mission** means a Fijian Government diplomatic and trade offices abroad;

**Permanent Secretary** means the permanent secretary responsible for foreign affairs;

[def insrt LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

**school** means an institution solely concerned with education accepting pupils at primary and secondary but excluding post-secondary levels.

(2) For the purpose of the definition of “dependant child”, where an officer is divorced or separated, only a child in his or her legal custody will qualify as a dependant child.

### reg 3 Application

These Regulations apply to the Head of Mission and officers serving in any overseas mission including where applicable, the locally recruited staff.

## PART 2 COMPOSITION OF OVERSEAS MISSIONS [regs 4, 5]

### reg 4 Composition

(1) Positions in an overseas mission may consist of—

(a) the Head of Mission;

(b) the Deputy Head of Mission;

(c) the Consular;

(d) the First Secretary;

(e) the Second Secretary;

(f) the Third Secretary; and

(g) the Attache.

(2) The Permanent Secretary may, with the agreement of the Minister, establish, abolish or amalgamate one or more positions listed under subregulation (1) in an overseas mission.

[subreg (2) am Act 2 of 2016 s 20, effective 16 February 2016; LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

(3) In exercise of the authority under subregulation (2), the Permanent Secretary must take into account the financial implications and current human resources needs of the overseas mission concerned.

[subreg (3) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

### reg 5 Overseas mission

The Minister with the approval of Cabinet, must by notice in the Gazette publish the creation or abolishment of an overseas mission.

[reg 5 am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

## PART 3 SELECTION AND APPOINTMENTS [regs 6, 7]

### reg 6 Selection and appointment

(1) In accordance with section 128 of the Constitution of the Republic of Fiji, the Prime Minister appoints Heads of Missions, Deputy Heads of Missions and Consulars.

[subreg (1) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

(2)

[subreg (2) rep LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

(3) The selection and appointment of any serving legal professional officer in the LG classification to serve in an overseas mission must be made in accordance with the approval of the Solicitor-General.

[subreg (3) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

(4) The Permanent Secretary may, in consultation with the permanent secretary of the relevant Ministry, select and appoint officers in the grade SS01 or equivalent or below that grade to serve as an officer in an overseas mission.

[subreg (4) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

(5) The Permanent Secretary may, in consultation with the Head of Mission, appoint any person on contract basis to serve as locally recruited staff in any overseas mission.

[subreg (5) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

(6) The contract of a locally recruited staff under subregulation (5) must include the following—

(a) the title and location of the position;

(b) the duration of the contract;

(c) the remuneration and other benefits;

(d) the duties and responsibilities;

(e) the resignation and termination of contract; and

(f) any other terms and conditions deemed necessary.

### reg 7 Duration of Service

(1) The duration of service of a Head of Mission and an officer in an overseas mission unless otherwise stated in his or her letter of appointment or contract of service is 4 years.

(2) The Permanent Secretary may, on his or her own volition or in consultation with the appropriate appointing authority, extend the duration of service of an officer other than the Head of Mission.

[subreg (2) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

(3) An officer posted to an overseas mission will be required to take home leave if he or she serves 2 consecutive tours.

(4) The extension of the duration of service of a Head of Mission must be made in accordance with the provision of section 128 of the Constitution.

[subreg (4) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

## PART 4 GENERAL INSTRUCTIONS [regs 8–30]

### reg 8 Conduct

An officer appointed to represent the Fijian Government abroad is expected at all times to maintain an exemplary standard of conduct and diligently and faithfully perform any duties on which the Government may think it desirable to employ him or her. He or she must not engage, in any activity that would tend to impair his or her usefulness in the position which he or she is holding or make use of his or her official position to further his or her private interests.

### reg 9 Official Secrets Act 1911 (UK)

It is the duty of all officers to acquaint themselves with the provisions of the Official Secrets Act 1911 (UK) and to observe strictly the rules on the security of premises, documents, and the handling of classified information that are issued from time to time.

### reg 10 Disclosure of interest

An officer who in his or her official capacity comes in contact with information concerning a business concern in which he or she has an interest must disclose the nature of that information to the Permanent Secretary through his or her Head of Mission, who must decide on the course of action to take.

[reg 10 am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

### reg 11 Employment of spouses

(1) The spouse of an officer must not accept paid employment in the country in which the officer is serving overseas without first obtaining the permission of the Head of Mission, who should obtain the approval of the Permanent Secretary in the case of an officer holding diplomatic or consular position.

[subreg (1) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

(2) As the spouses of diplomatic and consular officers at overseas posts are expected to make full contribution to representational activities it is unlikely that they will in many cases be in a position to accept gainful private employment.

(3) However, if the employment contemplated is not incompatible with the status and dignity of a diplomatic officer, and if it is not so demanding on the time of a spouse as to make it impossible for the spouse to fulfil appropriate representational duties adequately, permission may be given for the spouse to take paid employment, provided that (see also regulation 52)—

(a) if the authorities of the receiving government have no objection (the protocol division of the local foreign ministry must be consulted);

(b) if a waiver of immunity, and payment of tax on the remuneration for the employment is requested this must be agreed to (see also regulation 22).

[reg 11 am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

### reg 12 Indebtedness

(1) An officer must not assume financial commitments which extend beyond the term of his or her assignment.

(2) Where an officer serving overseas becomes indebted to the extent that he or she can no longer meet his or her financial obligations he or she should at once report the circumstances to the Permanent Secretary through his or her Head of Mission.

[subreg (2) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

(3) If the officer has acted dishonestly or in any way discreditably, the officer may be recalled summarily.

### reg 13 Public statements

No officer may make any public statement or speech, or give any interview to the press, unless with the prior authorisation of the Head of Mission.

### reg 14 Publication of articles and books

(1) An officer who wishes to publish a book or article which—

(a) is based in whole or in part upon the officer's experience in government service;

(b) draws upon or might be thought to draw upon knowledge gained during the officer's employment in the service; or

(c) deals with matters of current domestic or international political controversy,

must submit a draft manuscript to the Permanent Secretary through his or her Head of Mission for approval.

[subreg (1) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

(2) Publication must not take place before that approval is granted.

### reg 15 Marriage

(1) Any officer who proposes to marry a citizen of another country whilst posted to an overseas mission must, in writing, notify the Permanent Secretary of his or her Ministry through the Permanent Secretary as soon as possible.

[subreg (1) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

(2) The marriage of an officer to a citizen of another country may limit the officer's usefulness to his or her department, and the officer may, at the discretion of the Permanent Secretary, be called upon to accept transfer to another department.

[subreg (2) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

### reg 16 Divorce

(1) An officer who becomes involved in a divorce proceeding with his or her spouse, the officer must, as soon as possible and in writing notify the Permanent Secretary through his or her Head of Mission.

[subreg (1) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

(2) If the divorce proceeding will bring discredit upon the officer, or upon Fiji, the officer may be disciplined or transferred to another department.

### reg 17 Gifts and presentations

(1) No officer or member of his or her family may accept gifts or presentations, whether in the form of money, goods, free passages or other benefits from any person, organisation or firm with whom the officer is in contact by virtue of his or her official position, without the approval of the Head of Mission except small personal gifts from colleagues or friends.

(2) Officers serving overseas may not retain any fees for talks or speeches given in the country of posting or for articles for local publications.

### reg 18 Acceptance of foreign decoration

(1) All proposals for the bestowal of foreign decorations on Fijian officers should be referred immediately to the Permanent Secretary.

[subreg (1) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

(2) No officer serving abroad is eligible for the President's permission to accept and wear decorations or medals awarded by other States or governments, except where these are conferred for services in saving or attempting to save lives.

(3) Where the bestowal of a decoration on an officer is contemplated, notice must be first given to the Fijian Government before any final decision to confer it is made.

(4) If the permission to accept the decoration is not likely to be granted, the local authority concerned must be advised accordingly.

### reg 19 Diplomatic privileges and immunities

The members of diplomatic missions overseas enjoy under international law a special status which confers upon them certain immunities and privileges, not for the personal benefit of the individual in question but for the benefit of the Fijian Government, in order that such members may fulfil their diplomatic duties with necessary independence.

### reg 20 Persons to whom diplomatic immunities are extended

(1) The personal inviolability and jurisdictional immunities described in regulation 21 are accorded to the members of the staff of a mission having diplomatic rank and to members of their families who are residing with or are dependent on them except that members of the family of an officer with diplomatic rank, who are nationals of the local state, are not entitled to immunity from criminal or civil jurisdictions, or personal inviolability.

(2) The members of the clerical, administrative and technical staff of a Mission who do not have diplomatic rank and are not nationals of the local State enjoy personal inviolability except that in some States and in the Vienna Convention countries accord immunity from the local civil jurisdiction only in relation to acts performed in the course of their duties, and the Vienna Convention countries accord complete immunity from the local criminal jurisdiction.

(3) The members of the staff of a mission who—

(a) are not of a diplomatic rank; and

(b) are nationals of or permanent residents in the local State,

enjoy only such limited immunities are as are permitted to them by the law of the local State except that—

(a) the jurisdictional status may vary from country to country; and

(b) under the Vienna Convention that the local State may not exercise its jurisdiction over such persons in a manner that would interfere unduly with the performance of the functions of the mission.

(4) It is difficult to generalise about the status of service staff, such as chauffeurs, and domestic employees in the service of the mission itself (in the chancery or otherwise), and domestic employee employed privately by members of the mission.

(5) The Vienna Convention countries are required to accord immunity in respect of official's acts to employee employed in the chancery, but not to others, and in non-Vienna countries, the position of employee will depend on local law and practice, in most such countries some classes of employee will enjoy immunity.

### reg 21 Inviolability and immunity from local jurisdiction

(1) The Head of Mission and staff members having diplomatic rank enjoy personal inviolability.

(2) The personal inviolability from the receiving State's point of view means—

(a) the obligation to respect, and to ensure respect for the person or the individual concerned;

(b) exempt from any form of arrest or detention;

(c) immune from the criminal jurisdiction of the courts of the receiving State;

(d) for civil jurisdiction of the local courts, the traditional international law has accorded diplomatic personnel complete immunity, which should be the position in countries not parties to the Vienna Convention.

(3) In the Vienna Convention countries, diplomatic personnel do not enjoy complete immunity from the local civil jurisdiction. Therefore, while otherwise exempt, are subject to the ordinary local civil jurisdiction in the following cases—

(a) a real action relating to private immovable property situated in the territory of the receiving State, unless the person concerned holds it on behalf of the sending State for the purpose of the mission;

(b) an action relating to succession in which the person is involved as executor, administrator, heir or legatee as a private person and not on behalf of the sending State;

(c) an action relating to any professional or commercial activity exercised by the person concerned in the receiving State outside his or her official functions.

(4) In no circumstances can diplomatic personnel be subpoenaed to appear as a witness before any local court, whether civil or criminal (refer to regulation 25 regarding requests to give evidence in a foreign court).

(5) Because they are necessary to the mission for the proper performance of its functions, the Mission's premises (including the residence of the Head of Mission and his or her diplomatic staff), its property, archives and communications are inviolable, and in particular, the premises in question are not subject to entry by the local police or other agents of the receiving State except with the prior consent of the Head of Mission.

### reg 22 Engaging in private business or professional activity

(1) A member of the family of any diplomatic staff, who is engaged in private employment in the receiving State, will in Vienna Convention countries, forfeit some immunities to which he or she would otherwise be entitled (under regulation 21(1)(c) above).

(2) Subregulation (1) applies also to members of the administrative and technical staff of the mission. In addition, the Vienna Convention establishes and the same principle would be followed in most countries that the Head of Mission and the members of his or her staff who are of diplomatic rank “shall not in the receiving State practice for personal profit any professional or commercial activity.”

(3) For these reasons, the Head of Mission must be consulted in relation to any case where a member of a family of a diplomatic staff contemplates undertaking private employment or professional activity, whether for gain or otherwise.

### reg 23 Duty to observe local laws

(1) A person who possesses any measure of diplomatic immunity from legal proceedings in the receiving State is still generally subject to the operations of ordinary civil and criminal laws and legislation of that State and is expected to observe them, and in particular, such a person is expected to honour his or her private contractual obligations and to make reparation for any torts he or she commits.

(2) A person may not rely upon any relevant immunity so as to evade civil liability for his or her private actions, and any such abuse may result in a request by the receiving State for the recall of the person in question.

(3) If a civil dispute between a member of the mission and a citizen of the receiving State cannot be settled directly between the parties, the officer must report the matter promptly to the Head of Mission, who should inform the Permanent Secretary if it appears that the dispute cannot be properly settled without recourse to the courts or to private arbitration.

[subreg (3) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

(4) An officer entitled to immunity who is charged with an offence, or who has reason to believe that but for his or her status he or she would have been charged with an offence, must without exception report the circumstances promptly and fully to the Head of Mission.

### reg 24 Waiver of immunity

(1) Since the immunities of members of diplomatic missions are accorded in the interests of the government they represent, no officer may personally decide himself whether or not immunity from jurisdiction should be waived or maintained in a particular case, and any such decision must first be reported to the Permanent Secretary to authorise a waiver or plea of immunity in such particular instances.

[subreg (1) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

(2) Where there is insufficient time to obtain such authorisation, immunity should be claimed pending reference of the matter to the Permanent Secretary and receipt of instructions.

[subreg (2) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

(3) The Government may not waive immunity from criminal jurisdiction but may agree to a waiver in civil proceedings, particularly where this can be done without impeding the functions of the mission.

(4) Where immunity exists and it is not waived in civil proceedings, the Government will of course use its best endeavors to bring about a just settlement of the claim.

(5) As the initiation of proceedings by a person enjoying diplomatic immunity will, in the eye of the courts of most countries, preclude him or her from invoking immunity from jurisdiction in respect of any counterclaim against him or her directly connected with the principal claim, the prior approval of the Permanent Secretary must be obtained before an officer or member of his or her family brings any such action.

[subreg (5) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

### reg 25 Request to give evidence in a foreign court

(1) No member of a diplomatic mission may be compelled to give evidence in a foreign court.

(2) Any summons or other authority to appear as a witness must be returned to the local authority through the protocol division of the local ministry of foreign affairs.

(3) An officer may cooperate with the authorities of the local State if the giving of evidence would facilitate the ends of justice and permission must be given for the officer concerned to give evidence on a voluntary basis.

(4) For the purpose of subregulations (2) and (3), the permission of the Permanent Secretary must first be obtained.

[subreg (4) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

(5) This regulation extends to cases in Vienna Convention countries where, in certain limited circumstances under regulation 21 and subject to the ordinary civil jurisdiction of the local courts, the Convention establishes that even in such countries, officers cannot be compelled as a witness to give evidence.

### reg 26 Traffic accidents

(1) All traffic accidents which might give rise to a claim against a Fijian officer or a member of his or her family should be reported to the Head of Mission, and in serious cases by the officer to the Permanent Secretary so that instructions can be given where necessary.

[subreg (1) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

(2) All offices must carry ample passenger, third party and third party property insurance to secure compensation to any persons or property injured or damaged as a result of their negligence.

(3) Any compensation arising from traffic accidents involving members of a mission must be handled carefully to ensure that the reputation of the mission is not adversely affected.

(4) As a general rule the Government may not claim immunity for any officer against whom an action is brought as a result of a traffic accident and may only do so if the claim is clearly frivolous or totally unreasonable or if a criminal prosecution is likely to be involved.

(5) Where a Fijian officer or a family member is the injured party, he or she should make every effort to settle out of court any claim he or she may have for compensation.

(6) Where the officer or his or her insurance company cannot effect a settlement unless the officer's immunity is waived, the Government may agree to a waiver provided it is satisfied that this is necessary in order to achieve a settlement.

(7) If as a result of a traffic accident there is any question of a criminal prosecution being brought against a member of a Fijian mission who enjoys immunity, the officer concerned should make clear his or her status and indicate that the Government may wish to invoke diplomatic immunity if any such prosecution is brought.

### reg 27 Parking offences

(1) Any Fijian officer or family member who is notified that he or she has infringed the local parking regulations must pay promptly any fine for which he or she is liable unless the breach occurred during the transaction of official business and could not have been readily avoided.

(2) However, if the breach occurred in the course of official business the officer must report it to Head of Mission who, if satisfied that the breach was unavoidable in the circumstances, may arrange for the notice to be returned through the appropriate local authority with a note setting out the circumstances which led to the contravention.

### reg 28 Detention of members of the mission

(1) If for any reason, including mistaken identity, alleged impaired driving, civil disorder, or mere coincidence, the police or security authorities of the receiving State attempts to arrest or detain a member of a mission, the officer concerned must explain his or her official status and personal inviolability and must insist on getting in touch with the mission by telephone or the quickest available means.

(2) Inviolability from arrest applies at all times to the diplomatic staff and seconded administrative and technical staff.

(3) The other subordinate staff are entitled to inviolability only in respect of things done in the performance of their duty, and in case of doubt, inviolability should be claimed pending reference of the circumstances to the Head of Mission.

### reg 29 Diplomatic privileges and immunities

(1) The receiving State may, in addition to the immunities enjoyed by the members of a diplomatic mission, grant certain privileges including—

(a) exemption from most forms of direct taxation including income tax, property tax; and

(b) customs duties on goods intended for private and official use of members of the Mission.

(2) On arrival at a post, officers should ascertain the extent of privileges to which they may be entitled to under subregulation (1).

(3) The officers must accept any limitations even when such limitations conflict with some aspect of traditional diplomatic privileges, and the officers must not request or urge additional claims for privileges on the basis of their diplomatic status.

### reg 30 Abuse of diplomatic privileges and immunities

(1) The officers must avoid abusing the privileges and immunities granted to them.

(2) The officers must not, whether by importing or carrying goods into the receiving State for use by anyone other than themselves or their families, by selling goods on which no duty has been paid, or by other arrangement, allow a resident of the receiving State to defeat the tax or exchange control regulations of that State.

(3) The officers must take particular care in connection with supplies of wines, spirits and cigarettes, and other similar items obtained without the payment of taxes under diplomatic privilege, and in no case such supplies must be sold or used for the payment of financial obligations, or given to persons in more than nominal amounts.

(4) The officers must honour the laws of the receiving State and must not deliberately commit driving offences or damage a landlord's property in the comfort that they are immune from arrest and prosecution.

## PART 5 TRAVEL AND SUBSISTENCE [regs 31–47]

### reg 31 Travel on duty to and from overseas mission

Subject to this Part, the fares and other expenses of Head of Missions and officers travelling on duty to and from an overseas mission and their dependants are to be met from public funds.

### reg 32 Mode of travel

(1) The mode of travel to and from overseas posts must be by the cheapest and most direct route by air.

(2) The Permanent Secretary may approve travel by means or routes other than the cheapest and most direct by air, provided that—

(a) the officer shall take annual leave for any period of travel in excess of that required by the cheapest and most direct route by air;

(b) the cost of passages borne by Government must not exceed those by the cheapest and most direct route by air; and

(c) the cost of transportation of baggage and freight borne by Government shall not exceed that provided for under these Regulations.

[subreg (2) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

### reg 33 Passage entitlements

(1) All official travel for Heads of Missions shall be by business class and otherwise subject to the approval of the permanent secretary responsible for the civil service.

[subreg (1) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

(2) Other officers shall travel by economy class.

(3) No Heads of Missions or officers or their dependants shall exchange entitlement for business travel or any other benefit or to claim official payment for business class travel if he or she has not travelled business class.

(4) Any dependants who are to reside or are residing with a Head of Mission or officer during his or her services in an overseas mission and who travel to or from an overseas mission independently of the Head of Mission or officer on his or her appointment or transfer there or on his or her return to Fiji at the end of his or her tour of duty overseas is entitled to economy class at public expense if—

(a) the dependants join the Head of Mission or officer at the overseas post within 9 months of his or her arrival; or

(b) in the case of dependants returning in advance of the officer, if they do not return more than 9 months before the officer is due to complete his or her tour of duty at the overseas mission,

provided that—

(a) the permanent secretary responsible for the civil service may extend such period in exceptional circumstances;

(b) the dependant child travelling unaccompanied by either parent is entitled only to the cheapest student or unaccompanied minor concessionary fares available;

(c) a dependant residing with an officer who ceases while at the overseas mission to be a dependant is entitled to an economy class passage under this regulation to enable him or her to return to Fiji; and

(d) any passage entitlement under this regulation to permit a dependant residing with an officer, or a person so residing who ceases while at the overseas mission to be a dependant to return to Fiji shall lapse if not taken up at or before the end of the officer's service at the overseas post, except where such dependant or former dependant remains there for educational purposes, in which case the amount borne by Government in respect of such entitlement shall not exceed the amount that would have been payable by the Government had he or she returned at or before the end of the officer's service.

[subreg (4) am Act 2 of 2016 s 20, effective 16 February 2016; LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

### reg 34 Stopovers and expenses en route

Where it is unavoidable for a Head of Mission or an officer to leave his or her normal place of residence in Fiji before departure on appointment to an overseas mission, he or she may be reimbursed reasonable out-of-pocket expenses for himself or herself and his or her dependants at a hotel specified by the Permanent Secretary for a period not exceeding 3 days.

[reg 34 am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

### reg 35 Expenses on arrival at post

(1) On first arrival at an overseas mission, Heads of Missions or an officer will be entitled to reimbursement of taxi fares from the airport to their place of accommodation and such incidentals as porterage charges.

(2) Where no residential accommodation is immediately available, Head of Missions and officers may be reimbursed, or the overseas mission may pay direct, the actual and reasonable living expenses of the Head of Mission or officer and his or her dependants at a hotel for a period of up to 14 days.

(3) If it is unavoidable for a Head of Mission or an officer to remain at a hotel for a period exceeding 14 days, the officer may claim reimbursement of the amount by which his or her actual and reasonable living expenses at the hotel, and those of his or her dependants, exceed 50% of his or her salary and post allowances (excluding representation allowance) for the same period except that no such reimbursement for any period exceeding 3 months must be made without the prior authority of the Permanent Secretary.

[subreg (3) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

### reg 36 Expenses on departure from post

Where it is unavoidable for an officer to leave his or her normal place of residence at an overseas mission before his or her departure on transfer or return to Fiji, he or she may be reimbursed actual and reasonable living expenses for himself and his or her dependants at a hotel for a period not exceeding 48 hours.

### reg 37 Expenses on return to Fiji

(1) Where it is necessary for a Head of Mission or an officer returning to Fiji to spend the night at Nadi before proceeding to Suva or elsewhere, he or she may claim reimbursement of actual and reasonable living expenses for himself or herself and his or her dependants for a period not exceeding 24 hours spent at a hotel in Nadi.

(2) Where a Head of Mission or officer is to resume duty in Fiji immediately upon his or her return, he or she may in addition to subregulation (1), claim reimbursement of three-fifths of the cost of actual and reasonable living expenses for himself or herself and his or her dependants at a hotel at his or her place of duty in Fiji for a period not exceeding 7 days, which may be extended by the permanent secretary responsible for the civil service only in special circumstances.

[subreg (2) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

(3) Officers returning to Fiji for leave are not entitled to claim expenses under this regulation.

### reg 38 Baggage entitlement

(1) Subject to these Regulations, Heads of Missions or officers travelling at public expense on appointment to overseas missions, on transfer between overseas missions, or on return to Fiji on the completion of service, are entitled to transport, at public expense by the cheapest and most direct route, personal and household effects for themselves and their dependants not exceeding the following amounts in addition to the free baggage entitlement conferred by their passage tickets: provided that personal and household effects shall not include—

(a) motor cars, motor bicycles or motor scooters;

(b) building materials;

(c) workshop machinery, large engines and cultivating machinery; and

(b) overseas, actual and reasonable expenses to be certified by Head of Mission.

(2) The Heads of Missions or officers who are required to temporarily place their personal and household effects into store at their overseas mission because of unavoidable delays in obtaining accommodation are entitled to reimbursement of actual and reasonable out-of-pocket expenses incurred in storage.

(2) Mileage allowance may only be paid where neither an official car nor suitable public transport facilities are available for the journey in question, or where the cost of using the only available public transport would exceed the mileage allowance payable.

## PART 6 SALARIES AND ALLOWANCES [regs 48–55]

### reg 48 Types of allowances

In addition to basic salaries, Head of Missions and officers serving in overseas missions are entitled to the following post allowance—

(a) location allowance;

(b) representation allowance;

(c) representational supplementary allowance; and

(d) child allowance.

### reg 49 Definition of allowances

Under this regulation, the post allowances listed under regulation 48 are as follows—

**child allowance** the cost to compensate a Head of Mission or officer for additional costs, other than for education, necessarily incurred in maintaining a dependant child, if the said child is residing with him or her at an overseas mission, as compared with the costs necessarily incurred in maintaining that child in Fiji;

**location allowance** the cost to compensate for additional expenditures necessarily incurred in maintaining a Head of Mission or officer and his or her family at an overseas mission as compared with the costs necessarily incurred by an officer of equal rank and circumstances in Fiji;

**representation allowance** the costs to cover direct and necessary expenditures on official entertainment. Officers other than the Head of Mission must account for two-thirds of their representative allowance; and

**representational supplementary allowance** the cost to compensate a Head of Mission or officer serving in an overseas mission for costs additional to those covered in regulation 48(a) of which are necessarily incurred for domestic service, cleaning, clothing, hairdressing, minor travelling and casual hospitality as a result of representational responsibilities.

### reg 50 Rates of post allowance

(1) The Heads of Missions and officers are entitled to the post allowances.

(2) The Prime Minister has the powers to review and determine the rates of post allowances.

[subreg (2) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

(3) The location allowance, representation allowance and representation supplement allowance is to be disbursed at the rate for married officers as highlighted under regulation 51(1).

(4) Child allowance is to be disbursed only for the period during which a dependant child is in resident with the Head of Mission or officer in an overseas mission.

(5) Where an officer's spouse and dependant children remain at the first post for a period following his or her transfer to another overseas post or upon his or her return to Fiji, the officer will be paid, from the date of his or her departure until his or her spouse and children leave the first post, or until such date the Permanent Secretary may decide—

(a) location allowance at the single rate payable at the first post, and child allowance at the rate payable at the first post; and

(b) actual and reasonable living expenses incurred by him or her at the second post, together with such post allowance (other than location allowance and child allowance) as he or she may be entitled to, at the single rate payable at the second post; or, if he or she returns to Fiji, such expenses as are provided in the General Orders.

[subreg (5) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

### reg 51 Payment of post allowance

(1) Subject to these Regulations, a married officer is entitled to location and representations allowances only for periods during which his or her spouse is residing with him or her overseas.

(2) Subject to these Regulations, child allowance will be paid only for periods during which a dependant child is residing with the officers overseas.

(3) Subject to these Regulations, an officer is entitled to be paid the appropriate post allowances from the day of his or her arrival at the overseas post until the day of his or her departure from the post on a passage paid by Government or until the date of termination of his or her service at the post, whichever is the earlier, except during any period where an officer is accommodated at a hotel on arrival or immediately prior to departure from his or her overseas post and the officer receives reimbursement of expenses under regulation 35.

(4) Subject to these Regulations, post allowances will continue to be paid for—

(a) all periods of leave except those for which passages are paid by Government; and

(b) all periods of official duty which require the absence of the officer from his or her normal place of residence overseas, provided that no per diem allowance shall be paid under the General Orders in respect of any such absence.

(5) An officer serving on temporary duty at an overseas post is entitled to be paid such allowances as the Permanent Secretary may direct, provided that the allowances so authorised shall not exceed the allowances payable to permanent officers of equivalent rank.

[subreg (5) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

**Note:**

Regulation 51(4) applies to all types of leave. If an officer accumulates annual leave and takes it on his or her return to Fiji or anywhere else on a passage paid by the Government, no post allowances are payable in respect of that leave.

### reg 52 Dependants who are in employment

(1) Location allowance at the rate of married officer shall be reduced by the amount equivalent to the net after tax earnings from paid employment overseas of the officer's spouse in excess of the maximum net after tax salary of a clerical officer in the Fijian civil service provided that no officer shall receive less than the appropriate location allowance at the single rate on account of his or her spouse's earnings.

[subreg (1) am Act 2 of 2016 s 20, effective 16 February 2016]

(2) Child allowance shall be reduced by the amount equivalent to the net after tax earnings from paid employment overseas of a dependant child in excess of the maximum net after tax salary of a clerical officer in the Fijian civil service.

[subreg (2) am Act 2 of 2016 s 20, effective 16 February 2016]

### reg 53 Review of post allowances

Post allowances will be revised after every 4 years.

### reg 54 Official hospitality

Subject to any direction given by the Permanent Secretary, a Head of Mission may authorise his or her staff to grant official hospitality and entertainment in the country to which his or her Mission is accredited to the extent to which funds are provided for the purpose in his or her Mission's votes, and may authorise such payment in respect of claims for reimbursement of official hospitality and entertainment expenses approved by the Head of Mission.

[s 54 am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

### reg 55 Other allowance and benefits

(1) Heads of Missions officers serving in an overseas mission are entitled to the following additional allowances and benefits which are to be paid in accordance with the applicable current rate and interval as are set out in Schedule 2 —

(a) domestic help allowance, Heads of Missions;

(b) equipment allowance, other diplomatic officers;

(c) clothing allowance, Heads of Missions and other officers; and

(d) transfer allowance, Heads of Missions and other officers.

(2) Domestic help allowance is payable only to Heads of Missions to assist Heads of Missions employ a domestic help or maid to assist his or her spouse in carrying out daily chores.

(3) Equipment allowance is payable to an officer and is to be determined by reference to his or her substantive or acting grade at the date of taking up duty at the new post.

(4) Officers, other than the Head of Mission, are entitled to reimbursement of reasonable expenses incurred in the acquisition of household effects as specified under regulation 60(1).

## PART 7 MISCELLANEOUS GRANTS AND ALLOWANCES [regs 56–62]

### reg 56 Clothing allowance on first appointment

(1) Subject to these Regulations, an officer is, on first appointment overseas, eligible for a clothing allowance for himself or herself and his or her dependants who are to reside with him or her overseas at the rates prescribed in the Schedule 2.

(2) Allowance for dependants payable under subregulation (1) shall be paid only when the dependants concerned actually taking up residence at the overseas post and travelling there, otherwise than on a visit, on a passage paid by Government.

(3) Allowances payable under subregulation (1) shall be reduced by the value of any clothing allowance granted for training or study abroad within 6 years preceding the date of first appointment overseas.

### reg 57 Clothing allowance on subsequent appointment

An officer who has been paid an allowance under regulation 56(1) shall not qualify for another such allowance, otherwise than under regulations 58(1) and 58(2), unless he or she is subsequently appointed to an overseas post after a break in overseas service of at least 3 years.

### reg 58 Clothing allowance after marriage

(1) An officer who has been paid a rate of first appointment allowance is entitled, on marriage, to receive the difference between the current rate and rate of allowance at the date of marriage.

(2) If he or she is serving at an overseas post it will be paid immediately, otherwise if officer is serving in Fiji at the time of his or her marriage, the balance will be paid on the occasion of his or her next overseas posting.

(3) If an officer is promoted, the officer is entitled to the difference between the rate of first appointment allowance and the appropriate rate for the higher grade.

(4) If the officer is serving at an overseas post the difference must be paid immediately, otherwise if the officer is serving in Fiji at the time of his or her promotion, the balance will be paid on the occasion of his or her next overseas posting.

### reg 59 Clothing allowance on temporary duty abroad

Subject to these Regulations, an officer posted to an overseas mission on temporary duty is entitled to clothing allowance, not exceeding that payable under regulations 56 and 57 to an officer of equivalent rank on permanent assignment to that mission, as the Permanent Secretary may determine.

[s 59 am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

### reg 60 Equipment allowance

(1) On first appointment to an overseas post, an officer other than a Head of Mission is entitled to reimbursement of reasonable expenses incurred in the acquisition of crockery, cutlery, tableware, glassware and kitchen utensils which are certified by the Permanent Secretary as necessary for the performance of representational duties and which are not otherwise provided by Government, provided that expenses reimbursed does not exceed the amounts for the grade of post and Mission as shown in the Schedule 2.

[subreg (1) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

(2) An officer who has been paid an allowance under regulation 66(1) does not qualify for another such allowance under this regulation unless the officer is subsequently appointed to an overseas post after a break in overseas service of at least 3 years, provided that an officer who is promoted during his or her overseas service and continued to serve overseas thereafter may be paid the difference between the rate of allowance payable on first appointment under regulation 66(1) and that appropriate under that regulation after promotion.

(3) The rate of equipment allowance payable to an officer will be determined by reference to his or her substantive or acting grade at the date of taking up duty at the new post.

(4) On transfer from one post to another post, officers will be paid a transfer allowance in accordance with the provisions and the rates prescribed under the General Orders.

### reg 61 Hardship allowance

(1) The Prime Minister may, on the recommendation of the Permanent Secretary, declare an overseas country to be at risk and as such its Head of Mission and officers, are entitled for hardship allowance, and such hardship condition must be reviewed annually so as to determine the need for continued payment of hardship allowance, subject to regulation 81.

[subreg (1) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

(2) A declaration under subregulation (1) is to be published in the Gazette and must specify the following—

(a) the name of the overseas mission upon which the hardship allowance is to be applied;

(b) the reason or reasons for the institution of such an allowance;

(c) period upon which such allowance is to be instituted; and

(d) the rates of allowance payable.

### reg 62 Transfer allowance

If a Head of Mission or an officer is transferred from one overseas mission to another, the Head of Mission or the officer is entitled to transfer allowance in accordance with the provisions and the rates prescribed under the General Orders or any contractual arrangements applicable to the person concerned.

### reg 66 Administration of official accommodation

(1) An officer residing in a Government owned or leased accommodation must keep the premises in good repair having regard to their condition at the commencement of his or her occupation and with the exception of fair wear and tear.

(2) Where a property is leased on behalf of the Government, the Head of Mission or officer in residence must not by any act or omission place the lessee in breach of the terms of the lease.

(3) All overseas missions are to maintain a complete and up-to-date record of all accommodations owned or leased by the Government, including—

(a) the originals of all leases;

(b) the titles of all properties owned, floor plans of all properties owned or leased on a long term basis; and

(c) inventories of furniture and equipment owned or leased.

(4) Accommodations owned or leased by Government will be inspected and inventories checked at the beginning and conclusion of an officer's occupancy of the premises by a “Housing Officer” designated by the Head of Mission. Losses and damages are to be recorded and reported to the Head of Mission who shall decide in accordance with the regulation 65(2) whether the officer will be required to pay for the losses and damage.

(5) An officer residing in accommodation owned or leased by the Government must notify the Housing Officer when any maintenance is necessary. Regular inspections will be made to ensure that the premises, furniture, furnishings, fixtures, fittings and equipment are being maintained in good order and repair.

(6) When maintenance is necessary the Housing Officer will, after obtaining financial authority—

(a) arrange for the necessary repairs and maintenance to be carried out in the case of Government-owned accommodation; or

(b) in respect of leased accommodation, notify the landlord or arrange for the maintenance to be carried out, as appropriate under the terms of the lease, in respect of leased accommodation.

(c) place on record full details of such repairs and maintenance.

(7) An officer must not let, sublet, or allow any persons other than his or her dependants or bona fide visitors to occupy the whole or any part of the accommodation which is owned or leased by the Government; and which he or she is occupying for the time being. However, when such accommodation is not occupied by an officer or his or her family it may be let or sublet by the Head of Mission, provided that all rentals are paid into the Mission's trust account for maintenance and repairs or rentals.

### reg 71 Holiday visit passages

(1) Where a dependant child remains at school in Fiji, and both parents or parent have left Fiji at public expense to take up residence and serve at an overseas mission, public funds may be used subject to the approval of the permanent secretary responsible for the civil service to meet the cost of student concession air fares or, if such are not available, of economy class return air fares, to enable the child to visit his or her parents once during each 12 months period that the officer is at post, the duration of such visits being wherever possible, not less than 10 days.

[subreg (1) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

(2) In special circumstances, the permanent secretary responsible for the civil service may authorise a visit to Fiji by one of the parents in lieu of the annual visit to the post by the child, provided that the amount paid in fares does not exceed the cost of one economy class return air fare by the shortest route.

[subreg (2) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

## PART 10 MEDICAL ATTENTION [regs 72–78]

### reg 72 Predeparture medical clearance

(1) All appointments are subject to satisfactory medical and dental clearance before leaving Fiji for posting to an overseas mission. Heads of Missions or officers and their dependants are required to undergo at public expense a medical and dental examination through the Government Health Service 14 days before their departure.

(2) If a Head of Mission or an officer, or any of their dependants is found to require medical treatment before departure overseas, than this will be provided free of charge at a public hospital or health centre. Any inoculations or vaccinations required will also be provided free of charge.

(3) Heads of Missions or officers must ensure that they and their dependants are dentally fit before departure overseas. Failure to do so may result in the rejection of subsequent claims for reimbursement of dental expenses while at an overseas mission.

### reg 73 Medical arrangement following return to Fiji

Heads of Missions or officers and their dependants returning from posts overseas may, if they so wish, undergo a medical examination at a public hospital or health centre within 14 days of their return to Fiji, and the costs of the examination will be paid by the Government.

### reg 74 Medical arrangements overseas

(1) Each Head of Mission must, in consultation with the local authorities, keep and maintain a list of qualified medical practitioners whose charges are reasonable. In countries where a State-subsidised health scheme is in operation, only doctors who practice within the schemes are to be placed on the approved list.

(2) Medical assistance to the extent specified in the following regulations will be provided for officers posted overseas and their dependants, while they are at post in the country of posting, while they are in the same or another country for purposes of local leave or official duties; and while they are travelling to and from the post.

### reg 75 Medical expenses

The cost of medical expenses incurred during overseas travel will be met to the extent provided in regulation 76, and officers will not be reimbursed the cost of insurance against medical expense.

### reg 76 General medical expenses

(1) Medical and surgical expenses in the following categories, incurred by an officer or his or her dependants may, with the approval of a Head of Mission, be paid directly by a post, upon presentation of an account—

(a) emergency surgery or hospitalisation, in cases where, in the opinion of an approved medical practitioner, it was necessary that treatment be given without the delay involved in seeking authority from Suva;

(b) other surgery or hospitalisation recommended by an approved medical practitioner and authorised in advance by the Permanent Secretary;

(c) maternity expenses, including normal pre-natal or post-natal care, as recommended by an approved medical practitioner;

(d) convalescent home expenses, where nursing attention is essential, and where entry to a convalescent home has been recommended by an approved medical practitioner and authorised in advance by the Permanent Secretary.

[subreg (1) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

(2) Officers for whom admission to a hospital or convalescent home is proposed, should notify their Head of Mission for the purpose and estimated duration and cost of such hospitalisation.

(3) Subject to paragraphs (a) and (b), out-of-pocket expenses incurred by a Head of Mission or an officer for treatment in the following categories for themselves or their dependants, may be reimbursed to officers in full, upon presentation of receipted accounts—

(a) consultations with approved medical practitioners;

(b) consultations with specialists which are recommended by approved medical practitioners;

(c) x-rays, injections, inoculations, vaccinations and similar special services provided or recommended by an approved medical practitioner or by any specialist recommended by an approved medical practitioner;

(d) medicines and medical supplies provided or prescribed by an approved medical practitioner or by any specialist recommended by an approved medical practitioner;

(e) ambulance costs;

(f) treatment by a masseur or physiotherapist recommended by an approved medical practitioner.

(4) The following expenses are not payable or reimbursable from public funds—

(a) any treatment which could be obtained free of cost under a national health scheme in the country of posting, or the cost of which would be recoverable from any insurance scheme which may be in operation at the post;

(b) treatment provided by a medical practitioner other than an approved medical practitioner unless the treatment was recommended by an approved medical practitioner or unless the treatment was urgently required and it was not practicable to consult an approved medical practitioner;

(c) medical aids such as hearing aids, and appliances such as orthopedic appliances;

(d) costs arising from medical or surgical treatment which could without detriment be left until return to Fiji;

(e) optician's fees and the cost of spectacles.

### reg 77 Dental expenses

(1) Subject to subregulations (2) and (3), out-of-pocket expenses for dental treatment (other than orthodontic treatment) may be reimbursed to officers upon presentation of a receipted account, as follows—

(a) the full cost of treatment, less the equivalent of $10 Fijian dollars in respect of each and every visit to a dental surgeon, for the officer or a dependant;

(b) the full cost of dental treatment requiring hospitalisation provided the hospitalisation is recommended by both a dental surgeon and by an approved medical practitioner and is authorised in advance by the permanent secretary responsible for the civil service;

[subreg (1) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

(2) The following expenses will not be reimbursed or paid by the Government—

(a) any treatment the cost of which would be recovered from any insurance scheme which may be in operation at the post;

(b) orthodontic treatment;

(c) the cost of dentures or dental appliances.

(3) Any officer or dependant who was not cleared as dentally fit before departure from Fiji, will be refused reimbursement of expenditure on dental treatment undergone within the first 6 months of arrival at an overseas post, except where the treatment was necessitated by an accident or some other unforeseeable circumstance.

### reg 78 Special conditions relating to medical and dental treatment

(1) When a dependant child is visiting his or her parents at an overseas post, the only payable or reimbursable cost will be for emergency treatment which arises during the visit. The cost of treatment which could have been obtained before departure from Fiji or wherever the child is normally resident, or which could without detriment wait until his or her return, will not be paid or reimbursed.

(2) If there is doubt whether or not any particular medical or dental treatment should be payable or reimbursed, the approval of the Permanent Secretary must first be sought before the treatment is undergone.

[subreg (2) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

## PART 11 LEAVE [regs 79–84]

### reg 79 Leave entitlements

Leave entitlements for Heads of Missions or officers serving in an overseas mission are governed by the General Orders.

[reg 79 am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

### reg 80 Annual leave

(1) Annual Leave shall be granted as follows—

| | | | |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| | (a) Category A | — | 18 working days; and |
| | (b) Category B | — | 21 working days. |

(2) Annual leave earned by an officer whilst serving in a Fijian Mission should be taken by arrangement with the approval of the Permanent Secretary.

[subreg (2) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

### reg 81 Hardship leave

(1) Hardship Leave of not more than 10 working days per year may be granted by the Permanent Secretary, which is to be spent outside of the hardship country and it must be taken within the first 6 months of the leave year, and such leave must not be carried forward to another leave year.

[subreg (1) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

(2) The Head of Mission or the officer may not be absent from his or her post for a period of more than 15 working days, and the hardship leave must be reviewed annually so as to determine the need for the continued leave allocation.

### reg 82 Home leave

(1) Home leave of not more than 15 days in Fiji may be granted to a Head of Mission or officer who has served continuously for 2 consecutive tours in an overseas mission.

(2) Home leave travel under regulations 31 and 32(1) is to be paid from the vote of the overseas mission concerned and must be by the cheapest and most direct route.

### reg 83 Passages

(1) The officers and their dependants will be eligible to receive air passages after every third leave year, and such passages will be confined to the journey by the cheapest and most direct route from the overseas post to Fiji and return.

(2) The officers and their dependants are entitled to the classes of passages provided under regulation 33.

### reg 84 Payment of post allowance during leave period

Subject to these Regulations, post and other applicable allowances will continue to be paid to a Head of Mission or an officer for—

(a) all period of leave earned at an overseas mission except those for which passages are paid by Government; and

(b) all periods of official duty which require the absence of the Head of Mission or officer from his or her normal place of residence at an overseas mission.

**Note:**

Regulation 84 applies to all types of leave. If an officer accumulates annual leave and takes it on his or her return to Fiji or anywhere else on a passage paid by the Government, no post allowances are payable in respect of that leave.

## PART 12 COMPASSIONATE ASSISTANCE [regs 85–87]

### reg 85 Critical illness or death of near relative

(1) On the certification by a registered medical practitioner that—

(a) a near relative of a Head of Mission or of an officer serving at an overseas mission; or

(b) spouse (if in residence with the officer overseas), is dangerously ill in Fiji or on the death of such relative in Fiji, the Head of Mission or officer may be granted, subject to these Regulations, not more than 7 days leave and a salary advance equal to the cost of a return passage of the appropriate class by the cheapest and most direct route by air in order that the officer or spouse may visit Fiji, provided that the Permanent Secretary may grant an extension of leave in special circumstances, and further provided that all such leave, shall be debited against the normal entitlement of the officer to annual leave.

[subreg (1) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

(2) Officers who draw advances from public funds in accordance with subregulation (1) will be required to refund the full amount advanced, except that—

(a) where the near relative in question is the officer's spouse or his or her unmarried child under the age of 21, the permanent secretary responsible for the civil service may, in consultation with the Permanent Secretary, authorise the full amount advanced to stand charged to public, funds;

(b) where the near relative in question is the officer's parent or his or her married or adult child, the permanent secretary responsible for the civil service may, in consultation with the Permanent Secretary, authorise the full amount advanced to stand charged to public funds; and

(c) compassionate passage will also be granted to the officer's spouse when the latter travels to Fiji to see a seriously ill parent or to attend the funeral of either of them.

[subreg (2) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

### reg 86 Death of Head of Mission or an officer

(1) If a Head of Mission or an officer dies during his or her service overseas, all reasonable funeral expenses in the country in which he or she was serving or all reasonable expenses for the conveyance of his or her remains to Fiji shall be paid by the Government.

(2) If a Head of Mission or an officer dies during his or her service in an overseas mission, his or her spouse and dependant children if then in resident with the deceased Head of Mission or officer will be provided with the cost of appropriate class single passages by the most direct route by air to Fiji, provided they leave the country in which they have been in resident with the officer within such time as the Permanent Secretary may direct, in consultation with the permanent secretary responsible for the civil service.

[subreg (2) am Act 2 of 2016 s 20, effective 16 February 2016; LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]

### reg 87 Other compassionate assistance

Applications for other forms of compassionate assistance may be made to the Permanent Secretary who may, in consultation with the permanent secretary responsible for the civil service, decide any form of compassionate assistance that may be given at public expense.

[reg 87 am Act 2 of 2016 s 20, effective 16 February 2016; LN 99 of 2016 reg 32, effective 1 December 2016]
