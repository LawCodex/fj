# MENTAL HEALTH ACT FORMS ORDER 2012

**Table of Amendments**

Mental Health Act Forms Order 2012 (LN 30 of 2012) commenced on 12 March 2012, as amended by:

| Amending Legislation | Date of Commencement |
| --- | --- |
| Fijian Affairs (Amendment) Decree 2010 (No 31 of 2010) | 2 July 2010 |
| Revised Edition of the Laws (Consequential Amendments) Regulations 2016 (LN 99 of 2016) | 1 December 2016 |

## O 1 Short title and commencement

This Order may be cited as the Mental Health Act Forms Order 2012 and shall come into force on the date it is published in the Gazette.

[O 1 am LN 99 of 2016 reg 162, effective 1 December 2016]

## O 2 Voluntary Patient Forms

For the purposes of section 17 of the Act, an application for admission to a declared mental health facility must be made in accordance with Form 1 in Schedule 1.

## O 3 Consent for assessment

For the purposes of section 18 of the Act, a person’s consent for assessment must be made in accordance with Form 2 of Schedule 1.

## O 4 Involuntary Patient Forms

For the purposes of section 24 of the Act, recommendation for detention, treatment and assessment must be made in accordance with Form 1 of Schedule 2.

## O 5 Application for emergency surgery

For the purposes of section 78 of the Act, an application for emergency surgery must be made in accordance with Form 2 of Schedule 2.

## O 6 Request by Police

For the purpose of section 25 of the Act, a request by Police for assessment must be made in accordance with Form 3 of Schedule 2.

## O 7 Request by ambulance officer

For the purpose of section 26 of the Act, a request by an Ambulance Officer for assessment must be made in accordance with Form 4 of Schedule 2.

## O 8 Inpatient Treatment Order Forms

For the purposes of section 61 of the Act, an inpatient treatment order must be made in accordance with Form 1 of Schedule 3.

## O 9 Community Treatment Order Forms

For the purposes of section 57 of the Act, a community treatment order must be made in accordance with Form 1 of Schedule 4.

## O 10 Electro Convulsive Therapy Forms (ECT)

For the purposes of section 73 of the Act, an application for informed consent from a person subject to an administration of ECT must be made in accordance with Form 1 of Schedule 5.

## O 11 Certificate for administration of ECT

For the purposes of section 74 of the Act, a certificate for administration of ECT must be made in accordance with Form 2 of Schedule 5.

## O 12 Review Forms

For the purposes of section 98 an application for review of a community treatment order or an inpatient treatment order must be made in accordance with Form 1 of Schedule 6.

## O 13 Application for review

For the purposes of section 22 of the Act, an application for review of refusal to admit or discharge as a voluntary patient must be made in accordance with Form 2 of Schedule 6.

## O 14 Notification for periodic review

For the purposes of sections 21, 60 and 71 of the Act, a notification for a periodic review must be made in accordance with Form 3 of Schedule 6.

## O 15 Hospital Discharge Forms

For the purposes of section 47 of the Act, a hospital discharge summary must be made in accordance with Form 1 of Schedule 7.

## O 16 Miscellaneous Forms

For the purposes of section 46 of the Act, a notification to primary carer of treatment or assessment must be made in accordance with Form 1 of Schedule 8.

## O 17 Nomination of primary carer

For the purposes of sections 41 and 42 of the Act, a nomination of primary carer must be made in accordance with Form 2 of Schedule 8.

## O 18 Declaration

For the purposes of section 35 of the Act, a declaration of an explanation of an order and the patient’s rights must be made in accordance with Form 3 of Schedule 8.

| (a) | You will be given a brief general anaesthetic. This involves giving a drug to relax the muscles. The anaesthetist will generally give the anaesthetic by means of intravenous injection. | |
| (b) | While you are anaesthetised, another medical practitioner will use medical apparatus designed to pass a modified electrical current for a few seconds through your brain, with the intention of affecting those parts concerned with emotion and thought. | |
| (c) | While the current is passing, the anaesthetic will prevent you from feeling anything and also your body from moving more than slightly. | |
| (d) | Treatment may be given 2 or 3 times a week. | |
| (e) | A course of treatment will generally involve up to 12 treatments but, on some occasions, more treatments will be required. Any queries you have in relation to the number of treatments you may need can be raised with your doctor. | |

| Benefits depend upon the symptoms of the conditions for which treatment is given. Relief may be obtained from symptoms of depression, agitation and insomnia. | | |

| Possible complications of ECT: | | |
| Some patients notice a difficulty with their memory for recent events which almost invariably clears up within a month of receiving the last treatment. Some patients experience a headache or a brief period of confusion, or both, on awakening after the anaesthetic. Otherwise, because the treatment and anaesthetic are very brief and present no significant stresses to the body, serious complications are uncommon. All general anaesthetics carry some risk. | | |
