## PART 1 PRELIMINARY [regs 1, 2]

### reg 1 Short title and commencement

(1) These Regulations may be cited as the Corrections Service Regulations 2011.

(2) These Regulations are deemed to have come into force on the 1 January 2011.

### reg 2 Interpretation

(1) In these Regulations, unless the context otherwise requires—

**Act** means the Corrections Service Act 2006;

**officers** means corrections officers; and

[def am LN 99 of 2016 reg 48, effective 1 December 2016]

**Supervisor** means the Divisional Supervisor appointed under section 13 of the Act.

(2) These Regulations shall be applied in accordance with the guiding principles stated in section 3 of the Act.

## PART 2 POWERS AND DUTIES OF OFFICERS [regs 3–7]

### reg 3 Powers and duties of a Supervisor

(1) Every Supervisor shall—

(a) supervise and control all matters concerning the administration of the prisons under his or her authority;

(b) be responsible to the Commissioner in relation to the conduct and treatment of all prison officers and prisoners under his or her control;

(c) ensure that all such officers and prisoners observe the provisions of the Act, the regulations made under the Act and the Commissioners Orders;

(d) furnish all returns and reports required under the Commissioners Orders and as otherwise required by the Commissioner;

(e) receive and investigate all complaints made by prisoners and ensure that the provisions of the Commissioners Orders are implemented in this regard;

(f) inspect the prisons under his or her control as frequently as possible, and whenever cause arises for such inspections to be undertaken, and in particular the inspections shall include—

(i) an inspection and countersigning of all records and registers maintained in accordance with Commissioners Orders;

(ii) a visit to each part of the prison;

(iii) confirmation that all sick and separated prisoners, and prisoners under other forms of punishment, are being treated in accordance with the requirements of the Act and the Commissioners Orders;

(iv) an inspection of the provisions provided for prisoners, and a confirmation that these are consistent with the Commissioners Orders;

(v) ascertaining whether any complaints made by prisoners in relation to the rations have substance, and if so proper remedial action shall be ordered;

(vi) an investigation into any complaint or application made by a prisoner, and the ordering of any appropriate action to be taken in relation to any complaint or application; and

(vii) the recording of all pertinent matters noted during the inspection, and in particular of any orders or directions given by the Supervisor.

### reg 4 Powers and duties of an officer in charge

(1) Every officer in charge of a prison shall—

(a) supervise and control all matters connected with the administration of the prison for which he or she is responsible;

(b) be responsible to the Commissioner and the relevant Supervisor for the conduct and treatment of all corrections officers and prisoners under his or her control;

(c) ensure that all such officers and prisoners observe the provisions of the Act, the regulations made under the Act and the Commissioners Orders;

(d) report to the Supervisor any matter, event or circumstance which may affect the safety or health of the prisoners, or the efficiency and security of the prison;

(e) visit all parts of the prison at least twice each day;

(f) visit all separated prisoners and prisoners undergoing other forms of punishment twice each day;

(g) visit prisoners employed at labour each day;

(h) make an unannounced visit to all parts of the prison at least once each week;

(i) keep a record of any circumstance preventing any visit required by this regulation, and any occurrence, event or matter ascertained during any such visit;

(j) take every precaution to prevent escape by prisoners;

(k) require reports to be made accounting for all prisoners as required by the Commissioners Orders;

(l) ensure compliance with regulations and Commissioners Orders in all respects, including matters related to—

(i) the locking and unlocking of cells;

(ii) the implementation of arrangements for prison labour;

(iii) the admission and classification of prisoners;

(iv) the medical treatment of prisoners and the calling of a visiting medical officer whenever required;

(v) all aspects of the role of visiting medical officers, including matters associated with the review of prisoners undergoing punishment and the rights of female prisoners and their young children;

(vi) the reporting of the death of a prisoner whilst in custody, and the taking of all required action;

(vii) the keeping all records, returns and registers and the making of all required reports;

(viii) complaints and applications made by prisoners;

(ix) official visits to the prison;

(x) the use of force or instruments of restraint;

(xi) communications by prisoners and the processing of mail to and from prisoners;

(xii) visits to prisoners;

(xiii) the discipline of prisoners, and matters relating to the remission of sentences for prisoners;

(xiv) the recording and safe keeping of property belonging to prisoners;

(xv) the keeping of files for each prisoner;

(xvi) dietary scales of prisoners;

(m) promote the education and rehabilitation of prisoners to enable them to return to society as law abiding citizens upon their release;

(n) be responsible for all accounts, stores, furniture, tools, clothing, arms and ammunition kept in the prison, and report to the supervisor any shortfall in provisions and materials supplied to the prison;

(o) provide for the safe keeping of all warrants of commitment and other formal documents relevant to the detention of a prisoner;

(p) keep any other records required by the Commissioner;

(q) make weekly inquiries into prisoners who have been removed to hospital;

(r) make appropriate arrangement for the training of corrections officers; and

(s) promote the utmost economy in every department of the prison.

(2) The officer in charge must—

(a) ensure the submissions of prisoners assessment reports on a monthly basis to the Commissioner;

(b) be responsible for the prisoners’ reintegration programmes;

(c) assist the Supervisor in consultation with stakeholders and programme providers in regards to the rehabilitation programmes;

(d) assist the Sentence and Planning Team in conducting proper assessment of prisoners for suitable rehabilitation programmes.

### reg 5 Other designated officers within a prison

(1) The Commissioner may, from time to time, appoint corrections officers to any position required for the proper and efficient administration of any prison, including the positions of chief officer and any other position referred to in the Commissioners Orders, or which is necessary for the implementation of the Commissioners Orders.

(2) The duties and powers of any position approved by the Commissioner under subregulation (1) shall be as—

(a) prescribed by any regulation or the Commissioners Orders; and

(b) determined from time to time by the Commissioner, and where necessary for the proper administration of a prison by a Supervisor or officer in charge.

(3) Nothing in this regulation prevents the Commissioner from using any other terminology for an approved position.

(4) The Commissioner may delegate his or her powers under this section to a Supervisor or an officer in charge.

(5) A Supervisor or officer in charge shall have the power to make an appointment of an appropriate officer to any position approved by the Commissioner on a temporary basis, and shall immediately inform the Commissioner of the making of any such temporary appointment.

(6) An appointment made under subregulation (5) shall be effective for the period determined by the Supervisor of officer in charge making the appointment as being necessary for the proper administration of the prison, unless sooner revoked or varied by the Commissioner.

### reg 6 Other obligations and responsibilities of corrections officers

(1) In addition to the obligations, powers, protections and privileges accorded to corrections officers by the Act, the following obligations and responsibilities shall apply to all corrections officers—

(a) any contravention or breach of any provision of the Act, any regulation made under the Act or the Commissioners Orders shall be promptly reports to the immediate superior officer of the corrections officer;

(b) no officer shall be absent from a prison during his or her hours of duty without leave from the officer in charge;

(c) no force shall be used against prisoners except as authorised by the Act or the Commissioners Orders;

(d) no punishment or privation may be inflicted on any prisoner except in accordance with the provisions of the Act or the Commissioners Orders;

(e) no communication between prisoners shall be permitted where a lawful restriction has been imposed;

(f) no discussion may be had with any prisoner concerning an officer's duties, matters of discipline, prison administration arrangements or the officer's private affairs, and all communications and relations between an officer and any prisoner shall be such as to in no way compromise the proper and efficient functioning of the prisons;

(g) no testimonial or reference shall be provided on behalf of a prisoner unless it is authorised by a Supervisor as being in accordance with any approved rehabilitation programme or policy;

(h) no private visitors shall be received by an officer whilst on duty, unless approved by the officer in charge;

(i) no officer may introduce or consume liquor in any prison;

(j) no officer may introduce or use tobacco in any prison, unless authorised by the officer in charge;

(k) no officer may enter a prisoner's cell at night unless accompanied by another officer, except in cases of imperative necessity, in which case a report shall be immediately made to the officer's immediate superior officer;

(l) any complaint made by any prisoner, or any indication that a prisoner is desiring to make a complaint shall be reported as soon as possible to the officer in charge or any other designated senior officer;

(m) the proper safe custody of prisoners placed under the charge of an officer shall be the responsibility of that officer, and a count of the prisoners shall be made and recorded at least every 2 hours, and also whenever receiving charge of a party of prisoners, handing over such charge or upon leaving any building or work place;

(n) no officer placed in charge of any prisoners shall leave such prisoners unless and until he or she is properly relieved of that charge;

(o) a thorough search shall be conducted of all prisoners upon their return to a prison from any place outside the prison;

(p) any prison officer entrusted with keys of the prison shall not take them out of the prison or leave them lying about, and shall when leaving a prison deliver them to an authorised officer; and

(q) any officer placed in charge of prisoners on work detail shall—

(i) conduct such counts of the prisoners as are directed by a superior officer;

(ii) conduct a count of the prisoners whenever they, or any of them, are handed over to any other officer;

(iii) be responsible for the safe custody and regular conduct of the prisoners;

(iv) ensure that no prisoner straggles or becomes separated from the detail;

(v) ensure that no prisoner communicates with any person not being a prisoner; and

(vi) ensure that no prisoner obtains any prohibited article while on the detail.

(2) The breach of any obligation or responsibility prescribed in subregulation (1) shall constitute a disciplinary offence.

(3) In addition to the obligations prescribed in this section, any corrections officer allocated the role of gatekeeper at a prison shall—

(a) not allow any person, other than a corrections officer or person authorised by law, to enter a prison without the approval of the Commissioner;

(b) keep a record of all persons, other corrections officers on regular shifts, entering or leaving the prison, and shall require such persons to write their names in the appropriate register;

(c) not allow any person other than a corrections officer to enter or pass out of a prison unless accompanied by a corrections officer;

(d) ascertain the right of any person to enter a prison and when in doubt, refer the matter to a senior officer;

(e) notify all workmen entering a prison that they may not communicate with prisoners and give anything to them;

(f) ensure that all workmen who leave a prison were the person who entered the prison for that purpose;

(g) take charge of any article, food or letter brought to a prison for a prisoner and shall deliver them to an authorised officer;

(h) ensure that no prohibited article is brought into a prison and that no prison property or unauthorised letter or other communication is taken out of a prison;

(i) stop and search any person whom he or she reasonably suspects to be carrying any unauthorised or prohibited article, property, letter or communication; and

(j) stop and examine every vehicle entering and leaving a prison.

(4) Any search of a female undertaken under subregulation (3) must be done by a female corrections officer.

### reg 7 Duties of visiting medical officers

(1) The duties and responsibilities of visiting medical officers are—

(a) as soon as is practicable, to examine all prisoners after their admission;

(b) to examine prisoners ordered to be separated from other prisoners in accordance with the Commissioners Orders;

(c) to visit sick prisoners and render necessary medical treatment to prisoners;

(d) to report to the officer in charge any medical condition affecting a prisoner of which the officer in charge should be aware, and to give directions in relation to dealing with the prisoner's condition;

(e) to direct such modification of the labour and diet of prisoners which is considered necessary in any case;

(f) to keep accurate records of any treatment given to a prisoner, and of any order or direction given in relation to a prisoner;

(g) to give such directions and make such arrangement as are necessary to ensure that a prisoner's health records, and particularly any matter concerning the HIV status of a prisoner, are kept confidential;

(h) after each examination of a prisoner, to note on the prisoners file whether or not the prisoner is fit for normal labour, or whether any restrictions or conditions are to be applied to the prisoner's work;

(i) to record in the medical officers Day Book and the prisoner's file, the following details in relation to each prisoner reporting sick—

(i) the prisoner's name;

(ii) the illness, disease or medical condition;

(iii) the treatment applied and medication prescribed,

and the records shall be made available to the officer in charge;

(j) to report to the officer in charge any possible mental disorder considered to be suffered by any prisoner, and to give appropriate directions for dealing with the prisoner's condition;

(k) to report to the Commissioner any opinion that the medical officer has that—

(i) a prisoner's life is endangered for any reason;

(ii) a sick prisoner will not survive the sentence;

(iii) a prisoner is totally and permanently unfit for prison discipline; or

(iv) a prisoner is unfit to work;

(l) to inspect every part of a prison at least once each month, paying special attention to the state of sanitation, the health of prisoners and the adequacy and proper preparation and cooking of food provided to prisoners;

(m) to keep records of all prison inspections;

(n) to report any incident of infectious disease to the Ministry of Health and implement action required to deal with any such disease;

(o) to deal with incidences of the death of a prisoner in custody in accordance with these Regulations and the Commissioners Orders;

(p) to regularly inspect prison hospitals and report any irregularities to the officer in charge; and

(q) to be familiar with the provisions of regulations made under the Act and of the Commissioners Orders related to the sanitation of prisons, the health of prisoners and the obligations of visiting medical officers.

(2) A visiting medical officer may enter a prison at any time of day or night to perform any prescribed duty.

## PART 3 OFFICIAL VISITS [regs 8–12]

### reg 8 Functions and duties of Visiting Justices

(1) On each visit to a prison, a Visiting Justice shall—

(a) hear and inquire into every complaint made by any prisoner;

(b) pay special attention to separated prisoners and prisoners receiving medical treatment;

(c) consider all reports concerning the mind or body of any prisoner likely to suffer injury or illness arising from prison discipline, and make reports where necessary to the Commissioner;

(d) ensure that any abuses or breaches of regulations or Commissioner Orders relating to the proper management of the prison and the treatment of prisoners are reported to the Commissioner;

(e) furnish information in relation to such abuses and breaches if requested by the Minister;

(f) make inquiry into any matter referred by the Minister or the Commissioner;

(g) discharge any other prescribed or delegated function or responsibility; and

(h) keep records of all visits, and of observations, suggestions and recommendations made during a visit.

(2) A Visiting Justice may visit a prison at any time and may—

(a) call for any books, papers, records, returns and registers related to the management of the prison and inspect those records and documents;

(b) visit every part of a prison;

(c) converse with any prisoner or corrections officer;

(d) inspect the quality and quantity of food and drink provided to prisoners;

(e) ascertain the compliance or breach of all legal requirements applying to the management of the prison;

(f) inquire into any complaint made by a prisoner as the Visiting Justice thinks fit; and

(g) inquire into the state of the prison buildings and facilities and make appropriate reports to the Commissioner.

(3) On the completion of every visit, a Visiting Justice shall enter into the Official Visitors Book such remarks, suggestions and recommendation as he or she considers appropriate for the information of the officer in charge, any follow up action or resolution of the issue shall be recorded by the Visiting Justice at a subsequent visit.

(4) A copy of the entry made by a Visiting Justice under subregulation (3), together with any comments made by the officer in charge, shall be forwarded to the Commissioner immediately after the inspection has taken place.

### reg 9 Other official visits

(1) An official undertaken by judicial officers or officers of the Human Rights and Anti-Discrimination Commission or the Accountability and Transparency Commission shall have the same functions as are specified in regulation 8(1).

[subreg (1) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 48, effective 1 December 2016]

(2) In the course of any visit undertaken in accordance with subregulation (1), the relevant officer—

(a) may exercise the powers specified in regulation 8(2);

(b) shall make the record required by regulation 8(3), which shall be dealt with in the manner provided for in regulation 8(4).

(3) Appropriate notice of any visit proposed to be undertaken in accordance with section 20 of the Act and this regulation should be provided to the Commissioner to ensure that the security of the prison and the safety of the visitors is not compromised.

### reg 10 Functions and duties of visiting committees

(1) A visiting committee appointed under section 19 of the Act shall make arrangements with the officer in charge for any visit to a prison.

(2) Visits undertaken by a visiting committee—

(a) shall be between the hours of 9.00 am and 4.00 pm on any day, or at any other time requested by a Supervisor;

(b) may be to any part of a prison and to any prisoner;

(c) may involve the inspection of the food and drink provided to prisoners;

(d) shall have the purposes of—

(i) ascertaining, as far as possible, that the provisions of the Act, any regulation made under the Act and the Commissioners Orders are being applied and observed;

(ii) inquiring into any complaint, request or application made by a prisoner;

(iii) assisting the Commissioner and other senior officers in applying appropriate arrangements for the rehabilitation of prisoners and the enhancement of their opportunities to re-enter the community as useful, productive and law-abiding members; and

(iv) undertaking any task and making any report and recommendation requested by the Minister or the Commissioner;

(e) should be undertaken at regular intervals, and at least once in every 3 month period.

(3) On the completion of every visit, the chairperson of the visiting committee shall enter into the Official Visitors Book such remarks, suggestions and recommendation as the Committee considers appropriate for the information of the officer in charge.

(4) A copy of the entry made by the chairperson under subregulation (3), together with any comments made by the officer in charge, shall be forwarded to the Commissioner immediately after the visit has taken place.

### reg 11 Visits by religious representatives and other respected persons

(1) Person authorised to make visits to prisons under section 21 of the Act shall make arrangements with the officer in charge in relation to any proposed visit.

(2) The arrangements made under subregulation (1) may be for particular visits or for visits undertaken on a regular basis in accordance with an approved timetable.

### reg 12 Visits by officers

A welfare officer or other officer or representative of the Department of Social Welfare, or a representative of a community group whose objective is to provide aid and support to prisoners may be admitted to a prison at the discretion of the officer in charge, and may interview a prisoner in the sight and hearing of a corrections officer.

[reg 12 am Act 10 of 2018 s 21, effective 23 November 2022]

## PART 4 DISCIPLINING PRISONERS [regs 13–17]

### reg 13 Prison offences

(1) Any breach by a prisoner of—

(a) any provision of the Act, any regulation made under the Act or the Commissioners Orders; or

(b) any lawful direction given to the prisoner by a corrections officer,

shall be a prison offence.

(2) Without limiting the generality of subregulation (1), a prisoner commits a prison offence if he or she—

(a) participates in any mutiny or riot by prisoners, or incites any prisoner to mutiny or riot;

(b) commits any assault or act of violence to any prisoner, corrections officer, visitor to a prison or any other person within a prison;

(c) escapes, conspires to escape or assists with or procures the escape by any prisoner from lawful custody;

(d) possesses any instrument or other thing for the purpose of procuring any escape;

(e) fails or refuses to assist any corrections officer to prevent an escape;

(f) quarrels with any prisoner, or uses offensive or insulting language to a prisoner;

(g) malingers or wilfully injures himself or herself;

(h) makes any communication with a prisoner in disobedience of any regulation, order or direction;

(i) does any act calculated to create any unnecessary alarm among prisoners or corrections officers;

(j) fails or refuses to wear the issued clothing;

(k) exchanges, loses, discards, damages, alters or defaces any issued clothing or other issued property;

(l) commits any nuisance within any part of the prison or while on work detail;

(m) treats with disrespect any corrections officer or any person authorised to visit the prison, or any person employed in connection with the prison;

(n) is idle, careless, negligent at work or refuses to work;

(o) leaves the allocated cell, ward or place of work or any other place where the prisoner is directed to be, without permission;

(p) receives or has in his or her possession any prohibited article, or attempts to obtain any prohibited article;

(q) disfigures, damages, steals or interferes with any part of fitting of a prison, or any property not belonging to the prisoner;

(r) behaves in an immoral, disorderly or indecent manner;

(s) uses any abusive, threatening, insolent, indecent or other inappropriate language;

(t) makes any false accusation against a corrections officer or other prisoner;

(u) commits any act of gross misconduct or insubordination;

(v) breaches any condition applying to his or her early or short term release, or fails to return to prison immediately upon the expiration of any term of short term release;

(w) attempts to commit, or aids or abets the commission of any prison offence; or

(x) is guilty of any other act, conduct, disorder or neglect to the prejudice of the good order and discipline within the prison.

### reg 14 Proceedings for prison offences

(1) The breach of any prison offence which comes to the notice of a corrections officer shall be reported without delay to the officer in charge or his or her delegate, who shall investigate the report not later than the following day.

(2) Failure to comply with subregulation (1) shall not affect the right to commence proceedings in relation to the breach, or to hear and determine any allegation of breach.

(3) The following procedures shall apply to the taking of proceedings for any breach of a prison offence—

(a) a prisoner charged with any prison offence shall be supplied with a copy of the charge prior to the hearing of the matter;

(b) no documentary evidence shall be used in any proceeding unless the defendant prisoner has been given access to it prior to the hearing of the matter;

(c) the evidence of any witness given during any proceedings shall be given and recorded in writing in the presence of the defendant prisoner;

(d) the evidence taken in any proceedings need not be taken down in full, but the substance and material points shall be recorded in writing and read over to the defendant prisoner;

(e) the defendant prisoner shall have the right to cross-examine each witness giving evidence against him or her, and shall be asked if he or she wants to exercise this right after each witness has given evidence; and

(f) the defendant prisoner shall be asked if he or she desires to give evidence and to call witnesses, and if he or she does wish to do so a reasonable opportunity to exercise this right shall be accorded to the prisoner.

### reg 15 Hearing of offences

(1) The tribunals and officers authorised to hear and determine cases involving breaches of prison offences shall be—

(a) a Magistrate;

(b) the Commissioner;

(c) Supervisors; and

(d) senior officers.

(2) At the request of the Commissioner, a Supervisor or a senior officer be authorised to hear any proceeding; a Visiting Justice may inquire into, hear and determine any proceeding for a prison offence, and may impose any punishment which can be imposed by the Commissioner under regulation 16(1).

(3) Notwithstanding any other provisions of this regulation, any prison offence involving an allegation against a prisoner of—

(a) mutiny;

(b) incitement to mutiny; or

(c) any assault to a corrections officer or prisoner causing any bodily harm—

who may impose any penalty provided for such an offence by any law, and any additional punishment vested by this regulation in the Commissioner.

(4) Except as provided for in subregulation (2), the Commissioner shall determine, either for a specific case or by written determination applying to any class of prison offences, whether a proceeding shall be heard and determined by the Commissioner, or a Supervisor or any other senior officer.

### reg 16 Approved punishments

(1) The Commissioner shall have the power in any proceedings to impose any of the following punishments, or any combination of them—

(a) forfeiture of remission of sentence not exceeding 3 months;

(b) deprivation of earnings, or any part thereof, for a period not exceeding 3 months;

(c) forfeiture of privileges, including the right to have any food additional to the minimum rations prescribed in the Commissioners Orders, for any period not exceeding 14 days;

(d) withdrawal of any right to early release or short term release; or

(e) separation from other prisoners for a term not exceeding 30 days.

(2) A Supervisor or senior officer shall have the power in any proceedings heard by them, to impose any of the following punishments, or any combination of them—

(a) forfeiture of remission of sentence not exceeding one month;

(b) deprivation of earnings, or any part thereof, for a period not exceeding 6 weeks;

(c) forfeiture of privileges, including the right to have any food additional to the minimum rations prescribed in the Commissioner’s Orders, for any period not exceeding 6 weeks;

(d) withdrawal of any rights to early release or short term release; or

(e) separation from other prisoners for a term not exceeding 7 days.

(3) The separation of any prisoner as a punishment imposed under these regulations shall be in strict compliance with the Commissioner’s Orders, and shall not be continuous for a period of more than 7 days, and an interval of 7 days shall elapse before the next period is served.

### reg 17 Review by the Commissioner

(1) The Commissioner shall have the power to review all proceedings heard by any Supervisor or senior officer under these Regulations.

(2) Upon any such review, the Commissioner shall have the power—

(a) to quash a finding;

(b) to alter a finding and find the defendant prisoner guilty of another offence;

(c) with or without altering the finding—

(i) to reduce or increase the punishment;

(ii) with or without such reduction or increase, to alter the nature of the punishment; or

(iii) to remit the proceedings back to the Supervisor or senior officer who heard them, or to another supervisor, for rehearing.

(3) The Commissioner shall not increase the punishment in a proceeding under review, unless the defendant prisoner has had an opportunity to be heard.

## PART 5 REMISSIONS [regs 18, 19]

### reg 18 Officer in charge may forfeit remission for misbehaviour

(1) In addition to the procedures prescribed in Part 4 for dealing with prison offences, an officer in charge may forfeit any remission applying to any prisoner on the grounds of misbehaviour.

(2) The power provided for in subregulation (1) may be exercised without the need to formally proceed against the prisoner for the breach of a prison offence, but the grounds for exercising the power may include the breach of any prison offence.

(3) Forfeiture of remission under this regulation shall only be imposed after a warning has been given to the prisoner, and the misbehaviour has continued after the warning has been administered.

(4) A prisoner may appeal to the Commissioner against any decision made under this section to forfeit remission, and the Commissioner may uphold the decision, overturn it or vary it in any respect.

### reg 19 Criteria for granting further remission

The Commissioner may make a recommendation to the Minister for the granting of further remission in respect of the sentence of any prisoner on any compassionate or humanitarian grounds.

## PART 6 PRISON VISITS [regs 20]

### reg 20 Rights of prisoners to receive visitors

Every officer in charge of a prison—

(a) shall ensure that each prisoner is permitted at least 2 visits weekly of no less than 30 minutes duration;

(b) may permit extended visits in accordance with Commissioner's Orders; and

(c) shall provide areas that are suitable for conducting contact and non-contact visits and ensure that the appropriate security measures are in place with regard to the supervision of visits.

## PART 7 RELEASE [regs 21–23]

### reg 21 Schemes for early release

(1) In addition to any requirement for eligibility for early release provided for in the Act, a prisoner must meet the following criteria to be considered for early release—

(a) the prisoner must have a record of good behaviour and prison work;

(b) the prisoner must demonstrate a desire and ability to return to the community as a law abiding citizen; and

(c) the prisoner should have availed himself or herself of opportunities offered during the term of imprisonment aimed at rehabilitation and equipping the prisoner for a productive life upon their release.

(2) The Commissioner shall determine the suitability of any agency or organisation to participate in early release schemes, and may seek the advice of the Director of Social Welfare to confirm the agency or organisation's suitability.

(3) The reporting requirements and other conditions applying to any prisoner released under the supervision of any approved agency or organisation shall be determined by the Commissioner on a case by case basis, and may be generally imposed under Commissioner’s Orders.

(4) Any early release on medical grounds must be supported by a written report of a visiting medical officer, and a report submitted in accordance with regulation 7(l)(k) may be grounds for permitting the early release of a prisoner, if all other pre-conditions stated in the Act or these regulations are met.

(5) If a prisoner commits any prison offence whilst on release, or fails to comply with any condition applying to the release, all entitlements to early release shall be immediately forfeited, and the prisoner shall be returned to prison to serve the remainder of the sentence, taking into account any punishment imposed for the breach.

(6) Any other matter, consistent with the Act and these Regulations, relating to the implementation of schemes for the early release of prisoners may be provided for in Commissioners Orders.

### reg 22 Schemes for short term release

(1) The Commissioner may approve the release of a prisoner for a stated period of time in accordance with the Act.

(2) A prisoner must make written application for short term release and must demonstrate that the following criteria have been met in his or her case—

(a) the prisoner must possess a low security rating;

(b) the prisoner must be serving the last 12 months of an effective sentence;

(c) the prisoner must have demonstrated a good record of behaviour and of prison work;

(d) the prisoner should have availed himself or herself of opportunities offered during to term of imprisonment aimed at rehabilitation and equipping the prisoner for a productive life upon their release.

(3) Eligibility for weekend release shall be at the discretion of the Commissioner if all the requirements of the Act and the regulations have been met.

(4) Eligibility for release to attend a course of instruction shall be at the discretion of the Commissioner who must be satisfied that the proposed course has some accreditation and shall contribute to the rehabilitation of the prisoner.

(5) The Commissioner may approve the short term release of a prisoner into the care and supervision of traditional chiefs and other community leaders upon being satisfied that—

(a) the traditional chiefs or community leaders are willing to assume responsibility of the care and supervision of the prisoner;

(b) the traditional chiefs or community leaders are capable of making a contribution to the rehabilitation of the prisoner;

(c) any condition imposed by the Commissioner in relation to the release of the prisoner shall be complied with.

(6) If a prisoner commits any prison offence whilst on release, or fails to comply with any condition applying to the release, all entitlements to short term release shall be immediately forfeited, and the prisoner shall be returned to prison to serve the remainder of the sentence, taking into account any punishment imposed for the breach.

(7) The prisoner must return to prison immediately upon the expiration of any period of his or her release.

(8) Any other matter, consistent with the Act and this regulation, relating to the implementation of schemes for the early release of prisoners may be provided for in Commissioner’s Orders.

### reg 23 Release at the end of term in prison

(1) The officer in charge must ensure that at least 3 months before the prisoner is to be released after serving their sentence that the prisoner is properly counselled and prepared.

(2) The Commissioner may make an order to outline the process of the release of prisoners at the end of their prison term.

## PART 8 INSTRUMENTS OF RESTRAINT [regs 24]

### reg 24 Use of CS gas

Use may be made of CS gas as an instrument of restraint, but all such use shall be strictly in accordance with the requirements of section 42(5).

## PART 9 DEATHS OF PRISONERS IN CUSTODY [regs 25]

### reg 25 Dealing with deaths in custody

(1) Immediately upon the death of any prisoner whilst in custody, the officer in charge shall report the death to the Police and the coroner and take action in accordance with Commissioner's Orders.

(2) In addition to any police investigation, the Commissioner shall cause an administrative enquiry to be conducted into all deaths in custody and shall provide a report to the Minister.

(3) The next of kin of the deceased prisoner shall be promptly notified of the death.

(4) On the death of a prisoner, the visiting medical officer shall record the following particulars so far as they can be ascertained in relation to the deceased prisoner—

(a) the day on which the prisoner first complained of the illness or was observed to be ill or injured;

(b) the labour, if any, that the prisoner was engaged in on the day of death;

(c) any details of the prisoner's diet at the time;

(d) the date of admission to hospital;

(e) the date that the medical officer was first informed of the illness or injury;

(f) the nature of the illness or injury;

(g) the date of last being seen by a medical officer prior to death;

(h) the date and time of death and any details about the prisoner's appearance or other matters of significance or relevance; and

(i) the opinion of the medical officer as to the cause of death.

(5) All other matters relating to dealing with a death of a prisoner whilst in custody shall be prescribed in Commissioner’s Orders.

## PART 10 COMMISSIONER’S ORDERS [regs 26]

### reg 26 Commissioner’s Orders to make further provision

Without limiting the generality of the power to make Commissioner Orders in accordance with the Act, the Commissioner’s Orders may make provisions in relation to the following matters and procedures—

(a) the rights of prisoners to communicate with their lawyers and to prepare their defence and appeals;

(b) the prescription of prohibited articles;

(c) visits to prisoners;

(d) dealing with complaints made by prisoners, and the rights of access and complaint to the Human Rights and Anti-Discrimination Commission and to the Accountability and Transparency Commission;

(e) exercise and recreation by prisoners;

(f) communications by post and otherwise with and by prisoners;

(g) the separation of prisoners and the review of separated prisoners;

(h) arrangements for female prisoners and infant children;

(i) dealing with prison escapes;

(j) the use of force and firearms;

(k) transfer and transport of prisoners;

(l) the keeping of files, records, returns and registers;

(m) emergency procedures;

(n) radio communications;

(o) the discharge of prisoners;

(p) the use of prison vehicles;

(q) the counting of prisoners;

(r) the reporting of incidents;

(s) the diet of prisoners;

(t) communicable and infectious diseases;

(u) prison work;

(v) rehabilitation services and programmes;

(w) contact with the media;

(x) staff training and development; and

(y) any other matter necessary for the efficient management of the prison and the protection of safety and security within a prison.
