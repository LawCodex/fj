## NO 001 COMMISSIONER’S LOCAL ORDERS [ss 1, 2]

### cl 1 Introduction

1.1 These Orders must be observed by all officers of the Corrections Service and such orders will include Commissioner’s Local Orders, which will prescribe procedures for the management of prisons in accordance with the Act and Commissioner’s Orders.

1.2 Procedures contained in Commissioner’s Local Orders may vary slightly between prisons in order to address the local requirements of each prison.

### cl 2 Governing principles

2.1 The Commissioner must sign and date the Commissioner’s Local Orders which shall be reviewed annually.

2.2 No member of the Corrections Service management is allowed to change all or part of any Local Order without the written approval of the Commissioner. The Commissioner’s Order shall exceed in exceptional circumstances where inconsistency may arise between the Local Orders and the Commissioner’s Orders.

2.3 Divisional Supervisors must ensure that complete sets of Local Orders are made available to all prisons;

2.4 Supervisors must ensure that adequate training in the use and implementation of Local Orders is provided to all staff.

## NO 003 PRISON MANAGEMENT [ss 1–3]

### cl 1 Introduction

1.1 Every officer has a responsibility to be aware of his or her duties.

1.2 It is the responsibility of Supervisors and officers in charge to ensure that all officers possess or have ready access to copies of Commissioner’s Orders, Commissioner’s Local Orders and any other material governing the management of prisons.

### cl 2 Interpretation

2.1 Divisional Supervisor means any officer appointed by the Commissioner to be a supervisor of any or all prisons in any Division or Divisions;

**officer** means an officer of the Corrections Service; and

**officer in charge** means an officer appointed by the Commissioner to be in charge of a prison.

### cl 3 General duties

3.1 Duties of Divisional Supervisors

3.1.2 Divisional Supervisors are appointed by and responsible to the Commissioner under the provisions of the Corrections Service Act 2006 for the conduct and treatment of all corrections officers and prisoners within his or her Division.

3.2 The role of the Divisional Supervisors

3.2.1 The Divisional Supervisor shall—

(a) maintain good public and community relations in each Division;

(b) ensure effective liaison with Government officials, the judiciary, police officers, non-government and community organisations;

(c) maintain an appropriate level of training for all staff to ensure the effective discharge of their duties;

(d) maintain an effective level of communication between senior management and staff;

(e) ensure the timely submission of all returns and reports as required by the Commissioner;

(f) receive and investigate all complaints made by corrections officers or prisoners;

(g) conduct prison inspections as required by the Commissioner and in accordance with Commissioner’s Local Order No 003.

3.3 Duties of officers in charge

3.3.1 The officer in charge of a prison is responsible to the Supervisor for the supervision and control of all matters in connection with the management of the prison to which he or she is appointed.

3.3.2 The officer in charge must ensure compliance with Commissioner’s Orders and Commissioner’s Local Orders governing the security of the prison and the health, safety and welfare of all staff and prisoners.

3.3.3 All reports and returns must be submitted to the Supervisor by the officer in charge in accordance with the Commissioner’s Orders and directions.

## NO 004 VISITING MEDICAL OFFICERS [ss 1, 2]

### cl 1 Introduction

1.1 In order to maintain the good health of all prisoners. Divisional Supervisors must make sure that a visiting medical officer visits each prison once a week.

1.2 The visiting medical officer shall inspect and advise on—

(a) quantity, quality, preparation and service of food within the prison:

(b) hygiene and cleanliness of the prison and its prisoners;

(c) sanitation, lighting and ventilation of the prison; and

(d) suitability and cleanliness of the prisoners clothing and bedding.

1.3 Officers in charge must ensure that appropriate facilities are available for the visiting medical officer to conduct medical examinations and that the dignity and privacy of prisoners is respected.

### cl 2 Visits by medical officer and medical records

2.1 The visiting medical officer shall—

(a) visit each prison not less than once every week;

(b) inspect the prison;

(c) conduct a surgery and see all prisoners requesting healthcare and those referred by the officer in charge;

(d) ensure that international communicable disease protocols are being complied with;

(e) report to the Commissioner any observations with respect to the cleanliness of the institution and the health and hygiene of prisoners.

2.2 The officer in charge shall—

(a) make sure that confidential medical history records are maintained for each prisoner. All medical records are stored in a secure environment and made available to the visiting medical officer upon request;

(b) prepare a list of all those who wish to see the visiting medical officer;

(c) act upon any recommendations made by the visiting medical officer with regard to the health of prisoners.

## NO 005 OFFICIAL VISITORS [ss 1–3]

### cl 1 Introduction

1.1 “Official Visitors” means Visiting Justices, visiting committees appointed by the Minister, judicial officers, officers of the Human Rights and Anti-Discrimination Commission, the Accountability and Transparency Commission, church ministers, chiefs and respected persons appointed by the Commissioner.

[para 1.1 am LN 99 of 2016 reg 42, effective 1 December 2016]

1.2 Officers in Charge will facilitate visits to prisons by the following:

(a) Visiting Justices;

(b) Visiting committees appointed by the Minister;

(c) Judicial officers;

(d) Social welfare officers;

(e) Officers of the Human Rights and Anti-Discrimination Commission;

(f) the Accountability and Transparency Commission;

(g) Church ministers, chiefs and respected persons appointed by the Commissioner, under section 21 of the Act.

[para 1.2 am LN 99 of 2016 reg 42, effective 1 December 2016]

1.3 The official visitors intending to visit a prison shall give notice to the officer in charge.

1.4 If an official visitor does not give notice of a visit, the officer in charge shall make all necessary arrangements to ensure that the purpose of the visit by the official visitor is satisfied.

### cl 2 Responsibility of officer in charge during an official visit

2.1 The officer in charge must maintain a register of official visitors and each visit will be recorded, including complaints and any other issues raised during the visit.

2.2 The officer in charge must make sure that the staff fully cooperates with all official visitors.

2.3 When an official visitor gives notice of an intending visit, the officer in charge shall—

(a) inform all staff and prisoners of the date and time of the visit;

(b) ensure that all warrants and sentence calculations for all prisoners are available for review by the official visitor;

(c) ensure that all registers are available for inspection;

(d) ensure that facilities are available for meetings/interviews with prisoners or staff who wish to speak with the official visitor and that interviews are properly supervised and conducted out of the hearing of other persons;

(e) provide escort and adequate security for inspection of all areas of the prison;

(f) provide appropriate staff to assist the official visitor with enquiries;

(g) provide replacement staff for those who wish to speak with the official visitor.

### cl 3 Report by official visitors to be given to the Commissioner

3.1 Official visitors must be allowed to—

(a) inquire into the treatment and conduct of the prisoners;

(b) inspect and observe the prison facilities and programmes;

(c) hear any complaints;

(d) review the document of commitment for every detainee received into the correctional institution since the last visit, check for any errors or excess and assist a prisoner in the initiation of a classification review if appropriate;

(f) inquire into such matters as he or she thinks fit or the Commissioner directs.

3.2 Official visitors shall forward to the Commissioner a report concerning issues raised within 7 days of the visit

3.3 The Commissioner must ensure that an issue raised in the official visitors report is investigated.

3.4 The Commissioner must notify the Supervisor of the contents of the report, and give directions as to any appropriate action to be taken.

3.5 The Supervisor must direct the officer in charge accordingly.

## NO 006 PRISONER ADMISSION [ss 1, 2]

### cl 1 Introduction

1.1 Prisoners shall be admitted to a prison only on the authority of a signed warrant or other lawful order for detention. Admission times are prescribed in Commissioner’s Local Order No 2.

### cl 2 Admission of prisoners

2.1 The provisions of the Corrections Service Act 2006 must be satisfied with regard to the identity of the person being admitted in to custody. All details contained in the warrant or lawful order for detention must be correct.

2.2 If there is doubt as to the identity of a person or the validity of the warrant or order, immediate steps must be taken to confirm the legality of the warrant or order.

2.3 Prisoners who present an invalid or incorrect warrants or orders must be returned to court as soon as is practicable.

2.4 The officer in charge must ensure that there are separate accommodation for female prisoners, young prisoners and unconvicted and civil prisoners.

2.5 The officer in charge must ensure that upon admission, all prisoners are given the approved published information with regard to their rights, duties and responsibilities, having particular regard to safe health practices.

2.6 All newly admitted prisoners must be classified with a high security rating. Unsentenced and civil prisoners will retain a high security rating until such time as they are sentenced and can be classified in accordance with Commissioner’s Order No 011.

## NO 007 REMISSION [ss 1, 2]

### cl 1 Introduction

1.1 Prisoners serving sentences of more than 3 months are entitled to receive a one third remission of sentence.

### cl 2 Principles of remission

2.1 All prisoners upon initial classification will receive a release date calculated upon the remission permitted in accordance with the provisions of the Corrections Service Act 2006 and Regulations.

2.2 Remission may be forfeited by the officer in charge for breaches of the Corrections Service Act 2006, Regulations or Commissioner’s Orders, and the prisoner must be informed of the forfeiture.

2.3 Forfeiture of remission is to be used as a last resort but in any event must not exceed 7 days for any single breach, with a total not exceeding 10 days per month.

2.4 Prisoners who have had remission forfeited may appeal to the Commissioner within 14 days of forfeiture.

2.5 The Commissioner must review all remission appeals and may reinstate some or all of the forfeited remission if deemed appropriate.

2.6 The officer in charge may reinstate all or part of forfeited remission at his or her discretion.

2.7 A Prisoner Remission Register must be maintained in which the current status of all sentenced prisoner release dates is to be recorded. This will also include all sentences recalculated as a result of appeal outcomes.

2.8 A copy of the current status of sentenced prisoner release dates will be forwarded to the Commissioner at the end of each calendar month.

2.9 A record of the current prisoner’s release date must be maintained on the prisoner’s file.

## NO 008 PRISONERS DIET [ss 1, 2]

### cl 1 Introduction

1.1 All prisoners must be provided with adequate and sustaining food rations in accordance with the dietary scales prescribed in Attachment A, which are the minimum rations to be provided to prisoners.

### cl 2 Prisoners diet

2.1 No prisoner may be denied the minimum food rations for the purpose of punishment.

2.2 Additional rations and flavour or nutrition enhancing additives may be provided in accordance with the provisions of the Act. Substitution for food may also be done provided that the substitute is of equivalent nutrients to that which is being substituted.

2.3 Prisoners who are engaged in physical labor may be provided with additional food.

2.4 Prisoners who have children in prison are to be provided with food suitable to maintain the health and well-being of the child.

2.5 Female prisoners who are nursing an infant may receive additional food if recommended by the visiting medical officer.

2.6 Prison gardens must be fully utilised for the purpose of growing crops to supplement food rations.

2.7 All prisoners must be provided with foods from the 4 food groups on a daily basis.

2.8 Records must be maintained for the purchase, issue and return of foodstuffs.

2.9 All food purchased, grown or stored for consumption by prisoners will be stored in a manner appropriate to prevent contamination or deterioration.

2.10 Prisoners with special dietary needs, particularly with regard to health, religious or ethnic requirements will be provided with food appropriate to those needs.

2.11 Food preparation protocols, facilities and utensils will be maintained in an appropriate manner to achieve a high level of hygiene.

2.12 Unlimited fresh drinking water shall be made available to all prisoners.

2.13 On Sundays, the evening dietary scales shall replace the mid day dietary scale and vice versa.

## NO 009 PRISONER TRANSFERS [ss 1, 2]

### cl 1 Introduction

1.1 The transfer of prisoners may be undertaken for the following purposes—

(a) to attend court;

(b) to attend a hospital or other medical facility;

(c) to attend a psychiatric hospital or institution;

(d) for permanent or temporary transfer to another prison for any reason at the direction of the Commissioner;

(e) for temporary transfer to another prison for medical or compassionate reasons.

### cl 2 Transfer of prisoners

2.1 Prisoners being transferred to another prison for any reason will be notified of the date of and reason for the scheduled transfer.

2.2 The officer in charge of the originating prison will advise the officer in charge of the receiving prison of the pending transfer.

2.3 The next of kin is to be advised of a pending transfer to another institution.

2.4 The provisions of Commissioner’s Local Order No 010 will be adhered to with regard to the procedures for transfer and transport of prisoners.

## NO 010 COMMUNICABLE DISEASE [ss 1–3]

### cl 1 Right of prisoners to receive confidential health care

1.1 All prisoners have the right to receive confidential health care, equivalent to that available to the community through the Ministry responsible for health, without discrimination as to their legal status.

### cl 2 Confidential of information and treatment of prisoners with diseases

2.1 Compulsory testing of prisoners for HIV will not be conducted. Voluntary testing may occur if available, and with the informed consent of the prisoner.

2.2 Medical test results must be treated as confidential and support will be made available when the prisoner is notified of the results and, if required, in the period following notification.

2.3 Information regarding the infectious disease status of a prisoner may be provided to an officer in charge, if it is necessary to ensure the safety and well-being of prisoners and staff. A prisoner’s file, papers or accommodation area shall not be marked to indicate the prisoner’s health status.

2.4 Female prisoners must be allowed to care for young children, regardless of their HIV status and must receive regular gynecological consultations if available; to diagnose and treat sexually transmitted infections.

2.5 Because the risks of tuberculosis transmission in prisons are considered higher that in the general population every effort must be made to reduce prison environment risks. This must include ensuring adequate ventilation of prisoner accommodation areas, ensuring accommodation areas are not overcrowded and providing adequate nutrition to prisoners.

2.6 The rights of prisoners with HIV/AIDS or other infectious diseases must not be restricted, other than when absolutely necessary for medical reasons as determined by a medical officer. This decision must not be influenced by prison management and prisoners must not be separated or restricted from programmes and activities, such as work, recreation or religion, unless recommended by medical staff. Decisions regarding separation for health reasons must be made on the same basis as for the general community and in accordance with public health standards and regulations.

2.7 Prisoners with HIV/AIDS or other infectious diseases must not be excluded from placement in minimum security units, leave programmes or parole by reason of their illness. Prisoners with an advanced AIDS related illness can be considered for compassionate early release, to increase family contact.

2.8 Medical staff may provide information regarding the prisoner’s health status, treatment needs and prognosis to those authorities who decide early release only with the permission of the prisoner.

2.9 Prisoners who threaten or participate in aggressive or predatory sexual behaviour, or who exploit or victimise other prisoners, will be disciplined.

2.10 Prisoners must be discouraged from sharing toothbrushes and razors, and sufficient quantities of these items must be available so that sharing is unnecessary.

### cl 3 Education and training

3.1 Preventative measures for infectious disease transmission in prisons must be based on an informed understanding of the different modes of transmission for each disease. Preventive measures for HIV must be based on the risk behaviours occurring in prisons and must be complementary to and compatible with those in the community.

3.2 Information and education provided to prisoners must aim to promote realistically achievable changes in attitudes and risk behaviours, both in prison and after release.

3.3 Corrections officers and prisoners should be encouraged to participate in the development and distribution of educational material and peer support programmes in conjunction with the Ministry of Health. Written material must be appropriate to the educational level of prisoners or staff and in a language and format that can be understood. Risk prevention information must focus on the principles of Universal Health Precautions and the lack of HIV transmission in normal everyday contact and activities shall be emphasised.

3.4 HIV and other infectious diseases awareness and risk prevention information must be provided to staff during initial training and will be a component of ongoing training courses.

3.5 HIV and other infectious diseases awareness and risk prevention information must be provided to prisoners during reception and in pre-release programmes and prisoners must be encouraged to adopt supportive attitudes towards people affected by HIV/AIDS to address discrimination, fear and prejudice.

3.6 Prisoners in pre-release programmes must be provided with information on the use of condoms in preventing HIV and other sexually transmitted infections, and. if available, condoms must be provided to prisoners on leave of absence programmes and upon release.

3.7 Female prisoners must be provided with specific information on HIV and other sexually transmitted infections, in particular from mother to infant and with counselling on family planning.

3.8 Institutional medical staff must be provided with information and resources to reduce the transmission of HIV and other infectious diseases within institutions.

3.9 Churches, non-government and voluntary agencies with expertise in HIV and other infectious disease prevention, counseling and social support will be encouraged to provide advice and support to prisoners and staff.

## NO 011 CLASSIFICATION OF PRISONERS [ss 1, 2]

### cl 1 Introduction (aim of classification)

1.1 The rehabilitation of prisoners relies largely on the opportunities provided by correctional institutions for participation in resocialisation, work and education programmes, prior to release of prisoners. Such programmes are useful in providing prisoners with the necessary skills to return to the community as law abiding and productive members of society, thus significantly reducing the incidence of continuance commission of offences. The provision of these incentives through the classification system will contribute to the achievement of these aims.

1.2 Appropriate classification of prisoners enhances the security and good order of the institution.

1.3 The regular classification of prisoners provides a mechanism with which to—

(a) implement legislation with regard to the protection of the community and rehabilitation of prisoners;

(b) provide fair advice to the officer in charge to enable him or her to make informed decisions regarding the appropriate security rating for each prisoner;

(c) provide meaningful activity resulting in improved self-esteem, positive attitude and behaviour and skills transference which assists with reintegration into the community;

(d) utilise prisoner labour to increase productivity with a view to providing food for prisoners, income for the prison and prison maintenance.

### cl 2 Classifications

2.1 Central Allocation and Classification Board

2.1.1 Membership

The Central Allocation and Classification Board shall consist of—

(a) Assistant Commissioner Operations as the chairperson;

(b) Supervisor (Divisional);

(c) Director Education and Training/Rehabilitation;

(d) Officer in charge, Suva;

(e) Welfare officer;

(f) Prison chaplin;

(g) Medical orderly; and

(h) Staff officer operations as the secretary.

2.1.2 Roles and Functions

The roles and functions of the Central Allocation and Classification Board are to—

(a) consider all reports and documentation submitted to them in relation to classification of a prisoner;

(b) classify all prisoners serving effective sentences (after remission) of 5 years of more;

(b) monitor prison numbers and facilitate the transfer of prisoners to ensure that accommodation requirements appropriate to security rating are met;

(c) monitor Early Release Programmes and weekend and short term release to ensure compliance with the Act and Regulations and Commissioner’s Orders Nos. 015 and 016;

(d) report to the Commissioner as required.

2.2 Sentence Planning Board and Divisional Sentence Planning Board

2.2.1 Membership

The Divisional Sentence Planning Team shall consist of—

(a) Divisional Supervisor as the chairperson;

(b) Officer in charge (Western and Northern Division);

(c) Chaplin (depends on the religion of the prisoner);

(d) Government representative (depending on the need);

(e) Mentor of the prisoner;

(f) Divisional Sentence Planning Officer;

(g) Community representative;

(h) Religious group representative; and

(i) Institutional Rehabilitation Officer (I.R.O).

The Sentence Planning Board shall consist of—

(a) Director Educational Training as the chairperson;

(b) Assistant Commissioner for Prison;

(c) Prison chaplin; and

(d) Psychologist.

2.2.2 Role and Function of the Divisional Sentence Planning Team and the Sentence Planning Board

The role and function of Divisional Sentence Planning team and the Sentence Planning Board are to—

(a) recommend to the Supervisor, a prisoner’s suitability for a particular security rating, accommodation location, work and education programmes and approved release programmes;

(b) review every 3 months, in accordance with specified procedure, security ratings of all convicted prisoners;

(c) make recommendations to the Supervisor with respect to leave of absence programmes for the purposes of education, employment, re-integration or compassionate grounds;

(d) interview prisoners when required;

(e) interview prisoners who, due to inappropriate behaviour have been returned to the secure compound by the officer in charge; and

(f) ensure that all prisoners who are undergoing classification or review are made fully aware of the classification process and in particular their obligations and responsibilities.

2.3 Issues to be considered by Central Allocation and Classification Board

2.3.1 The Central Allocation and Classification Board must be aware of and take into account the following criteria when considering the security rating of a prisoner—

(a) type of offence(s);

(b) circumstances of offence(s);

(c) criminal history (previous record of offences, particularly similar offences, offences in company with others’ and offences committed ‘with violence’;

(d) protection requirements;

(e) remorse level in relation to the offence(s);

(f) location of co-offenders (if prisoners);

(g) alliances with other prisoners;

(h) prison behaviour and record of discipline (for example, an escapee must serve at least 50% of his or her current sentence [not greater than 5 years] before being considered for a low security rating. If the prisoner escapes twice in the same sentence, he or she must serve at least 5 years before being considered again for a low security rating;

(i) reasons for transfer from other prisons;

(j) prisoner’s family situation (especially conflict or situations which cause anxiety, especially illness and death);

(k) whether or not prisoner receives visits, and from whom;

(l) prisoner’s health and physical fitness in relation to particular work;

(m) the need to attend education and awareness programmes to prevent further crime (eg conflict resolution, literacy);

(n) commitment to rehabilitation programmes which may include work, education or release programmes;

(o) previous work skills and/or vocational training requirements;

(p) age of prisoner and capacity to undertake specific work/education or release programmes.

2.4 Central Allocation and Classification Board

2.4.1 Frequency of meetings

Meetings of the Central Allocation and Classification Board will occur fortnightly, chaired by the officer in charge. Meetings must be conducted according to the prescribed schedule, regardless of whether or not there are cases to be reviewed.

2.4.2 Recording of meetings

Minutes must be recorded at all meetings, detailing all particulars relating to the classification of each prisoner as per the attached Prisoner Classification Report (Appendix A).

2.4.3 Reporting of meetings

The Central Allocation and Classification Board will provide a monthly report to the Commissioner.

2.5 Classification Process

All convicted prisoners shall be classified.

2.5.1 Short Sentence Class

(a) Short Sentence Class are those serving sentences not exceeding 3 months.

(b) Classification of sentenced short sentence class must occur as soon as possible but within 2 weeks after admission/sentencing. In order to ensure the stability or otherwise of a short sentence class prisoner and his or her suitability to participate in work and education programmes, the Central Allocation and Classification Board must consider the classification of Short Sentence Class prisoners with reference to—

(a) the prisoner’s file;

(b) other relevant written reports (eg discipline, medical etc)

(c) the age and gender of the prisoner;

(d) the offence for which the prisoner has been sentenced;

(e) the circumstances of the offence;

(f) any previous convictions;

(g) the prisoner’s attitude and demeanour;

(h) known associates of the prisoner in the prison;

(i) the prisoner’s skills and training;

(j) the prisoner’s needs, which may include medical, psychiatric, psychological consultation, referral to counselling;

(k) level of family support

(l) the prisoner’s preference with regard to work location;

(m) the current security assessment;

(n) interview of the prisoner.

(c) Recommendations are to be summarised in the Minutes of the Central Allocation and Classification Board and verified at the next scheduled full meeting of the Board.

(d) Classification recommendations are to be recorded on the Prisoner Classification Report and submitted to the Supervisor for consideration.

(e) The Supervisor’s decision regarding a prisoner’s security rating is to be recorded on the prisoner’s file, together with the reasons for the decision.

(f) The Supervisor is to ensure that the prisoner is advised of his or her security rating within 24 hours.

2.5.2 Other classes

(a) The Central Allocation and Classification Board shall convene to classify those prisoners serving sentences of 3 months or more.

(b) The officer in charge of sections where a prisoner resides, is working or is attending an education program, will provide either a verbal or written recommendation regarding an appropriate security rating for the prisoner.

(c) Classification of prisoners other than short sentence class prisoners must occur within 2 weeks after admission/sentencing with reference to—

(a) the prisoner’s file;

(b) other relevant written reports (eg discipline, medical etc)

(c) the age and gender of the prisoner;

(d) the offence for which the prisoner has been sentenced;

(e) the circumstances of the offence;

(f) any previous convictions;

(g) the prisoner’s attitude and demeanour;

(h) known associates of the prisoner in the institution;

(i) the prisoner’s skills and training;

(j) the prisoner’s needs, which may include medical, psychiatric, psychological consultation, referral to counselling;

(j) level of family support;

(l) the prisoner’s preference with regard to work location;

(m) the current security assessment;

(n) interview of the prisoner;

(o) the prisoner’s place of origin or family residence.

(d) The secretary of the Board shall be responsible for the recording of the minutes of meetings, which must include the discussion relating to that recommendation on the Prisoner Classification Report.

(e) The Prisoner Classification Report is to be submitted to the Supervisor for the Prison that the prisoner must be allocated to for consideration.

(f) The Supervisor’s decision regarding a prisoner’s security rating is to be recorded on the Prisoner Classification Report, together with the reason(s) for the decision.

(g) The Supervisor is to ensure that the prisoner is advised within 24 hours of his or her security rating.

2.5.3 Classification Reviews

(a) All short term class and other classified prisoners must be reviewed by the Classification Committee once every 3 months.

(b) Any changes to the circumstances and/or recommended security rating of the prisoner are to be documented on the Prisoner Classification Report.

(c) The Prisoner Classification Report is to be submitted to the Supervisor for the Prison that the prisoner will be allocated to for consideration or information.

(d) The Supervisor’s decision regarding a change in the prisoner’s security rating is to be recorded on the Prisoner Classification Report, together with the reason(s) for the decision.

(e) The Supervisor is to ensure that the prisoner is advised within 24 hours of the change in his or her security rating if applicable and it is recorded in the prisoner’s file.

2.6 Officer in charge, reception and discharge

2.6.1 The reception and discharge officer plays an important role in the collation of information relating to prisoners and the documentation of classification details (see Commissioner’s Local Order No 007). The duties of this officer are as follows—

(a) maintain a list of prisoners to be classified and reviewed and allocate review dates;

(b) ensure prisoner file covers are accurately maintained, updated and available for Central Allocation and Classification Board Meetings;

(c) ensure that any details relating to escape, including the sentence received as a result are included in prisoners’ files;

(d) ensure that all necessary information is available at the Central Allocation and Classification Board meeting;

(e) prepare a list of all prisoners to be classified and reviewed.

2.7 Security Ratings

2.7.1 High Security

A high security rating means that strict security is required for prisoners who are considered high escape risks or who threaten the good order, security and management of the prison. Prisoners classified with a high security rating will be required to remain in high security accommodation. High security prisoners will not be permitted to be outside the secure area, except under close escort for specific and approved purposes.

2.7.2 Medium Security

A medium security rating is required for prisoners who are to be considered a lesser security risk, but for whom supervision is still required. A medium security prisoner can be supervised outside a secure area for work purposes.

2.7.3 Low Security

A low security rating provides for the limited supervision of those prisoners who have proved themselves to be trustworthy. Low security prisoners are permitted to work outside secure areas within the prison and participate in community work and other approved programmes outside the correctional institution. Low security prisoners can be assigned to undertake general and specific work duties and may be considered for low security accommodation areas, where there is an emphasis on trust, self care and responsibility and less restrictive routines apply.

2.7.4 Escape criteria

Prisoners who have one escape recorded during a current sentence are not eligible for low security until half of the accumulated sentence (remainder of current sentence at the time of the escape sentence received for the escape any additional sentence for offence(s) committed whilst at large) has been served. In the event that this exceeds a period of 5 years, such a prisoner will be eligible for low security after having served 5 years (escape free).

## NO 012 PRISONER DISCIPLINE [ss 1, 2]

### cl 1 Purpose of prisoner discipline

1.1 The purpose of prisoner discipline is to ensure the good order and security of the prison and the safety and equitable treatment of all members of the prison community. All prisoners are required to obey any lawful order given by an officer and must abide by the rules prescribed in the Act and Commissioner’s Orders.

### cl 2 Breach of prison discipline

1.1 A breach of prison discipline must have occurred if a prisoner—

(a) assaults or threatens another person;

(b) instigates, encourages or participates in a riot;

(c) escapes, attempts to escape or conspires with others to escape from custody;

(d) acts in a disruptive, abusive or indecent manner, whether by language or conduct;

(e) engages in gambling or trafficking in unauthorised articles or substances;

(f) has in his or her possession an article or substance—

(i) listed as a prohibited item (Commissioner’s Local Order No 012);

(ii) not issued or authorised by a member; or

(iii) not prescribed by a medical officer or medical practitioner;

(g) takes or uses alcohol, a drug of dependence or an unauthorised substance that has not been issued to the prisoner;

(h) tattoos on any part of his or her body or that of another prisoner;

(i) sends or receives a letter or parcel containing an article that the prisoner knows to be an unauthorised article or substance;

(j) acts in a way that is prejudicial to or threatens prison property;

(k) is in a place where he or she is not required or permitted to be;

(l) leaves the place where he or she is required to be;

(m) refuses to work or works in a careless or negligent way;

(n) disobeys a lawful instruction of a corrections officer;

(o) commits an act or omission that is contrary to the good order, management or security of the prison;

(p) repeatedly behaves in a way that is prejudicial to the good order and management of a correctional institution;

(q) prevents or hinders a corrections officer or other authorised person from carrying out their duties.

2.2 The collective punishment of prisoners is not permitted.

2.3 Prisoners must not be disciplined twice for the same breach of discipline.

2.4 Prisoners are not to be disciplined before they are advised of the offence(s) alleged, and of the action that is to be taken.

2.5 Prisoners must not be subjected to shackling, solitary confinement, corporal punishment, placement in a dark cell, sensory deprivation or any cruel, inhumane or degrading treatment as a form of discipline. There shall be no reduction of water or the minimum food rations as a form of discipline.

2.6 Prisoners must be provided with an interpreter if necessary.

2.7 Prisoners are not to be used to discipline other prisoners.

2.8 Punishments authorised by the Act or the Regulations may be imposed by the officer in charge.

## NO 013 USE OF FORCE [ss 1, 2]

### cl 1 No unnecessary use of force

1.1 No officer shall use force against any prisoner unnecessarily.

1.2 It is recognised that there are occasions where the use of force is unavoidable in maintaining the security and good order of the prison. Such use of force however will not exceed that which is considered necessary in the circumstances.

### cl 2 Circumstances for use of force

2.1 The use of force against a prisoner may be necessary in the following circumstances—

(a) in self defence or in the defence of other officers, prisoners or members of the public against any attack or imminent attack;

(b) in the prevention of an escape by a prisoner;

(c) to prevent a prisoner from self harm;

(d) in the seizure of a dangerous article;

(e) to ensure compliance where a prisoner fails to obey a reasonable direction and where he or she cannot be otherwise adequately controlled;

(f) to control prisoners acting in a deviant or defiant manner;

(g) to avoid violent and/or destructive behaviour by a prisoner;

(h) to prevent a riot or other similar disturbance.

2.2 Instruments of restraint may be used in accordance with section 47 of the Act and Commissioner’s Local Order No 013. (Note: Refer to section 47 of the Act and Commissioner’s Local Order No 013)

2.3 Incidents where force is used on a prisoner shall immediately be reported to the officer in charge with the reasons as to why such force was used. This shall also be put in writing by the reporting officer and filed in the prisoners file.

2.4 The officer in charge shall prepare a report on the force used in order 2.3 above and must submit the report to the Commissioner no later than 24 hours from receiving the first initial report.

## NO 014 PRISONER LABOUR [ss 1, 2]

### cl 1 Meaningful prisoner work

1.1 Meaningful work aimed at providing and improving skills, quality of life within the prison and rehabilitation in preparation for their return to the community, will be made available to prisoners.

### cl 2 Types of prisoner labour

2.1 The following prisoners must not be directed to work—

(a) Unconvicted prisoners;

(b) Pregnant women 3 months prior to delivery;

(c) Female prisoners 2 months post delivery;

(d) Prisoners with severe mental or physical disabilities;

(e) Prisoners who are ill or injured;

(f) Aged prisoners.

2.2 Unconvicted and aged prisoners must be permitted to work if they so desire.

2.3 Prisoners are to be given at least one day of rest in every week.

2.4 Prisoners must not be forced to work for the private benefit of any person.

2.5 All sentenced prisoners must be assessed for work by the Central Allocation and Classification Board within 2 weeks of sentencing as prescribed in Commissioner’s Order No 011.

2.6 Prisoners who possess particular skills or qualifications should be placed in work where their skills can be utilised.

2.7 Prisoners who work may receive additional remuneration as prescribed in Attachment A.

2.8 The work performance of each prisoner must be recorded in the prisoner’s personal file each month, so that it may be considered when reviewing the classification of the prisoner or considering requests for remission or early release programmes.

2.9 Where possible, work opportunities must be provided by growing farm produce to supplement prisoner rations.

2.10 The location and type of work should be appropriate to the security classification of the prisoner. Prisoners, who are classified high security, may not be put to work outside the secure compound.

2.11 Any prisoner refusing to work must be subject to disciplinary action.

## NO 015 EARLY RELEASE [ss 1, 2]

### cl 1 Prisoners for early release

1.1 In accordance with the principles of rehabilitation, prisoners who meet the criteria as stipulated in this Commissioner’s Order, the Corrections Service Act 2006 and Regulations will be eligible to apply for Early Release.

### cl 2 Circumstances where prisoners may apply for early release

2.1 Prisoners may apply to the Central Allocation and Classification Board for early release for the purposes of undertaking community work, paid employment or undertaking a course of education or instruction.

2.2 Prisoners may apply for early release on medical grounds but require supporting documentation from the visiting medical officer and must meet other prescribed requirements.

2.2 Prisoners applying for early release must meet the following criterias—

(a) the prisoner must possess a low security rating;

(b) the prisoner must be serving the last 12 months of an effective sentence;

(c) the prisoner will have demonstrated a good record of behaviour and prison work;

(d) in the case of a prisoner requesting early release to undertake study, proof must be provided of acceptance to an accredited course.

2.3 Prisoners may only be released to participate in community or paid work with organisations or enterprises that meet any prescribed requirements.

## NO 016 SHORT TERM RELEASE [ss 1, 2]

### cl 1 Introduction

1.1 In accordance with the principles of rehabilitation, prisoners who meet the criteria as stipulated in this Commissioner’s Order, the Corrections Service Act 2006 and Corrections Service Regulations 2011 will be eligible to apply for short term release.

1.2 Short term release includes weekend release or release from prison for a specified period not exceeding 14 days.

### cl 2 Criteria for grant of a short term release

2.1 Prisoners may apply to the Central Allocation and Classification Board for short term release in the following circumstances—

(a) to undertake an approved course of education or instruction;

(b) for compassionate reasons (eg to attend a funeral of a relative);

(c) to attend to urgent family matters;

(d) to be released into the care and supervision of a traditional chief or other community leader to facilitate reintegration into a village or community;

(e) to facilitate a visit with family members.

2.2 Prisoners applying for short term release must meet the following criteria—

(a) the prisoner must possess a low security rating;

(b) the prisoner must be serving the last 12 months of his or her sentence;

(c) the prisoner will have demonstrated a good record of behaviour and prison work;

(d) in the case of a prisoner requesting early release to undertake study, proof must be provided of acceptance to an accredited course.

## NO 017 PRISONER VISITS [ss 1, 2]

### cl 1 Purpose of prisoner visits

1.1 The maintenance of family and community relationships is critical to the successful reintegration of prisoners into the community. Access to programmed visits provides one method of achieving this end and enables prisoners to maintain legal contacts and support mechanisms.

1.2 Prisoner visits are to be facilitated in accordance with the provisions of the Corrections Service Act 2006 and Corrections Service Regulations 2011 and officers must act in a manner consistent with issues identified in Chapter 6 of the National Security and Human Rights Handbook.

### cl 2 Prisoner visits

2.1 A prisoner may receive at least two visits per week of no less than 30 minutes each except for special occasions like church services and so on that may be organised by the Corrections Service for the prisoners.

2.2 An area shall be set aside for the purpose of non-contact visits.

2.3 The officer in charge may approve extended visits if circumstances permit and is encouraged to be flexible in the approval of same. Visits outside the normal visit times may also be approved by the officer in charge where visitors have traveled long distances.

2.4 Every prisoner has the right to refuse a visit.

2.5 Visits must take place in the rooms or areas set aside for the purpose. The visiting area must be as clean and comfortable as possible.

2.6 Prisoners must be allowed to have reasonable limited physical contact with their visitors.

2.7 The rules and conditions applying to visits to prisoners must be posted in notice form at the visit area.

2.8 All officers must be familiar with visiting procedures and must remain alert but unobtrusive when supervising visits.

2.9 The officer in charge may by way of notice allow for food to be brought in by visitors to prisons to be provided to prisoners. Food brought in to prisons for prisoners of high risk shall be carefully checked by the corrections officers before the food is allowed to be given to the prisoner.

2.10 The officer in charge may by way of notice list down the types of food that may not be allowed to be brought into the prison for the prisoner.

2.11 Officers must treat visitors with courtesy and respect.

2.12 Legal advisers may visit prisoners at any time between 9.00 am and 5.00 pm weekdays for any periods necessary. Legal visits must be conducted within the sight but not the hearing of a supervising officer and are to be considered additional to normal visit entitlements.

2.13 Official visitors other than legal advisers may visit prisoners at the discretion of the officer in charge.

2.14 Police officers shall be permitted to visit a prisoner upon the production of a written request by the senior police officer of the section or station concerned. A prisoner may not refuse a visit from an approved police officer.

2.15 Prisoners who are seriously ill in a hospital shall be permitted unrestricted visits at the discretion of a medical officer.

## NO 018 SEPARATION OF PRISONERS [ss 1, 2]

### cl 1 Prisoners are to have separate accomodation

1.1 Male and female prisoners must be accommodated separately.

1.2 A young prisoner or prisoners considered to be at some risk shall be accommodated in separate facilities.

1.3 Where facilities permit, the following prisoners must be separated from the general prisoner population—

(a) unconvicted prisoners;

(b) prisoners who have escaped from prison, or acted contrary to the security and good order of the prison;

(c) prisoners convicted of serious crimes from minor offenders;

(d) prisoners diagnosed with a physical or mental illness or injury where there is a risk to their health or that of other prisoners;

(e) prisoners who may be vulnerable to abuse from other prisoners.

### cl 2 Officer in charge responsible for separation of a prisoner

2.1 Upon the separation of a prisoner, the officer in charge shall ensure that—

(a) a written report in accordance with attached form (Attachment A) is forwarded to the Commissioner, stating the name of the prisoner and reason for separation;

(b) the officer in charge or delegate and the visiting medical officer regularly visits the prisoner and assesses whether the terms of the separation be amended in accordance with the prisoner’s physical or mental health; and

(c) a written report (Attachment B) is forwarded to the Commissioner when a prisoner is removed from separation, stating the name of the prisoner and reason why the separation is no longer required.

2.2 In addition to inspections conducted as in accordance with order 2.1(b). the officer in charge and medical officer will conduct a formal review of separated prisoners every 30 days to assess the ongoing need for separation.

2.3 At the completion of the 30 days’ separation review, the officer in charge will submit a report detailing the findings (Attachment C), shall be forwarded to the Commissioner.

2.4 The officer in charge must ensure that all separated prisoners as well those prisoners in remand and sedentary labour are provided with a minimum of one hour of exercise per day.

2.5 If a prisoner is placed in separate accommodation, the medical officer is to ensure that the prisoner is regularly medically assessed, to determine if the separation should continue.

2.6 In the event that a female prisoner with a child is separated for health reasons, the officer in charge must consult with the medical officer to determine the ability of the mother to care for the child.

2.7 If the medical officer is of the opinion that the mother is unable to care for the child, the officer in charge must facilitate alternative arrangements for placement of the child with relatives in the first instance or a suitable foster mother within the prison.

2.8 The officer in charge must facilitate alternative arrangements for placement of a child of a female prisoner separated for any other reason, with relatives in the first instance or a suitable foster mother within the prison.

## NO 019 PRISONER REHABILITATION [ss 1, 2]

### cl 1 Prison rehabilitation objective

1.1 The Corrections Department is a key component in the multi-sectoral approach to the administration of criminal justice and must provide a secure and humane containment and rehabilitation service to sentenced prisoners. Rehabilitation in the first instance refers to offering prisoners options to change offending behaviour in order to live in the community in a law abiding manner upon their release. It involves a gradual process of developing new skills and enhancing existing skills, which challenge offence related behaviours. A rehabilitative environment encompasses all aspects of the prison environment, including prison operations, programmes and prison culture. It is therefore important that prisons provide an environment that encourages lawful behaviour.

1.2 Programmes which seek to contribute to a rehabilitative environment can reduce the opportunity for bad behaviour to occur within prison and provide an environment in which staff can offer positive examples. Such programmes provide the opportunity for developing basic skills and interests that may assist prisoner to undertake more education, vocational training, sports and recreation. Programmes which aim to reduce offending behaviour include numeracy and literacy, small business management, vocational training (work skills) parenting skills, drug and alcohol.

### cl 2 Programmes in prison to allow a rehabilitative environment

2.1 The prison environment should—

(a) encourage positive changes in behaviour and attitude;

(b) provide good social examples;

(c) challenge offending behaviour and promote self esteem;

(d) encourage prisoners to take responsibility for their actions; and

(e) promote prisoner self control.

2.2 Prisoner classification must be undertaken in accordance with Commissioner’s Order No 011.

2.3 Educational and vocational training opportunities should be made available to prisoners to develop existing skills and learn new skills to assist in their integration back into the community.

2.4 The provision of prisoner education should be developed by establishing links with NGOs. Government ministries, volunteer organisations and the private sector.

2.5 Officers and prisoners with appropriate vocational and/or education skills should be identified and utilised to assist in the delivery of education and vocational training.

2.6 Records of programmes provided to prisoners shall be maintained, which will include all achievements by prisoners in education and vocational training and participation in industry activities.

2.7 Upon admission, each prisoner must be assessed for programme needs, with particular regard to the offence committed, and regularly assessed through the Classification process. All sentenced prisoners must be provided with the opportunity to participate in programmes, including drug and alcohol, parenting, personal hygiene, sports and recreation and spiritual development.

2.8 The development and implementation of prisoner industries that provide meaningful work for prisoners supplement prison incomes and provide improved prisoner nutrition is to be encouraged.

2.9 Prison industries provide improved nutrition by supplementing prisoner diets, meaningfully occupy prisoner time, improve prisoner work skills and off-set the costs of imprisonment. Each prison must design and implement a prison industries management plan and establish effective links with private enterprise, NGOs and other community organisations in order to explore industry partnership opportunities.

2.10 The focus of prison industries is primarily on agricultural activities, including the growing of food and raising of livestock, which supplement prisoner diets and improve nutrition, whilst reducing the cost of imprisonment. Industry activities should include workshop type industries, which improve prisoner work skills. Courses providing accreditation for the training undertaken are to be facilitated by each prison.

2.11 The upbringing of a prisoner’s spiritual life is also an essential part of the rehabilitation programme.

2.12 Each prison shall be able to hold combined church services and family days for the prisoners with the prior written approval and directives of the Commissioner. The Officers in Charge or the person acting in the position of officer in charge in the absence of the officer in charge must ensure that prior written approval for such programmes has been obtained from the Commissioner or during the Commissioners absence the person acting in the position of the Commissioner before organising the event.

2.13 Organised sports builds positive self regard and fosters positive self-esteem, a healthier lifestyle and teaches social values such as sportsmanship, participation, teamwork, cooperation, respect, discipline and goodwill. All sentenced prisoners are to be permitted and encouraged to participate in and an organised sport or organised sports and recreation indoors every now and than.

2.14 The officer in charge with the approval of the Commissioner and with conditions may allow for a prisoner to take part in competitive sports outside the prison.

2.15 Upon the completion of the prisoners participation in such sports the officer in charge must ensure that a written report of the prisoners’ participation is forwarded to the Commissioner and a copy of which is kept in the prisoners file.

2.16 The officer in charge with the approval of the Commissioner and with conditions may allow for prisoners to be engaged in activities such as entertainment, general cleaning, catering, beautification. corporate activities, religious programmes, funeral gatherings and other public engagement.

2.17 Upon the completion of the prisoners participation in the above activities the officer in charge must ensure that a written report of the prisoners’ participation is forwarded to the Commissioner and a copy of which is kept in the prisoners file.

## NO 020 CHILDREN AND PREGNANT WOMEN IN PRISON [ss 1, 2]

### cl 1 Purpose of special consideration for pregnant women or mother in prison with their child

1.1 The bond between a mother and his or her child is of the utmost importance. Every endeavor should be made to ensure that any female prisoner who is incarcerated with a child is supported and encouraged to nurture the child’s proper development.

1.2 Support should be provided to a mother to assist in the development of parental skills.

### cl 2 Special consideration for pregant women or mothers in prison with their child

2.1 Consideration must be given to the best interests of a child with regard to the placement of a child with the mother in prison.

2.2 A child may live with the mother in prison until the age of 6 years.

2.3 If after assessment it is deemed inappropriate for a child to reside with the mother, steps must be taken to either place the child with relatives or appropriate alternatives via the relevant Government agency.

2.4 Proper measures must be taken to ensure the health and welfare of a prisoner during pregnancy and after childbirth. This includes—

(a) a balanced diet;

(b) medical assistance;

(c) the opportunity to give birth in hospital;

(d) appropriate clothing for the baby.

2.5 Children residing in prison are to be provided with—

(a) medical care;

(b) suitable clothing;

(c) a balanced diet;

(d) opportunities for physical and educational development.

## NO 021 MEDIA CONTACT [s 1]

### cl 1 Commissioner to grant approval for access by the media

1.1 No information or access to the prison or prisoners is to be provided to any person employed in the media without prior approval from the Commissioner.

1.2 All requests from the media for access to or information concerning the Corrections Department, prison or prisoners must be forwarded to the Commissioner. Disciplinary action will be instituted against any member of staff who knowingly provides access to or information concerning the Corrections Service or any prison or prisoner to a member of the media without prior approval from the Commissioner.

1.3 If approval is given by the Commissioner for a staff member to be interviewed by the media, he or she must not provide any information that may compromise the security of the prison, or discuss any specific prisoner or prisoner’s case.

1.4 Photographing of any prisoner by the media is prohibited.

## NO 022 STAFF TRAINING AND DEVELOPMENT [s 1]

### cl 1 Prison staff to attain proper training and development

1.1 All Corrections Service staff are to be provided with the appropriate training and development opportunities to maintain the skills and knowledge necessary to undertake their daily duties in an efficient and effective manner.

1.2 All new staff must undergo an orientation programme to familiarise them with the Prison, prison rules and the prison routine.

1.3 All staff must receive regular updates with regard to new procedures, revised procedures and new laws which impact upon prison operations.

1.4 Records must be maintained of all training conducted at a prison, which shall include—

(a) type of training;

(b) date of training;

(c) duration of training;

(d) name of trainer;

(e) names of participants.

## NO 024 PRISON ROUTINES [ss 1, 2]

### cl 1 Prison routines to vary from prison to prison

1.1 Prison routines will vary from prison to prison due to size, mix of prisoner population, location, security classifications of prisoners, work availability for prisoners, number of visitors and climate.

### cl 2 Prison routines

2.1 The prison routine must meet the needs of each prison and include:

(a) meals;

(b) visits;

(c) work;

(d) prisoner education programmes;

(e) prisoner rehabilitation programmes;

(f) medical treatment;

(g) recreation.

2.2 The officer in charge must ensure that all prisoners receive their minimum out of cells time in accordance with Prison Procedure No 2 (Classification) and the National Security and Human Rights Handbook.

2.3 The routine of each prison must be documented by the officer in charge to suit the requirements of the prison and must include—

(a) unlock times;

(b) lockup times;

(c) meal times;

(d) medical visits;

(e) visit days and times;

(f) prisoner work times;

(g) prisoner educational and vocational training;

(h) prisoner recreation times;

(i) prisoner ablutions;

(j) cell checks and inspections.

## NO 025 SMOKING BAN [ss 1, 2]

### cl 1 Smoking ban

Passive smoking is regarded as the greatest cause of heart and liver problems in the country and in a place like the prison the risks are greater due to the close proximities of prisoners and staffs.

### cl 2 Procedures

2.1 Notwithstanding provisions in the Prison Standing Orders smoking is banned in all Prison institutions with no exceptions.

2.2 The officer in charge must ensure that signs are put up stating a smoking ban for both prisoners and staff within the walls of the prisons and during work parties outside the prison.

2.3 No officer shall bring or offer any cigarettes to prisoners at any time.

2.4 Prisoners are not to receive or smoke any cigarettes from family members, friends or other visitors during visitation time.

2.5 The ban is to help improve the health of the prisoners and staff and help in the prevention of chronic disease.

## NO 026 DISCHARGE OF PRISONERS FROM PRISON [ss 1, 2]

### cl 1 Discharge from prison

1.2 The officer in charge shall be responsible for the due discharge or release from prison of all prisoners under his or her control immediately after their becoming entitled thereto.

### cl 2 Process

2.1 In accordance with the principles of rehabilitation, prisoners being discharged are to be escorted to their homes by the corrections officers to enhance the prisoners’ successful re-integration with their families and with communities at large.

2.2 The officer in charge shall, at least at the beginning of every month check the release dates of all prisoners who are due for discharge that month.

2.3 Before the discharge date the officer in charge must ensure that the family of the prisoner to be discharged as well the headman of the village or community of the prisoner is informed of the prisoners discharge and briefed about the prisoner and skills that may have been attained while in prison.

2.4 The officer in charge must ensure that before the prisoner is discharged from the prison, the prisoner is properly counselled and prepared for his or her discharge.

2.5 The corrections officer must ensure that upon taking the prisoner home, the prisoner has been accepted by his or her family, community or village headman. If this is not the case than the corrections officers shall act as a mediator between the prisoner, his or her family and the community or village headman.

2.6 The officer in charge shall on the day of discharge of any prisoner notify the appropriate authorities of such discharge and provide a written report of the discharge to the Commissioner.

## NO 027 SUPPORT FOR PRISONERS AFTER RELEASE [ss 1, 2]

### cl 1 Objectives

1.2 In line with the principles of Rehabilitation to ensure that there is support for prisoners after release.

### cl 2 Process

1.1 The officer in charge must ensure that post-release visits are conducted for prisoners who have been discharged and are out in the community to ensure that they are engaged in a more meaningful life outside the prison.

1.2 These visits shall be conducted at least twice every 3 months for a period of up to one year from the time the prisoner was released.

1.3 The officer in charge shall forward to the Commissioner a written report of each visit conducted and the outcome of the visit.
