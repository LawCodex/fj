## NO 001 PRISON VISITS [rr 1–4]

### r 1 Objective

To outline procedures for the efficient administration and facilitation of visits to prisoners.

### r 2 Scope

This Local Order applies to all operational and administrative personnel involved in the reception of person visiting prisoners.

### r 3 Definitions

3.1 legal visitor means a legal practitioner providing legal advice;

3.2 official visitors means those persons mentioned in the Commissioner’s Orders, No 005;

3.3 strip search means a search of a prisoner which requires the prisoner to remove clothing; and

3.4 visitor means a person who visits a prison either as a personal or professional visitor. Visitors to prisoners under 18 years of age must be accompanied by an adult unless otherwise approved by the officer in charge.

### r 4 Visitation procedure

4.1 Visits to remand prisoners shall occur between 9.00 am to 11.00 am on Saturdays.

4.2 Visits to convicted prisoners shall occur between 9.00 am to 11.00 am and 2.00 pm to 4.00 pm on Saturdays.

4.3 The officer in charge must assist the official visitor in whatever way he or she can.

4.4 Legal and police visits shall be permitted in accordance with Commissioner’s Order No 017.

4.5 A low security prisoner serving the last 12 months of an effective sentence, who has received no visits for 12 months, may be transferred to the prison nearest to his or her home.

4.6 The Visit Supervisor must make sure that a ‘Visitors to Prisoners Register’ is maintained and the following details recorded—

(a) the name and address of the visitor;

(b) the name and number of the prisoner visited;

(c) the date and time of the visit;

(d) in the case of a professional visit, the purpose of the visit (ie, legal, police, welfare etc); and

(e) the relationship of the visitor to the prisoner.

4.7 If the Visit Supervisor believes that there is a threat to institutional security or safety, a visitor may be denied permission to visit a prisoner.

4.8 In the event that a visitor is denied permission to visit, the reason and details of the prisoner and visitor will be recorded in the ‘Visitors to Prisoners Register’.

4.9 If the Visit Supervisor believes that there is a threat to security or safety, visits can be suspended and the officer in charge shall be advised.

4.10 A prisoner may have up to 3 visitors visiting at any one time.

4.11 High and medium security prisoners must be strip searched in accordance with Commissioner’s Local Order No 006 before and after each visit.

4.12 Corrections Service officers are to observe the prisoners and their visitors at all times to ensure that trafficking of prohibited items does not occur.

4.13 If a Corrections Service officer suspects that prohibited items have been passed, the visit is to be terminated and the prisoner immediately removed and strip searched.

## NO 002 PRISON PATROLS [rr 1–3]

### r 1 Objective

To outline procedures for the conduct of patrols in the prison.

### r 2 Scope

This Local Order applies to all staff. It is not for distribution to prisoners.

### r 3 Prison patrols procedure

3.1 The officer in charge will ensure that routine patrol responsibilities are issued for the following—

(a) posts;

(b) prisoner compounds;

(c) night guards.

3.2 All officers rostered for compound duties on day shift shall, prior to unlocking—

(a) inspect for any defect in fences, doors, locks, windows, bars;

(b) inspect for any attempted entry;

(c) ensure that services are functioning appropriately eg power, water; and

(d) report the status of the compound to the officer in charge.

3.3 Upon unlocking and during the remainder of the day shift, the compound officer shall—

(a) inspect for any irregularity in the behaviour of prisoners;

(b) conduct regular and random patrols of the compound;

(c) search all prisoners leaving or entering the compound;

(d) ensure that no unlawful assembly occurs;

(e) report any incidents or concerns immediately to the officer in charge;

(f) remain at the post at all times.

3.4 The officer in charge must ensure that a patrol of the external institution property is conducted at a minimum of one per shift.

3.5 The officer in charge must conduct regular and random patrols to ensure that all staff are at their posts and performing their allocated duty.

3.6 The officer in charge must ensure that the appropriate number of night guards and a senior ranking officer are rostered during the evening to ensure that security and good order of the prison is maintained.

3.7 Night guards shall—

(a) test the emergency siren;

(b) inspect for any defects in fences, doors, locks, windows and bars;

(c) ensure that areas required to be secure from general access are secure;

(d) ensure that services are functioning appropriately (eg power, water, security, lighting);

(e) report any incidents that occur during a shift immediately to the senior officer on duty and enter all details in the occurrence book by the completion of the shift;

(f) conduct regular and random patrols of the compound;

(g) report twice hourly to the senior officer on duty either by radio, telephone or in person;

(h) remain at the post at all times.

3.8 Night guards must not patrol quarters or enter cells during the night without the prior approval of the officer in charge.

3.9 Night guards performing external patrol duties must—

(a) inspect for any irregularity in the behaviour of persons in or near the prison;

(b) conduct regular and random patrols of the perimeter;

(c) report twice hourly to the senior officer on duty either by radio, telephone or in person;

(d) report any incidents that occur during a shift immediately to the senior officer on duty and enter all details in the occurrence book by the completion of the shift; and

(e) remain at the post at all times.

## NO 003 INSPECTION OF PRISONS [rr 1–3]

### r 1 Objective

To outline procedures for programmed and random inspections of prisons.

### r 2 Scope

This Local Order applies to all staff.

### r 3 Procedure

3.1 Inspections by Supervisors

3.1.1 The Divisional Supervisor shall conduct regular inspections of each prison within his or her Division. At least one inspection will be conducted of each prison within the Division each quarter.

3.1.2 The Divisional Supervisor must forward the inspection itinerary to the Commissioner one month prior to the inspection.

3.1.3 The officer in charge of the prison shall be notified at least 2 weeks in advance of the inspection date.

3.1.4 On each inspection, the Divisional Supervisor must—

(a) inspect all buildings and fixtures, with particular attention given to hygiene and sanitation;

(b) conduct a formal inspection of all officers except those on essential duties;

(c) inspect all stores and cash and countersign all books, registers, journals and other records required by the provisions of the Act, Regulations, Commissioner’s Orders or Commissioner’s Local Orders;

(d) inspect all arms, ammunition and special equipment with particular attention given to the age and condition of weapons, including gas containers and cartridges;

(e) see every prisoner detained at the time of the inspection and provide the opportunity to present applications, petitions or complaints. These shall include separated and sick prisoners;

(f) inquire into all complaints and applications made by prisoners and issue appropriate orders and directions with respect thereof;

(g) conduct a security inspection that includes—

(i) emergency orders;

(ii) fire orders and equipment;

(iii) vehicles and radio equipment;

(iv) locks, keys, windows, bars, doors, grilles, hinges, walls and fences;

(h) inspect the quality and quantity of rations;

(i) provide a written report which will be submitted to the Commissioner within 2 weeks of the inspection. The officer in charge shall be entitled to comment in writing, and a copy of his or her remarks shall be forwarded to the Commissioner.

3.2 Inspections by officers in charge

3.2.1 The officer in charge will conduct or cause to be conducted twice daily inspections of all wards, cells, yards, workshops, kitchens, latrines and all other parts of the prison.

The inspection must—

(a) establish that all officers rostered for duty are present and attending to their duties;

(b) ascertain when the last count of prisoners occurred, that it was properly recorded and confirm that it was correct;

(c) observe the condition of the prison, its facilities for cleanliness, orderliness, state of repair and security;

(d) ensure that all books, registers, journals and other records required by the provisions of the Act, Regulations, Commissioners Orders or Commissioners Local Orders are current and complete;

(e) observe all prisoners, including separated and sick prisoners; and

(f) observe the stores and equipment for consistency with inventories.

3.2.2 In addition to daily inspections as prescribed in section 3.2.1 the officer in charge or other delegated officers by the officer in charge will conduct weekly random visits, without warning, to every part of the prison at any hour of the night between 11.00 pm and 5.00 am.

3.3 The outcome of each inspection will be recorded and signed to include details of—

(a) the date and the time of the inspection;

(b) matters inspected;

(c) observations;

(d) actions taken;

(e) follow-up action required.

3.4 Where the inspecting officer’s observations relate to security or significant management issues he or she must advise the Supervisor within 24 hours.

3.5 The officer in charge will ensure that all issues requiring follow up are completed and recorded on the inspecting officer’s report.

3.6 Night inspection

3.6.1 The officer in charge or a designated officer, nominated by the officer in charge shall visit the Prison at least once a week between the hours of 11.00 pm and 5.00 am for security purposes.

3.6.2 During such visits, the inspecting officers shall make sure that everything is in order. They shall see all sentries on duty.

3.6.3 The officer in charge or the designated officer shall record their night inspection in the Occurrence Book together with any relevant remarks and sign the entry with the time and date.

3.6.4 At the end of each month the officer in charge shall present a copy of the entries in the Occurrence Book for the month to the Supervisor for his or her information and inspection.

## NO 004 PRISONER PROPERTY [rr 1–3]

### r 1 Objective

To outline procedures for the recording and storage of prisoner property.

### r 2 Scope

This Local Order applies to all staff and prisoners.

### r 3 Procedure storage of personal properties of prisoners

3.1 Prisoners are permitted to keep a limited amount of personal property in their possession in accordance with the following list—

(a) Items of clothing (ie T-shirts, trousers, shorts, jumper, jacket, socks, thongs, underwear, hat/cap);

(b) Books;

(c) Eye glasses (prescription);

(d) Sunglasses;

(e) Legal papers;

(f) Private papers;

(g) Musical equipment;

(h) Photographs;

(i) Towels;

(j) Toiletries;

(k) Religious jewellery, books and materials;

(l) Watch;

(m) Plastic water bottle;

(n) Writing material (ie pen, pencil, paper, postage stamps).

3.2 The officer in charge may authorise additional property upon request, giving consideration to security rating and behaviour of the prisoner.

3.3 The officer in charge of the prison shall determine the amount of property that a prisoner retains.

3.4 Property in excess of the amount permitted in cells by the officer in charge will be stored in a secure storage area.

3.5 Remand prisoners shall be permitted to wear their own clothing in prison and to court if it is appropriate, clean and in a reasonable state of repair.

3.6 The following prohibited articles will not be permitted in the prison—

(a) tools of any description, unless authorised by the officer in charge;

(b) flammable fluid;

(c) combustible material;

(d) drugs of any description;

(e) syringes and other devices for administering illegal drugs;

(f) firearms and replica firearms;

(g) weapons of any description;

(h) ammunition, explosive or detonation device;

(i) acidic, caustic or flammable substance;

(j) grappling hook, rope or ladder;

(k) cutting or spreading device;

(l) keys or replica keys, security card or electronic security device;

(m) liquor of any description;

(n) money and credit cards;

(o) passport;

(p) false identification;

(q) tattoo guns or device capable of changing appearance of resident or causing risk to his or her health;

(r) any other articles considered by the officer in charge to, either threaten prison security, or considered injurious to a person.

3.7 The officer in charge may include additional items that are considered to be a threat to the good order and management of the prison.

3.8 The officer in charge must ensure that upon admission of a prisoner, all property belonging to the prisoner is clearly labelled with the prisoner’s name and recorded in a Prisoner Property Register.

3.9 Prisoners shall not be permitted to have money in his or her possession. The stores officer will ensure that all money belonging to the prisoner is counted, valuable items documented in the prisoner’s presence and recorded in a Prisoner Property Register. All monies and valuables must be securely stored.

3.10 A receipt must be issued for all property and money that the prisoner will sign to indicate that all items have been correctly recorded. The prisoner will be issued with a copy of the receipt.

3.11 A prisoner property sheet (Attachment A) must be completed detailing all property belonging to the prisoner, which is then placed in the prisoner’s personal file. The prisoner property sheet is to be updated when property is removed from or brought into the prison.

3.12 A prisoner may request that items of property or money are handed to family members or nominated visitors.

3.13 Any medication found in the possession of a prisoner must be removed and given to the visiting medical officer.

3.14 Any items of the prisoner that is infested with vermin will be destroyed and the prisoner notified. This action is to be recorded in the Prisoner Property Register.

3.15 The reception and discharge officer must ensure where a prisoner’s personal clothing is—

(a) destroyed by staff because it is unfit for further use; or

(b) destroyed or seriously damaged in circumstances that were not under the control of the prisoner,

that suitable replacement clothing is provided upon the prisoner’s release from custody.

3.16 Property belonging to a prisoner who is transferred from another prison will be checked against the property sheet contained in the prisoner’s file and the property recorded in the Prisoner Property Register.

3.17 Property belonging to a prisoner who is being transferred to another prison will accompany the prisoner to the destination prison. The stores officer will ensure that the property sheet is correct at the time of transfer.

3.18 The stores officer must ensure that upon release from prison, all property is checked and accounted for and returned to the prisoner. The prisoner will sign the Prisoner Property Register to indicate the return of property.

3.19 In the event that a prisoner dies whilst in custody, the officer in charge will ensure that the prisoner’s property is handed to the family of the deceased prisoner.

3.20 The officer in charge must investigate all allegations of loss, damage, theft or destruction of any personal property belonging to a prisoner.

3.21 The officer in charge must document the findings of the investigation and forward the report to the Supervisor.

3.22 The Supervisor shall advise the prisoner of the outcome of the investigation. A copy of the report will be placed in the prisoner’s personal file.

3.23 No member of staff shall receive or have in his or her possession any property belonging to a prisoner.

## NO 005 PRISONER MAIL [rr 1–3]

### r 1 Objective

To outline procedures relating to all prisoner mail.

### r 2 Scope

This Local Order applies to all staff and prisoners.

### r 3 Procedure

3.1 The officer in charge must ensure that sufficient writing paper, pens, pencils, envelopes and postage stamps are made available to prisoners for the purpose of letter writing.

3.2 Prisoners may have a maximum of 2 letters posted to friends and family each week.

3.3 A prisoner may in addition to the above, send letters to the Minister, Commissioner, a visiting Magistrate, the Human Rights and Anti-Discrimination Commission and the Accountability and Transparency Commission at any time.

[para 3.3 am LN 99 of 2016 reg 41, effective 1 December 2016]

3.4 All requests by prisoners for writing materials must be directed to the compound officer at morning roll call.

3.5 The compound officer will request the writing materials from chief officer and arrange delivery to the prisoner.

3.6 The prisoner must return outgoing letters unsealed to the compound officer with the exception of letters as prescribed in order 3.3 above, which may be sealed.

3.7 The compound officer shall ensure that outgoing prisoner letters are delivered promptly to the officer in charge.

3.8 Incoming and outgoing mail may be subject to censoring. The officer in charge may inspect letters for objectionable matter and contraband, other than those listed in order 3.3 above.

3.9 Objectionable matter includes—

(a) discussion criminal methods and activity;

(b) contemptuous references to persons in authority;

(c) improper language;

(d) threats of violence or intimidatory statements.

3.10 Incoming mail that contains objectionable matter will be returned to the sender (if known), advising that the letter may be resubmitted if the material objected to is omitted.

3.11 If the contents of an outgoing letter is considered objectionable, it shall be returned to the writer.

3.12 Incoming and outgoing correspondence that is written in a foreign language may be submitted to the Commissioner for translation at the discretion of the officer in charge, prior to forwarding to the prisoner or addressee.

3.13 No inspection or other interference with mail directed to those listed in order 3.3 is permitted

3.14 The officer in charge must ensure all mail that complies with the provisions of this Local Order is posted within 24 hours of receipt.

3.15 The officer in charge must maintain a Mail Register and record the following incoming and outgoing mail information—

Incoming mail

(a) addressee;

(b) date received;

(c) date and time of delivery to prisoner;

(d) sender (if known);

(e) whether the item has been inspected and any action taken.

Outgoing mail

(a) sender;

(b) date received by chief officer;

(c) date posted;

(d) addressee;

(e) whether the item has been inspected and any action taken.

3.16 The officer in charge must ensure that all incoming mail that complies with the provisions of this Local Order is delivered within 24 hours of receipt.

3.17 Mail including legal documentation, educational material and other printed material may be retained by a prisoner, or placed in the prisoner’s property.

## NO 006 SEARCHES [rr 1–4]

### r 1 Objective

To outline procedures for conducting searches.

### r 2 Scope

This Local Order applies to all staff.

### r 3 Preamble

3.1 For the purpose of this Local Order, searching shall fall into these categories—

(a) strip;

(b) rub down;

(c) prisoner accommodation;

(d) vehicle;

(e) perimeter;

(f) workshops/gardens;

(g) articles introduced by visitors.

3.2 Searches of prisoners, visitors and areas in the prison are to be conducted to ensure the safety and security of the prison by—

(a) preventing prisoners from having weapons and other dangerous items;

(b) detecting possible escape attempts and breaches of security;

(c) discouraging theft among prisoners;

(d) minimising damage to prison property;

(e) preventing the introduction of prohibited items as described in Local Order No 012.

### r 4 Procedure

Strip Searches

4.1 The officer in charge or delegate must ensure that all prisoners are strip searched upon arrival at the prison.

4.2 The officer in charge or delegate must ensure that all prisoners are strip searched after visits.

4.3 The officer in charge or delegate may order a strip search when it is believed that there is a threat to the security or good order of the prison or prisoners.

4.4 An officer shall conduct a strip search in the following manner—

(a) all strip searches are to be conducted in a screened area;

(b) all strip searches are to be conducted by 2 officers of the same sex as the person being searched;

(c) where a female prisoner is to be searched and 2 female correctional officers are unavailable, 2 female civilian members may conduct the search;

(d) where there is only one female officer available for the strip search of the female prisoner, the male correctional officer will place himself or herself in a position where he or she can observe the female officer but not the female prisoner;

(e) the prisoner must be required to remove all clothing and footwear and face the officer with arms to the side horizontal to the ground with fingers fully extended and feet apart (approximately 50 cm);

(f) the prisoner will hand clothing as removed to an officer, who will then search the clothing;

(g) a visual search shall be conducted of the person’s mouth, hair and head;

(h) the person being searched shall comb through the hair with fingers while bending forward;

(i) the person being searched must be directed to turn away from the officer conducting the search and lift each foot off the ground and wriggle the toes to enable visual examination of the feet;

(j) where considered necessary, the person being searched shall be directed to bend over and part the cheeks of the buttocks or to lift the testicles or breasts where appropriate to ensure that nothing is concealed in those areas.

4.5 Strip searches must be conducted expeditiously, preserving the dignity of the person being searched as much as possible.

4.6 The searching officers must not touch a person whilst unclothed during the strip search unless—

(a) the person has refused to cooperate with the search; or

(b) it is considered necessary in the interests of security.

4.7 Where order 4.6 applies, force may be used to conduct a strip search, but only the minimum necessary after all other efforts to obtain cooperation have failed.

4.8 An officer shall not conduct a strip search for any reasons other than those above unless directed by the officer in charge or an officer authorised by the officer in charge.

Rub Down Searches

4.9 Any officer may conduct a rub down search of a prisoner when an officer suspects that the prisoner is carrying a prohibited item.

4.10 Officers responsible for a work party will ensure that all prisoners in the party are searched before they leave and when they return to the compound.

4.11 Escorting officers shall conduct rub down searches prior to prisoners leaving the prison.

4.12 The compound gate officer shall rub down all prisoners when leaving and returning to the institution.

4.13 Rub down searches must be conducted by one or more officer(s) in the following manner—

(a) check the head and hair before rubbing both hands along and under the arms, into the armpits, down the body to the waist area;

(b) move the hands completely around the waist area, probing underneath the waistband or belt with a thumb or finger;

(c) run both hands down the legs on the outside and up on the inside;

(d) search the crutch area by taking one hand from back to front and back again between the legs; and

(e) order the person being searched to lift the feet and extend toes to ensure that nothing is concealed under the feet.

Prisoner Accommodation

4.14 The compound officer must ensure that accommodation searches are conducted on a daily basis in the following manner—

(a) all bars are tapped;

(b) walls, floors, ceilings and roofs are observed for signs of disruption;

(c) all bedding is removed and checked;

(d) all articles are searched to ensure that no prohibited articles or substances are concealed Commissioners Local Order 012.

4.15 The main gate officer is responsible for ensuring that all vehicles are searched in the following manner—

(a) the driver and any passengers shall be ordered to stop the vehicle, turn off the engine, disembark and move away from the vehicle to an area indicated by the officer;

(b) the keys to the vehicle must be handed to the officer;

(c) a rub down search will be conducted of the driver and all passengers by another officer at the same time that the vehicle is being searched;

(d) all the doors, the boot and bonnet shall be open and a thorough inspection made of all areas of the vehicle, including the underside;

(e) any articles in the vehicle shall be removed prior to the inspection where practicable and subjected to separate inspection;

(f) details of the search are to be recorded in a Search Register kept at the entrance to the prison;

(g) vehicles may be searched at the entrance to the prison by the senior officer on duty immediately prior to leaving the prison where it is considered necessary in the interests of security;

(h) vehicles that have entered an area where prisoners are or likely to be, shall be searched;

(i) the main gate officer must record in the gate log the following details:

(i) vehicle owner;

(ii) make of the car;

(iii) registration number;

(iv) colour of vehicle;

(v) driver of vehicle;

(vi) number of passengers;

(vii) time and date;

(viii) detail any contrabands.

Perimeter Search

4.16 The officer in charge of the shift will ensure that an officer is directed to search the compound and perimeter fence prior to an unlock and at the commencement of each shift.

4.17 The officer in charge of the shift shall not give the order to unlock until satisfied that the area is secure.

4.18 The searching officer must ensure the following—

(a) fences have not been tampered with/breached;

(b) no contraband is present in the area;

(c) no suspicious persons or vehicles in the area;

(d) there is an unobstructed view of the fence;

(e) that the immediate area is free of any articles/items that may aid an escape;

4.19 The officer in charge of the shift must record the outcome of the search in the Compound Register.

Workshops

4.20 The officer in charge of the workshop must ensure that the workshop building and surroundings are searched thoroughly before and after use by prisoners.

4.21 The officer in charge of the shift must ensure that all buildings accessed by prisoners are searched a minimum of once per week. These buildings shall include shower blocks, chapel, library, classrooms, medical facility and any other buildings or areas considered necessary.

Items entering the institution

4.22 The main gate officer must ensure that all articles either leaving or entering the institution are searched for prohibited articles and details recorded in the Main Gate Search Register.

## NO 007 CLASSIFICATION OF PRISONERS [rr 1–3]

### r 1 Objective

To outline procedures for the classification of prisoners.

### r 2 Scope

This Local Order applies to all prisoners serving effective sentences (after remission) of less than 5 years.

### r 3 Procedure

3.1 There shall be a Central Allocation and Classification Board, Sentencing Planning Board and Divisional Sentencing Planning Team established.

3.2 The main role (other roles as cited in the Commissioner’s Orders No 011) of the Central Allocation and Classification Board is to assign which institution the prisoner is to be allocated to according to classes—

The unconvicted class: consisting of criminal prisoners on remand, awaiting trial, debtors and civil prisoners.

Convicted class comprising of—

(a) the young prisoner class, those prisoners under 20 years of age;

(b) the star class, first or second offenders or relatively good character and behaviour;

(c) the ordinary class, adult prisoners who are not suitable for the star class;

(d) the short sentence class, those prisoners with sentences not exceeding 3 months;

(e) the women class, all convicted criminal women prisoners.

3.3 The Central Allocation and Classification Board shall meet fortnightly.

3.4 The Central Allocation and Classification Board must ensure that all convicted prisoners are classified within 2 weeks of admission or being convicted.

3.5 The Central Allocation and Classification Board shall consist of—

(a) Assistant Commissioner Operations as the chairperson;

(b) Supervisor (Divisional);

(c) Director Education and Training/Rehabilitation;

(d) Officer in charge, Suva;

(e) Welfare officer;

(f) Prison chaplin;

(g) Medical orderly; and

(h) Staff officer operations as the secretary.

3.6 The Central Allocation and Classification Board must, when determining a classification of a prisoner, consider the following—

(a) the age and sex of the prisoner;

(b) the offence for which the prisoner is charged and circumstances of offence of which prisoner is convicted;

(c) any previous convictions against the prisoner;

(d) the prisoner’s attitude and demeanour; eg level of anger;

(e) any known associates of the prisoner in the institution;

(f) the prisoner’s skills and training;

(g) the prisoner’s needs; which may include medical, psychiatric, psychological consultation, referral and or counselling re; family conflict or family support;

(h) the prisoner’s preference regarding work location; and

(i) the current security assessment.

(j) the prisoner’s Province of origin

3.7 The role of the Sentence Planning Board or the Divisional Sentencing Planning team is to—

(a) receive reports from the Central Allocation and Classification Board;

(b) analyses the needs of the prisoners;

(c) makes recommendations to the Commissioner; and

(d) prepare the training and rehabilitation or prisoners.

In fulfilling its roles the Planning Board and the Planning Team shall take into consideration the behaviour, attitude and other characteristics of the prisoner that the Planning Board deems fit.

3.8 The Divisional Sentence Planning team shall facilitate the rehabilitation frameworks in the Northern and Western Divisions.

3.9 The Divisional Sentence Planning Team shall consist of—

(a) Divisional Supervisor as the chairperson;

(b) Officer in charge (Western and Northern Division);

(c) Chaplin (depends on the religion of the prisoner);

(d) Government representative (depending on the need);

(e) Mentor of the prisoner;

(f) Divisional Sentence Planning Officer;

(g) Community representative;

(h) Religious group representative; and

(i) Institutional Rehabilitation Officer (I.R.O.).

3.10 The Supervisor is the only person authorised to approve a convicted prisoner’s security rating.

3.11 The Sentence Planning Board team facilitates the rehabilitation frameworks in Suva, Naboro and Nasinu Prisons.

3.12 The Sentence Planning Board shall consist of—

(a) Director Educational Training as the chairperson;

(b) Assistant Commissioner for Prison;

(c) Prison chaplin; and

(d) Psychologist.

3.13 All convicted classified prisoners will be reviewed by the Central Allocation and Classification Board at least once every 3 months. Prisoners who have been classified by the Central Allocation and Classification Board at high or medium security will be reviewed within 3 months.

3.14 Prisoners who are transferred from Maximum Security to Minimum Security must be reviewed by the Central Allocation and Classification Board within 2 weeks of the transfer.

3.15 Any convicted prisoner may apply to have their classification reviewed by the Central Allocation and Classification Board.

3.16 If the application is refused, the prisoner may only re-apply after 6 months.

3.17 The officer in charge will report monthly to the Central Allocation and Classification Board. The report will contain the number of prisoners classified in that month and the outcome of security classifications and placements.

3.18 The reception and discharge officer must ensure that all convicted prisoners upon admission to the institution, are placed on the classification list.

3.19 The reception and discharge officer must ensure, on a daily basis, that any remanded prisoners who have been convicted are identified and placed on the classification list.

3.20 The reception and discharge officer must notify the officer in charge of the Prison every Tuesday and Friday of the prisoner’s requiring classification.

3.21 The officer in charge of the Prison must schedule a classification meeting each fortnight.

3.22 The reception and discharge officer must ensure that the following information is available for the classification meeting—

(a) completed convicted prisoner file

(b) Medical Report

(c) Officer’s Report/Behaviour

(d) Work Party Report

(e) Police Reports

3.23 The reception and discharge officer must record the outcomes of the meeting and forward to the officer in charge who will in turn forward it to the Sentence Planning Board or the Divisional Sentence Planning Team.

3.24 The Planning Board or the Planning Team must forward a recommendation report within 24 hours to the Commissioner.

3.25 The Commissioner shall review the recommendation and advise the Planning Board or the Planning Team accordingly who will in return advise the Supervisor.

3.26 The Supervisor must ensure that the prisoner is advised of the outcome within 24 hours.

3.27 The officer in charge must ensure that the Supervisor’s decision is recorded on the prisoners’ file.

3.28 The reception and discharge officer must place all classified prisoners on the 3 monthly review list and advice the officer in charge when a prisoner is due for review.

## NO 008 USE OF FIREARMS [rr 1–3]

### r 1 Objective

To outline procedures for the use and storage of firearms.

### r 2 Scope

This Local Order applies to all staff.

### r 3 Procedure on the use of firearms

3.1 Authorised firearms and ammunition must only be issued to corrections officers. Use of privately owned firearms is not permitted.

3.2 The officer in charge must ensure that all officers are appropriately trained in the use of firearms and refresher training is provided every 6 months.

3.3 Firearms shall only be issued to officers who have successfully completed firearms training.

3.4 A firearm is to be discharged only as a last resort, when all other non-lethal means have been exhausted, in self defence or in the defence of others, and only in the following circumstances—

(a) where there is an imminent threat of death or serious injury to staff or other prisoners;

(b) to prevent a serious crime involving a grave threat to life;

(c) to prevent escape and only when less extreme means are insufficient to achieve this objective.

3.5 The officer in charge must ensure that a secure building/room is provided to store firearms and ammunition.

3.6 The officer in charge must ensure that all firearms are stored securely within the prison and the serial number, make, year of manufacture and condition of each firearm recorded in the Firearms Register.

3.7 The officer in charge shall maintain a register of all officers trained in the use of firearms.

3.8 The officer in charge shall maintain a Firearms Issue Sheet, which shall include the following particulars—

(a) purposes for which firearms have been issued;

(b) the name and signature of the corrections officer to whom the firearm has been issued;

(c) date and time of issue and return of the firearm;

(d) serial number of the firearm;

(e) amount of ammunition issued and returned;

(f) name and signature of the correctional officer issuing the firearm; and

(g) name and signature of officer receiving returned firearms.

3.9 Officers must not carry firearms within the secure perimeter while prisoners are not secured unless authorised by the officer in charge.

3.10 The officer in charge may authorise the issue of firearms and ammunition to officers in the following circumstances—

(a) when escorting high security prisoners to court or hospital;

(b) when patrolling outside the prison walls during daylight hours;

(c) when transferring prisoners between prisons;

(d) when patrolling the prison at night or when all the prisoners are secured in their cells; or

(e) during emergencies.

3.11 Corrections officers who are issued with a firearm are to check that the firearm and ammunition are in good working order and condition before signing the firearms register.

3.12 Officers who are issued with a firearm shall—

(a) inspect the firearm and ammunition for defects and if found, report them immediately to the officer in charge;

(b) keep the firearm in their possession at all times;

(c) carry the firearm in a safe manner and take all necessary precautions to prevent accidents or damage, including—

(i) ensuring that the safety catch is on at all times;

(ii) pointing to the ground when walking;

(iii) holding the firearm across the chest when running;

(iv) holding the firearm above the head when travelling through water;

(v) storing ammunition separately;

(vi) ensuring that no prisoner or unauthorised person gains access to the firearm or any ammunition;

(vii) ensuring that prisoners do not approach within 5 metres.

3.13 An officer who discharges a firearm must provide an incident report to the officer in charge and include the following details—

(a) the place, time and reason the firearm was used and the number of bullets fired;

(b) the events leading up to the use of the firearm;

(c) a description of the incident and reasons for the use of the firearm;

(d) action taken after the firearm was used;

(e) names of all persons involved in the incident;

(f) any injury or damage caused.

3.14 If the use of a firearm causes injury to any person, the officer in charge must ensure that assistance and medical aid is provided to the injured person(s), as soon as possible.

3.15 If the use of a firearm has caused death to any person, the officer in charge must report the incident to the Police.

3.16 The officer in charge shall report to the Commissioner when an officer has discharged a firearm for any reason, other than during training.

3.17 Officers living on prison property must not keep privately owned firearms or ammunition on prison property.

## NO 010 TRANSFER AND TRANSPORT OF PRISONERS [rr 1–3]

### r 1 Objective

To outline procedures for the transport of prisoners to a court, medical facility or for transfer to another prison.

### r 2 Scope

This Local Order applies to all staff and prisoners.

### r 3 Procedures for transfer and transportation of prisoners

3.1 Upon receiving advice pertaining to the transfer of a prisoner to another prison, the officer in charge shall ensure that the officer in charge, reception and discharge—

(a) notifies the prisoner;

(b) notifies the officer in charge of the receiving institution; and

(c) notifies the prisoner’s next of kin.

3.2 The officer in charge, reception and discharge shall ensure that—

(a) the transfer authority is in order and correct;

(b) the prisoner is properly identified;

(c) the personal property of the prisoner is accounted for;

(d) all documentation pertaining to the prisoner is ready for transfer; and

(e) all prison issue equipment is returned.

3.3 All documentation and prisoner property will be transferred with the prisoner.

3.4 When all of the above is certified correct the officer in charge, reception and discharge will hand over the prisoner to the escorting officer.

3.5 The Escorting officer(s) shall—

(a) search all prisoners immediately prior to leaving the prison;

(b) receive and check property in the presence of the prisoner;

(c) check the prisoner particulars against the transfer authority;

(d) identify the prisoner’s security classification in order to apply appropriate restraint and escort as follows—

(i) High security — hand cuffs and 2 officers.

(ii) Medium security — handcuffs and one officer.

(iii) Low security — no handcuffs and one officer.

(iv) 2 or more prisoners of any security rating will be accompanied by at least 2 escorting officers. Two prisoners may be handcuffed together with one pair of handcuffs if appropriate to security rating;

(e) ensure constant supervision and arrange comfort breaks whenever required if transported by road;

(f) ensure arrangements are made for a secure area to hold the prisoner on transit if transported by air;

(g) ensure that the prisoner is handcuffed (if appropriate) and kept under observation at all times;

(h) hand over the prisoner, all documentation and personal property to the receiving officer; and

(i) ensure that the receiving officer certifies and signs for the prisoner.

3.6 The receiving officer in charge must ensure that the transfer documentation is receipted and returned to the originating prison, with a copy provided to the Divisional Supervisor of the originating prison.

3.7 Prisoners being transferred from another prison must only be received between the hours of 9.00 am and 4.00 pm, Monday to Friday unless special arrangements have been made by the officer in charge of the receiving prison.

3.8 Escort arrangements as stipulated in 3.5 above must be applied to prisoners being transported to a court, hospital or medical facility.

## NO 011 PRISONER DISCIPLINE [rr 1–3]

### r 1 Objective

To outline procedures for the discipline of prisoners.

### r 2 Scope

This Local Order applies to all staff and prisoners.

### r 3 Procedures for discipline of prisoners

3.1 A prisoner who attempts to commit or commits the following offences can either be cautioned or charged by the officer in charge—

(a) assaults or maliciously threatens another person;

(b) acts in a disruptive, abusive or indecent manner, whether by language or conduct;

(c) engages in gambling or trafficking in unauthorised articles or substances;

(d) has in his or her possession an article or substance—

(i) not issued or authorised by a member of staff;

(ii) not prescribed by a medical officer or medical practitioner; or

(iii) not permitted pursuant to the Corrections Service Act 2006 or Commissioner’s Orders;

(e) takes or uses alcohol, a drug of dependence or an unauthorised substance or article that has not been issued to the prisoner, or takes or uses alcohol or a drug of dependence lawfully issued, in a manner that was not prescribed or authorised;

(f) sends or receives a letter or parcel containing an article that the prisoner knows to be an authorised article or substance;

(g) acts in a way which is prejudicial to or threatens prison property;

(h) without the direction or permission of a member of staff—

(i) is in a place where he/she is not required or permitted to be;

(ii) leaves the place where he or she is required to be;

(iii) works in a careless or negligent way;

(iv) breaches a condition of leave of absence from the prison;

(i) disobeys a lawful order of a member of staff;

(j) commits an act or omission that is contrary to the good order, management or security of the prison or the prisoner;

(k) repeatedly behaves in a way that is prejudicial to the good order and management of a prison.

3.2 Where a member of staff reports a prisoner, the following procedures will be followed—

3.2.1 The prisoner must be informed of the report by the reporting officer.

3.2.2 The reporting officer must report the incident in writing to the officer in charge and record the offence in the Prisoners’ Punishment Register and on the prisoner’s file.

3.2.3 The reporting officer must ensure that any material required as evidence is forwarded to the officer in charge.

3.2.4 The officer in charge must ensure that an investigation is conducted which will include—

(a) obtaining a statement from the reporting officer;

(b) obtaining a statement(s) from any witness(es);

(c) viewing physical evidence of the complaint; and

(d) obtaining a statement from the accused prisoner.

3.2.5 If satisfied that there is a case to answer, the officer in charge will determine an appropriate punishment in accordance with the Act and Commissioner’s Order No 012 and ensure that a record of the punishment is entered in the Prisoners’ Punishment Register and on the prisoner’s file.

3.2.6 Criminal proceedings may be instituted against a prisoner if the officer in charge is satisfied that the prisoner has committed a criminal offence.

3.2.7 A prisoner may appeal to the Commissioner regarding any penalties within 14 days of the decision.

## NO 013 INSTRUMENTS OF RESTRAINT [rr 1–4]

### r 1 Objective

To outline procedures for the use of instruments of restraints.

### r 2 Scope

This Local Order applies to all staff and prisoners.

### r 3 Definition

Instruments of restraint means handcuffs, chemical (CS gas) and batons.

### r 4 Procedure

4.1 The officer in charge must ensure that sufficient handcuffs (plastic/metal) are available.

4.2 The officer in charge shall ensure that a Restraint Register is maintained and the following information relating to all instruments of restraint recorded—

(a) serial number;

(b) date of issue;

(c) purpose of issue;

(d) member’s name;

(e) date of return.

4.3 The officer in charge must ensure that instruments of restraint and the Restraint Register are stored in a secure area.

4.4 The officer in charge is responsible for ensuring that all instruments of restraint are in proper working order.

4.5 Escorting officers must ensure that the handcuffs are operating efficiently upon receipt and report any defects to the chief officer upon return.

4.6 All prisoners shall be handcuffed when—

(a) attending court cases;

(b) transporting to/from hospital;

(c) transporting to/from police stations;

(d) the officer in charge or delegate believes that it is necessary to prevent an escape or injury to any person; or

(e) unless directed otherwise by the officer in charge or his or her delegate.

4.7 Consideration should be given to the following when determining restraint—

(a) security rating;

(b) health of prisoner;

(c) age of prisoner;

(d) area to be visited eg court, hospital, funerals, home visits, police stations.

4.8 The escorting officer must ensure that the handcuffs do not restrict the prisoner’s blood circulation but remain secure at all times.

4.9 The escorting officer may only remove the handcuffs when it is determined safe to do so.

4.10 The escorting officer must remove the handcuffs when a prisoner is presented in court unless otherwise directed by the Judge or Magistrate.

4.11 The escorting officer may make representation to the court regarding the security of the prisoner if it is considered that there is risk of an escape.

4.12 Where a prisoner has been restrained for a continuous period of more than 24 hours or for a cumulative period of 48 hours in any 96 hour period, the officer in charge must notify the Commissioner.

4.13 Where a prisoner has been restrained as above, the visiting medical officer must examine the prisoner daily and report to the officer in charge.

4.14 If an incident occurs whereby the only effective method of restraint available is by chemical means (CS gas), the officer in charge must seek the authority of the Supervisor prior to its use.

## NO 015 EMERGENCY PROCEDURES [rr 1–4]

### r 1 Objective

To outline procedures for managing emergencies.

### r 2 Scope

This Local Order applies to all staff.

### r 3 Defintions

3.1 An emergency is defined as any incident involving the following—

(a) Fire;

(b) Flood;

(c) Earthquake;

(d) Cyclone;

(e) Any other disaster (natural or man-made).

### r 4 Procedure

4.1 The officer in charge must ensure that all prison management and staff are provided with emergency training every 6 months.

4.2 The officer in charge must develop a Prison Rule describing local emergency procedures in accordance with this procedure, which include the following—

(a) designated secure evacuation areas;

(b) location of fire fighting equipment;

(c) access points for emergency vehicles;

(d) location of emergency command area.

4.3 The officer in charge must ensure that an appropriate method of raising an alarm in the event of an emergency is installed in a prominent position at the prison (bell, alarm, siren or whistle).

4.4 In the event of an emergency, the first officer on the scene must raise the alarm and advise the officer in charge of the emergency.

4.5 The officer in charge or any officer authorised by him/her shall ensure that—

(a) all prisoners are secured in cells or an alternative safe location and counted;

(b) all visitors are removed from the prison or directed to a safe area;

(c) all gates and doors are secured unless there is a safety risk;

(d) perimeter security is increased;

(e) the municipal or police or fire brigade is notified and assistance requested if necessary; and

(f) that the Commissioner is notified.

4.6 All prisoner movement shall be restricted throughout the duration of the emergency.

4.7 In the event of fire, officers shall attempt to extinguish the fire, however they should not place themselves or others at risk.

4.8 The officer in charge must conduct an investigation into the cause of a fire.

4.9 Prisoners must be counted and returned to their cells or an alternative secure area as soon as the emergency situation is resolved.

4.10 At the conclusion of the incident the officer in charge is to prepare a report in accordance with Commissioner’s Local Order No 025 and report upon—

(a) the action taken;

(b) the extent of any permanent damage to the prison; and

(c) the extent of any injuries to any persons.

In the case of a fire, the chief must also include the findings of the investigation into the cause of the fire. This report must be submitted to the Commissioner.

## NO 016 PRISONER ESCAPE [rr 1–3]

### r 1 Objective

To outline procedures in the event of prisoner escape or attempted escape.

### r 2 Scope

This Local Order applies to all staff and prisoners.

### r 3 Procedure

3.1 An officer who discovers or observes an escape or attempted escape must immediately ensure that the alarm is sounded and shall advise the officer in charge.

3.2 If a prisoner or prisoners escape from a work group outside the prison, the officer responsible for the work group shall immediately return the remaining prisoners to the prison and report the incident to the officer in charge.

3.3 If a prisoner or prisoners escape while on escort outside the prison the officer supervising the escort is to immediately return to the prison with the remaining prisoners and report the incident to the officer in charge upon arrival at the prison.

3.4 In the event of an escape or attempted escape, the officer must inform the officer in charge who must than immediately—

(a) order that the prison alarm be turned on and secure the breached area to ensure no further escapes and remove all non-essential personnel;

(b) secure all prisoners and conduct a roll call;

(c) notify local Police of the escape and provide prisoner particulars and description;

(d) order that the neighbourhood be searched providing for the vicinity and the range of the area to be searched;

(e) allow for the use of dogs for the search for escaped prisoner(s);

(f) ensure that all duty staff are accounted for; and

(g) notify the Commissioner.

3.5 The officer in charge will complete an incident report in accordance with Commissioner’s Local Order No 025 and forward to the Commissioner within 24 hours of the escape/attempted escape.

3.6 The officer in charge must conduct an investigation into the escape/attempted escape and forward the report with findings and recommendations for any action necessary, to the Commissioner within 7 working days.

3.7 All reasonable force necessary shall be used for the recapture of the prisoner (s).

3.8 Any use of firearms shall in accordance with Commissioner’s Local Order No 008.

3.9 The search for the prisoner shall continue until the prisoner has been recaptured.

## NO 017 HOSTAGE AND RIOT INCIDENTS [rr 1–3]

### r 1 Objective

To provide guidelines for the management of hostage and riot incidents in prisons.

### r 2 Scope

This Local Order applies to all staff. It must not be distributed to prisoners.

### r 3 Procedure to be followed in the event of hostage and riot incidents

3.1 In the event of a hostage or riot incident occurring, the officer in charge must—

(a) assume responsibility for the incident operation;

(b) advise the local Police for the purpose of providing external perimeter security; and

(c) nominate an appropriate officer as negotiator, if necessary.

3.2 The officer in charge or his or her delegate shall advise the Commissioner.

3.3 The incident scene is to be isolated and as many officers and prisoners as possible removed from the area.

3.4 All movement of prisoners must cease and prisoners normally accommodated within the area, but not involved in the incident, are to be secured in an alternative area.

3.5 All visits should stop and all visitors are to be ushered to safety.

3.6 The officer in charge must appoint an officer to maintain services for the duration of the incident.

3.7 The officer in charge must nominate a minimum number of officers to remain as close to the area as is safely possible to observe the situation and record all relevant details including—

(a) initial actions by prisoners or other persons;

(b) demands made;

(c) time lines or deadlines given;

(d) identify of offender(s), including spokesperson(s);

(e) identity of other persons in the area;

(f) number of offenders;

(g) number of hostage(s) if applicable;

(h) chronological sequence of events;

(i) locations if known, of individuals within the incident scene;

(j) any weapons used;

(k) attitudes and behaviour of the offender(s);

(l) physical/medical condition of other person(s).

3.8 The officer in charge must ensure that a count of all prisoners is undertaken.

3.9 All other persons within the prison are to be accounted for.

3.10 The offenders involved in the incident are not to be provided with any items and in the event of a hostage situation. Orders or directions from any hostage(s) or hostage taker(s) are not to be acted upon. Exchanges of any hostage(s), provision of additional hostage(s) or release of hostage taker(s) must not occur.

3.11 The officer in charge must ensure that normal operation of the prison is resumed as soon as possible after the resolution of the incident.

3.12 The officer in charge shall provide a full report on the incident to the Commissioner within 24 hours of resolution in accordance with Commissioner’s Local Order No 025.

3.13 The officer in charge shall ensure that an operational debrief is conducted within 5 days of the incident occurring.

## NO 018 DEATH IN CUSTODY [rr 1–3]

### r 1 Objective

To outline procedures in the event of a prisoner’s death in custody.

### r 2 Scope

This Local Order applies to all staff.

### r 3 Procedure

3.1 A prisoner is deemed to have died in custody if the death occurs in the following places—

(a) in a prison;

(b) during transfer between prisons;

(c) in hospital;

(d) on day leave.

3.2 All deaths that occur in prison shall be considered suspicious until the Police find otherwise or the medical officer confirms death through natural causes as a result of an existing medical condition.

3.3 In the event of death of a prisoner in prison the officer in charge shall—

(a) ensure that the area where the body is found is secured by removing all personnel and prisoners from immediate area;

(b) ensure that exit and entry points to the scene are established;

(c) appoint an officer to control access to and from the scene;

(d) ensure that any prisoner who may have witnessed the death or events prior to or immediately after the death is isolated;

(e) advise the medical staff and the Police;

(f) advise the Commissioner;

(g) complete an incident report in accordance with Commissioner’s Local Order No 025 and include the following details—

(i) the name, age and last known place of residence of the deceased prisoner;

(ii) the place of death;

(iii) the time and date of death;

(iv) the cause of death, if known;

(v) the location of the deceased’s body;

(vi) the circumstances leading to the death and if the death is suspicious, the name of the police officer investigating the death;

(vii) the deceased prisoner’s date of admission to prison and the reason for imprisonment.

3.4 If a prisoner dies in hospital, the officer in charge shall advise the Commissioner. If the medical officer considers that the death of the prisoner as suspicious the officer in charge shall advise the Police and records of such shall be put in the prisoners file.

3.5 The officer in charge must ensure that the next of kin or other appropriate person is notified as soon as possible and that arrangements are made for the burial or cremation of the body. In the event that the next of kin is unable to facilitate removal of the body, the officer in charge will make the necessary arrangements.

3.6 The officer in charge must ensure that the next of kin or appropriate person is provided with as much information as possible about the death;

3.7 The officer in charge must ensure that personal property belonging to the deceased is returned to the next of kin or appropriate person as soon as possible, in accordance with Commissioner’s Local Order No 004 (Prisoner Property). If the prisoner was a foreign national, the personal property must be forwarded to the relevant embassy or consulate.

3.8 If the deceased prisoner is a foreign national, the officer in charge must advise the relevant embassy.

3.9 Within 3 days after the death of a prisoner, the officer in charge must submit a written report of the death to the Commissioner and the family of the deceased prisoner.

3.10 The officer in charge must forward the Death Certificate of the prisoner to the Commissioner as soon as it is issued and made available and a copy of it put in the prisoners file.

3.11 The officer in charge shall mark the deceased prisoner’s personal file with “Died in Custody”. Copies of all reports pertaining to the prisoner are to be placed on the prisoner’s file which is then to be forwarded to the Fiji Corrections Service, Headquarters for storage.

## NO 019 PRISONER FILES [rr 1–4]

### r 1 Objective

To outline procedures for the maintenance of prisoner files.

### r 2 Scope

This Local Order applies to all staff.

### r 3 Preamble

3.1 A personal file must be maintained for each prisoner in the institution. All documentation relating to the prisoner, with the exception of medical reports, is to be placed in the prisoner’s file.

3.2 Medical reports must be placed on a medical file.

### r 4 Procedure

4.1 The officer in charge is to ensure that a personal file is raised for each prisoner within 24 hours of admission.

4.2 Upon initially raising a personal file, the interviewing officer must ensure that all relevant sections on the front of the prisoner file are completed and a photograph of the prisoner attached to the inside cover.

4.3 In the event that the prisoner has transferred from another prison, the officer in charge must ensure that the personal file accompanying the prisoner from the transferring prison is forwarded to the admitting officer for updating during the admission process.

4.4 All subsequent information pertaining to the prisoner, including behaviour, work status and classification must be updated in the prisoner file as it becomes available.

4.5 The officer in charge must ensure that prisoner files are kept in a secure location at the prison. Prisoner files must not be removed from the prison for any purpose other than the transfer of a prisoner or for filing at the Corrections Service Headquarters.

4.6 If a prisoner’s file is lost or accidentally destroyed a written report is forwarded to the Commissioner, detailing the circumstances surrounding the loss or destruction of the file. A new file is to be raised for the prisoner marked “COPY”, which is to contain as much of the original information as can be obtained from other prison records, the Judicial, Police and courts.

4.7 Access to the information contained in a prisoner’s file shall be restricted to the Commissioner, Supervisor, officer in charge and authorised Corrections Service officers and officials by the Supervisor or officer in charge.

4.8 Any Corrections Service officer or official who obtains information from a prisoner’s file without authorisation will be subject to disciplinary action.

4.9 A prisoner may be granted permission to view information contained in his or her personal file, unless the Supervisor or officer in charge considers that the information may endanger the safety of another person or may affect the security and good order of the prison.

4.10 In the event of a prisoner transfer, the officer in charge must ensure that a prisoner’s personal file and medical reports accompany the prisoner to the destination prison.

4.11 The officer in charge must ensure that upon the release or death of a prisoner, the prisoner’s personal file and any medical reports are forwarded to the Corrections Service for storage.

## NO 020 PRISON SECURITY [rr 1–4]

### r 1 Objective

To outline procedures for maintaining the general security of prisons and prisoners.

### r 2 Scope

This Local Order applies to all staff.

### r 3 Preamble

3.1 Officers are to maintain a high level of supervision and observation of prisoners at all times. Officers are to look for and report any unusual or suspicious actions by prisoners.

3.2 Officers are to be alert at all times whilst on duty.

### r 4 Procedure

4.1 Key and Lock Control

4.1.1 Officers must ensure that prisoners do not have access to prison keys or locks at any time.

4.1.2 The officer in charge must ensure that a Lock and Key Register (Attachment A) is established and a Daily Lock and Key Issue Sheet (Attachment B) is maintained.

4.1.3 The senior operations officer must maintain the Lock and Key Register.

4.1.4 The senior operations officer must ensure that all locks and keys that are issued on a daily basis are registered in the Daily Lock and Key Issue Sheet.

4.1.5 Any keys issued to officers that are not required must be returned immediately to the senior operations officer.

4.1.6 The senior operations officer must ensure that all keys are kept in a secure location that is inaccessible to prisoners. Duplicate keys are to be securely stored in a separate location.

4.1.7 Officers must immediately report any lost or damaged key or lock to the senior operations officer.

4.1.8 The officer in charge shall ensure that all locks are maintained in a useable condition and oiled regularly.

4.2 Prison Entry Security

4.2.1 Front gate officers shall ensure that—

(a) all persons wishing to enter or leave the prison are identified and authorised;

(b) all authorised low security prisoners are permitted outside the prison walls to work;

(c) the prison gate remains secure at all times;

(d) the identities of all prisoners being admitted, discharged or on escort are recorded;

(e) all prisoners entering and leaving the prison are searched;

(f) all vehicles entering and leaving the prison are searched; and

(g) all movement into and out of the prison is recorded in the front gate register.

4.3 Movement control

4.3.1 The senior operations officer shall ensure that a Prisoner Daily Record Book is maintained and the following recorded—

(a) daily number of prisoner admissions, releases, transfers and other movement to and from the prison recorded;

(b) the names of prisoners attending court;

(c) the names of prisoners in hospital;

(d) the number and names of prisoners by cell location;

(e) the total number of prisoners; and

(f) the names of prisoners allocated to work outside the prison and the name(s) and signature of officer(s) responsible.

4.3.2 Before a prisoner is taken outside the prison walls, the nominated officer must ensure that all the above details are recorded in the Prisoner Daily Record Book.

4.3.3 All escorts must be undertaken in accordance with the provisions of Commissioners Local Order No 010.

4.3.4 The officer in charge must check and sign the Prisoner Daily Record Book each day.

4.4 Tool Control

4.4.1 The senior operations officer must ensure that all tools are issued with a serial no and a register of tools is maintained. (Attachment C).

4.4.2 The senior operations officer must ensure that all tools used in workshops, gardens and kitchens are securely stored when not being used.

4.4.3 The senior operations officer must ensure that the issue of tools is recorded on the Daily Tools Issue Sheet (Attachment D) and that all issued tools are returned and accounted for at the end of each day.

4.4.4 Officers who supervise prisoners in workshops, gardens and kitchens are to check the list of tools before giving any work tool to prisoners and then count and secure all tools at the conclusion of work, before allowing prisoners to leave the work area. Corrections Service officers are only to issue tools necessary for prisoners to complete the work required.

4.4.5 In the event that a tool or tools are missing at the conclusion of work, the senior operations officer will ensure that—

(a) prisoners are not permitted to leave the work area;

(b) a thorough search of the work area and prisoners is carried out;

(c) the officer in charge is advised if the tool is not found.

4.4.6 Officers must report any broken or damaged tools to the officer in charge.

4.4.7 Tools that belong to a officer may only be used in the prison with the approval of the officer in charge.

## NO 021 RADIO COMMUNICATION [rr 1–3]

### r 1 Objective

To outline procedures for the use of portable hand-held radios.

### r 2 Scope

This Local Order applies to all staff.

### r 3 Procedure

3.1 The officer in charge shall ensure that the radio system and all handheld radios are in operating order.

3.2 The communication officer is responsible for—

(a) issue and recording of all handheld radios;

(b) ensuring that the batteries are fully charged;

(c) maintaining a Maintenance Register for each unit; and

(d) safe storage of the handheld radios when not in use.

3.3 The communication officer shall monitor radio communication to ensure that all transmission is restricted to Corrections Service business.

3.4 When using radios, officers are to—

(a) be brief;

(b) be accurate;

(c) transmit in slightly slower than normal speech;

(d) keep messages simple and explicit;

(e) hold the microphone approximately 2 to 4 cm from the mouth when speaking;

(f) not speak directly into the microphone — speak across the microphone; and

(g) not shout into the microphone as the message will become distorted and clarity will be lost.

3.5 All officers in receipt of radios shall ensure the following—

(a) that batteries are fully charged;

(b) the radio is in good working order; and

(c) test calls are conducted.

3.6 The communication officer must ensure that upon receipt of an emergency transmission, all radio traffic is monitored and restricted to management of the emergency.

## NO 022 PRISONER DISCHARGE [rr 1–3]

### r 1 Objective

To outline procedures for the discharge of prisoners from prison.

### r 2 Scope

This Local Order applies to all staff and prisoners.

### r 3 Procedure

3.1 The officer in charge is to ensure that a prisoner is released immediately—

(a) upon completion of the sentence;

(b) upon the granting of bail, conditional release;

(c) upon acquittal; or

(d) upon the granting of early release.

3.2 The admissions and discharge officer will ensure that—

(a) all prison property issued to the prisoner is returned and accounted for;

(b) all of the prisoner’s property is returned to the prisoner and accounted for in accordance Commissioner’s Local Order No 004;

(c) the prisoner is searched;

(d) the prisoner’s personal file is marked ‘Released’ and dated; and

(e) the prisoner is removed from the prison count and cell location book.

3.3 The officer in charge shall, upon the release of a prisoner who has been in prison for a period exceeding one year, provide the prisoner with civilian clothing, shoes and the necessary amount money for food and travel.

3.4 The officer in charge must ensure that where a prisoner is to be deported the prisoner is handed to the appropriate authority on the due date in accordance with the signed letter of authority or the order of the relevant legally constituted court.

## NO 023 USE OF PRISON VEHICLES [rr 1–4]

### r 1 Objective

To outline procedures for the use, control and maintenance of corrections vehicles.

### r 2 Scope

This Local Order applies to all staff.

### r 3 Preamble

3.1 Only authorised officers holding current Fijian driver’s licence applicable to the type of vehicle are to drive prison vehicles.

3.2 The Corrections Service vehicles are only to be use for official Corrections Service business.

3.3 The Corrections Service staff are not permitted to drive Corrections Service vehicles whilst under the influence of alcohol.

### r 4 Procedure

4.1 The transport officer shall ensure that—

(a) a record of all Departmental motor vehicles within the prison is maintained, detailing type, make, model and year of manufacture, condition and passenger capacity of the vehicle;

(b) all Corrections Service vehicles are serviced regularly and maintained in a roadworthy condition;

(c) all Corrections Service vehicles have current motor vehicle registration;

(d) all Corrections Service vehicles are housed in a secure area when not in use;

(e) a vehicle log is maintained for each vehicle;

(f) the vehicle log book is signed weekly:

(g) sufficient fuel is available;

(h) vehicles are used for authorised purposes only; and

(i) Corrections Service vehicles are allocated on the basis of the priorities of Corrections Service business.

4.2 All Corrections Service staff using a Departmental vehicle must—

(a) complete the Departmental Vehicle Log (Attachment A) each time a vehicle is used;

(b) check the vehicle prior to each use (eg tyres, fuel, oil, water and general condition);

(c) report to the Deputy Chief of Corrections Service (Administration) any defects found;

(d) drive the vehicle in a safe and proper manner;

(e) not use a Departmental vehicle for private or unauthorised purposes; and

(f) ensure that the vehicle is cleaned after use.

4.3 If a prison vehicle is involved in an accident resulting in damage to the vehicle or injury to any person, the driver must—

(a) advise the local police;

(b) advise the officer in charge; and

(c) complete a Vehicle Accident Report (Attachment B) and forward to the officer in charge.

## NO 024 PRISONER COUNTS [rr 1–3]

### r 1 Objective

To outline procedures for the regular counts of prisoners.

### r 2 Scope

This Local Order applies to all staff.

### r 3 Procedure

3.1 The officer in charge must ensure that a Prisoner Count Board is maintained at both the compound gate and the Reception and Discharge Office.

3.2 The officer in charge must ensure that a prison count is conducted at the following times:

(a) 7.30 am morning unlock;

(b) 8.30 am roll call;

(c) 12.00 pm lunch;

(d) 1.00 pm after lunch;

(e) 5.00 pm evening lock down,

provided in exceptional circumstances and based on good reasons the officer in charge may decide to unlock before 7.30 am or 5.00 pm.

The prison count will be conducted in the following manner:

| | | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| • | Morning unlock | All prisoners must be counted as they leave the dormitories by the compound officers. |
| • | Morning roll call | All prisoners must assemble in line at the designated roll call area. The compound officers must conduct a physical count and the reception and discharge officer will call the roll and reconcile the prison count. |
| • | Lunch count | A head count must be conducted by the compound officers as the prisoners enter the compound. The officer in charge must ensure that the institutional count is reconciled. |
| • | After lunch | All prisoners must line up at the roll call area and a head count shall be conducted by the compound officers and work party officers. All work party prisoners are detailed to work. The officer in charge must ensure that the prison count is reconciled. |
| • | Return of work parties | Work party officers must ensure that all prisoners are searched and counted on return to the compound. The compound gate officer must confirm the work party count. |
| • | Evening lock up | At the completion of evening meal, all prisoners must line up adjacent to their respective dormitories or cells. The compound officers must conduct a head count followed by roll call conducted by the duty reception and discharge officer. The prisoners must answer to the roll and move into their dormitories or cells and be secured by the compound officers. The duty reception and discharge officer must reconcile the prison count. The officer in charge must be satisfied that the count is correct. |

3.3 Following the completion of each count, the officer in charge, reception and discharge must reconcile the Compound Tally Board, Shift Report Book, Reception and Discharge Master Board and the Roll Book to determine the prison count.

3.4 The officer in charge, reception and discharge must report the count to the Supervisor, morning, lunch time and evening.

3.5 Work party officers must ensure that they have the appropriate number of medium and low classified prisoners prior to leaving the institution.

3.6 Work party officers must ensure that the prisoners in their charge are sighted at all times and counted at 15 minute intervals.

3.7 On leaving the prison, the main gate officer must count and record on the board, all prisoners leaving the institution, eg court, hospital, work parties etc.

3.8 The compound gate officer must record all returns and new admission on the tally board.

3.9 Shift officers must ensure that a minimum of 4 physical counts (including night shift takeover) of low security prisoners, occurs between the hours of 6.00 pm and 6.00 am and recorded in the shift report book by the officer in charge, reception and discharge.

3.10 The officer in charge, reception and discharge must ensure that the prison count is correct before any officer is stood down or relieved.

3.11 In the event of an escape, or the count is incorrect during day shift, the officer in charge, reception and discharge will ensure that the following procedures are adopted immediately—

(a) the siren is activated;

(b) recall all prisoners and conduct full roll call;

(c) prisoners must be physically sighted and noted as recorded in the roll book; and

(d) the roll book total is to be compared with the morning head count for consistency, by the officer in charge.

## NO 025 INCIDENT REPORTING [rr 1–4]

### r 1 Objective

To outline procedures for the reporting of incidents in the prison.

### r 2 Scope

This Local Order applies to all staff and is not for distribution to prisoners.

### r 3 Definitions

Incidents that occur in prison are defined as the following—

(a) escape or attempted escape of a prisoner/s from any location;

(b) prisoner unrest/riot;

(c) death of a prisoner;

(d) death or injury of an officer whilst on duty;

(e) serious injury to a prisoner;

(f) assault by a prisoner on any other person;

(g) assault by an officer on a prisoner;

(h) a fire which causes damage to Corrections Service property or injury to any person;

(i) a natural disaster or any other event which results in the evacuation of prisoners;

(j) attack or threat to the prison from outside;

(k) action by a prisoner or officer which may result in criminal charges;

(l) any incident involving a Corrections Service vehicle or any vehicle hired by the Corrections Service or damage to a Corrections Service vehicle.

(m) detection of drugs, alcohol, poisonous substances or weapons on a prisoner or a visitor to the prison;

(n) any other event that poses a serious threat to staff or the security and good order of the prison.

### r 4 Procedure

4.1 Whenever an incident occurs the officer in charge shall immediately notify the Commissioner.

4.2 The officer in charge shall submit an Incident Report to the Commissioner (Attachment A) within one week of the incident occurring and include the following information—

(a) the type of incident;

(b) the location of the incident;

(c) the time and date of the incident;

(d) the names of any prisoners, officers and any other persons involved;

(e) details of how the incident occurred;

(f) details of any investigation conducted by the Police;

(g) details of any injuries to prisoners, officers or other people;

(h) details of any damage to prison property and the estimated time and cost of repair;

(i) details of any action being taken against prisoners, corrections officers or other persons as a result of the incident;

(j) photographs, drawing or maps relating to the incident; and

(k) comments on what action is being taken, or may be taken to prevent similar incidents occurring.

4.3 If a serious incident occurs which requires investigation by the Police, the officer in charge must ensure that the scene and any evidence is preserved and no person enters the area where the incident occurred until the police give approval to do so.

4.4 The officer in charge must ensure that copies of all incident reports are filed at the prison.

## NO 026 CHILDREN AND PREGNANT WOMEN IN PRISON [rr 1–3]

### r 1 Objective

To provide procedures for the management and care of pregnant women and their children in prison.

### r 2 Scope

This Local Order applies to all staff.

### r 3 Procedure

3.1 The officer in charge must ensure that appropriate measures are taken to ensure the health and welfare of a prisoner and child during pregnancy and during and after childbirth in accordance with Commissioner’s Order No 020.

3.2 The visiting medical officer must monitor a pregnant prisoner’s health monthly during the first trimester, fortnightly during the second trimester and weekly during the third trimester. Any serious medial concerns must be reported to the officer in charge, who must ensure that medical treatment is provided when necessary.

3.3 Pregnant women must not be directed to work 2 months prior to and 3 months after delivery.

3.4 When a prisoner gives birth inside the prison, a visiting medical officer must attend the birth.

3.5 If a prisoner gives birth inside the prison the place of birth shall not be recorded as a prison on the child’s registration of birth.

3.6 While a child or children are accommodated with the mother in prison the mother is responsible for the care and safety of such child/ren.

3.7 When allocating work duties, the officer in charge must ensure that the child/ren’s mother has sufficient time to adequately provide for the care and safety of the child/ren.

3.8 The officer in charge must ensure that the child/children residing in prison are provided with the appropriate support in accordance with Commissioner’s Order No 020.

3.9 When a prisoner is unable to adequately care for her or his child/ren in the prison for any reason, the officer in charge, after making reasonable efforts to assist the prisoner in giving adequate care, must arrange with the appropriate Government agency or family member, for the child/ren’s care outside the prison.

3.10 In the event that a child is removed from the prison, the officer in charge will provide a comprehensive report to the Commissioner.

3.11 A child accommodated in the prison may leave and return to the prison with the approval of the officer in charge in the company of an approved person. Every effort will be made to ensure that the child has regular contact with family members.

3.12 Children may be searched by a female corrections officer or visiting medical officer in the company of their mother.

3.13 When a child reaches the age of 6 years, the officer in charge will facilitate the removal of that child from the prison. A suitable care provider will be nominated in consultation with the mother and family members and/or relevant Government agency representatives.

3.14 In the event of a child dying while accommodated in the prison, the officer in charge will act in accordance with Commissioner’s Local Order No 018.

## NO 027 PRISONERS TELEPHONE AND MOBILE CALLS [rr 1–3]

### r 1 Objective

To outline procedures relating to all prisoners telephone and mobile calls.

### r 2 Scope

This Local Order applies to all staff and prisoners.

### r 3 Procedure

3.1 The officer in charge must ensure that prisoners are provided with an opportunity to make telephone or mobile calls only for reasons of urgent matters and where communication by letter would cause undue delay, inconvenience or inefficiency.

3.2 The prisoner shall fill out the appropriate form before making the approved urgent phone call and the approved form shall be kept in the prisoners file while a copy of the form will be kept in the officer in charge’s file.

3.3 The officer in charge shall determine matters which are urgent after careful considerations and the appropriate times for the phone calls to be made.

3.4 Once the urgent phone call has been approved by the officer in charge, the prisoner must be briefed that the telephone call shall only be for the urgent purpose for which the call is to be made and shall not divert otherwise.

3.5 The telephone conversation shall be made in the officer in charge’s room or a room approved by the officer in charge in the presence of an officer permitted by the officer in charge.

3.6 The officer shall than make the mobile or telephone phone call (which ever method is approved by the officer in charge) and speak to the person to whom the prisoner would like to speak to, informing the person of the reason for the phone call before handing the phone to the prisoner.

3.7 The telephone conversation shall be within sight and hearing of the officer.

3.8 The officer has the right to stop the conversation if he or she feels that the prisoner is diverting from the urgent reason for the call and shall submit a written report of the incident to the officer in charge with 12 hours from the incident.

3.9 A copy of this report shall be filed in the prisoners file.

3.10 The officer in charge will maintain a prisoners mobile and telephone call book in which will be recorded the—

(a) date;

(b) time;

(c) prisoner making the call;

(d) person receiving the call;

(e) length of the call.

## NO 028 PRISONERS LABOUR — OUTSIDE NORMAL PRISON ROUTINE [rr 1–3]

### r 1 Objective

To outline procedures relating to all prisoners doing work outside normal prison routine or during combine prison labour.

### r 2 Scope

This Local Order applies to all prisoners and corrections officers.

### r 3 Prison labour — outside normal prison routine

3.1 The officer in charge may in exceptional cases allow the prisoner to be able to work outside the normal prison routine, apart from the type of work specified in the Commissioner’s Orders (Order No 14).

3.2 The officer in charge in such cases should select prisoners based on the skills relevant to the work required, behaviour and attitude.

3.3 The officer in charge must ensure that the prisoners as well as the corrections officer are clearly briefed on the details of the task required.

3.4 The officer in charge must clearly choose the number of prisoners that are to be involved in this type of work and this shall be recorded in the prisoners file.

3.5 The officer in charge shall ensure that there is proportionate number of corrections officers to the prisoners.

3.6 The officers shall ensure that the prisoners are provided with meals before they labour.

3.7 The officer in charge shall closely monitor the progress of the work being done and shall present a report to the Divisional Supervisor during every stage of work done.

## NO 029 COMBINE PRISON LABOUR [rr 1–3]

### r 1 Objective

To outline procedures relating to all prisoners and corrections officers doing combine prisoners labour.

### r 2 Scope

This Local Order applies to all prisoners and corrections officers.

### r 3 Combine prisoner labour

3.1 The officer in charge may in exceptional cases allow prisoners and the officers to be able to perform combine labour apart from the type of work specified for prisoners in the Commissioner’s Orders (Order No 14).

3.2 This type of labour may include projects on civic pride or any other projects that the officer in charge may see fit that would enable the prisoner to use his or her skills for the greater good and also help in the prisoners’ rehabilitation.

3.3 The officer in charge during such cases should select prisoners based on the skills relevant to the work required, behaviour and attitude.

3.4 The officer in charge shall ensure that the prisoners as well as the officer are clearly briefed on the details of the task required.

3.5 The officer in charge shall clearly choose the number of prisoners that are to be involved in this type of work and this shall be recorded in the prisoners file.

3.6 The officer in charge must ensure that there is proportionate number of officers to the prisoners.

3.7 The officers must ensure that the prisoners are provided with meals before they labour.

3.8 The officer in charge shall closely monitor the progress of the work being done and shall present a report to the Divisional Supervisor during every stage of work done.
