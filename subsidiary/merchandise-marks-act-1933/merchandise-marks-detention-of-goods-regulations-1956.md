# MERCHANDISE MARKS (DETENTION OF GOODS) REGULATIONS 1956

Merchandise Marks (Detention of Goods) Regulations 1956 (LN 91 of 1956) commenced on 5 October 1956, as amended by:

| --- | --- |
| Revised Edition of the Laws (Consequential Amendments) Regulations 2016 (LN 99 of 2016) | 1 December 2016 |
| Trademarks Act 2021 (No 36 of 2021)1 | |

1
As at the date of publication of Service 24, the Trademarks Act 2021 (No 36 of 2021) has not commenced and its amendments have not been incorporated.

## reg 1 Short title

These Regulations may be cited as the Merchandise Marks (Detention of Goods) Regulations 1956.

## reg 2 Interpretation

In these Regulations, unless the context otherwise requires—

**officer of customs** means an officer acting under general or special direction of the Comptroller; and

**value of goods** means value irrespective of duty.

## reg 3 Detention of goods

All goods referred to in section 15 of the Act which, upon examination, are detected by an officer of customs, are to be detained by him or her without the requirement of previous information.

## reg 4 Conditions

In giving information with a view to detention, an informant shall fulfill the following conditions—

(a) he or she shall give to the Collector of Customs for the port of expected importation, notice in writing stating—

(i) the number of packages expected, as far as he or she is able to state the same;

(ii) the description of the goods by marks or other particulars sufficient for their identification;

(iii) the name or other sufficient indication of the importing ship;

(iv) the manner in which the goods infringe the Act; and

(v) the expected date of arrival of the ship;

(b) he or she shall deposit with the Collector of Customs as aforesaid a sum sufficient, in the opinion of the Collector of Customs, to cover any additional expenses which may be incurred in the examination required by reason of his or her notice.

## reg 5 Security may be required

If, upon arrival and examination of the goods, the officer of customs is satisfied that there is no ground for their detention, they shall be delivered. If he or she is not so satisfied, he or she shall decide either to detain the goods, as in a case of detention upon ordinary examination, or to require security from the informant for reimbursing the Comptroller or his or her officers in respect of all expenses and damages incurred in respect of the detention made on his or her information and of any proceedings consequent thereon.

## reg 6 Form of security

The security thus required must be an immediate *ad valorem* deposit of 10% on the value of the goods, as fixed by the officer of customs from the quantities or value shown by the entry; and, also, subsequently a bond to be completed within 4 days in such sum not less than double the value of the goods as the Comptroller may require, with 2 approved sureties. The *ad valorem* deposit shall be returned upon completion of the bond, and shall not be required if, as an alternative where time permits, the informant prefers to give a like bond before examination upon estimated value of the goods declared to by him or her under statutory declaration. If the security is not duly given as above required there shall be no further detention of the goods.

## reg 7 Forms

The “notice” and “bond” required as above shall be in the forms contained in the Schedule, or in such other form as the Comptroller may from time to time order and direct.

## reg 8 Discharge of security

The security taken under these Regulations shall be given up at the times following—

(a) where given before examination, and if no detention, forthwith;

(b) where given on detention—

(i) if the forfeiture is completed, either by lapse of time or ultimate condemnation by a court, then on such completion of forfeiture;

(ii) if the forfeiture is not completed, then—

(aa) if the goods are released by the Comptroller and no action or suit has been commenced against him or her or any of his or her officers, in respect of the detention, then at the expiration of 6 months from the time of detention; or

(bb) if the goods are released for failure of proceedings taken for the forfeiture and condemnation thereof upon complaint under Part 21 of the Customs Act 1986, and no action or suit has been commenced against the Comptroller or any of his or her officers, in respect of the detention, then at the expiration of 6 months from the trial of such complaint;

(iii) if within such periods as aforesaid any such action or suit as aforesaid has been commenced then upon the ultimate conclusion of such action or suit, and the fulfilment of the purpose for which the security was given.

[reg 8 am LN 99 of 2016 reg 164, 1 December 2016]

## reg 9 Application

These Regulations apply to transhipment and transit goods as well as to goods landed to be warehoused or for home consumption.
