## r 1 Short title and interpretation

(1) These Rules may be cited as the Legal Practitioners (Remuneration for Non­Contentious Business) Rules 2004.

(2) In these Rules—

**collateral security** means a security that is taken in addition to any other security that may be taken by a mortgagee;

**final certificate** means a certificate provided by a practitioner (whether unqualified or qualified) to a mortgagee after the Interim Certificate under which the completed, stamped and registered (as the cases may be) documents are sent by the practitioner to the mortgagee and in which he or she certifies the validity and correctness of the documents;

**interim certificate** means a certificate provided by a practitioner (whether unqualified or qualified) to a mortgagee upon instructions received by the solicitor as to the validity and correctness of the documents obtained by him or her on behalf of the mortgagee;

**non-contentious business** includes any business connected with the sale, transfer, purchase, lease, mortgage, charges, property settlements and other matters connected in the general matter of conveyancing;

**practitioner** has the same meaning as in the Act;

**solicitor** means a practitioner carrying on non-contentious business; and

**standard form** means a document that is of a pre-printed form used routinely by the mortgagee (in cases of a bill of sale, mortgage, debenture and any other charges) to which these Rules apply.

## r 2 Remuneration of practitioner

Subject to rule 4, the remuneration of a practitioner for all professional work done by him or her in respect of non-contentious business shall be regulated by these Rules.

## r 3 Gross sum special provisions

In respect of business regulated by these Rules a practitioner may, at his or her option, charge a gross sum in lieu of the detailed charges set out in the Schedules, provided that within 3 months after the delivery of a charge made under this rule or within one month after payment, whichever shall be the later date, the client may require that a detailed bill of charges shall be delivered, and the practitioner shall there upon comply with the requisition and any bill so delivered shall be subject to taxation as if the provisions of this rule with respect to the regulation of remuneration by gross sum had not been made.

## r 4 Exemption of powers of court and certain written agreements

Nothing in these Rules shall affect—

(a) the power of the court to order delivery of a bill of costs or direct the taxation thereof; or

(b) the right of a practitioner to enter into a written agreement with his or her client as to his or her remuneration as provided by section 77 of the Act.

## r 5 Probate and administration

(1) A practitioner shall be entitled to charge and shall be allowed the fees set out in Schedule 1 for work done in connection with the granting of probate and or letters of administration.

(2) The fees set out in Part A of Schedule 1 include all work usual and necessary to obtain the grant of probate from the court and to transmit the assets into the names of the personal representatives.

(3) The fees set out in Part B of Schedule 1 include all work usual and necessary to obtain the grant of letters of administration from the court and to transmit the assets into the names of the personal representatives, and include the usual affidavits such as affidavits of justification of surety.

(4) The scale of fees set out in Schedule 1 is for use ordinary cases; in extraordinary cases the taxing officer may increase or diminish the charges if for any special reasons he or she thinks fit.

## r 6 Bills of sale and mortgages of chattels

A practitioner shall be entitled to charge and shall be allowed the fees set out in Schedule 2 for work done in connection with bills of sale and mortgages of chattels.

## r 7 Crop liens

A practitioner shall be entitled to charge and shall be allowed the fees set out in Schedule 3 for work done in connection with crop liens.

## r 8 Conveyancing

A practitioner shall be entitled to charge and shall be allowed the fees set out in Schedule 4 for work done in connection with conveyancing matters.

## r 9 Companies

A practitioner shall be entitled to charge and shall be allowed the fees set out in Schedule 5 for work done in connection with matters pertaining to companies.

## r 10 Collateral securities

Where any security as taken as a collateral security, the charge for such collateral security shall be as follows—

(a) for the first such collateral security, the charge shall be one-half of the relevant scale;

(b) for the second and ensuing collateral securities, the charges shall be one-fifth of the relevant scale.

## r 11 General remuneration

(1) In respect a non-contentious business for which remuneration is not otherwise specifically prescribed, a practitioner shall be entitled to charge and be allowed the fees set out in Schedule 6.

[para (1) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 150, effective 1 December 2016]

(2) The scale of fees set out in Schedule 6 does not include disbursements reasonably and properly paid on behalf of the client.

(3) A legal practitioner may be allowed according to the circumstances a proper additional remuneration for his or her special exertion when any business to which this rule applies is required to be and is carried through in an exceptionally short space of time.
