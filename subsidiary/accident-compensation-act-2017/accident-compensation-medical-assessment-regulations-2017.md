# ACCIDENT COMPENSATION (MEDICAL ASSESSMENT) REGULATIONS 2017

Accident Compensation (Medical Assessment) Regulations 2017 (LN 109 of 2017) commenced on 1 January 2018, as amended by:

| Amending Legislation | Date of Commencement |
| --- | --- |
| Accident Compensation (Medical Assessment) (Amendment) Regulations 2018 (LN 108 of 2018) | 1 January 2019 |

## PART 1 PRELIMINARY [regs 1-2]

### reg 1 Short title and commencement

(1) These Regulations may be cited as the Accident Compensation (Medical Assessment) Regulations 2017.

(2) These Regulations come into force on 1 January 2018.

### reg 2 Interpretation

In these Regulations, unless the context otherwise requires—

**Act** means the Accident Compensation Act 2017;

**Impairment Assessment Training** means the training of local medical practitioners by a medical consultant or physician on the evaluation or assessment of the percentage of impairment or disability of an injured person as a result of an accident;

**medical practitioner** means a licensed medical practitioner under the Medical and Dental Practitioner Act 2010; and

**representative** means—

(a) in the case of a child, the parent or legal guardian of the child; or

(b) in the case of a person who is incapable of making an application because of a mental disorder, mental illness or being of unsound mind, the legally appointed representative of that person.

## PART 2 MEDICAL ASSESSMENT [regs 3-8]

### reg 3 Initial medical assessment

(1) A person who suffers a personal injury or from a prescribed disease as a result of an accident must have an initial medical assessment of the personal injury or the disease conducted by a medical practitioner prior to the making of an application claiming compensation for the personal injury or disablement caused by the disease.

(2) A medical practitioner who conducts an initial medical assessment under subregulation (1) must provide a medical report to the person suffering from the personal injury or disease or his or her representative.

(3) Where a medical practitioner finds that—

(a) a person is suffering from a disease prescribed under Schedule 1 to the Accident Compensation (Employment Accidents) Regulations 2018;

(b) the disease is causing the person disablement; and

(c) the disease is due to the nature of the person’s employment and was contracted within 24 months before the date of disablement,

the medical practitioner must grant the person or his or her representative a certificate attached to the report under subregulation (2) confirming the facts listed in paragraphs (a) to (c) .

[reg 3 subst LN 108 of 2018 reg 2, 1 January 2019]

### reg 4 Medical report from initial medical assessment to be submitted with application

An applicant must submit a certified copy of the medical report from the initial medical assessment with the application to the Commission.

### reg 5 Commission may refer application to medical practitioner trained in impairment assessment

(1) The Commission may, on a case by case basis, refer an application to a medical practitioner appointed by the Commission who has undergone Impairment Assessment Training.

(2) The Commission must refer the application in subregulation (1) using the Medical Assessment Referral Form set out in Schedule 1.

### reg 6 Commission may direct person to undergo further medical assessment

The Commission may direct the person who has suffered a personal injury to undergo further medical assessment conducted by a medical practitioner appointed by the Commission within 14 working days from the date of receipt of the application.

### reg 7 Medical assessment

(1) A medical practitioner must conduct a medical assessment as soon as practicable following the referral by the Commission.

(2) A medical practitioner who conducts a medical assessment on a person who has suffered a personal injury and whose application has been referred by the Commission must, where applicable, complete the Medical Assessment Form set out in Schedule 2 and submit such form to the Commission.

(3) The Commission must bear the medical expenses for any medical assessment required under this Part, and the obtaining of any medical report.

(4) If the applicant has already paid for such medical expenses, the Commission must reimburse the applicant.

### reg 8 Conflict of interest

A medical practitioner must disclose any conflict of interest when dealing with an application referred to him or her by the Commission.

## PART 3 ADDITIONAL ADMINISTRATIVE CONSIDERATIONS [regs 9-13]

### reg 9 Commission may refer an application to an appointed committee

(1) For the purposes of section 8(3) and 35(2)(g) of the Act, the Commission may, on a case by case basis, refer an application to an appointed committee for further assessment.

(2) The appointed committee in subregulation (1) must provide a written report and recommendation on the application, to the Commission within 21 days from the date of receipt of the application.

### reg 10 Commission not bound by recommendation of an appointed committee

The Commission is not bound by any recommendation of an appointed committee.

### reg 11 Conflicting medical reports of medical assessments

If an application has conflicting medical reports on medical assessments conducted, the Commission has the discretion to decide which medical report to take into consideration.

### reg 12 Commission may refer to guides

The Commission may refer to any guide for the evaluation and assessment of permanent impairment as approved by the Commission for the assessment of applications and determination of lump sum payments under the no fault compensation scheme.

### reg 13 Forms

The Commission may amend any form prescribed in the schedules to these Regulations, as it deems necessary.
