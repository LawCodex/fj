## O 1 Short title and commencement

This Order may be cited as the Trade Standard (Toothpaste Standard) Order 2011 and shall come into force on 1 January 2012.

## O 2 Application

This Trade Standards apply to toothpaste imported, manufactured and marketed for use in Fiji.

## O 3 Definition

In this Order, unless the context otherwise requires—

**Minister** means the Minister responsible for industry and trade;

**Officer** means a person appointed by the Regulator;

**Regulator** means the permanent secretary responsible for health;

**Standards** means the standards contained in the Schedule; and

**toothpaste** means fluoridated toothpaste.

## O 4 Responsibilities of importers, manufacturers and retailers

A person who imports, manufactures or sells any toothpaste regulated under this Order must comply with this Order and the Standards.

## O 5 Regulator

(1) The permanent secretary responsible for health shall be the Regulator of the Standard, the contact address of which is—

(2) The duties and powers of the Regulator under this Order or the Standard are to be exercised by the permanent secretary or a person authorised in writing by the permanent secretary.

(3) The Regulator or any person authorised in writing by the Regulator is deemed to be a duly appointed inspector under the Act for the purposes of this Order and the Standard.

(4) The Regulator may perform the functions set out in the Act of the Department of National Trade Measurement and Standards so far as it relates to this Order and the Standard.

[para (4) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 280, effective 1 December 2016]

(5) This Order does not affect the powers of an inspector appointed under the Act of the Department of National Trade Measurement and Standards to exercise the powers and functions under this Order or the Standard.

[para (5) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 280, effective 1 December 2016]

(6) The Standard may be inspected at the Office of the Regulator or at other offices approved by the Regulator.

## O 6 Sample and reporting

The permanent secretary or any person authorised in writing by the permanent secretary shall take any sample at anytime for testing for the purposes of compliance with these Standards.

## O 7 Penalties

Any manufacturer, importer or retailer of toothpaste who breaches the provisions of this Order shall be subjected to the penalties under the Trade Standards and Quality Control Act 1992.

## O 8 Power to make amendments

The Minister shall have the power to make, amend, suspend or revoke this Order or the guidelines under this Order in relation to labelling and standards.

### SCHEDULE STANDARDS

#### O 1.0

Labelling and standards guidelines—

(a) any toothpaste that does not contain recognised anti-caries active ingredients by the dental profession shall not be made available to the general public on the pretext of promoting oral health;

(b) all toothpaste made available in Fiji, shall not have ingredient(s) that may be, in anyway, harmful to the user;

(c) all toothpaste made available in Fiji, should have been manufactured and packaged utilising internationally accepted manufacturing and packaging process standards; and

(d) any person who imports, or sells any toothpaste regulated under this Order must comply with this Order and the Standard.

#### O 2.0

Recognised anti-caries active agents in toothpastes

(a) Fluoride—

(i) stannous fluoride (850-1500ppm fluoride ion);

(ii) sodium monofluorophosphate (800-1500ppm fluoride ion);

(iii) sodium fluoride (850-1500ppm fluoride ion);

(iv) specially formulated toothpastes for children-fluoride levels (400-600ppm fluoride ion);

(b) CPP-ACP Complexes

Casein phosphopeptide — amorphous calcium phosphate (13mg/kg);

(c) Others

Manufacturers or distributers will have to provide credible scientific evidence on the efficacy of any other newer active anti-caries agent that may be contained in the toothpaste which they intend to make available to the public.

#### O 3.0

Basic requirements relative to physical properties and chemical composition

(a) Any toothpaste that is intended to be made available to the general public should have full ingredients disclosure labelling, with the exception of therapeutic toothpaste accepted for supply in Australia/New Zealand, where TGA/Medsafe compliant labelling shall be acceptable;

(b) Total fluoride should not exceed a mass fraction of 0.15% when tested and the total amount of fluoride in a single package/container should not exceed 400mg;

(c) Total maximum concentration of heavy metals should not be more than 20mg/kg;

(d) The pH of the toothpaste should be below 10.5 level;

(e) Levels of abrasivity in the toothpaste shall comply with the relevant ISO standard.

(f) The toothpaste should be stable at room temperature and the recommended storage temperature should take into account and be compatible with Fiji's tropical climate;

(g) Compounds containing fermentable carbohydrates should not be present in the toothpaste.

#### O 4.0

Marketing

(a) Automatic Entry

Any toothpaste that is manufactured in compliance with most aspects of the relevant ISO standards, and/or—

(i) attained the American, Australian or New Zealand Dental Associations “Seal of Approval”, or

(ii) has been accepted for supply as therapeutic toothpaste by the Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) of Australia or Medsafe of New Zealand, or

(iii) has the World Health Organisations endorsement as suitable for use in Developing Countries, may be made available to the general public without any further scrutiny.

(b) Testing—

(i) the manufacturers or distributors of any toothpaste that does not fall in the above 4(a) category will have to make available all scientific data of all tests done pertaining to safety, toxicity, efficacy and manufacturing processes;

(ii) testing and basic safety evaluation may be conducted by an accredited laboratory such as that of the Institute of Applied Sciences of the University of the South Pacific;

(iii) all costs associated with testing of the product to be borne by the manufacturer;

(iv) requests may be made to determine basic cyto-toxicity (brine shrimp test), trace/heavy metal analysis, physical pH analysis and fluoride concentration levels, amongst others.

(c) Labelling—

(i) toothpaste package should clearly state effects on teeth and should identify the product as an anti-cariogenic agent in nature;

(ii) the packaging should not contain any misleading or unsubstantiated statements or claims;

(iii) active anti-caries agents in the toothpaste should be listed with appropriate concentrations;

(iv) information on the type and concentration of fluoride present (in fluoridated toothpaste) should be clearly stated;

(v) clear demarcations should be placed on packaging that identifies toothpaste manufactured for children 6 years or less;

(vi) products should be marked to identify expiry (where shelf-life is less than 2 years), at a minimum, production batch codes to identify date of manufacture;

(vii) storage conditions should be stated if storage conditions are other than room temperature;

(viii) clear labelling in English language is mandatory;

(ix) labelling and instructions in iTaukei and Hindi, preferably in a leaflet inside the packet is desirable and encouraged.

(d) Packaging—

(i) the product container or dispensing system should be packaged as such that it does not get contaminated during handling and transport;

(ii) a single package of dentifrice should not exceed more than 260g NET;

(iii) the name and contact of the manufacturer and local distributor (if not imported to Fiji by related company), should be clearly stated on the packet.

(e) Advertising—

(i) there should be no exaggeration or unsubstantiated claims made on any forms of promotional material or medium;

(ii) voluntary submission of all advertising material used for promotional purposes is encouraged.
