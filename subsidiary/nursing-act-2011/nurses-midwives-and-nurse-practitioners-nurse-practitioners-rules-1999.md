# NURSES, MIDWIVES AND NURSE PRACTITIONERS (NURSE PRACTITIONERS) RULES 1999

Nurses, Midwives and Nurse Practitioners (Nurse Practitioners) Rules 1999 (LN 133 of 1999) commenced on 5 November 1999, as amended by:

| --- | --- |
| Nurses, Midwives and Nurse Practitioners (Nurse Practitioners) (Amendment) Rules 2013 (LN 76 of 2013) | 27 December 2013 |

## r 1 Short title

These Rules may be cited as the Nurses, Midwives and Nurse Practitioners (Nurse Practitioners) Rules 1999.

## r 2 Interpretation

In these Rules—

**medical practitioner** means a practitioner admitted to the register of medical practitioners kept by the Medical Council pursuant to section 30 of the Medical and Dental Practitioners Act 2010.

[r 2 am LN 99 of 2016 reg 187, 1 December 2016]

## r 3 Qualification of nurse practitioners

A person is qualified for admission to the register of nurse practitioners if the person—

(a) is registered on the register of nurses;

(b) is not disqualified or ineligible for admission to any register of nurses, midwives or nurse practitioners under section 36 of the Nursing Act 2011;

(c) has undertaken additional training approved by the Council; and

(d) has demonstrated a level of knowledge, skill and competence, acceptable to or approved by the Council, that qualifies the person in the opinion of the Council to carry out an expanded primary care role which recognises the person's enhanced capacity, knowledge, skill and expertise to make independent and collaborative clinical and public health judgements.

[r 3 am LN 76 of 2013 r 2, 27 December 2013 LN 99 of 2016 reg 187, 1 December 2016]

## r 4 Application for admission as a nurse practitioner

(1) A person who is qualified under rule 3 as a nurse practitioner may apply to be registered as a registered nurse practitioner.

(2) An application must be made to the Council on an approved form and accompanied by the fee prescribed in the Schedule to these Rules.

(3) A suitably qualified person may be registered concurrently on more than one of the registers.

## r 5 Scope of practice of nurse practitioners

(1) A nurse practitioner may, in the context of government service—

(a) practice as a registered nurse in Fiji;

(b) practice as a registered midwife in Fiji;

(c) assume primary clinical responsibility for patients;

(d) exercise independent clinical judgement, collaborate with and refer patients to medical practitioners or other health providers and institutions, as appropriate;

(e) give instructions to or delegate tasks to other health providers under his or her supervision as appropriate;

(f) counsel and educate patients, other individuals, families and communities about health promotion, the prevention of illness, disease or injury, the management and treatment of disability and of impaired functions and palliative care;

(g) provide health assessments of individuals, families and communities;

(h) manage, administer, supervise and evaluate health care services.

(2) A nurse practitioner may for the purposes mentioned in subrule (1) refer to a medical practitioner if appropriate, accept directions or instructions from a medical practitioner if appropriate, perform triage activities, admit patients to health facilities and discharge patients from health facilities.

(3) A nurse practitioner may also observe, assess and record the physiological, behavioural, psychological and social history of patients, their symptoms and signs of health, illness, disease and injury, perform examinations, investigations and tests and interpret and evaluate the results and monitor, supervise, evaluate and manage patients.

(4) A nurse practitioner may assess and give medical diagnosis of patients, give clinical or public health advice, and prescribe therapeutic and corrective measures, including medication, in accordance with protocols or guidelines issued by the Council or acceptable to the Council.

(5) A nurse practitioner may teach the profession or practice of being a nurse, a midwife or a nurse practitioner.

(6) A nurse practitioner must endeavour to keep reasonably up to date with new developments affecting his or her knowledge and skills to practice as a nurse practitioner and conduct research appropriate for a nurse practitioner.

(7) A nurse practitioner may undertake medicolegal work, including the preparation of reports, and give evidence for use in or potential use in, proceedings in courts or tribunals (however described) on matters within the scope of practice of a nurse practitioner.

(8) A nurse practitioner must comply with professional ethical requirements reasonably expected of nurse practitioners.

(9) Every certificate or other document (including patient records) issued, made or completed by a nurse practitioner, must be signed or initialled with his or her usual signature or initials and an annotation sufficient to identify him or her as a nurse practitioner, and dated.

## r 6 Limits on nurse practitioners’ practice

(1) A nurse practitioner must, without delay, refer a patient to a medical practitioner if the nurse practitioner knows—

(a) that the patient requires medical or hospital resources other than those provided by nursing establishments or requires tests or analyses for the patient's assessment, diagnosis, care, treatment or clinical management; or

(b) that the patient's condition is beyond the nurse practitioner's knowledge and competence to assess, investigate, diagnose, treat or manage.

(2) A nurse practitioner must not engage in private practice.

## r 7 Issue of medical certificate

(1) If a medical practitioner is not available for consultation with the patient, a nurse practitioner may issue a certificate that a patient is incapable of performing work or specified types of work.

(2) A certificate may only be issued under subrule (1) if—

(a) the nurse practitioner personally consults with the patient and assesses the patient on each occasion that the nurse is asked to issue a new certificate or a renewal or extended certificate of incapacity for the patient;

(b) the total periods of incapacity certified by the nurse for the patient do not exceed 7 working days in any period of 4 weeks;

(c) the nurse practitioner and the patient—

(i) do not live in the same household nor are part of the same family;

(ii) are not related to each other by blood or marriage;

(iii) are not related to each other as *de facto* spouses;

(iv) are not in an intimate personal or sexual relationship with each other; and

(d) the nurse practitioner honestly believes, to the best of his or her knowledge, the facts or opinions which he or she expresses in the certificate.

## r 8 Issue of death certificates

A nurse practitioner may issue a certificate that a person (“the deceased”) has died and the manner and cause of death if—

(a) a medical practitioner is not available to attend to the deceased for the purpose of issuing a certificate to certify death;

(b) the nurse practitioner personally attends on the deceased and personally assesses the deceased;

(c) the cause of death is within the scope of practice of a nurse practitioner under rule 5(1) ;

(d) the deceased had previously consulted a medical practitioner not more than 14 days prior to death, and the nurse practitioner reasonably forms the opinion that there is sufficient information available to the nurse practitioner in medical records for or relating to, the deceased to assist the nurse practitioner to form an opinion as to the manner and cause of death;

(e) the nurse practitioner and the deceased person—

(i) did not live in the same household nor were part of the same family immediately prior to or at the time of the deceased's death;

(ii) were not related to each other by blood or marriage;

(iii) were not related to each other as *de facto* spouses;

(iv) were not in an intimate personal or sexual relationship with each other; and

(f) the nurse practitioner honestly believes, to the best of his or her knowledge, the facts or opinions which he or she expresses in the certificate.

## r 9 Misconduct

(1) A nurse practitioner who contravenes rule 5 or 6 may, after due inquiry by the Council under section 36 of the Nursing Act 2011, be found guilty of serious misconduct in a professional respect and if so found is liable to have his or her name removed from the register of nurse practitioners.

(2) If the Council is inquiring into an allegation of serious professional misconduct against a person, the person must give to the Council any document, record or material which the Council requires to assist the Council in making its decision.

(3) A person who contravenes subrule (2) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to a fine of $200.

[r 9 am LN 99 of 2016 reg 187, 1 December 2016]

## r 10 Service requirements

(1) This rule applies to a person whose training to qualify as a nurse practitioner has been paid for by Government of the Republic of Fiji in whole or in part, by way of enrolment or tuition fees and charges, or study or training allowances for the person.

(2) The Council may, before admitting a person to the Register or register of nurse practitioners, require the person to reimburse Government of the Republic of Fiji, in whole or in part, for the balance of any fees, charges or allowances that were paid by Government of the Republic of Fiji in respect of the person's training a nurse practitioner.

(3) As an alternative to requiring reimbursement of fees, charges or allowances, the Council may allow a person wishing to be registered as a nurse practitioner to give a written undertaking (“undertaking”) to the Council, to serve as a nurse practitioner in Fiji for a period and at locations or facilities, or in the service or employment of an employer or contractor, specified by the Council.

(4) An undertaking must be in a form approved by the Council.

(5) An undertaking may be varied by mutual consent of the Council and the person who gave the undertaking.

(6) A nurse practitioner who does not comply with an undertaking or any amendment to an undertaking may, after due inquiry by the Council, be removed by the Council from the register of nurse practitioners until the nurse practitioner agrees in writing with the Council to comply with outstanding terms of the undertaking or amended undertaking, and upon such agreement the person may be restored by the Council to the register of nurse practitioners.

(7) The Council must release a person from further performing an undertaking which the person has given the Council under this Rule if the person reimburses Government of the Republic of Fiji the balance of any fees, charges or allowances that were paid by Government of the Republic of Fiji in respect of the person's training as a nurse practitioner.
