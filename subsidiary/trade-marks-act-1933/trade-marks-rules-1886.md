## r 1 Short title

These Rules may be cited as the Trade-Marks Rules 1886.

## r 2 Fees

The fees specified in Schedule 1 shall be payable to the Solicitor-General.

[r 2 am Decree 56 of 2012 s 3, effective 9 July 2012]

## r 3 Application for registration

Any person desiring to register a trade-mark shall make application to the Solicitor-General in the form of Schedule 2. The application to be on foolscap paper having a margin on the left-hand of not less than 1.5 inches.

[r 3 am Decree 56 of 2012 s 3, effective 9 July 2012]

## r 4 Nature and size of representation of trade-mark

A description of a trade-mark shall be given, and shall be accompanied when practicable by a drawing or other representation, in duplicate, not more than 3 inches square, on foolscap paper, by pasting or otherwise fastening on such paper a specimen of the trade-mark.When a drawing or other representation or specimen cannot be given in a manner aforesaid, a specimen or copy of the trade-mark may be sent either of full size or on a reduced scale. The Solicitor-General may, if dissatisfied with the drawing or other representation of a trade-mark, require a fresh drawing or other representation, either before he or she proceeds with the application or before he or she registers the trade-mark.The Solicitor-General may also, in exceptional cases, receive and keep in his or her office a specimen or copy of any trade-mark which cannot conveniently be placed on his or her register.

[r 4 am Decree 56 of 2012 s 3, effective 9 July 2012]

## r 5 Advertisement of trade-mark application

As soon as may be after the receipt of an application, the Solicitor-General shall require the applicant to advertise such application in the Gazette, and in such other newspaper as the Solicitor-General may, if he or she think necessary, direct, during such time, in such form, and generally in such manner as the Solicitor-General may determine.

[r 5 am Decree 56 of 2012 s 3, effective 9 July 2012]

## r 6 Means of advertising trade-mark to be supplied

For the purposes of such advertisement the applicant may be required to furnish the Government Printer, and the printer of such other newspaper, with a wood block or electrotype of the trade-mark of such dimensions or with such information or means of advertising the trade-mark as may be determined by the Solicitor-General.

[r 6 am Decree 56 of 2012 s 3, effective 9 July 2012]

## r 7 Notice and proceedings for objection

Notice of objection to any application may be given within 3 months of the publication of such advertisement to the Solicitor-General by sending the same in duplicate on foolscap paper, stating the grounds of the objection. The Solicitor-General shall send one copy of such notice to the applicant.Within 3 weeks after receipt of such notice, or such further time as the Solicitor-General may allow, the applicant may send to the Solicitor-General a counter statement on foolscap paper, and if he fails to do so his or her application shall be deemed to be withdrawn.The Solicitor-General, after hearing the applicant and objector may order costs to be paid by either of them as he or she may think fit.

[r 7 am Decree 56 of 2012 s 3, effective 9 July 2012]

## r 8 Time of registration of trade-mark

On the expiration of 3 months from the date of the first appearance of the advertisement in the Gazette, the Solicitor-General may, if he or she is satisfied that the applicant is entitled to registration, register the trade-mark in respect of the description of goods for which he or she may be entitled to be registered, and the applicant as the proprietor thereof.

[r 8 am Decree 56 of 2012 s 3, effective 9 July 2012]

## r 9 Date of registration

When a trade-mark shall be registered, the date on which the application for registration was received by the Solicitor-General shall be deemed to be the date of registration.

[r 9 am Decree 56 of 2012 s 3, effective 9 July 2012]

## r 10 Assignment

When a trade-mark has been assigned any person claiming to be registered as assignee shall send to the Solicitor-General an application in the form of Schedule 3, and shall forward therewith an assignment by deed duly executed.

[r 10 am Decree 56 of 2012 s 3, effective 9 July 2012]

## r 11 Transmission

When a trade-mark is transmitted by operation of law, the person applying to be registered as the transmittee shall send to the Solicitor-General an application in the form in Schedule 3, and shall produce to the Solicitor-General such evidence as he or she may desire in support of such application.

[r 11 am Decree 56 of 2012 s 3, effective 9 July 2012]

## r 12 Removal of trade-mark after 14 years unless fee paid

At the expiration of 14 years from the date of registration, or within 6 months thereafter, unless the fees prescribed for continuance be paid, the trade-mark may be removed from the register.

[r 12 subst LN 83 of 1984 s 2, effective 31 August 1984]

## r 13 Payment of additional fee after expiration of 14 years

If before the expiration of the said 6 months the registered proprietor pays the said fees together with the additional prescribed fee, the Solicitor-General may, without removing such trade-mark from the register, accept the said fee as if it had been paid before the expiration of the said 14 years.

[r 13 subst LN 83 of 1984 s 2, effective 31 August 1984; am Decree 56 of 2012 s 3, effective 9 July 2012]

## r 14 Power of Solicitor-General to restore trade-mark

When a trade-mark has been so removed from the register, the Solicitor-General may, if he or she is satisfied that it is just so to do, restore such trade-mark to the register on payment of the additional fee prescribed and compliance with such conditions as he or she may think fit.

[r 14 am Decree 56 of 2012 s 3, effective 9 July 2012]

## r 15 Publication of rectification or alteration of register

Whenever the register is rectified or altered in any particular, the Solicitor-General shall, if he or she considers that such rectification or alteration should be made public, publish at the expense of any person interested, by advertisement or otherwise, the circumstances attending such rectification or alteration.

[r 15 am Decree 56 of 2012 s 3, effective 9 July 2012]

## r 16 Notice to Solicitor-General of opposition in any matter

When any person objects to the registration of any assignee or transmittee, or to any rectification or alteration of the register, the Solicitor-General shall give to the applicant or his or her agent for such registration, rectification, or alteration, the like notice. The Solicitor-General in such case may, if he or she think fit, require the parties interested to submit their claims to the High Court.

[r 16 am LN 157 of 1969 s 3, effective 14 November 1969; Decree 56 of 2012 s 3, effective 9 July 2012]

## r 17 Submission to court of conflicting claims

Where the Solicitor-General refuses to comply with the claims of any persons until their rights have been determined by the High Court, the manner in which the rights of such claimants may be submitted to the High Court by the Solicitor-General, or by the claimants, if the Solicitor-General so require shall, unless the High Court otherwise order, be by a special case or statement of questions of fact without pleadings, and the same shall be filed and proceeded with in like manner as in the case of any other special case or statement of questions of fact without pleadings submitted to the High Court.

[r 17 am LN 157 of 1969 s 3, effective 14 November 1969; Decree 56 of 2012 s 3, effective 9 July 2012]

## r 18 Solicitor-General may settle case

The special case or statement of questions of fact may be agreed to by the parties, and, if they differ, may be settled by the Solicitor-General.

[r 18 am Decree 56 of 2012 s 3, effective 9 July 2012]

## r 19 Service of documents

Applications, statements, notices and documents, required by these Rules to be served or sent, shall be in writing or print, or partly in writing and partly in print, and may be delivered personally or sent by post, and if sent by post shall be deemed to have been served and received respectively at the time when the letter containing the same would be delivered in the ordinary course of post; and in proving such service or sending it shall be sufficient to prove that the letter containing the notice was prepaid, duly stamped and put into the post properly stamped.

## r 20 Extension of time

Where the Solicitor-General is satisfied that the circumstances are such as to justify an extension of time for doing any act or taking any proceeding under the provisions of these Rules, not being a time expressly provided in the Act or prescribed in rules 12 and 13, he or she may extend the time upon such notice to the parties and upon such terms as he or she may direct, and the extension may be granted although the time for doing the act or taking the proceedings has expired.

[r 20 insrt LN 85 of 1967 s 2, effective 13 October 1967; am Decree 56 of 2012 s 3, effective 9 July 2012]

## r 21 Address for service

The Solicitor-General may require an applicant, opponent or agent, or a registered proprietor or registered user of a trade-mark, who does not reside or carry on business within Fiji to give an address for service within Fiji and such address shall be treated as the actual address of that person for all purposes connected with the matter in question.

[r 21 insrt LN 157 of 1969 s 2, effective 14 November 1969; am Decree 56 of 2012 s 3, effective 9 July 2012]
