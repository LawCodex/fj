# EMPLOYMENT RELATIONS (EMPLOYMENT AGENCIES) REGULATIONS 2008

Employment Relations (Employment Agencies) Regulations 2008 (LN 58 of 2008) commenced on 2 April 2008, as amended by:

| Amending Legislation | Date of Commencement |

| Business Licensing (Repeal) Act 2020 (No 28 of 2020) | 1 August 2020 |

## PART 1 PRELIMINARY [regs 1-3]

### reg 1 Short title and commencement

(1) These Regulations may be cited as the Employment Relations (Employment Agencies) Regulations 2008.

(2) These Regulations shall come into force on 2 April 2008.

### reg 2 Scope

These Regulations shall apply to all activities defined as “employment agency” or “employment business” under these Regulations.

### reg 3 Interpretation

(1) In these Regulations, unless the context otherwise requires—

**Act** means the Employment Relations Act 2007 ;

**advertisement** includes any advert in any publication or broadcast on any radio or television station or cable network;

**applicant for employment** includes any person who seeks to avail himself or herself of opportunities for employment offered by an employment agency or employment business;

**authorisation** means authorisation by the Permanent Secretary under section 37(4) of the Act or under regulation 4(1) ;

**authorised person** also includes any person to whom an authorisation is transferred upon the death of the holder in terms of subregulation 4(8) ;

**competent person** means a fit and proper person approved by the Permanent Secretary to manage an employment agency or employment business and who meets the requirements of regulation 9 ;

**conditions of employment** includes wages, the period of employment, the hours of work, leave and repatriation;

**employment** also includes self-employment;

**employment agency** or “employment business” means any activity carried out by a natural or legal person in Fiji for the enlisting, registration, recruitment or deployment of persons for employment in Fiji or outside Fiji but excludes any public employment exchanges or public authority;

**employment services** include the keeping of any register of applicants for employment or the referral of applicants for employment, the placing in employment of applicants for employment; the placing of adverts for the filling of employment vacancies, the interviewing and selection of applicants for employment and the offering of employment contracts for or on behalf of any employer to applicants for employment;

**mercenary** means any person who—

(a) is specially recruited locally or abroad in order to fight in an armed conflict;

(b) is motivated to take part in the hostilities essentially by the desire for private gain and, in fact, is promised, by or on behalf of a party to the conflict, material compensation substantially in excess of that promised or paid to combatants of similar rank and functions in the armed forces of that party;

(c) is neither a national of a party to the conflict nor a resident of the territory controlled by a party to the conflict;

(d) is not a member of the armed forces of a party to the conflict; and

(e) has not been sent by a State which is not a party to the conflict on official duty as a member of its armed forces; and in any other situation, means a person who—

(f) is specially recruited locally or abroad for the purpose of participating in a concerted act of violence aimed at—

(i) overthrowing a government or otherwise undermining the constitutional order of a State; or

(ii) undermining the territorial integrity of a State;

(g) is motivated to take part therein essentially by the desire for significant private gain and is prompted by the promise or payment of material compensation;

(h) is neither a national nor a resident of the State against which such an act is directed;

(i) has not been sent by a State on official duty; and

(j) is not a member of the armed forces of the State on whose territory the act is undertaken.

**Ministry** means the ministry responsible for the administration of the Act;

**Permanent Secretary** means the permanent secretary for the Ministry, and includes any officer or inspector of the Ministry who is authorised by the permanent secretary to act on his or her behalf;

**person** includes a body of persons and a body corporate established by law;

**user** includes any employer or any person other than an applicant for employment who makes use of any services provided by an employment agency or employment business; and

**worker** includes a jobseeker.

(2) For all other definitions, reference should be made to section 4 of the Employment Relations Act 2007.

## PART 2 AUTHORISATION OF EMPLOYMENT AGENCIES [regs 4-6]

### reg 4 Employment agencies and businesses to be authorised

(1) A person who carries on an employment agency or an employment business must be the holder of a current authorisation granted by the Permanent Secretary under this Part authorising the person to carry on such an agency or business in the premises specified in the authorisation.

(2) An applicant for an authorisation under this Part shall, not less than 21 days after making his or her application, cause a notice of the application to be published in a daily newspaper and the Gazette.

(3) A notice under subregulation (2) shall—

(a) state the name and address of the applicant, and where the applicant is a company or other body of persons the name and address of all the directors or like officers of the company or body of persons; and

(b) state the address of the premises at which the employment agency or employment business is or is to be carried on and the class of business carried on or to be carried on at those premises.

(4) No application for an authorisation under this Part shall be entertained by the Permanent Secretary unless it is accompanied by a certificate, signed by or on behalf of the applicant, stating that he or she has complied with the provisions of subregulation (2) and a copy of the newspaper containing notice of the application.

(5) An application for an authorisation in respect of an employment agency or an employment business may be refused by the Permanent Secretary on the grounds that—

(a) the applicant is an individual under the age of 21;

(b) the applicant is a person who on account of misconduct or for any other sufficient reason is unsuitable to hold an authorisation in respect of an agency or business of the intended class;

(c) the applicant is the subject of an un-discharged receiving order or is an un-discharged bankrupt, or is or was a director of a company wound up by a court in the last 5 years preceding the application;

(d) a person, other than the applicant, who is or is to be concerned with the carrying on of the employment agency or employment business is a person who, on account of misconduct or for any other reason is unsuitable to be associated with an agency or business of the intended class;

(e) the premises at which the employment agency or employment business is or is to be carried on are unsuitable for an agency or business of the class in question; or

(f) the employment agency or employment business has been or is being improperly conducted.

(6) An employment agency or employment business shall not levy a charge on an applicant for employment in respect of the applicant’s registration of interest or enlisting with the agency or its database but may levy a charge for the provision of employment related services as prescribed in Schedule 4 .

(7) Unless revoked, an authorisation shall continue in force for one year (or such longer period, not exceeding 5 years, as the Permanent Secretary may specify in any particular case) beginning with the date specified therein for its commencement, provided that where the holder of an authorisation has duly applied before its expiry for a further authorisation, the previous authorisation shall not expire until the commencement of the further authorisation or, if the application is refused, until the refusal is notified to him or her by the Permanent Secretary.

(8) Where the holder of an authorisation dies, the authorisation shall be deemed to have been transferred on his or her death, if he or she was the sole holder, to his or her next of kin, and if he or she was a joint holder, to the surviving holder, or if the holder is a business or company, to any successor under law, so however that no such transfer shall be made unless the transferee, or transferees if more than one, are persons qualified to hold an authorisation under this Part.

(9) It shall be the duty of the holder of an authorisation under this Part, within 30 working days of any change in the particulars accompanying the application for that authorisation under subregulation (1) , to give to the Permanent Secretary notice in writing of the change, provided that nothing in this subregulation shall be taken as authorising the holder of an authorisation to carry on any business otherwise than in accordance with the provisions of the authorisation,

(10) An authorisation granted under this Part shall be endorsed with a notice to the effect as in subregulation (9) .

(11) The holder of an authorisation under this Part in respect of any employment agency or employment business shall keep displayed on the premises to which the authorisation relates in such a position that it can be readily seen by persons resorting to those premises—

(a) the authorisation; and

(b) a copy of any regulations under the Act which apply to the employment agency or employment business, as amended from time to time.

(12) A person who contravenes any of the provisions of subregulations (1) , (6) , (9) and (11) shall be guilty of an offence.

### reg 5 Representations on refusal or revocation of an authorisation

(1) Where the Permanent Secretary intends to refuse the grant or transfer of an authorisation, or to revoke the same under regulation 4, he or she shall notify the applicant or the holder of the authorisation or the person to whom the authorisation is requested to be transferred, of his or her intention and his or her reasons.

(2) A person who receives a notification of the intention of the Permanent Secretary as is mentioned in subregulation (1) may make written representations to the Permanent Secretary within 30 working days of the receipt of such notification.

(3) If the Permanent Secretary receives representations within the time specified in subregulation (2) , he or she shall consider them and—

(a) if he or she decides not to proceed with his or her intentions and accordingly decides to grant or not to revoke or transfer the authorisation, he or she shall notify the applicant or holder of his or her decision;

(b) in any other case, the Permanent Secretary shall notify the applicant or holder accordingly, and the latter may, within 15 days from such notification, appeal to the Minister.

(4) Any aggrieved person may appeal to the Minister within 3 months of refusal, and specify any new or further information which may assist the Minister to make a determination.

(5) The Minister must notify the applicant of his or her decision within 30 days.

### reg 6 Exemption for authorisation

The provisions of regulation 4 do not apply where an employer carries out its own recruitment or deployment of persons for employment within its operation in Fiji or outside Fiji.

## PART 3 CONDITIONS FOR OPERATING EMPLOYMENT AGENCIES [regs 7-19]

### reg 7 Application

(1) An application for an authorisation to carry out an employment agency or employment business shall be submitted to the Permanent Secretary in the form prescribed in Schedule 1 to these Regulations.

(2) An application shall include the following particulars—

(a) name, address, photo identification, Value Added Tax registration number and Income Tax registration number of the applicant;

(b) address of the premises where the activity is proposed to be carried out;

(c) detailed information about the activities to be carried out. Such information shall include—

(i) a description of the services proposed to be provided;

(ii) the procedures proposed to be followed by applicants for employment and by users;

(iii) the schedule of fees to be charged to any job seeker or for any service to be provided;

(iv) a statutory declaration by the applicant that a notice of intention has been advertised in one newspaper and the Gazette, as required by the provisions of regulation 4. A copy of the page of the newspaper and Gazette showing the advertisements shall accompany the application;

(v) the name, address and the curriculum vitae showing the date of birth, educational and professional qualifications and professional experience of the applicant and the person or substitute who is to manage the employment agency or employment business; and

(vi) a cash flow projection or a business plan.

(3) An application shall be accompanied by a recent Police clearance of the applicant and of the person, if different from the applicant, who is to manage the employment agency or employment business.

(4)

(a) An applicant must pay an authorisation fee of $1,000 and a surety bond of $20,000 to the Ministry at the time when its application is submitted.

(b) The authorisation fee shall not be refunded to the applicant if the application is refused but the Ministry will return the surety bond where the application is refused, provided that the Ministry will keep the surety bond for the duration of the authorisation and return it to the applicant at the expiration of the authorisation period, conditional on the applicant or employment agency honoring its commitments under the Act.

(5) An applicant or authorised person must ensure that the surety bond is current for the duration of the authorisation.

(6) Subject to the provisions of regulations 8 and 9, a new authorisation granted or authorisation renewed shall be valid for a period of 12 months (or such longer period, not exceeding 5 years, as the Permanent Secretary may specify in any particular case) from the date of issue, and notification of the grant or renewal shall be published in the Gazette.

### reg 8 Conditions of authorisation

(1) An authorisation issued by the Permanent Secretary shall be in the form prescribed in Schedule 2 to these Regulations and to include the conditions specified in the Act or these Regulations and such other conditions as the Permanent Secretary may specify to ensure the proper management of the employment agency or employment business or for the protection of users and jobseekers, which conditions may be varied by the Permanent Secretary by notice to the applicant.

(2) The period of an authorisation to carry on an employment agency or employment business shall be one year or such longer period, not exceeding 5 years, as the Permanent Secretary may specify and any renewal shall be for a further period of one year, or such longer period not exceeding 5 years, as the Permanent Secretary may specify in any particular case, provided that when an application for renewal is received prior to the expiry of an authorisation, the authorisation shall remain in force unless it is revoked by the Permanent Secretary or until a decision is made by him or her with regard to the application for renewal of the authorisation.

(3) The authorised person shall keep the authorisation, together with a notice stating that a copy of these Regulations is available for inspection, displayed on the premises to which the authorisation relates in a location where it can be readily seen by persons who resort to the premises, and shall also keep a copy of these Regulations available for inspection by any person.

(4) It shall be a condition of authorisation that a competent person is available at all times to manage the employment agency or employment business, provided that the authorised person may nominate one or more competent persons to act as substitutes for the authorised person nominated by him or her, if such person is not available at any time for any reason.

(5) Where it is intended to effect any change in the place where the activity of the employment agency or employment business is carried out, or in the class of activity to be carried out, or any, substantial change in the procedures or any transfer of business is intended, the authorised person shall notify the Permanent Secretary in writing of the proposed change within 30 working days of the proposed change.

(6) Where an authorisation has been transferred by succession in terms of subregulations 4(8) and 4(9) , the authorised person shall produce the authorisation to the Permanent Secretary together with the following—

(a) a copy of the death certificate of the previous holder and a statutory declaration witnessed by a justice of peace or a commissioner for oaths, that the authorised person is entitled to the authorisation by succession on inheritance or by the operation of law;

(b) photograph identification, a recent Police clearance of the person to whom the authorisation has been transferred or is sought to be transferred;

(c) the Value Added Tax registration number and the Income Tax registration number of the person to whom the authorisation has been transferred or sought to be transferred; and

(d) the name, address, identity card number, recent Police clearance and curriculum vitae showing the educational and professional qualifications and professional experience of the person, if different from the authorised person, who is to manage the employment agency or employment business, provided that this information need not be submitted if the competent person who was managing for the previous authorisation holder is retained.

(7) Pending the registration of the transfer by succession, if the identity and whereabouts of the executor of the estate is known, the transferee shall notify the executor of the estate of the deceased in writing and of the heirs of the steps the transferee is taking, to ensure that the provisions of these Regulations are complied with.

(8) An application for a transfer of an authorisation must include all the particulars and documents specified in subregulation 6 and comply with the provisions of regulation 7(4) .

(9) A transfer of an authorisation to another person shall be complete when it is approved in writing by the Permanent Secretary.

(10) An applicant may appeal a revocation or variation of authorisation to the Minister within 3 months of such revocation or variation.

### reg 9 Competent person

(1) A competent person nominated by an authorised person or by an applicant for an authorisation to manage an employment agency or employment business, or to act as substitute for such person, shall—

(a) be not less than 21 years of age;

(b) have—

(i) not less than 5 years’ experience in any activity which includes the management of human resources and finance; or

(ii) be in possession of a University degree or diploma in a field which, in the opinion of the Permanent Secretary, is relevant to the management of an employment agency or employment business.

(2) A competent person nominated by the applicant for an authorisation or by the authorised person to manage an employment agency or employment business shall be responsible for the proper management of the employment agency.

(3) An applicant for an authorisation or an authorised person, as the case may be, must notify the Permanent Secretary in writing of the name, address, identity card number, curriculum vitae, qualifications and a recent Police clearance, of the person nominated by him or her as a competent person to manage the employment agency or employment business and of any person or persons nominated by him or her to act as substitute for such person.

(4) The Permanent Secretary may revoke an authorisation where a competent person is not employed to manage the employment agency or business.

(5) Nothing in these regulations shall prevent an applicant for an authorisation or an authorised person from nominating himself or herself as a competent person, provided that the applicant for an authorisation or the authorised person, as the case may be, is qualified according to these Regulations and is approved by the Permanent Secretary as a competent person.

### reg 10 Refusal or revocation or varying of authorisation

(1) The Permanent Secretary may refuse or revoke an application for authorisation or vary an authorisation on the grounds that—

(a) the applicant is under 21 years of age; or

(b) the applicant has failed to comply with the provisions of these Regulations;

(c) the applicant or the person nominated by him or her as a competent person to manage the employment agency or employment business is unsuitable;

(d) the applicant is the subject of an un-discharged receiving order or is an un-discharged bankrupt, or is or was a director of a company wound up by a court in the last 5 years preceding renewal or transfer;

(e) the premises where the employment agency or employment business is proposed to be carried out or where any of the employment services contemplated in the application for an authorisation, renewal or transfer, as the case may be, do not comply with the Public Health Act 1935 or the Health and Safety at Work Act 1996 ;

(f) the applicant has failed to comply with the provisions of the Act or any Regulations made under it, or has failed to comply with any conditions specified in the authorisation;

(g) the authorised person or the competent person nominated by him or her to manage the employment agency or business or any other person responsible for the running of the employment agency or employment business has charged any unauthorised fees or demanded any unauthorised payment from applicants for employment;

(h) the applicant for an authorisation or the authorised person or any competent person nominated by the applicant for the authorisation or by the authorised person has been convicted by a court of fraud, an offence under these Regulations, or has been imprisoned for 6 months or more for a felony;

(i) the authorised person has failed to register the employment agency or employment business under the Companies Act 2015 or Registration of Business Names; or

(j) the authorised person has failed to keep the Permanent Secretary informed of any change in the nature of the activities carried out or the procedures adopted or has failed to furnish the Permanent Secretary with the particulars of the person nominated by him or her as a competent person to manage the employment agency or employment business.

(2) The provisions of regulation 5 shall also apply where the Permanent Secretary intends to refuse to grant or transfer or intends to revoke or vary an authorisation.

### reg 11 No deduction from wages without consent

(1) An authorised person may charge a fee as authorised for any services provided but shall not demand any deductions from the wages of a worker for services rendered by an employment agency or employment business unless consented to by the worker.

(2) An employment agency or business whose fee for services rendered or provided in the contract has been paid by the user must not deduct from the wages of a worker a sum for such services.

### reg 12 Procedures to be observed

(1) An employment agency or employment business shall observe the following procedures and requirements in its operations—

(a) the authorisation number of an employment agency or business or user must be quoted in any advertisement for the filling of vacancies or when engaged in any form of publicity;

(b) a person seeking employment outside Fiji shall not be referred for employment outside Fiji unless a copy of the contract of employment proposed to be offered to such person has been attested by the Permanent Secretary or a labour officer, provided that the Permanent Secretary may exempt the authorised person from submitting such contract where it appears to him or her that the interests of the person selected are adequately protected;

(c) an authorised person shall ensure that all the conditions specified under Schedule 3 to these Regulations are included in a foreign contract of employment;

(d) where a person is employed by an employment agency or employment business and referred to an employer by it to perform a service temporarily for that employer, the employment agency or employment business shall not charge such employer rates for the services provided which is less than those payable by such employer to his or her regular workers for similar work;

(e)

(i) an authorised person shall keep records showing the names, address, identity card number, qualifications and job experience of applicants for employment and records showing the name, address, Value Added Tax registration number and Income Tax registration number of job seekers of employment services.

(ii) the records of an authorised person shall be retained for a period of at least 6 years and shall be subject to inspection at any time by a labour officer or labour inspector appointed under the Act.

(iii) the authorised person must ensure that the particulars including the name, address and identity card number of persons employed through an employment agency, if any, as well as the name, address, Value Added Tax registration number and Income Tax registration number, if any, of the user with whom such persons have been employed, are submitted to the Ministry not later than 4 weeks from the date on which such employment was effected;

(f) an employment agency or employment business must ensure that applicants for employment are only referred to a user where the user has furnished the employment agency with suitable information about a vacancy to be filled, including an adequate job description, the terms and conditions of employment offered, and any special requirements of the user. The user shall furnish an applicant for employment engaged by him or her with a written agreement incorporating the terms and conditions of employment; and

(g) in the employment of seafarers, an employment agency or employment business and the user must comply with the requirements of Schedule 3 .

(2) A person who fails to comply with any provisions of this regulation commits an offence.

### reg 13 Advertisements

(1) An employment agency or employment business that publishes advertisements in the course of its dealings must not place an anonymous advertisement inviting applicants for employment to forward their photograph or any document which includes any photograph of the applicant for employment, except in cases where the need for a photograph is a condition of engagement.

(2) An employment agency or employment business must reply in writing within one month to an applicant for employment who submits a written application in response to an advertisement, which reply must include an acknowledgement of the application, a receipt for any fee paid, an indication of the timeframe by which certain services may or will be provided, and the name of the employer or of the employment agency or employment business.

(3) When an advertisement is placed by any employment agency or employment business without any specific vacancy in view, the employer or the employment agency or employment business, as the case may be, shall specify such fact in the advertisement.

### reg 14 Confidentiality

(1) Subject to the provisions of this regulation, an employment agency or business that receives information furnished to it by an applicant for employment or by a user shall be confidential and is not to be divulged for any purpose other than for which it had been furnished.

(2) An employment agency or business may release any information in its custody with the consent in writing of the person furnishing the information, or in compliance with the requirements of regulation 12(e), or by order of a tribunal or court, or if requested by the Permanent Secretary in pursuance of an investigation into a complaint.

### reg 15 Access to records and information

(1) The Permanent Secretary, or a labour officer or labour inspector appointed under the Employment Relations (Administration) Regulations 2008 , may at any time examine the books, including any records, of an authorised employment agency or employment business, or demand any information, which in his or her opinion is reasonably required for ensuring that the conditions of any authorisation issued by him or her or the provisions of these Regulations are complied with, or for protecting the interests of applicants for employment, or for advising the Minister on the review of any policy relating to these Regulations.

(2) An authorised employment agency or employment business shall comply with the provision of subregulation (1) of this regulation.

### reg 16 Restriction on providing job seekers in industrial disputes

(1) Subject to subregulation (2) , an employment agency or employment business shall not introduce or supply a job seeker to a hirer or employer to perform—

(a) the duties normally performed by a worker who is taking part in a strike or other industrial action (“the first worker”), or

(b) the duties normally performed by any other worker employed by the hirer or employer and who is assigned by the hirer or employer to perform the duties normally performed by the first worker,

unless in either case the employment agency or employment business does not know, and has no reasonable grounds for knowing, that the first worker is taking part in a strike or other industrial action.

(2) Subregulation (1) shall not apply if, in relation to the first worker, the strike or other industrial action in question is an unlawful strike for the purposes of section 177 of the Act.

### reg 17 Offences

A person who contravenes any provision of these Regulations shall be guilty of an offence and is liable on conviction to a fine of not less than $4,000 and not more than $10,000 or to a term of imprisonment not exceeding one year, or both.

### reg 18 Prohibition Order

(1) The Employment Relations Tribunal or the Employment Relations Court may, on application of the Permanent Secretary, make an order prohibiting a person from carrying on, or being concerned with the carrying on, of an employment agency or employment business for up to 10 years on the grounds that the person concerned is unsuitable by reason of misconduct or other reasons.

(2) A prohibition order may—

(a) prohibit a person from operating an employment agency or employment business, or any description of employment agency or employment business specified in the order for the period of time specified in the order; or

(b) impose certain conditions under which a person may be allowed to operate an employment agency or employment business.

### reg 19 Schedule of Fees

(1) An applicant for authorisation must submit to the Permanent Secretary with his or her application a schedule of fees that he or she intends to charge.

(2) The Permanent Secretary may vary a schedule of fees submitted by an applicant in order to make it justifiable or reasonable.

(3) A person may not charge any job seeker or user any fees which is unauthorised.

(4) The fees stated in Schedule 4 will apply in the absence of a schedule of fees.

## PART 4 RESOLUTION OF COMPLAINTS ETC [regs 20-21]

### reg 20 Raising of complaints

A worker, former worker or other person may complain to the Permanent Secretary that a person has contravened any provision of these Regulations.

### reg 21 Resolving of complaints

(1) Upon receiving a complaint, the Permanent Secretary shall exercise the relevant powers under the Act and the Employment Relations (Administration) Regulations to investigate it and to ensure compliance.

(2) A complaint that cannot be settled by either party or by the Permanent Secretary may be referred by any party or the Permanent Secretary to the Mediation Services or the Employment Relations Tribunal or the Employment Relations Court, consistent with the requirements and relevant procedures under the Act.

## PART 5 PENALTIES [regs 22]

### reg 22 Fine and/or imprisonment

A person who contravenes any provision of these Regulations commits an offence, and unless otherwise provided, shall be liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding $20,000 or to imprisonment not exceeding 2 years or both.

## PART 6 PROHIBITION OF MERCENARIES [regs 23]

### reg 23 Prohibition

(1) An employment agency or authorised person is prohibited from recruiting, using, financing or training of mercenaries.

(2) A person who contravenes a provision of this regulation shall be guilty of an offence and is liable on conviction to a fine of $20,000 or to a term of imprisonment of 2 years or both.

## PART 7 REVOCATION [regs 24]

### reg 24 Revocation of Regulations and Schedules

(1) Subject to section 265 of the Act, the following Regulations and Schedules, including Parts 9 to 12 of the Sugar Industry Act 1984 are revoked—

(a) Employment Regulations;

(b) Schedule 1, General Form of Written Contract of Service (regulation 4(1));

(c) Schedule 2, Form of Written Batch Contract of Service (regulation 4(2));

(d) Schedule 3, Form of Foreign Contract of Service (regulation 4(3));

(e) Schedule 4, Form of Bond for Security for the Payment of Wages (regulation 5);

(f) Schedule 5, Scale of Fees (regulation 20); and

(g) All Orders, Legal Notices and Schedules made under the Employment Regulations of the Employment Relations Act 2007.

(2) By virtue of subregulation (1) , the responsibilities of the Industrial Commissioner under section 22 and the functions and jurisdiction of the Sugar Industry Tribunal under section 26 of the Sugar Industry Act 1984 that relate to matters covered under the jurisdiction of the Act, are hereby revoked.

(d) The employer shall, unless the worker has broken his or her contract of service or the contract is frustrated or its performance prevented by an act of God, provide his or her worker with work in accordance with the contract during the period for which the contract is binding, on a number of days equal to the number of working days expressly or impliedly provided for in the contract, and if the employer fails to provide work as aforesaid he or she shall pay to the worker, in respect of every day on which he or she shall so fail, wages at the same rate as if the worker had performed a day’s work.

\*The employer shall at all times, at his or her own expense provide reasonable housing accommodation for the worker/and his or her family\* at or near to place of employment. (Where the employer elects not to provide housing accommodation, he or she must pay the worker such sufficient sum, as rental, in addition to his or her wages, as will enable the worker to obtain reasonable accommodation.)

The contract, except in so far as is inconsistent with its express terms, shall be subject to the provisions of the Employment Relations Act 2007 , the Workmen's Compensation Act 1964 and the Health and Safety at Work Act 1996 , provided that if there are in force at the place of employment laws in relation to labour standards and workers compensation more favourable to the worker than those in force in Fiji, the contract shall be subject to the provisions of the laws in force at the place of employment.

**Other Conditions of Contract** — In addition to the “General Conditions of Contract” specified under paragraph *(A)*of Part 3 , the following additional conditions of contract shall also be provided by the employer—

Local Agent to have a valid Agency Agreement with parent Overseas Company;

Declaration of status of Overseas Company;

Local Agent to have a relevant Business Registration;

[Repealed]

Local Agent to have a Foreign Contract Authorisation from the Permanent Secretary;

Local Agent to provide a Bond to the Labour Ministry as required under the Regulations;

Clear repatriation procedures in case of death, injury or abscondment to be in place;

Decent wages during on-the-job training overseas to be provided to the worker before actual contract employment commenced;

Appropriate Occupational Health and Safety (OHS) Policy to be designed and procedures to be implemented as part of contract compatible with Fiji OHS legislation;

Effective OHS Committee or equivalent representation established by employer in workplace;

Effective OHS Risk Management system put in place by the employer to safeguard the worker;

Issuing of acceptable Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) to workers where relevant;

Employer to deliver appropriate OHS Training for the worker;

Granting of annual holiday return trip for the worker;

Granting of annual leave, bereavement leave and sick leave provisions etc relative to the Employment Relations Act 2007 and its subsidiary legislations;

Granting of special leave for voting in National Election;

Compulsory pre-departure Orientation and Counselling by the employer;

Employer to undertake pre-departure Training;

Employer to have in place clear hostage taking policy and procedures, where applicable;

Compulsory pre-departure Police Clearance for all workers;

Compulsory pre-departure medical examination to be compatible with contract content;

Essential pre-departure Clearance from the Disciplined Forces, where applicable; and

Post-Traumatic Stress management de-briefing and counselling on return.

### SCHEDULE 4 SCHEDULE OF FEES

An employment agency or employment business must not charge more than the fees set out below—

| | |
| --- | --- |
| To secure job seeker employment locally | a charge to be paid by the user |
| To secure job seeker employment overseas | a charge to be paid by the user |
| To search an agency’s database | $1.13 per search. |
| For a print out from a database | $0.56 per page. |
| For additional services | Maximum of $50 per hour or part thereof. |
