## PART 1 PRELIMINARY [regs 1–5]

### reg 1 Short title

These Regulations may be cited as the National and Trade Measurement Regulations 1989.

### reg 2 Commencement

These Regulations come into force on 4 August 1989.

### reg 3 Interpretation

In these Regulations, unless the context otherwise requires—

**Act** means the National and Trade Measurement Act 1989;

**accuracy** means the closeness of the agreement between the result of a measurement and the true value of the measured quantity;

**approved** means approved pursuant to this Act;

**approved pattern** means a pattern in relation to which there is in force a pattern approval certificate issued pursuant to Part 8 of these Regulations;

**area measuring instrument** means an instrument designed for measuring and indicating the area of leather or similar material passed through it;

**capacity** means the maximum load a measuring instrument is constructed to measure and—

(a) in the case of a weighing instrument (other than a belt conveyor weigher, the capacity of which is stated in terms of mass per hour or mass per cycle) the maximum load that the instrument is constructed to measure and—

(i) includes the mass represented by any subtractive tare mass device, or other similar device; and

(ii) does not include the mass value of any auxiliary device, such as a small bar and poise, designed to determine masses that are less than the mass represented by the smallest graduation on the principal bar, where the maximum mass value of the device does not exceed approximately 1% of the sum of the principal mass value; and

(b) in the case of a liquid or a semi-liquid measuring instrument, the maximum quantity that it is constructed to measure for an individual delivery;

**certifying certificate** means a certifying certificate issued pursuant to regulation 14;

**certifying stamp** means a certifying stamp affixed or stamped pursuant to regulation 14 and a reference in these Regulations to a certifying stamp, shall where the context so requires, include a reference to a certifying certificate;

**chart** includes a dial carrying price computing scale marks;

**Class 1** and Class 2 when used in relation to a weighing instrument, means a high accuracy or special accuracy weighing instrument designed, constructed and approved for the weighing of precious stones or precious metals, but which may also be suitable for general trade use, which complies with the maximum permissible error for a Class 1 weighing instrument, or as the case may be, a Class 2 weighing instrument;

**Class 3** when used in relation to weighing instrument means a medium accuracy weighing instrument suitable for general trade use which complies with the maximum permissible error for a Class 3 weighing instrument;

**Class 4** when used in relation to a weighing instrument means a low accuracy weighing instrument used only as—

(a) an instrument for weighing persons (other than babies) with a scale interval not exceeding 500 grams;

(b) an airport baggage instrument with a scale interval not exceeding one kilogram;

(c) an instrument with a capacity of less than 250 kg for weighing freight only and marked as such, with a scale interval not exceeding 500 grams;

(d) an instrument for the weighing of earth, sand, gravel and similar materials only; or

(e) an instrument approved to operate while suspended from the hook of a crane so as to weigh materials moved by the crane, and which complies with the maximum permissible error for a Class 4 weighing instrument;

**Class A** when used in reference to balances and beam scales includes diamond, chemical and assay balances and such beam scales provided with means for relieving all the end bearings and knife edges when the instrument is not being used;

**Class B** when used in reference to beam scales includes beam scales other than those included in Class A, which are provided with means for relieving at least the end bearings when the instrument is not being used;

**Class C** when used in reference to beam scales includes all beam scales other than those included in Classes A or B;

**compartment** means a subdivision of a vehicle tank consisting of a complete container, and includes the whole tank if it is not subdivided;

**correct** means, in relation to a specified measuring instrument, that it is correct within the maximum permissible error;

**counter scale** means a non-self-indicating weighing instrument for use on a counter, of a capacity not exceeding 50 kilograms, having lever arms of equal length with the pan or pans above the beam;

**crane weigher** means a weighing instrument designed and constructed to operate while suspended from the hook of a crane, so as to weigh materials moved by the crane;

**dial** means the part of an indicating device fixed or moving which carries the scale or scale marks;

**digital indication** means an indication in which the scale marks, generally composed of a sequence of aligned figures, do not permit interpolation to fractions of the scale interval;

**direct weighing** means the direct weighing of a vehicle on a weighbridge, whether loaded or not, by means of a single operation, with all of the wheels of the vehicle being wholly supported throughout the weighing operation by the platform or platforms of the weighbridge;

**discrimination** means the ability of a measuring instrument to react to small variations of load;

**dispensing measure** means a measure designed and intended for use in pharmaceutical dispensing;

**driveway flowmeter** means a flowmeter used for measuring the volume of petroleum or a similar article delivered in the presence of the purchaser, or by the purchaser, at a driveway service station or other public place or place of public resort;

**drum filling flowmeter** means a fixed flowmeter for measuring a pre-determined volume of liquid for delivery into a drum;

**e** means the verification scale interval of a measuring instrument;

**error** means the indication or nominal value shown by a measuring instrument, minus the true value of the measured quantity;

**farm milk tank** means a refrigerated farm milk tank;

**Fiji Coat of Arms** means the Fiji Coat of Arms set out in Schedule 4;

**Fiji Design and Performance Specifications** means the design and performance specifications designated by the Director under section 31 of the Act and in accordance with Part 8 of these Regulations;

[def am LN 99 of 2016 reg 179, effective 1 December 2016]

**flowmeter** means a measuring instrument which measures the volume of liquid flowing through the instrument and includes the system by which liquid is supplied, prepared for delivery and delivered of which the flowmeter is part;

**hopper weighing instrument** means a weighing instrument with a mass receptor in the form of a hopper;

**hose dilation** means the increase in volume of a hose, when subjected to an increase in internal pressure;

**instrument** means a measuring instrument;

**length measuring instrument** means an instrument designed for measuring and indicating the length of cable, canvas hose, carpet, cordage, fabric material, rope, rubber hose, wire or similar flexible materials passed through the instrument;

**levelling device** means a device for setting an instrument to its reference point;

**liquid measure** means a simple container denominated in multiples or decimal submultiples related to the litre that is designed to contain or deliver the denominated volume of liquid without the operation of a mechanical device other than a simple tap, but does not include a vehicle tank or a measure with a detachable dipstick, a farm milk tank, a lubricating oil measure or a dispensing measure;

**liquid measuring instrument** means an instrument capable of measuring a quantity of liquid, and transforming that quantity into an indication or equivalent information;

**load cell** means a transducer which measures force by converting the measured quantity (input), that is to say, mass or force, into another measurable quantity (output);

**lubricating oil measure** means a transparent measure designed to measure a volume of lubricating oil;

**mass** in relation to physical quantity means the amount of matter of an object, regardless of location and when used to refer to a measuring instrument means a mass of one piece construction, intended for use with weighing instrument but does not include any proportional mass intended for use on a non-self-indicating weighing instrument;

**mass receptor** means the load part of a weighing instrument, intended to receive the mass or load to be weighed;

**maximum capacity of a weighing instrument** means the maximum weighing capacity, not taking into account any additive tare capacity;

**maximum permissible error** means the extreme value of permissible error specified in Schedule 2 in respect of any measuring instrument or class of measuring instrument;

**measure of length** means a simple instrument used for the measurement of length with or without sliding arms or caliper arms;

**minimum capacity** or minimum delivery means the value of the load or delivery below which the measurement results may be subject to an excessive relative error;

**overhead weighing instrument** means a weighing instrument with a mass receptor in the form of a overhead rail or track;

**pattern** means, in relation to a measuring instrument, the definitive design of the instrument (including its components) of which all the elements affecting its metrological properties are suitably defined;

**pattern approval certificate** means a certificate issued pursuant to regulation 93;

**pattern approved** means of an approved pattern;

**personal weighing instrument** means a weighing instrument used only for weighing persons, other than babies;

**platform weighing instrument** means a weighing instrument with a mass receptor in the form of platform;

**pointer** means a fixed or moving indicator the position of which with reference to the scale marks, enables an indicated value to be determined;

**poise** means a sliding mass used on a steelyard or tare bar to effect equilibrium;

**reference position** means the position of an instrument at which the adjustment of its operation is carried out;

**reference standard** means a reference standard of measurement under this Act;

**rejection certificate** means a rejection certificate issued pursuant to regulation 13 in respect of measuring instruments not found to be in compliance with this Act;

**rejection stamp** means a stamp affixed or stamped pursuant to regulation 13 to measuring instruments not found to be in compliance with this Act;

**reverify** means the examination and testing of a measuring instrument, in respect of which there is in force a certifying stamp or a certifying certificate or any other thing prescribed by this Act;

**scale marks** means a line or other mark on an indicating device, corresponding to one or more defined values; and in the case of digital or semi-digital indications, the numbers themselves;

**self-indicating counter instrument** means a self-indicating, a semi-self-indicating or a digital instrument for use on a counter;

**sensitivity reciprocal** means the mass required to move the position of equilibrium of the indicating device of a non-digital weighing instrument, a definite amount at capacity, or at a lesser load;

**spring balance** means a weighing instrument in which the mass indications are dependent on the extension, compression or bending of one or more springs and the mass receptor is connected to the spring or springs without the use of levers;

**steelyard** means a weighing instrument having lever arms of unequal length with pans or hooks below the lever;

**vehicle mounted flowmeter** means a flowmeter—

(a) mounted on a trolly or other device to facilitate transportation, so that the flowmeter may be moved from place to place for operation; or

(b) mounted on a vehicle to measure the liquid delivered by the vehicle,

and includes a flowmeter system with a ticket-printer but without price computing facilities;

**vehicle tank** means a measuring instrument in the form of a tank (whether or not subdivided into compartments), fitted to or forming part of a vehicle and intended to be used with a dipstick or fixed capacity marks for the measurement of a liquid product other than milk;

**verification scale interval** means the scale interval specified by or under this Act to be used for the inspection of measuring instruments of a particular class;

**verify** means the examination and testing of a new or of a repaired or modified measuring instrument, or any other thing prescribed by this Act;

**visible bowl liquid measuring instrument** means a measuring instrument for measuring a volume of liquid delivered, such measurement being by means of a transparent measuring chamber or chambers filled by mechanical means, and includes the means by which the product is supplied to and delivered by the instrument;

**volumetric drum filling instrument** means a measuring instrument for measuring a fixed volume of liquid for delivery into a drum, and includes the means by which the product is supplied to and delivered by the instrument;

**weighbridge** means a weighing instrument of a capacity of 3 tonnes or more and that has a platform by the use of which the instrument is capable of determining the mass of a vehicle, or of livestock;

**weighing and counting scale** means a weighing instrument designed and constructed to count articles; and

**wheel load weigher** means a portable weighing instrument approved for determining the load supported by an axle of a road vehicle.

### reg 4 Adoption of rules, codes and specifications

(1) Where any standard rule, code or specification adopted by or under these Regulations adopts by way of reference any other rule, code or specification or any part thereof, then such rule, code or specification or part thereof, as the case may be, shall be deemed to be adopted by these Regulations to the extent necessary to give full force and effect to the first mentioned rule, code or specification.

(2) Wherever by or under these Regulations there is adopted by reference any standard rule, code or specification that adoption, unless the relevant adoption specifies otherwise, shall not include the adoption of any provision—

(a) specifying or defining the respective rights, responsibilities or obligations as between themselves, of any manufacturer, supplier or purchaser; or

(b) permitting a departure from any provision of the standard rule, code or specification at the sole discretion of the manufacturer or purchaser, or by arrangement between the manufacturer and purchaser.

### reg 5 Forms

(1) The forms set forth in Schedule 1 shall be used for the purpose for which they are respectively applicable, povided that no such form shall be deemed invalid if it is to the effect of the respectively proper form in Schedule 1 with such modification as the circumstances may require.

(2) Where a form prescribed by these Regulations requires completion by the insertion of particulars or other matters referred to in the form, those particulars or other matters are prescribed as the particulars or other matters required under the provisions of this Act for the purpose for which the form is prescribed.

(3) A form prescribed by these Regulations shall be completed in accordance with such directions as are specified in the form as so prescribed.

### DIVISION 2 SUBMISSION OF MEASURING INSTRUMENTS FOR INSPECTION [regs 7–10]

#### reg 7 Payment of fees, particulars of owner etc and purpose of measuring instrument to be stated

The person producing a measuring instrument to any inspector for inspection shall pay the fees and charges as specified in Schedule 3 to these Regulations and inform the name and address of the owner thereof or, where some person other than the owner is in possession thereof for use for trade, of the name and address of that other person, and the purposes for which the instrument is intended to be used.

#### reg 8 Preparation of a measuring instrument for inspection

(1) The person producing a measuring instrument to an inspector for inspection shall upon an instruction from an inspector—

(a) clean the instrument; and

(b) remove such part or parts of the instrument as are necessary to enable an inspection to be properly carried out.

(2) Until the person producing an instrument to an inspector complies with any instruction of an inspector pursuant to this regulation, the inspector may—

(a) himself or herself carry out the actions referred to in subregulation (1); or

(b) refuse to carry out any inspection of that instrument.

(3) An inspector shall not be liable for any damage caused in the course of the inspection of a measuring instrument, unless it can be proved that such damage arose from the negligence or recklessness of the inspector.

#### reg 9 Conveyance of reference standard etc

(1) Where the place of inspection of any measuring instrument is a place other than the office of the inspector carrying out the inspection, the owner of the instrument or where the owner is not in possession thereof, the person in possession thereof, shall, when required by the inspector, convey or cause to be conveyed from and to the office of the inspector, such reference standards, and other departmental equipment as the inspector requires for purposes of the inspection.

(2) Where a person is required to convey or to cause to be conveyed reference standards, and other departmental equipment pursuant to subregulation (1) such person shall—

(a) take proper care of the reference standards and departmental equipment including preventing them from becoming wet, rusted or otherwise damaged;

(b) convey or cause to be conveyed the reference standards and other departmental equipment at his or her own risk;

(c) return the reference standards and other departmental equipment in good condition;

(d) be liable for all expenses incurred in respect of—

(i) the conveyance of the reference standards and other departmental equipment; and

(ii) cleaning or adjusting reference standards and other departmental equipment; or

(iii) replacing or renewing reference standards and other departmental equipment damaged or lost during the inspection.

#### reg 10 Provision of dead mass, labour, inspections, expenses etc

(1) Where the place of inspection of any measuring instrument is a place other than the office of the inspector carrying out the inspection, the owner of the measuring instrument or, where the owner is not in possession thereof, the person in possession thereof shall—

(a) provide at the place of the inspection such dead mass and such labour as the inspector requires for the proper conduct of the inspection;

(b) be liable for the reasonable expenses of the inspector incurred in travelling to and from the place of inspection; and

(c) when such person has received notice from an inspector of the time when the inspector proposes to undertake an inspection of any instrument, ensure that the instrument is available for inspection at the appointed time and is clean and otherwise in a suitable condition for inspection.

(2) A claim for the expenses of an inspector under subregulation (1)(b) or regulation 9(2)(d) shall—

(a) be made by the Director in Form 15 in Schedule 1 within 14 days of the date of completion of the inspection to which the claim relates;

(b) be paid within 28 days of the date of the claim.

[subreg (2) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 179, effective 1 December 2016]

(3) Where a claim is not paid within the time specified in subregulation (2), the Director may sue in a court of competent jurisdiction for the amount of the claim as a debt due.

[subreg (3) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 179, effective 1 December 2016]

### DIVISION 3 PROCEDURE ON INSPECTION [regs 11, 12]

#### reg 11 Examination of a measuring instrument

On inspection, an inspector shall examine a measuring instrument in the condition in which it is being used to determine whether or not it complies with requirements of this Act.

#### reg 12 Inspection procedure

When making any inspection of a measuring instrument, an inspector shall—

(a) ensure the measuring instrument is of an approved pattern;

(b) ensure, if the measuring instrument is of a design which requires it to be fixed in a position for use, that any testing shall be carried out in its fixed position;

(c) ensure if the measuring instrument is moveable and has a base, that any testing shall be carried out with the measuring instrument on a level plane or, if this is not practicable, on a plane that is as nearly level as possible;

(d) ensure if the measuring instrument is transportable and the results obtained by its use are affected by gravity, that any testing if not carried out in the region in which it is used or to be used, shall be carried out so as to compensate for the conditions applicable in that region;

(e) test the measuring instrument by comparison with appropriate reference standards and by the use of departmental equipment but so that a Class 1 or Class 2 weighing instrument is tested by comparison with non-ferrous reference standards;

(f) if he or she is inspecting a suspended weighing instrument at the place at which it is being used, ensure that the rim of its goods pan or scoop is at a height of not more than 1.5 metres from the floor or ground level; and

(g) carry out the inspection in accordance with any other provisions of this Act relating to the inspection of any specified instrument or class of instruments.

### DIVISION 4 MEASURING INSTRUMENT NOT COMPLYING [regs 13]

#### reg 13 Rejection stamp and certificate etc

(1) An inspector shall refrain from certifying and shall, subject to subregulation (2), impress with a rejection stamp any measuring instrument which—

(a) does not fulfil the requirements of any test specified in any provision of this Act providing for the manner in which such instrument shall be inspected;

(b) in the case of a mass or measure, is not of a denomination permitted by this Act;

(c) unless exempted under subregulation (3) of this regulation, is not denominated and graduated in Fiji legal units of measurement;

(d) is not sufficiently strong to withstand the wear and tear of ordinary use;

(e) is damaged or is not complete;

(f) bears any mark, including a mark by a manufacturer, which may be mistaken for a certifying stamp;

(g) is of a rough, crude or unworkman-like construction or made of unsuitable materials;

(h) is not clean or the painted parts of which are not dry; or>

(i) is not in accordance in every respect with the requirements of this Act.

(2) Where it is impracticable for an inspector to impress a rejection stamp on any measuring instrument by reason of its size, he or she shall in lieu of impressing thereon a rejection stamp, issue in respect thereof a rejection certificate in Form 1 of Schedule 1.

(3) Where any measuring instrument is used or is in the possession of person for trade, solely in connection with the exportation from Fiji of articles where units of measurement other than under the International System of Units (SI) are used, the owner of such instrument may apply in writing to the Director for the verification or reverification thereof, notwithstanding that such measuring instrument is not denominated and graduated according to Fiji legal units of measurement and the Director may accept or reject such an application.

[subreg (3) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 179, effective 1 December 2016]

(4) An inspector is not required to impress with a rejection stamp, a measuring instrument which is not in accordance in every respect with the requirements of this Act or to issue a rejection certificate in respect thereof if—

(a) on inspection the inspector adjusts such instrument so that it meets the requirements; or

(b) the instrument is not in accordance in every respect with the requirements of this Act due to damage, being unclean or other defect, in any case being so minor that the same remains true and just despite the defect and the owner or the person in possession thereof complies with a notice given to him or her by the inspector within the time limit, not exceeding 14 days, specified in the notice as a result of which the defect is remedied.

(5) A rejection stamp shall be in the form of a 6 pointed star as set out in Schedule 4.

### DIVISION 5 MEASURING INSTRUMENT COMPLYING ETC [regs 14]

#### reg 14 Certifying stamps etc

If any measuring instrument is found by an inspector when making an inspection to correspond with appropriate reference standards and is found to be in accordance in every other respect with the requirements of this Act, the inspector shall—

(a) in the case of lubricating oil measures, dispensing measures and graduated measuring cylinders in each case made of glass or other approved rigid material, stamp the same with a certifying stamp consisting of the words “Fiji” and “WM”;

(b) in the case of metric carat masses and metric masses of a denomination of 0.5 metric carat or one gram or more, impress the same with a certifying stamp consisting of the Fiji Coat of Arms together with the last 2 figures of the year of the inspection;

(c) in the case of an instrument other than one referred to in paragraphs (a) and (b) —

(i) where it is practicable having regard to the size, the materials or the construction of the same, impress the same with a certifying stamp, consisting of the Fiji Coat of Arms and the letter or number allotted to the inspector by the Director, together with an impression of the figures representing the month and year, or the year of the inspection;

(ii) where it is practicable having regard to the size, the materials or the construction of the same to impress thereon the certifying stamp referred to in subparagraph (i) but impracticable to allow impression of the figures therein referred to, impress the same with the certifying stamp referred to in subparagraph (i) only;

(iii) where it is impracticable having regard to the size, the materials or the construction of the same to impress thereon the certifying stamp referred to in subparagraph (ii), issue in respect thereof a certifying certificate in Form 2 of Schedule 1.

[reg 14 am LN 99 of 2016 reg 179, effective 1 December 2016]

### DIVISION 6 GENERAL [regs 15]

#### reg 15 Breaking of seals and re-sealing

An inspector may, when making an inspection, break a seal on a supply tank or on a filling point of a supply tank for the purpose of returning to a supply tank, liquid measured when testing an instrument but on completion of such inspection, he or she shall immediately reseal the supply tank or filling point.

## PART 3 REQUIREMENTS FOR USE OF MEASURING INSTRUMENTS [regs 16–21]

### reg 16 Requirements for measuring instruments not in use etc for trade

When any measuring instrument is in the possession of any person carrying on trade, or at, in, or on any place or vehicle which is used by any person for trade, but the instrument is not used, or intended for use for trade or in possession for trade, the owner thereof or, if the owner is not in possession thereof, the person in possession thereof shall—

(a) durably and legibly mark the measuring instrument with the words “Not in use for trade”; and

(b) lodge with the Director a statutory declaration made by him or her that the measuring instrument is not used or intended for use for trade, or in possession for trade.

[reg 16 am LN 99 of 2016 reg 179, effective 1 December 2016]

### reg 17 Change of ownership or possession of measuring instrument

(1) When there is a change of ownership of a measuring instrument, the transferring owner shall, within 30 days after the change of ownership, notify the Director in writing of—

(a) the name and address of the new owner; and

(b) the new, or proposed, location, if known, of the measuring instrument.

[subreg (1) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 179, effective 1 December 2016]

(2) When there is a change of possession of a measuring instrument, but no change of ownership thereof, the owner shall, within 30 days after the change of possession, notify the Director in writing of—

(a) the name and address of the person newly in possession; and

(b) the new, or proposed, location, if any, of the measuring instrument.

[subreg (2) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 179, effective 1 December 2016]

### reg 18 Use of measuring instruments to measure liquid

A person shall not use for trade any measuring instrument to measure a liquid unless he or she complies with the following conditions—

(a) if the instrument is so constructed that correct measurement is determined by an overflow, the measuring chamber of the instrument shall be filled until the graduation strip representing the quantity ordered by or measured for a purchaser is submerged by at least 6 mm;

(b) in discharging liquid from the instrument all the liquid shall be completely discharged into the receptacle in which the article is to be supplied to the purchaser;

(c) the instrument shall be suitably illuminated during any measurement between sunset and sunrise or at any other time when artificial light is necessary for the proper observation of the operation of measurement;

(d) if the instrument is a flowmeter fitted with a zero resetting device, it shall be reset to zero before the commencement of each transaction; and

(e) if the instrument is a driveway flowmeter, the indicated volume, unit price and total price shall be retained on the indicators of the driveway flowmeter until the sale relating to all those indications is completed.

### reg 19 Measuring instruments not to be used for trade for certain purposes

(1) A person shall not use for trade—

(a) a measure pattern approved and certified only for measuring a liquid to measure any article other than a liquid;

(b) any scale by suspension in the hand;

(c) on a weighing instrument, proportional masses other than those marked or otherwise identified for use on that weighing instrument;

(d) a weighing instrument for weighing quantities greater than the capacity of the instrument; or

(e) a personal weighing instrument other than for weighing a person.

(2) A person shall not use for trade a weighing instrument fitted with a taring device of fixed denomination, constructed or operating so that to determine the correct net measurement of an article weighed thereon, it is necessary to add to or subtract from the net measurement indicated by such weighing instrument, unless such weighing instrument is used to weigh trucks and—

(a) the trucks are used to convey sugar cane to a sugar mill;

(b) the scale interval of such weighing instrument used to weigh trucks conveying sugar cane to a sugar mill does not exceed 0.01 tonne or 10 kilograms; and

(c) unless there is in force a pattern approval certificate permitting the use of the taring device with that weighing instrument.

### reg 20 Prohibited use of instruments

(1) A person shall not—

(a) subdivide any measure in respect of which there is in force a certifying stamp or a certifying certificate;

(b) use for trade for weighing diamonds or other precious stones a measuring instrument other than a Class A balance or a Class 1 weighing instrument, and if such instrument has an assigned scale interval, such—

(i) a scale interval greater than 10 milligrams;

(ii) a capacity of less than 5000 metric carats, and a scale interval greater than 0.01 metric carat; or

(iii) has a capacity of 5000 metric carats or more and a scale interval greater than 0.1 metric carat;

(c) use for trade for weighing gold, silver or other precious metals and articles made thereof, an instrument other than a Class A or a Class B, or a Class 1 or a Class 2 weighing instrument, and if such instrument has an assigned scale interval that—

(i) has a scale interval greater than 10 milligrams for a capacity less than one kilogram;

(ii) has a scale interval greater than 100 milligrams for a capacity of one or more but less than 10 kilograms; or

(iii) has a scale interval greater than 1 gram for a capacity of 10 kilograms or more;

(d) use for trade for weighing yaqona, a spring balance of a capacity exceeding 100 kg, or a spring balance with single dial.

(2) A person shall not deliver elsewhere than at the premises of the seller or carry for delivery milk or other liquid in a measure not provided with a lip or retaining edge or some other means to prevent spilling.

(3) A person shall not use for trade, a measuring instrument, which is the subject of a rejection stamp or a rejection certificate, until the instrument has again been submitted to an inspector for verification and has been stamped with a certifying stamp or had issued in respect of it, a certifying certificate.

(4) A person shall not sell, lease, hire or otherwise part with the possession of an instrument which is the subject of a rejection stamp or rejection certificate unless he or she first informs the intending purchaser, lessee, hirer, or person acquiring possession thereof that the said instrument is the subject of such stamp or certificate.

### reg 21 Marking of proportional masses and load receptors

Where there are on the same premises 2 or more weighing instruments which are equipped with—

(a) proportional masses, the owner of each such weighing instrument shall mark or otherwise permanently distinguish the proportional masses used on each weighing instrument so that such masses are readily identified with the weighing instrument with which they were certified;

(b) load receptors, the owner of each such weighing instrument shall mark or otherwise permanently distinguish the load receptor, used on each weighing instrument so that such load receptor is readily identified with the weighing instrument with which it was certified.

## PART 4 CONTROL OF PARTICULAR TYPES OF MEASURING INSTRUMENTS [regs 22–52]

### DIVISION 1 MEASURING INSTRUMENTS [regs 22–34]

#### reg 22 Measures of length

(1) A person shall not use for trade a measure of length and a measure of length is not in accordance with the requirements of this Act unless—

(a) the measure of length is of an approved pattern;

(b) the measure of length is in accordance with the Fiji Design and Performance Specifications as they relate to measures of length; and

(c) the measure of length does not exceed the maximum permissible error for measures of length.

(2) When making an inspection of a measure of length an inspector shall ensure that it accords with the provisions of subregulation (1) and shall—

(a) test each scale mark on the measure of length against an appropriate reference standard;

(b) test only the visible scale marks of a measure of length which is permanently fixed to a base;

(c) test each flexible tape measure when subjected to a tension (±10%) of 20 newtons for synthetic materials and 50 newtons for metals, or otherwise as marked on the measure by the manufacturer; and

(d) ensure that whilst under test, a measure of length must be supported throughout its whole length on a level base or in such a manner as will enable its length to be correctly determined.

#### reg 37 Balance and beam scale of any class

#### reg 38 Use of weighing instruments

A person shall not use a weighing instrument for trade and a weighing instrument is not in accordance with the requirements of this Act unless—

(a) the instrument is of an approved pattern;

(b) the instrument is in accordance with the Fiji Design and Performance Specifications relating to weighing instruments of its type;

(c) the instrument is at all times when in use, free from vibration, abnormal conditions, dust, air movement and any other influences likely to affect accurate performance;

(d) an instrument, other than a suspended weigher, a belt conveyer weigher or a crane weigher, is at all times, when in use, level;

(e) an instrument returns to zero to within ±0.25 of a scale interval, when the load is removed;

(f) when the knives and bearings on a mass receptor fitted to the instrument, of a maximum capacity not exceeding 30 kilograms, are moved to the limits if any of their movement, with the instrument loaded to half its capacity, the indications of the instrument does not exceed the maximum permissible error for the instrument;

(g) any transportable instrument capable of being moved for use in different places, is level at all times when in use and carries a permanent notice to this effect;

(h) any instrument approved for operation when suspended from a hanger, hangs vertically and freely from the point of suspension;

(i) where the weighing instrument is fitted with a balance box—

(i) any loose balancing material is completely and securely enclosed in that box; and

(ii) the balance box is constructed so as to allow for easy addition to, or removal of any balancing material;

(j) where the instrument is fitted with a steelyard, lever or beam, there is no excessive lateral play in the steelyard, lever or beam;

(k) the instrument has no parts capable of being interchanged or capable of being reversed which upon being interchanged or reversed, affect the accuracy of the weighing instrument except where such parts are suitably marked, so as to identify the position in which they are intended to be used;

(l) the instrument does not operate if any part affecting its accuracy is removed;

(m) where the instrument is fitted with an indicator in the form of a pointer—

(i) the tip of the pointer reaches the shortest scale mark but does extend beyond the middle of that mark except where—

(A) the pointer consists of a fine wire or thread stretched over the scale marks;

(B) the pointer consists of a hairline on a ground glass screen;

(C) the instrument has 2 concentic sets of scale marks; or

(D) the pointer and dial or chart on which the scale marks appear are in the same plane, and the pointer is not more than one millimetre from such dial or chart;

(ii) the pointer is not wider than the thickness of any scale mark; and

(iii) stops are fitted to the instrument which permits the pointer to move below zero up to 4 scale intervals, and above maximum indication at least 4 scale intervals, but prevent the pointer from striking the framework of the instrument;

(n) where the instrument is fitted with damping device, the device operates so as to stabilise the indicator after not more than 3 deviations of the indicator from the measurement indicated;

(o) when the instrument is designed, constructed and approved only for a particular use, such limitation is legibly and permanently affixed on the instrument adjacent to all indications of measurement;

(p) the error in the weighing instrument does not exceed the maximum permissible error for the appropriate class of weighing instrument;

(q) the maximum limitation of error for a instrument on inspection at zero indication, is ±0.25e;

(r) where an instrument is used in conjunction with an indicating or printing device interfaced with the method of indication on the instrument—

(i) if the method of indication on the instrument is analogue, and the indicating or printing device is analogue or digital, the difference in indications does not exceed ±0.5e; or

(ii) if the method of indication on the instrument is digital and the indicating or printing device is digital, the indications are identical;

(s) where an instrument has indications of measurement for both the seller and the purchaser, the indications are identical; and

(t) notwithstanding the other provisions of these Regulations, where an instrument computes price, such price is computed without error.

#### reg 39 Non-self-indicating weighing instruments-sensitivity

(1) The sensitivity of a non-self-indicating weighing instrument shall be such that when on inspection, a mass equal to the maximum permissible error for the load applied is added to or taken away from the load on the weighing instruments, the beam, steelyard or, in the case of a beam scale, the pointer, shall move to but not necessarily remain at, the limit of its travel.

(2) Such test for sensitivity shall be made at zero, at full capacity, and at any other intermediate load.

#### reg 40 Inspection of weighing instruments

When making an inspection of a weighing instrument an inspector shall—

(a) test the discrimination of an instrument at any load—

(i) if the method of indication on the instrument is analogue, by depositing without shock on the mass receptor, in equilibrium, an additional load equivalent to the limitation of error for the load, which shall cause a permanent displacement of the indicator corresponding to 0.7 of the additional load applied; and

(ii) if the method of indication on the instrument is digital, by depositing without shock on the mass receptor in equilibrium, an additional load equivalent to 1.4e, which shall increase the initial indication;

(b) where a Class 3 or Class 4 instrument is fitted with a levelling device, test the sensitivity of the levelling device by tilting the instrument in both longitudinal and transverse directions so that the moving indicating part of the levelling device moves at least 2 millimetres, and ensure that the zero indication does not vary more than 2e, and when loaded, shall not vary by more than 0.5e;

(c) test any non-suspended load receptor instrument, of a maximum capacity not exceeding 30 kilograms—

(i) by application of reference standards distributed centrally on the mass receptor;

(ii) by application of an eccentricity test at one-third maximum capacity, distributed in turn on the different edges of the mass receptor, without excessive stacking, and then with the whole of the load on the mass receptor, without any overlapping of the edges;

(iii) by the application of all test loads in not less than 5 approximately equal steps, increasing to the maximum capacity;

(iv) in the case of a digital instrument, when at maximum capacity, and correcting for any error, ensure that the indicator blanks out, or shows non-numeric characters, when a load equal to 11e, is applied to the mass receptor;

(v) remove the load equal to 11e and ensure that the original indication remains the same;

(vi) remove the test loads in approximately 5 equal steps back to zero, ensuring that the zero indication is within +0.25e;

(vii) in the case of a multi-interval instrument, test the instrument in accordance with the steps in (i) to (iii) and, when at the limit of the first range, ensure that the instrument is within the maximum permissible error, for an instrument of that capacity; and

(viii) continue the testing of the instrument within the new range, ensuring that the steps as per (iii) to (vi) are carried out, but in this instance, re-commence the application of the maximum permissible error, as if the instrument was a separate instrument, within the range being tested; and

(d) where proportional masses form part of an instrument, adjust such proportional masses to the accuracy of the instrument.

### DIVISION 4 PARTICULAR WEIGHING INSTRUMENTS [regs 41–52]

#### reg 41 Balances and beam scales

When making an inspection of a balance or a beam scale an inspector shall—

(a) level the instrument;

(b) if the instrument is fitted with a graduated dial test the dial to all of its scale marks;

(c) if the mass receptor fitted to the balance or beam scale is other than in the form of a scoop, by placing masses equal to one-third of the capacity of the balance or beam scale successively against the midpoint of each edge of the mass receptors without excessive stacking;

(d) if the mass receptor fitted to the balance or beam scale is in the form of a scoop, by placing masses equal to one-half of the capacity of the balance or beam scale against the middle of the back of the scoop and, at the same time, by placing masses equal to one-half of the capacity in any other position on the scoop;

(e) test any equal armed balance or beam scale for sensitivity at zero, capacity and at any midpoint position;

(f) test any electronic balance for discrimination; and

(g) ensure that the balance or beam scale is in accordance in every respect with the requirements of this Act.

#### reg 42 Counter scales

When making an inspection of a counter scale an inspector shall—

(a) if the mass receptor fitted to the counter scale is other than in the form of a scoop, test the counter scale by placing masses equal to one-third of the capacity of the counter scale successively against the midpoint of each edge of the mass receptor without excessive stacking;

(b) if the mass receptor fitted to the counter scale is in the form of a scoop, by placing masses equal to one-half of the capacity of the counter scale against the middle of the back of the scoop and at the same time, by placing masses equal to one-half of the capacity in any other position on the scoop;

(c) test for sensitivity at zero, capacity and at any midpoint position; and

(d) ensure that the counter scale is in accordance in every respect with the requirements of this Act.

#### reg 43 Spring balances

(1) A person shall not use a spring balance for trade and a spring balance is not in accordance with the requirements of this Act unless—

(a) on inspection, the instrument has a balance adjustment above and below zero; and

(b) where fitted with a vertical slide with graduations, such graduations are legible and durably marked.

(2) When making an inspection of a spring balance an inspector shall ensure that it accords with the provisions of subregulation (1) and—

(a) shall allow the springs to vibrate before taking a reading;

(b) shall not for the purpose of any one measurement decrease the load on the spring balance by a mass exceeding one-sixth of the capacity of the spring balance; and

(c) shall test in the following manner—

(i) if the mass receptor fitted to a spring balance is other than in the form of a scoop, by placing masses equal to one-third of the capacity of the spring balance successively against the mid-point of each edge of the mass receptor without excessive stacking; and

(ii) if the mass receptor fitted to a spring balance is in the form of a scoop, by placing masses equal to one-half of the capacity of the spring balance against the middle of the back of the scoop and at the same time, by placing masses equal to one-half of the capacity in any other position on the scoop; and

(d) ensure that the spring balance is in accordance in every respect with the requirements of this Act.

#### reg 44 Self-indicating counter instruments

(1) A person shall not use a self-indicating counter weighing instrument for trade and a self-indicating counter weighing instrument is not in accordance with the requirements of this Act unless—

(a) the self-indicating counter instrument is fitted with suitable means of levelling and is fitted with a levelling device; and

(b) a notice marked “instrument incorrect if not truly level” or similar wording is legibly and durably marked on the self-indicating counter weighing instrument, adjacent to the levelling device.

(2) When making an inspection of a self-indicating counter instrument an inspector shall ensure that it accords with the provisions of subregulation (1) and—

(a) shall test the instrument by placing masses equal to one-third of the capacity of such instrument successively against the midpoint of each edge of the mass receptor without excessive stacking;

(b) in all instances, shall test any chart or dial to its maximum capacity, in not less than 5 approximately equal steps ensuring—

(i) any chart computations compute correctly in all columns; and

(ii) if digital, ensure that the indications blank or show non numeric figures when at capacity, taking into account any error, when a load of 11e is applied;

(c) shall ensure that the test loads are removed in approximately 5 equal steps back to zero and that zero is correct to within ±0.25e; and

(d) shall ensure that the instrument is in accordance in every respect with the requirements of this Act.

#### reg 45 Steelyards

(1) A person shall not use a steelyard for trade and a steelyard is not in accordance with the requirements of this Act—

(a) unless the steelyard has a straight shank;

(b) unless the poise and suspending hooks are securely fitted to the steelyard;

(c) unless the poise carrier fitted to the steelyard is prevented from falling off the steelyard and moves freely without damaging the notches;

(d) unless the steelyard is fitted with stops to prevent excessive oscillation of the steelyard; and

(e) if the steelyard—

(i) has an acceleration action;

(ii) is of a capacity of less than 25 kilograms; or

(iii) is reversible or has 3 hooks.

(2) When making an inspection of steelyard, an inspector shall ensure that it accords with subregulation (1), and test the steelyard with reference standards, ensuring that—

(a) the indications of both the minor and major poises are correct, from its minimum to maximum capacity;

(b) a sensitivity test is made at zero capacity and at any mid-point position;

(c) when the test load is removed, that zero indication is within ±0.25e; and

(d) the steelyard is in accordance in every respect with the requirements of this Act.

#### reg 46 Platform weighing instruments, overhead weighing instruments and hopper scales

(1) A person shall not use a platform weighing instrument, an overhead weighing instrument or a hopper weighing instrument for trade and any such instrument is not in accordance with the requirements of this Act unless such instrument or scale is adequately protected from the influence of the weather.

(2) When making an inspection of an overhead weighing instrument, an inspector shall test the instrument by suspending from any point on the overhead rail or track, test loads in 5 approximately equal steps to capacity, and—

(a) place approximately one-third capacity at each end of the rail, in turn;

(b) ensure that any error is within the maximum permissible error; and

(c) remove all test loads in approximately 5 equal steps back to zero, ensuring that zero is within ±0.25e

(d) ensure that the instrument is in accordance in every respect with the requirements of this Act.

(3) When making an inspection of a platform weighing instrument or a hopper weighing instrument, an inspector shall—

(a) test the instrument for eccentricity in the manner set out in the following table—

| | | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ECCENTRICITY TABLE | | |
| Number of load bearings | Test load over a bearing point | Area covered by load |
| 4 | 1/3 capacity | 1/5 platform area |
| 6 | 1/5 capacity | 1/7 platform area |
| 8 | 1/7 capacity | 1/9 platform area |
| 10 | 1/9 capacity | 1/11 platform area |

(b) conduct any other tests for sensitivity, or discrimination for a digital indicator, as appropriate.

(c) test the instrument to full capacity in appropriately 5 equal steps;

(d) ensure that any error is within the maximum permissible error; and

(e) remove the test load in approximately 5 equal steps, ensuring zero is within ±0.25e; and

(f) ensure that the instrument is in accordance in every respect with the requirements of this Act.

#### reg 47 Weighbridge

(1) A person shall not use a weighbridge for trade and a weighbridge is not in accordance with the requirements of this Act unless—

(a) the weighbridge is so installed and constructed that—

(i) the operator has a clear and unobstructed view of the platform or platforms;

(ii) the indicator used by the operator is located not more than 6 metres from the nearest edge of the platform or in an elevated or other position approved by the Director, and

(iii) in the case of a road weighbridge, there is direct access to an indicator of the weighbridge to allow the driver of the vehicle or his or her agent to readily see and easily read the indication of mass;

(b) where the weighbridge is fitted with a sliding poise, a stop is fitted to the weighbridge in order to prevent the sliding poise travelling behind zero;

(c) the headwork of the weighbridge is enclosed so as to prevent damage thereto by the weather;

(d) the platform of a weighbridge, if constructed and installed, or installed in another place after the coming into operation of these Regulations, is made of concrete or steel;

(e) the weighbridge is situated so that any road or rail vehicle using the weighbridge is able to use the weighbridge without turning on the platform;

(f) the approaches at each end of a weighbridge—

(i) are at the same level as the platform of the weighbridge;

(ii) are not less than 3 metres in length; and

(iii) are made of concrete or other materials approved by the Director;

(g) for a weighbridge with a pit—

(i) any entrance to the pit is of dimensions not less that 1000 millimetres high and 900 millimetres wide;

(ii) the neck of the pit is of a dimension not less than 900 millimetres in width with a clearance of not less than 150 millimetres to each side and beneath any lever contained therein;

(iii) a cover is provided for the neck of the pit;

(iv) automatic mechanical means of draining the pit, or a free draining provision are provided; and

(v) the pit is kept free from an accumulation of water, mud or debris;

(h) the weighbridge has foundations of such strength and is so constructed as to be capable of withstanding the wear and tear of use up to the capacity of the weighbridge;

(i) in the case of a pitless or a portable weighbridge, there—

(i) is at least 150 millimetres clearance under the lowest live portion of the deck;

(ii) is floor concreted between the load cell supports to a depth not less than 75 millimetres and suitably drained;

(iii) is clear space of at least 1,000 millimetres from the external edge of the frame of the weighbridge and in the same plane as or lower than a floor complying with the provisions of subparagraph (ii); and

(iv) are load cell footings which are stable both individually and in relation to each other;

(ia) in the case of a weighbridge with a pit and with a load cell or load cells installed, there—

(i) is a clear space of at least 5 millimetres under the lowest live portion of the deck;

(ii) is a clear space of at least 5 millimetres from the external edge of the frame of weighbridge;

(iii) are load cell footings of sufficient height to bring the platform of the deck to the same level as the approaches at each end of the weighbridge;

(iv) are load cell footings which are stable both individually and in relation to each other; and

(v) are load cell footings of such strength and of such construction to be capable of withstanding the wear and tear of use up to the capacity of the weighbridge;

(j) in the case of a weighbridge other than a pitless weighbridge where any lever is installed, there is not less than 400 millimetres clearance under the lowest lever points except when access is provided all from above, then not less than 150 millimetres shall suffice;

(k) where the weighbridge is a portable weighbridge, the base of the weighbridge is stable at all times when the weighbridge is in use and any exposed levers of the weighbridge are adequately protected from the influence of the weather and, the floor is suitably treated to prevent the growth of foliage;

(l) where a weighbridge is a multi-deck weighbridge it has—

(i) a distance not exceeding 2 metres dead space between the platforms;

(ii) no interference between the platforms; and

(iii) the weighbridge is provided with a visual summing indicator;

(m) where any electronic devices installed in a weighbridge—

(i) they are protected from electrical interference and the direct rays of the sun; and

(ii) ample access is provided to read the data plates on the load cells.

[subreg (1) am LN 35 of 1993 reg 3, effective 24 May 1993; LN 99 of 2016 reg 179, effective 1 December 2016]

(2) When making an inspection of a weighbridge, an inspector shall ensure that it accords with the provisions of subregulation (1) and—

(a) ensure that the zero indication returns to within ±0.25e before apply test loads;

(b) apply reference masses ensuring that—

(i) in the case of an analogue instrument, that tests are made at 10%, 25%, 30%, 50%, 75% and 100% of the dial's capacity, increasing to full capacity of the instrument;

(ii) in the case of a digital instrument, ensure that tests are made at least at 10%, 25%, 30%, 50%, 60%, 75% and 100% of capacity; and

(iii) ensure that when at capacity, a test load equal to 11e is added, after correcting for any errors at capacity, that the indications either blank or show nonnumerical figures; and

(iv) after removal of the 11e, that the indication returns to the original capacity indication;

(c) in the case of a weighbridge the capacity of which is in excess of the number of available reference masses, apply substitution test loads wherever appropriate, ensuring that any tests for errors and either sensitivity or discrimination are provided for at each substitution load;

(d) from capacity, decrease the test loads in at least 5 approximately equal steps, ensuring that zero is within ±0.25e; and

(e) ensure that the weighbridge is in accordance in every respect with the requirements of this Act.

(3) Subregulation (1) shall not apply to weighbridges intended for weighing in-motion systems.

#### reg 48 Crane weighers

(1) A person shall not use a crane weigher for trade and a crane weigher is not in accordance with the requirements of this Act unless—

(a) any steelyard fitted to the crane weigher has a vibrating action;

(b) where the crane weigher is fitted with a sliding poise, a stop is fitted to the crane weigher in order to prevent the sliding poise travelling behind zero; and

(c) the crane weigher measures correctly with the hook in any position.

(2) On inspection of a crane weigher, an inspector shall ensure that it accords with the provisions of subregulation (1) and shall ensure that—

(a) the craneweigher is tested to its maximum capacity;

(b) any error is within the maximum permissible error;

(c) upon removal of the test load, that zero is within ±0.25e; and

(d) the craneweigher is in accordance in every respect with the requirements of this Act.

#### reg 49 Personal weighing instruments

(1) A person shall not use a personal weighing instrument for trade and a personal weighing instrument is not in accordance with the requirements of this Act unless—

(a) when the personal weighing instrument is not situated at the owner's premises, the personal weighing instrument is legibly and durably marked with the name and address of the owner; and

(b) the personal weighing instrument is legibly and durably marked with any special instructions which are necessary to obtain correct measurements.

(2) When making an inspection of a personal weighing instrument an inspector shall ensure that it accords with the provisions of subregulation (1) and shall—

(a) carry out the test by placing a load equal to two-thirds of the capacity of the personal weighing instrument, first on the front half of the platform and then on the back half of the platform;

(b) test to maximum capacity, ensuring any error is within the maximum permissible and remove the test loads;

(c) ensure that zero is within ±0.25e; and

(d) ensure that the instrument is in accordance in every respect with the requirements of this Act.

#### reg 50 Weighing and counting scales

A person shall not use a weighing and counting scale for trade and a weighing and counting scale is not in accordance with the requirements of this Act unless—

(a) it complies with the general requirements as prescribed by these Regulations, for a similar counter or platform weighing instrument;

(b) it is level and stable at all times when in use; and

(c) upon inspection, any error does not exceed the maximum permissible error and that any count carried out by the instrument is correct to within ±1.

#### reg 51 Wheel load weigher

When making an inspection of a wheel load weigher an inspector shall—

(a) test the instrument by the application of a static load incorporating appropriate reference standards, as approved by the Director; and

(b) test the instrument to capacity, at each principal graduation and ensure that upon removal of the load, that zero is within ±0.25e.

[reg 51 am LN 99 of 2016 reg 179, effective 1 December 2016]

#### reg 52 Belt conveyor weighers

(1) Notwithstanding any other meaning assigned to the expressions “Class 1” and “Class 2” by this Act, those expressions when used in relation to a belt conveyor weigher, are references to the accuracy class of that belt conveyor weigher.

(2) Upon an inspection of a belt conveyor weigher, the owner or operator thereof shall supply a calibration weighing instrument for weighing the test load, to the satisfaction of the Director, and such instrument shall be capable of weighing the test load, either in a single draft or in multiple drafts, with a limitation of error not to exceed 20% of the maximum permissible error for the belt conveyor weigher.

[subreg (2) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 179, effective 1 December 2016]

(3) A person shall not use a belt conveyor weigher for trade and a belt conveyor weigher is not in accordance with the requirements of this Act unless—

(a) on inspection, the limitation of error for the belt conveyor weigher at zero, does not exceed the limitation of error—

(i) of one scale interval when fitted with an analogue indicating device, or

(ii) 2 scale intervals when fitted with a digital indicating device; and

(b) when loaded, the belt conveyor weigher does not exceed the maximum permissible error for the belt conveyor weigher.

(4) When making an inspection of a belt conveyor weigher, an inspector shall ensure that it accords with the provisions of subregulation (3) and he or she shall—

(a) test the belt conveyor weigher without a load at the zero indication, for a period equivalent to the time taken for the minimum totalised load to pass over the belt conveyor weigher at the minimum flow rate, provided that this period is not less than the time for the belt to complete at least one complete circuit of the belt conveyor system;

(b) test the belt conveyor weigher with the material for which it is approved;

(c) test the belt conveyor weigher at load with not less than the minimum totalised load which shall be—

(i) in respect of Class 1 belt conveyor weighers, a quantity of material equal to 1,000 analogue scale intervals, or 2,000 digital scale intervals; or

(ii) in respect of Class 2 belt conveyor weighers, a quantity of material equal to 500 analogue scale intervals or 1,000 digital scale intervals; or

3 times at each of the following constant flowrates—

(iii) 100% of the maximum flow rate of the belt conveyor weigher;

(iv) 20% of the maximum flow rate of the belt conveyor weigher;

(v) 40% of the maximum flow rate of the belt conveyor weigher;

(vi) 60% of the maximum flow rate of the belt conveyor weigher; and

(vii) 80% of the maximum flow rate of the belt conveyor weigher.

## PART 5 PUBLIC WEIGHBRIDGES [regs 53–69]

### DIVISION 1 REGISTRATION OF PUBLIC WEIGHBRIDGES [regs 53–59]

#### reg 53 Interpretation — Part 5

In this Part, unless the context otherwise requires—

**licensee** means the person named in a certificate of registration of a public weighbridge as the holder of such certificate;

**public weighbridge** means a weighbridge in use for trade, made available to the public by the licensee; and

**public weighman** means the holder of a licence as a public weighman issued pursuant to this Part.

#### reg 54 Public weighbridges to be registered

A person shall not make available a weighbridge as a public weighbridge unless—

(a) the public weighbridge is registered in accordance with the provisions of this Part; and

(b) the public weighbridge is inspected and stamped in accordance with this Act and an inspector has issued a certificate in Form 3 in Schedule 1 signifying that the public weighbridge has been found upon inspection to be in accordance in every respect with the requirements of this Act.

#### reg 55 Application for registration

A person who wishes to register a public weighbridge shall make application, accompanied by the prescribed fee, to the Director in Form 4 in Schedule 1.

[reg 55 am LN 99 of 2016 reg 179, effective 1 December 2016]

#### reg 56 Requirements for registration

The Director shall not register a public weighbridge unless he or she is satisfied that—

(a) the weighbridge is pattern approved;

(b) the weighbridge does not indicate graduations in excess of 10 kilograms or 0.01 tonne;

(c) the weighbridge is of maximum capacity of not less than 10 tonnes;

(d) the weighbridge has a platform not less than 6.5 metres in length;

(e) the head work of the weighbridge is suitably enclosed so as to prevent interference therewith by any person who is not authorised by the licensee;

(f) the weighbridge is constructed of such approved materials as to be suitable for use by the public; and

(g) the public weighbridge is constructed so that the public weighman is able to see the whole of the platform or platforms when weighing.

[reg 56 am LN 99 of 2016 reg 179, effective 1 December 2016]

#### reg 57 Transfer of registration

(1) A licensee of a public weighbridge may make application to the Director in Form 5 in Schedule 1 for the approval of the transfer of the registration thereof, and such application shall be accompanied by the prescribed fee, the inspector's report and the certificate of registration in relation thereto.

[subreg (1) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 179, effective 1 December 2016]

(2) Where a licensee ceases to use a public weighbridge before an application for approval of the transfer of the registration thereof has been approved by the Director, the registration is upon the cessation cancelled, and the licensee shall deliver up to the Director the certificate of registration, within 7 days.

[subreg (2) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 179, effective 1 December 2016]

#### reg 58 Register and certificates of registration

(l) The Director in respect of any application for registration of public weighbridges or for approval of the transfer of registration of a public weighbridge upon being satisfied that the requirements of this Act have been complied with and upon payment of the prescribed fee, shall issue a certificate of registration in the name of the applicant or the transferee as the case may require in Form 6 of Schedule 1.

[subreg (1) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 179, effective 1 December 2016]

(2) A certificate of registration shall remain in force until it is cancelled, suspended or surrendered.

(3) The Director shall keep a register of all applications received, and certificates of registration of public weighbridges and approvals of transfer of registration of public weighbridges issued, suspended or cancelled.

[subreg (3) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 179, effective 1 December 2016]

#### reg 59 Cancellation or suspension of registration

(1) If at any time the Director considers—

(a) that a public weighbridge is no longer suitable for use by the public;

(b) that a licensee has shown himself or herself to be incompetent or inefficient in the conduct of the duties required of him or her by virtue of his or her certificate of registration;

(c) that a licensee obtained his or her certificate of registration by a false statement or a misrepresentation or by any other means whatsoever, has not properly obtained his or her certificate of registration; or

(d) that a certificate of registration has been altered otherwise than by the Director,

the Director may, subject to subregulation (2) by notice in writing, cancel or suspend the relevant certificate of registration.

[subreg (1) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 179, effective 1 December 2016]

(2) Before he or she gives a notice under subregulation (1), the Director shall afford to the licensee in question an opportunity to show cause why the notice should not be given by notifying the licensee in writing of a day (not being earlier than 30 days after the giving of the notification), a time and a place when and where he or she may show cause why such certificate of registration should not be cancelled or suspended.

[subreg (2) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 179, effective 1 December 2016]

(3) Any licensee to whom a notification is given under subregulation (2) —

(a) may appear at the day, time and place so notified and take such steps as are calculated to show the specified cause; or

(b) may endeavour to show the specified cause in writing furnished to the Director at any time before the time so notified.

[subreg (3) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 179, effective 1 December 2016]

(4) When under these Regulations a certificate of registration is cancelled or suspended, the licensee shall deliver up to the Director the cancelled or suspended certificate of registration forthwith.

[subreg (4) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 179, effective 1 December 2016]

### DIVISION 2 DUTIES OF LICENSEES [regs 60]

#### reg 60 Duties of licensees

The licensee shall, in respect of a public weighbridge for which he or she holds a certificate of registration—

(a) provide and maintain in a prominent position in view of the public a sign in letters not less than 100 millimetres in height and in clear contrast with the background stating—

(i) the words “Registered Public Weighbridge No” together with the number allocated to the public weighbridge in the certificate of registration; and

(ii) the capacity of the public weighbridge;

(b) prominently exhibit in the public weighbridge cabin the certificate of registration and the certificate issued by an inspector pursuant to regulation 54(b), suitably and securely framed and covered by glass;

(c) provide printed mass tickets in Form 7 of Schedule 1 for issue when loaded vehicles are weighed;

(d) provide the following tare mass tickets for issue when unloaded vehicles are weighed—

(i) a tare mass ticket in Form 8 in Schedule 1; or

(ii) a mass ticket in Form 7 in Schedule 1 with the words “Tare Mass Only” permanently and legibly printed or stamped thereon and on duplicates thereof, in capital letters not less than 5 millimetres in height;

(e) ensure that the mass tickets and tare mass tickets for issue—

(i) are numbered consecutively except as provided for in paragraph (g) of this regulation;

(ii) are printed so as to provide for an original and at least one duplicate of each ticket; and

(iii) have the word “original” printed on the original of each ticket and the word “duplicate” printed on each duplicate thereof;

(f) ensure that the original ticket remains at all times in the ticket book, and is not issued;

(g) provide mass tickets and tare mass tickets for issue as copies of previously issued mass tickets and tare mass tickets, in the form of the original tickets previously retained in the book, for at least 12 months after the date of the last issued ticket, in that book; and

(i) having the words “copy of” printed immediately before the words “Mass Ticket No…” or “Tare Mass Ticket No…” as the case may be; and

(ii) having the words “Date of issue of copy” printed at the bottom left hand corner of such tickets;

(h) where the particulars or other matters required to complete mass tickets or tare mass tickets are inserted by a mechanical or electronic means, keep the original of such tickets bound in numerical sequence in books, pads or singularly in numerical sequence;

(i) retain for at least one year from the date of issue thereof—

(i) original mass tickets and original tare mass tickets and original copies thereof together with any unissued duplicates thereof, where the particulars of other matters required to complete the same are inserted therein in handwriting in numerical sequence in the books in which they are bound; and

(ii) original mass ticket and original tare mass tickets and original copies thereof together with any unissued duplicates thereof where the particulars of other matters required to complete the same are inserted therein by mechanical or electronic means, in numerical sequence in an office system so that the same are readily accessible, and produce such original, original copy and duplicate mass tickets and tare mass tickets on request by an inspector or by any other person having an interest in the transaction to which they relate;

(j) if the licensee knows or has reason to believe that the public weighbridge is incorrect or unjust forthwith—

(i) inform the inspector for the district in which the public weighbridge is situated of such knowledge or belief; and

(ii) close the public weighbridge to further use;

(k) forthwith inform the inspector in whose district the public weighbridge is situated if the public weighbridge is repaired or adjusted or moved from its tested position;

(l) cause or permit only a public weighman to act as a weighman at the public weighbridge.

### DIVISION 3 LICENSING OF PUBLIC WEIGHMAN [regs 61–66]

#### reg 61 Weighman at a public weighbridge

A person shall not act as a weighman at a public weighbridge unless he or she is registered as a public weighman.

#### reg 62 Application for licence

A person who wishes to obtain a licence as a public weighman shall make application accompanied by the prescribed fee, to the Director in Form 9 in Schedule 1.

[reg 62 am LN 99 of 2016 reg 179, effective 1 December 2016]

#### reg 63 Requirements for grant of licence

The Director shall not grant a licence as a public weighman unless he or she is satisfied that the applicant—

(a) has attained the age of 18 years;

(b) is of good fame and character;

(c) is a fit and proper person to hold such a licence;

(d) has sufficient ability to read and write in the English language so as to be able to carry out fully and correctly the duties of a public weighman;

(e) has a sufficient knowledge of public weighbridges and their operation so as to be able to carry out fully and correctly the duties of a public weighman;

(f) has sufficient mathematical ability so as to be able to calculate masses correctly; and

(g) has paid the prescribed fee for issue of such a licence.

[reg 63 am LN 99 of 2016 reg 179, effective 1 December 2016]

#### reg 64 Examination of applicants

An applicant for a licence as a public weighman may be examined by written or oral examination or by a combination of these methods and he or she may also be required to give a practical demonstration of the skill required for the performance of the duties of a public weighman.

#### reg 65 Period of licence

A licence as a public weighman shall remain in force until it is cancelled, suspended or surrendered.

#### reg 66 Cancellation or suspension of licence

(1) If at any time the Director considers that a public weighman—

(a) has carried out his or her duties as a public weighman in a negligent, unsatisfactory or incompetent manner;

(b) has failed to comply with the lawful order, direction, notice, demand or requisition or part thereof of an inspector;

(c) obtained his or her licence by a false statement or misrepresentation or by any other means whatsoever has not properly obtained his or her licence; or

(d) is not a fit and proper person to hold a licence as such,

the Director may, subject to subregulation (2), by notice in writing cancel or suspend the licence as a public weighman.

[subreg (1) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 179, effective 1 December 2016]

(2) Before he or she gives a notice under subregulation (1) the Director shall afford to the public weighman in question an opportunity to show cause why the notice should not be given by notifying the public weighman in writing of a day (not being earlier than 30 days after the giving of the notification), a time and a place when and where he or she may show cause why such licence should not be cancelled or suspended.

[subreg (2) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 179, effective 1 December 2016]

(3) Any licensee to whom a notification is given under subregulation (2) —

(i) may appear at the day, time and place so notified and take such steps as are calculated to show the specified cause; or

(ii) may endeavour to show the specified cause in writing furnished to the Director at any time before the time so notified.

[subreg (3) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 179, effective 1 December 2016]

(4) When under these Regulations a licence as a public weighman is cancelled or suspended, the licensee shall deliver up to the Director the cancelled or suspended licence, forthwith.

[subreg (4) am LN 99 of 2016 reg 179, effective 1 December 2016]

### DIVISION 4 PUBLIC WEIGHMAN’S DUTIES [regs 67–69]

#### reg 67 Duties of a public weighman

A public weighman shall, in respect of a public weighbridge for which he or she is the weighman—

(a) keep the public weighbridge truly balanced, the platform clean and the space between the frame and platform free from obstructions;

(b) unless the public weighman has a proper and just cause to refuse to do so, weigh any loaded or unloaded vehicle brought to the public weighbridge to be weighed if—

(i) the public weighbridge has the capacity to weigh such vehicle; and

(ii) the fee usually charged by the licensee for such weighing is offered to the public weighman;

(c) subject to regulation 68, immediately after weighing a loaded vehicle, issue to the driver thereof a mass ticket signed by the public weighman in Form 7 in Schedule 1 with the particulars or other matters required to complete such mass ticket fully and correctly;

(d) subject to regulation 68, immediately after weighing an unloaded vehicle, issue to the driver thereof, a tare mass ticket as follows—

(i) a tare mass ticket signed by the public weighman in Form 8 in Schedule 1 with the particulars or other matters required to complete such tare mass ticket fully and correctly; or

(ii) a mass ticket in Form 7 in Schedule 1 with the words “Tare Mass Only,” permanently and legibly printed or stamped thereon and on duplicates thereof in capital letters not less than 5 millimetres in height, signed by the public weighman with such of the particulars or other matters required to complete such mass ticket in relation to tare mass fully and correctly;

(e) issue each mass ticket or tare mass ticket consecutively according to its number;

(f) when he or she issues a mass ticket or tare mass ticket or a copy thereof, make at least one duplicate thereof;

(g) when an error is made in the particulars or other matters required to complete any mass ticket or tare mass ticket, cancel such ticket and the duplicates thereof and retain such ticket and the duplicates thereof in the book in which they are bound or, as the case may be, in the office system maintained pursuant to regulation 60(i)(ii);

(h) upon demand by the buyer or seller of goods weighed on the public weighbridge or by a person having an interest in such goods and, if requested, by the public weighman, upon payment by such person of a reasonable fee, therefor, the public weighman shall issue to such person a duplicate of the original copy as prescribed in regulation 60(g) of the original ticket thereof;

(i) on demand by an inspector, produce the original mass ticket or tare mass ticket or the original copy thereof retained by the licensee pursuant to regulation 60(i);

(j) on demand of by an inspector in the execution of his or her duties and without charge, weigh or reweigh any loaded or unloaded vehicle;

(k) on demand by an inspector produce his or her licence as a public weighman;

(l) prominently exhibit in the public weighbridge cabin a copy of his or her licence as a public weighman suitably and securely framed and covered by glass;

(m) if the public weighman knows or has reason to believe that the public weighbridge is incorrect or unjust—

(i) forthwith inform the inspector for the district in which the public weighbridge is situated of such knowledge or belief; and

(ii) close the public weighbridge to further use.

#### reg 68 Exemption

A public weighman is exempted—

(a) from issuing a mass ticket or a tare mass ticket pursuant to regulation 67(c) when a weighing is carried out for the information of the public weighman only; and

(b) from issuing a tare mass ticket pursuant to regulation 67(d) when a weighing is carried out for the information of the public weigh man only.

#### reg 69 Prohibited conduct by public weighman

A public weighman shall not—

(a) cause or permit a person other than a public weighman to act as a weighman at a public weighbridge;

(b) alter the original of a mass ticket or tare mass ticket after a duplicate or copy thereof has been issued;

(c) issue any duplicate or copy of a mass ticket or tare mass ticket which is not a correct copy of the original;

(d) remove an original mass ticket or an original tare mass ticket or an original copy thereof from the book in which it is bound or, as the case may be, from the office system maintained pursuant to regulation 60(i)(ii);

(e) remove an unissued mass ticket or tare mass ticket from the book or numerical sequence in which it is kept;

(f) issue a mass ticket or tare mass ticket for any loaded vehicle or unloaded vehicle unless the public weighman has, at the time of the issue, personally weighed such vehicle;

(g) issue a mass ticket showing the tare mass of a vehicle without—

(i) himself or herself having weighed the vehicle and ascertained the correct tare mass on the same day as the date of issue of the tare mass ticket; or

(ii) having produced to him or her by the driver of the vehicle a tare mass ticket showing the tare mass of the vehicle ascertained by another public weighman on the same public weighbridge or on another public weighbridge on the same premises in each case on the same day as the date of issue of the tare mass ticket; and

(h) weigh a loaded vehicle or an unloaded vehicle for the purpose of issuing a mass ticket or as the case may be, a tare mass ticket without the whole of such vehicle being on the public weighbridge platform.

## PART 7 TIMING DEVICES [regs 84, 85]

### reg 84 Interpretation

(1) In this Part—

**service** means vehicle parking, laundry operation, vehicle washing and any other service, wherein the use of a facility is provided on a time basis; and

**timing device** means a time measuring instrument used or use for measuring time for the purposes of the provision of a service in return for money's worth.

(2) A person providing a service shall not use a timing device for trade and a timing device is not in accordance with the requirements of this Act unless—

(a) there is in place, clear and unambiguous instructions in close proximity to the timing device, advising in English—

(i) the correct method of operation of the device; and

(ii) the charges that apply;

(b) if provided with a ticket-printing device, any printed ticket issued is clearly and correctly printed with—

(i) the date on which the service is given;

(ii) the time of the day when the service commenced; and

(iii) upon the ceasing of such service, the time of the day when the service ceased;

(c) if the ticket-printing device is temporarily incapable of producing a ticket, either—

(i) a visible indication to this effect is displayed; or

(ii) the instrument must cease to operate;

(d) the timing device provides the advertised service for which it is operated for the correct money inserted; and

(e) the timing device does not exceed the maximum permissible error for timing devices.

(f) the service for which the timing device is used is provided as offered or advertised.

(3) When making an inspection of a timing device, an inspector shall ensure that it is in accordance with the provisions of subregulation (2) and shall test the timing device by—

(a) comparing the timing device with an appropriate reference standard; and

(b) where applicable, the insertion of the correct money,

to establish that the service is correctly provided.

### reg 85 Inspection of timing devices

A timing device shall be subject to inspection—

(a) at least once in each 12 months;

(b) by random sampling; or

(c) by reference to the investigation of a complaint.

(a) uses for trade or causes or permits any person to use for trade, any measuring instrument of the pattern referred to in the withdrawn certificate or a measuring instrument in respect of which an approval given under regulation 97 has been withdrawn; or

(b) disposes of any such measuring instrument in a condition in which it is capable of being used for trade, to a person without informing that person that the pattern approval certificate or the approval has been withdrawn,

shall be guilty of an offence.

## PART 9 UNITS OF MEASUREMENT OF A PHYSICAL QUANTITY [regs 103–105]

### reg 103 Units to be used for stating price

The Fiji legal units of measurement of physical quantity that shall be used for stating the price per unit of measurement of a physical quantity of an article (other than one that is pre-packed) offered, exposed or advertised for sale shall be as set out hereunder—

(a) in the case of mass—

(i) one kilogram;

(ii) one kilogram, with another reference to an integral number of kilograms or an integral number of grams;

(iii) an integral number of tonnes; or

(iv) if the article is a precious metal, one gram or one troy ounce;

(b) in the case of volume—

(ii) one litre, with another reference to an integral number of litres or an integral number of millilitres; or

(iii) an integral number of cubic metres;

(c) in the case of linear measurement—

(i) one centimetre;

(ii) one metre; or

### reg 104 Restrictions on expressions of price per integral number of units

(1) Where under this Act, a price is required or authorised to be expressed by reference to any Fiji legal unit of measurement, and as a integral number which is greater than one of that or another unit, such price shall be when expressed, as an integral number greater than one, of that unit or other unit be not more prominent than the expression of the price in term of a single unit.

(2) In the case of a price authorised to be expressed in an integral number of grams, the only 2 integral numbers which shall be used, shall be 100 and 250, and where a statement of a price is expressed in terms of 100 grams or 250 grams, the price per kilogram and not price per gram, shall also be expressed with no less prominence.

(3) A price may not be expressed by reference to an integral number of square centimetres.

### reg 106 Fees and charges

(1) The fees set out in Schedule 3 are the fees prescribed for the purposes of this Act as the fees payable in respect of the matter in relation to which the fees are specified.

(1) An inspector who seizes any thing under section 19(9) of this Act shall upon seizure supply to the person in possession of that thing with a voucher in Form 14 in Schedule 1.

9.4 Weighing-in-motion Instruments

9.4.1 Maximum permissible static errors

Refer to 9.2.2.

9.4.2 Maximum permissible dynamic errors

After the instrument has been adjusted to zero at no load, the maximum permissible dynamic errors, at any speed with in the specified range, shall not be greater than the following—

(a) total train mass: ±5t or ±0.2% of the total train mass, whichever is the larger;

(b) wagon mass: 1t or ±1.0% of the wagon mass, whichever is the larger.

9.5 Weighing and Counting Scales

The limitations of error of the weighing and counting scale on inspection does not exceed plus or minus the mass of the smallest article the weighing and counting scale is designed and constructed to count.
