# NATIONAL AND TRADE MEASUREMENT (MISLEADING MARKINGS AND DECEPTIVE PACKAGING) REGULATIONS 1989

## reg 1 Short title

These Regulations may be cited as the National and Trade Measurement (Misleading Markings and Deceptive Packaging Regulations) 1989.

## reg 2 Commencement

These Regulations shall come into force on 6 October 1989.

## reg 3 Interpretation

In these Regulations, unless the context otherwise requires—

**cap or lid** means any device, whether consisting of a single part or more parts that is used to close the neck, top or outlet of a package when it is ordinarily exposed for sale;

**cavity** means space within the substance of a package, or any part thereof, and includes space enclosed by a cap or lid when it forms part of a package;

**free space** means any part of the usable internal capacity of a package that is a vacuum or is occupied by matter other than the article for sale contained in the package;

**lining** means an integral part of the inner surface of a package, made or formed in the same operation as that by which the package is made;

**perceived volume** means the space that would appear to a person when observing a package, when it is in a position in which it is ordinarily exposed for sale, to be the space contained within the outline of the package;

**recess** means space within or about the substance of a package which forms part of a package, but is hidden when a person observes the perceived volume of a package;

**toilet preparation** means a toiletry product intended to be applied directly to the body and includes cosmetics; and

**usable** means, in relation to the internal capacity of package, the capacity of a package able to be used for the purpose of packing for sale the article contained in the package.

## reg 4 Features of packages likely to deceive

(1) No person shall sell or offer for sale any package to which these Regulation apply which is likely to deceive a purchaser.

(2) For the purposes of this regulation a package shall be taken to be one likely to deceive a purchaser when—

(a) within the package there is free space that exceeds the maximum volume or measurement in relation to packages prescribed in regulation 4;

(b) within or about the package there is a concealed recess, or a number of such recesses, with a volume that exceeds the maximum volume prescribed in regulation 7; or

(c) within the substance of the package there is a cavity or a number of cavities that exceeds the maximum volume or measurement prescribed in regulation 10; and

(d) any such free space, recess or cavity is likely to be concealed from a person looking at the package when it is in a position usual for displaying for sale packages of the article contained therein.

(3) The provisions of these Regulations shall not apply to—

(a) packages intended for export from Fiji which are not offered for sale within Fiji;

(b) shipping transport containers, for transport only;

(c) a core, whether hollow or solid, around which is wound an article;

(d) packages containing therapeutic goods of which the sale or supply to the public is prohibited by law except on the written prescription of a person recognised by law as competent to prescribe them;

(e) packages containing articles of furniture, hardware or tools; or

(f) packages containing an article that before sale is ordinarily inspected by the purchaser.

## reg 5 Free space

(1) The maximum volume of free space within a package containing an article of a description specified below shall not exceed the percentage specified—

(a) cakes, confectionery, biscuits, soap tablets or other toilet preparations, whether wrapped or unwrapped, packed as single units, in separate formed or moulded compartments in a package containing 2 or more units of such goods, 40%;

(b) chocolate slices or chocolate coated slices (sometimes called “thins”) not exceeding 5 millimetres in thickness, 40%;

(c) any article packed in an aerosol package, 40%;

(d) any construction toy contained in a package suitable for use, for a substantial period after the package has been opened, for storing the toy therein, 40%;

(e) therapeutic goods ordinarily sold by number otherwise than on prescription, 35%;

(f) articles of any other description, 25%.

(2) The maximum measurement of free space within a package that incorporates 2 or more blisters of pockets for the purpose of holding any article or articles shall be as follows—

(a) the space between a point at the base of the external surface of one blister or pocket and the nearest point at the base of the external surface of another blister or pocket next adjacent to that blister or pocket shall not exceed 5 millimetres;

(b) the space between a point at the base of the external surface of a blister or pocket (being a peripheral blister or pocket of the package) and the nearest point on the edge of the package next adjacent to that blister or pocket, shall not exceed 5 millimetres, except as is prescribed by paragraph (c) of this subregulation; and

(c) the space between a point at the base of the external surface of a blister or pocket (being a peripheral blister or pocket of the package) and the nearest point on the edge of the package next adjacent to that blister or pocket, if the edge may reasonably be regarded as an end of the package, shall not exceed 7.5 millimetres.

(3) For the purposes of subregulation (2), no more than 2 edges of a package shall be regarded as ends of the package and no 2 edges of a package shall be so regarded unless they are opposite to each other.

## reg 6 Non-application of free space requirements

(1) Free space requirements do not apply in relation to a package, or packages containing an article specified in the circumstances described below—

(a) anaerobic polymer products;

(b) the contents of a cosmetic compact, in respect of the contents and the compact, but not in respect of any additional outer package;

(c) insecticide pest strip, in respect of the strip and its immediate package, but not in respect of any additional outer package;

(d) lipstick, in respect of the lipstick and its immediate package, but not in respect of any additional outer package;

(e) packages, being flexible bags or pouches, containing potato or similar flakes, chips or crips;

(f) packages in which free space is necessarily provided to serve a particular function when it is necessary that some process be applied to the package or its contents before its contents are used;

(g) packages of paints, which are to be mixed, where free space is provided in the package containing the greatest quantity of paint for the purpose of mixing;

(h) dispensing packages containing sweeteners in tablet form where free space is required for the operation of the dispensing mechanism;

(i) transparent packages, not being inner packages, if the volume of each article contained therein is apparent through the transparency.

(2) A package, which is not wholly transparent, shall be taken to be a transparent package only—

(a) if its transparency consists of a transparent display window, centrally situated in the main display portion of the package, and the area of the window accounts for—

(i) 60% or more of the area of the panel of the package that includes the window, where the area of the panel is less than 300 square centimetres; or

(ii) 75% or more of the area of the panel of the package that includes the window, where the area of the panel is, or exceeds, 300 square centimetres;

(b) if its transparency consists of 2 or more transparent display windows situated in the main display portion of the package (2 of such windows being at opposite ends of the main display portion of the package) and the aggregate area of the windows accounts for—

(i) 60% or more of the area of the panel of the package that includes the windows where the area of the panel is less than, 300 square centimetres; or

(ii) 75% or more of the area of the panel of the package that includes the windows where the area of the panel is, or exceeds 300 square centimetres;

(c) if its transparency consists of 2 transparent display windows, each such window being situated at the opposite ends of opposite panels of the package (excluding the base panel on which the package sits and with one of those panels being the main display portion of the package), and the area of each of the windows accounts for—

(i) 30% or more of the area of the panel of the package that includes the window, where the area of the panel is less than 300 square centimetres; or

(ii) 37.5% or more the area of the panel of the package that includes the window where the area of the panel is, or exceeds, 300 square centimetres;

(d) if its transparency consists of 2 transparent display windows, each such window being situated at the opposite ends of adjacent panels of the package (excluding the base panel on which the package sits and with one of those panels being the main display portion of the package), and the area of each of the windows accounts for—

(i) 30% or more of the area of the panel of the package that includes the window, where the area of the panel is less than 300 square centimetres; or

(ii) 37.5% or more of the area of the panel of the package that includes the window where the area of the panel is, or exceeds, 300 square centimetres;

(e) if its transparency consists of transparent display windows situated in opposite or adjacent panels (excluding the base panel on which the package sits and with one of the opposite or adjacent panels being the main display portion of the package) and the aggregate area of the window(s) in each panel accounts for—

(i) 60% or more of the area of the panel of the package that includes the window(s) where the area of the panel is less than 300 square centimetres; or

(ii) 75% or more of the area of the panel of the package that includes the window(s) where the area of the panel is, or exceeds, 300 square centimetres.

## reg 7 Determination of free space

(1) A volume of free space shall be expressed as a percentage of the internal capacity of the package and shall be calculated in accordance with the formula—

(D−C)D × 100

where the symbol C represents the volume occupied by the article or articles contained in a package and the symbol D represents the usable internal capacity of a package.

(2) Where, for the purpose of calculating a measurement of a volume of free space, it is necessary to determine the volume of any article if a package, the volume of the article shall be determined having regard to conditions that might reasonably be expected to affect it at the time the article was packed in the package.

(3) Where a package contains immediately within itself a single solid article that is not a regular geometric figure or a tube, whether or not that article is an inner package, the volume of that article shall be taken to be—

(a) if the package is not a cylinder, the volume of a rectangular prism having the least dimensions able to enclose that article; or

(b) if the package is a cylinder, the volume of a cylinder having the least dimensions able to enclose that article.

(4) Where a package contains immediately within itself 2 or more solid articles, whether or not of the same description, and whether or not any of them is an inner package, the volume of those articles shall be taken to be—

(a) if the package is not a cylinder, the volume of a rectangular prism having the least dimensions able to enclose that article; or

(b) if the package is a cylinder, the volume of a cylinder having the least dimensions able to enclose that article.

(5) The volume of a tube shall be taken to be the product of the length of the tube including its cap or lid, the diameter of the tube at the shoulder or at the wider or widest shoulder, as the case may be, the length of the crimped or sealed edge of the tube and the decimal submultiple 0.8.

(6) Where a package contains immediately within itself an article that is powder, flakes or grains, the volume of that article shall be determined after the package has been set in a jig to produce perpendicular sides and to eliminate any bulges and turned steadily upside down and returned to the original position—

(a) in the case of an article that is powder, 6 times; and

(b) in the case of an article that is flakes or grains, 3 times.

## reg 8 Recess

The maximum volume of any concealed recess within or about a package or where there is more than one recess, the aggregate volume of all such recesses shall not exceed 10%.

## reg 9 Non-application of recess requirements

Recess requirements shall not apply in relation to packages, or packages containing an article specified, in the circumstances described below—

(a) articles ordinarily sold by mass packed in a quantity of less than 15 grams;

(b) articles ordinarily sold by volume packed in a quantity of less than 15 millilitres;

(c) packages, being cans of a design approved by the Director that, in respect of any recess or recesses therein, conform to that approved design; and

(d) packages, being aerosols, if the depth of the bottom recess does not exceed 15 millimetres.

## reg 10 Calculation of recess

The volume of any concealed recess or recesses within or about a package shall be calculated as a percentage of the internal capacity of the package in accordance with the following formula— (B−A)D × 100 where the symbol B represents the perceived volume of a package, the symbol A represents the volume occupied by the article or articles contained in a package, and the symbol D represents the usable internal capacity of a package.

## reg 11 Cavity

The maximum volume of any cavity in a package or where there is more than one cavity, the aggregate volume of all cavities, shall not exceed 15% of the internal capacity of the package.

## reg 12 Non-application of cavity requirements

(1) Cavity requirements shall not apply in relation to packages, or packages containing an article specified in the circumstances described below—

(a) articles ordinarily sold by mass packed in a quantity of less than 15 grams;

(b) articles ordinarily sold by volume packed in a quantity of less than 15 millilitres; and

(c) liquid mascara and liquid eye, lip or nail colour packed in a bottle fitted with an applicator; or

(d) a package that is an aerosol or a pump action package, when the height of clearance between the top surface of the activator and the underside of any cap or lid of the package does not exceed 10 mm, and the cavity immediately surrounding such activator shall not extend laterally beyond the limits of the part of the package other than the cap or lid, and there shall not be any other cavity in the package; and

(2) A pump action package is one that is activated by action of a pump valve at the top of the package (other than the cap or lid).

## reg 13 Calculation of cavities

The volume of any cavity or cavities in a package shall be calculated as a percentage of the internal capacity of the package.

## reg 14 Permits for sale of packages not complying

Nothing in these Regulations shall apply to any package in respect of which a permit has been granted under section 46(i) of the Act if the permit has been granted in respect of a contravention of these Regulations.

## reg 15 Offences

Any person who contravenes these Regulations shall be guilty of an offence and liable upon conviction to the penalties specified in section 54 of the Act.
