# PORTS SECURITY UNIT REGULATIONS 2007

Ports Security Unit Regulations 2007 (LN 38 of 2007) commenced on 18 January 2007, as amended by:

| Amending Legislation | Date of Commencement |
| --- | --- |
| Revised Edition of the Laws (Consequential Amendments) Regulations 2016 (LN 99 of 2016) | 1 December 2016 |

## reg 1 Short title and commencement

These Regulations may be cited as the Ports Security Unit Regulations 2007, and are deemed to have come into force on 18 January 2007.

## reg 2 Interpretation

In these Regulations, unless the context otherwise requires—

**after hours** means the period between 2000 hours and 0600 hours;

**container** means an article of transporting equipment—

(a) of a permanent character designed for repeated use;

(b) specifically designed to facilitate the transportation of goods by one or more means of transport without intermediate reloading; and

(c) designed to be secured and readily handled, having corner fittings for those purposes;

**Customer** [def rep LN 99 of 2016 reg 207, 1 December 2016]

**day** includes part of a day;

**devanning** means the unstuffing of the contents of any container;

**escort** means any member of the Unit accompanying any delivery of a container or other imported commodity;

**Fiji Ports Corporation Limited** means the Fiji Ports Corporation Limited established under the Act;

**Force** means the Republic of Fiji Military Forces established under the Republic of Fiji Military Forces Act 1949 ;

**HMC** means from Port to Port as defined under the Act;

**hour** includes part of an hour; and

**Unit** means the Ports Security Unit.

## reg 3 Establishment and functions of the Unit

(1) This regulations establishes the Ports Security Unit consisting of such officers and soldiers of the Force, as may be assigned to the Unit from time to time.

(2) All officers and soldiers serving under the Unit—

(a) shall be deemed to be duly appointed and employed customs officers for the purpose of carrying out and assist in carrying out of any of the provisions of the written laws set out in paragraph (b); and

(b) may exercise any powers and functions under the following Acts—

(i) Customs Act 1986 ;

(ii) Sea Ports Management Act 2005 ;

(iii) Customs Tariff Act 1986 ;

(iv) Excise Act 1986 ; and

(v) Maritime and Ports Authority of Fiji (Tariff) Regulations 2001.

## reg 4 Fees for services

The following fees (VEP) shall be payable by a shipping company or an importer, as the case may be, for the services rendered by the Unit—

(a) devanning of containers, $53.33 per container;

(b) inspection of containers, $31.11 per container inspected;

(c) meal allowance, $5.33 per person per meal;

(d) escort allowances, $4.44 per hour per person (time begins from source to final destination);

(e) accommodation allowance will be borne by the shipping companies if escort is required to stay overnight at any destination;

(f) after hours to early morning HMC delivery allowance will be as follows—

(i) 1700 - 2100 $6.22 per person;

(ii) 2100 - 0100 $7.11 per person;

(iii) 0100 - 0500 $6.22 per person.

(g) return transport of the escorts will be borne by the shipping company or importer.

## reg 5 Meal hours

The meal hours shall be as follows—

(a) breakfast is from 6am to 8am;

(b) lunch is from 12pm to 2pm; and

(c) dinner is from 6pm to 8pm.

## reg 6 Container escort number

The following number of escort from the Unit shall be provided when conducting HMC or any delivery to other destinations—

(a) for 3 containers trucks, one escort;

(b) from 4 to 9 containers trucks, 3 escorts;

(c) from 10 to 15 containers trucks, 4 escorts;

(d) from 16 to 20 containers trucks, 5 escorts.
