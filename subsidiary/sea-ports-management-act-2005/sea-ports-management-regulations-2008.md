## PART 1 PRELIMINARY [regs 1-4]

### reg 1 Short title

These Regulations may be cited as the Sea Ports Management Regulations 2008.

### reg 2 Interpretation

In these Regulations, unless the context otherwise requires—

**commercial operation** includes plying for hire with a vessel, the carriage of passengers or goods on a vessel for a fee or reward, the hire or charter of a vessel a hire and drive vessel; operating a vessel as a tug, ferry, transport, vehicle, excursion vessel, dredge, lighter, storage hulk or survey vessel for fee or reward or in the course of any business or in connection with any commercial transaction; providing restaurant or entertainment facilities, carrying on the business of a stevedore or a wharfinger; plying for hire as a boatman, carrier or porter, selling or exposing for sale or barter any goods or articles of trade, conducting any other business, but does not include any commercial operation engaged in by a vessel, engaged in going between a port or place outside of that port, or based in a port and engaged in a marine operation outside that port;

**contaminated** means the presence of any harmful substance in a discharge;

**Convention** means the International Convention for the Prevention of Pollution from Ships 1973 as modified and added to by the Protocol of 1978 and as amended from time to time;

**dangerous vessel** means any vessel that is disabled either wholly or partly or is in a leaking condition, on fire or the cargo of which is on fire, likely to pollute the waters of the port, carrying cargo which includes oils or chemicals or any other substance of a flammable or combustible nature where fire has occurred in or in proximity to that cargo within 24 hours prior to the proposed arrival of the vessel;

**derelict vessel** means any vessel that is abandoned within the port harbour for a period of 21 days and does not have the minimum manning requirement to operate;

**enclosed area** means an area designated under regulation 63 ;

**garbage** means all kinds of victual, domestic and operational waste generated during the normal operation of a vessel;

**harmful substances** means any substance which, if introduced into the sea is liable to create hazards to human health, to harm living resources and marine life, to damage amenities or to interfere with other legitimate uses of the sea and includes any substance subject to control under the Convention;

**incident** means an event involving the actual or probable discharge into the sea of a harmful substance or a contaminated effluent;

**limited speed area** means an area so designated under regulation 90(1)(b) ;

**master**, in relation to a vessel means the person having lawful command or charge of the vessel (other than a pilot), and includes the agent of the vessel, and a reference to the master of the vessel is also to be read as including the owner of the vessel and each is severally liable for the due performance and observance of his or her obligations under these Regulations;

**oily mixture** means a mixture with any oil content;

**parking area** means an area so designated under regulation 90(1)(a) ;

**port user** means a person licensed pursuant to Part 7 of the Act and Part 9 of these Regulations;

**roadway** means a roadway within a port and includes that part of a wharf which is used or is capable of being used by vehicles and part of a roadway;

**servicing of the vessel** includes the loading or unloading of cargo, stores, gear, fuel or any other activity in connection with the vessel while it is in a port;

**sewage** means drainage and other wastes from any form of toilets, urinals and water closet (WC) scuppers, drainage from medical premises (dispensing, sick bay and similar facilities), drainage from spaces containing living animals or other waste waters mixed with or likely to be mixed with the drainages referred to herein;

**ski observer** means a person in a water ski boat who is responsible for watching the skier at all times and relaying the signals of the water skier to the driver;

**structure** includes but is not limited to wharves, jetties, landing, dolphins, pontoons, platforms retaining walls, building, loading or unloading structures, conveyors, ramps, docks, slip-ways, davits, drainage outfalls, buoys, moorings, permanent anchors, land created by reclamations, breakwaters and groins;

**traffic inspector** means an inspector appointed under regulation 87 ;

**traffic sign** means a sign erected under regulation 90(2) ;

**undesirable substance** means rubbish, gravel, earth, stone or wreak, flammable, corrosive or offensive substances (whether solid, liquid or gaseous) including dangerous substances, or any article or thing, or a substance (whether solid, liquid or gaseous) which is capable of constituting a hazard to navigation or of preventing the development of or the proper use of a port, but does not include oil;

**vehicle** has the same meaning as in the Land Transport Act 1998 ;

**water ski** means any water ski, surf board, aquaplane or planning device including power skis and ski-copters; and

**water ski boat** or ski boat means a vessel that is engaged in towing a water skier and includes a power ski.

### reg 3 Administration of the Regulations

These Regulations shall be administered by the port management company.

### reg 4 Application

(1) These Regulations shall apply to all ports and approaches to ports owned or under the control of a port management company, declared or deemed to be such pursuant to section 5 of the Act.

(2) Nothing contained in these Regulations shall affect the right of access to any transit shed, to any part of the wharf or to any enclosure in a wharf to any public officer for the purpose of law enforcement.

## PART 2 DUTIES OF MASTER OF A VESSEL [regs 5-39]

### reg 5 Appointment of an agent

(1) Subject to subregulation (4) , the master of a vessel intending to berth the vessel in a port, shall, before the vessel enters the port, appoint a person to be the agent of the vessel in the port.

(2) The master who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(3) An agent appointed under subregulation (1) shall act on behalf of the master of the vessel in respect of all matters which arise under the Act in respect of the vessel while the vessel is in the port for which he or she is appointed.

(4) Subregulation (1) does not apply to a vessel that is arriving at a port from another port or place within Fiji.

### reg 6 Application to berth

(1) The master of a vessel shall not berth the vessel at a wharf owned by the port management company except with the prior written approval of port management company.

(2) The master who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(3) An application for approval under subregulation (1) must be made at least 48 hours prior to the arrival of the vessel in the port.

### reg 7 Duties of master of a vessel

(1) The master of a foreign registered vessel of 35 metres in length or more shall, prior to entering or departing the Ports of Suva, Lautoka, Malau, Levuka or Wairiki, engage the services of a licensed pilot approved by the port management company.

(2) The master who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(3) Subregulation (1) does not apply where the master of the vessel is the holder of a valid pilot exemption certificate for the particular port which has been issued by the proper authority.

### reg 8 Assistance to the port management company

(1) The master of a vessel in a port shall obey all lawful orders given to the master by the port management company or by a person authorised by the port management company to give such orders.

(2) The master of a vessel shall give and afford to the port management company or to that person authorised under subregulation (1) all necessary assistance in the performance of its or his or her duties in relation to the vessel.

(3) The master who contravenes subregulation (1) or (2) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 9 Clear all cables

(1) The master of a vessel moored or anchored within a port shall have all cables clear and in readiness to move away when required.

(2) The master who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 10 Vessels less than 12 metres long to give way

(1) The master of a vessel less than 12 metres in length that is under way in a port shall keep the vessel out of the way of—

(a) larger vessels that are under way; or

(b) tugs engaged in towing.

(2) The master who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(3) For the purposes of subregulation (1) , a vessel is under way, unless it is at anchor, made fast to the shore or aground.

### reg 11 Prior approval for speed testing within a port

(1) The master of a vessel shall not test the speed of the vessel within a port except with the prior written approval of the port management company.

(2) The master who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(3) The port management company may give its approval under subregulation (1) subject to conditions.

(4) The master of a vessel to whom approval is given under subregulation (1) shall comply with all conditions imposed under subregulation (3) .

(5) The master who contravenes subregulation (4) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 12 Specified speed to be followed in certain circumstances

(1) The master of a vessel in a port who is navigating the vessel within 100 metres—

(a) of a dredge, diving plant, rock breaking machine, drill or other plant; or

(b) of any port work in progress,

shall navigate the vessel at a speed and in a manner specified by the port management company.

(2) The master who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 13 Report on condition of dangerous vessel

(1) The master of a dangerous vessel shall—

(a) before the vessel enters a port, give the port management company a report on the condition of the vessel and its cargo; and

(b) on the arrival of the vessel proceed, anchor or berth as directed by the port management company.

(2) The master who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 14 Prior approval for use of lifeboats and life-rafts

(1) Subject to subregulation (3) , the master of a vessel which is in a port and is more than 60 metres long shall not allow a boat, lifeboat, life-raft or similar lifesaving appliance belonging to the vessel—

(a) to be lowered into the water; or

(b) to leave the vessel’s side,

except with the prior written approval of the port management company.

(2) The master who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(3) Subregulation (1) shall not apply where the use of the boat, lifeboat, life-raft or lifesaving appliance is necessary in an emergency.

(4) The port management company may give its approval under subregulation (1) subject to conditions.

(5) The master of a vessel to whom approval is given under subregulation (1) shall comply with all conditions imposed under subregulation (4) .

(6) The master who contravenes subregulation (5) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 15 Prior approval for adjusting compasses or calibrating navigational equipment

(1) The master of a vessel which is more than 35 metres long shall not manoeuvre the vessel to adjust compasses or calibrate navigational equipment on the vessel while it is within a port except with the prior written approval of the port management company.

(2) The master who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(3) The port management company may give its approval under subregulation (1) subject to conditions.

(4) The master of a vessel to whom approval is given under subregulation (1) shall comply with all conditions imposed under subregulation (3) .

(5) The master who contravenes subregulation (4) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 16 Discharge to start after berthing

(1) Except with the prior written approval of the port management company, the master of a vessel shall cause the discharge or loading of the vessel to be started within a port as soon as practicable after the vessel has berthed and to be continued until completed.

(2) The port management company may give its approval under subregulation (1) subject to conditions.

(3) The master of a vessel to whom approval is given under subregulation (1) shall comply with all conditions imposed under subregulation (2) .

(4) The master who contravenes subregulation (3) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule.

### reg 17 Prior approval to come near wharf

(1) The master of a vessel shall not, except with the prior written approval of the port management company, may navigate the vessel within a port,—

(a) which is within a 100 metres of a wharf; or

(b) berth or un-berth the vessel at a wharf,

while a red pennant is flying from the flagstaff on the port office or while a red light is exhibited from the staff.

(2) The master who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 18 Obeying and executing orders by port management company

(1) The master of a vessel, and the master of any tug having the vessel in tow, shall obey and execute orders given by the port management company in relation to—

(a) the place where the vessel is to be anchored;

(b) the manner in which the vessel is to be anchored;

(c) the manner in which the vessel is to be brought alongside a wharf; or

(d) the manner in which the vessel is to leave a wharf.

(2) The master of a vessel who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 19 Fixing and removing buoys and buoy-ropes

(1) The master of a vessel when ordered by the port management company to do so shall—

(a) have buoys and buoy-ropes fixed to the vessel’s anchors sufficient to show the position of the anchor; and

(b) remove any such buoys and buoy-ropes.

(2) The master who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 20 Use of fenders

(1) The master of a vessel berthing alongside a port management company wharf shall use fenders of such description and in such manner as the port management company directs.

(2) The master who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 21 Gangways

(1) The master of a vessel lying alongside a wharf shall—

(a) fix and maintain safe and proper gangways from the vessel to the wharf; and

(b) comply with any directions given by the port management company in respect of the construction, position, removal, size, number and lighting of such gangways.

(2) The master who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 22 No embarking on or leaving from vessel in motion

(1) The master of a vessel shall not allow a person to embark on or leave from the vessel while the vessel is underway within a port.

(2) The master who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 23 Prior approval for berthing in tier

(1) The master of a vessel shall not allow the vessel to lie in tier from a berth alongside a wharf except with the prior written approval of the port management company.

(2) The master who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(3) The port management company may give its approval under subregulation (1) subject to conditions.

(4) The master of a vessel to whom approval is given under subregulation (1) , who fails to comply with all conditions imposed under subregulation (3) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 24 Watchman on board

(1) The master of a vessel in a port shall, at all times, keep a watchman on board the vessel, subject to subregulation (3) .

(2) The master who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(3) The port management company may exempt any vessel or any class of vessels from compliance with subregulation (1) .

(4) An exemption under subregulation (3) may be granted subject to conditions.

(5) If an exemption under subregulation (3) is granted subject to conditions imposed under subregulation (3) , a person shall not exercise the benefit of that exemption without complying with the conditions.

(6) A person who contravenes subregulation (5) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(7) An exemption under subregulation (3) and details of any conditions imposed under subregulation (4) shall be notified—

(a) in respect of a vessel, to the owner or master of the vessel; or

(b) in respect of a class of a vessel, by notice in the Gazette and in a newspaper published and circulating widely in Fiji.

### reg 25 Openings in vessels during hours of darkness

(1) The master of a vessel moored at a wharf shall ensure that during the hours of darkness every opening in the vessel used for loading or handling cargo, stores or fuel is—

(a) closed and secured, if not in use; or

(b) well and sufficiently lit and protected, if in use.

(2) The master who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 26 Prior approval for repairs to a vessel at the wharf

(1) The master of a vessel berthed at a wharf shall not carry out repairs to the vessel except with the prior written approval of the port management company.

(2) The port management company may give its approval under subregulation (1) subject to conditions.

(3) The master of a vessel to whom approval is given under subregulation (1) shall comply with all conditions imposed under subregulation (2) .

(4) The master who contravenes subregulation (1) or (3) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 27 Prior approval for engine test

(1) The master of a vessel moored to a wharf shall not allow any engine of the vessel to be tested except with the prior written approval of the port management company.

(2) The port management company may give its approval under subregulation (1) subject to conditions.

(3) The master of a vessel to whom approval is given under subregulation (1) shall comply with all conditions imposed under subregulation (2) .

(4) The master who contravenes subregulation (1) or (3) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 28 Use of passenger landing stage or steps

(1) The master of a vessel using any passenger landing stage or steps within a port shall not permit the vessel to remain at the landing stage or steps longer than the time required to—

(a) receive or land passengers; and

(b) load or unload baggage.

(2) The master who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(3) The master of a vessel at a passenger landing stage or steps shall not allow the landing or shipping of goods from or to the vessel except goods capable of being loaded or unloaded by hand.

(4) The master who contravenes subregulation (3) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 29 Prohibition against discharging from the vessel

(1) The master of a vessel lying alongside a wharf—

(a) shall not—

(i) wash down the decks of the vessel; or

(ii) discharge water from the vessel,

in a manner which may damage or cause damage to goods lying on the wharf; and

(b) shall close from use or screen all openings in the vessel out of which water or other fluid may be discharged onto the wharf.

(2) The master who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 30 Prior approval to go over or across deck of another vessel

(1) The master of a vessel lying alongside another vessel at a wharf shall not, except with the prior written approval of port management company, embark or ship, or disembark or unship goods or passengers over or across the decks of that other vessel.

(2) The master who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(3) If a vessel is lying between a wharf and another vessel, the master of the first mentioned vessel shall allow goods and passengers to pass over the decks to and from the vessel subject to such conditions as the port management company may impose.

(4) The master who contravenes subregulation (3) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 31 Use of silencers

(1) The master of a vessel shall not use any engine on the vessel unless the engine is fitted with a silencer or otherwise adapted to prevent excessive exhaust noise within a port.

(2) The master who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 32 Loud devices to be used sparingly

(1) The master of a vessel shall ensure that any steam-whistle, steam-horn, siren or other similar devices on the vessel is not used during the period starting at 7 o’clock in the afternoon and ending at 6 o’clock in the morning, of the next day except—

(a) so far as it may be necessary to do so for the safe navigation of the vessel; or

(b) to such extent as may be permitted by the port management company.

(2) The master who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(3) The master of a vessel in a port shall not sound any whistle, siren, bell or fog horn on the vessel except for the purpose of navigation.

(4) The master who contravenes subregulation (3) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 33 Explosives on board to be particularised

(1) The master of a vessel which has on board—

(a) gunpowder or other explosives; or

(b) kerosene, benzene or other inflammable oils or material,

shall give particulars of that cargo to the port management company 48 hours before the arrival of the vessel at a port.

(2) The master who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 34 Lights to be exhibited during hours of darkness

(1) The master of a vessel lying alongside another vessel at a wharf during hours of darkness shall display such lights on the vessel as the port management company may determine.

(2) The master who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 35 Requirement for diving in progress

(1) The master of a vessel in a port which divers are operating from shall, whilst diving is in progress, exhibit on the vessel in a conspicuous manner—

(a) International Code Flag ‘A’; or

(b) the lights and signals required by the International Regulations for Preventing Collision at Sea 1972, as from time to time amended.

(2) The master of a vessel in a port shall take such measures as are necessary to avoid interference with a vessel exhibiting the recognition signals in subregulation (1)(a) or (b) .

(3) The master who contravenes subregulation (2) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 36 Obligation to report accident

(1) If, in a port—

(a) damage is caused to a vessel, wharf or other property; or

(b) a vessel collides, grounds or sinks,

the master of the vessel or owner of the property shall, as soon as possible but otherwise within 48 hours, report the incident verbally or in writing to the port management company.

(2) The master who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(3) When requested to do so by the port management company, the master of the vessel or the owner of the property shall in accordance with subregulation (1) give the port management company a written report of the accident.

(4) The master of a vessel or owner of the property who contravenes subregulation (3) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 37 Prior authority to careen or haul vessel

(1) The master of a vessel shall not careen or haul the vessel on shore within a port unless authorised to do so by the port management company.

(2) The master who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule of $500.

(3) The master of a vessel careened or hauled ashore contrary to subregulation (1) shall remove the vessel when requested to do so by the port management company.

(4) The master who contravenes subregulation (3) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(5) The master of a vessel does not commit an offence under this regulation if the vessel was careened or hauled ashore in an emergency.

### reg 38 Requirements when servicing the vessel

(1) The master of a vessel in a port shall ensure that—

(a) at the time of the unmooring of the vessel; or

(b) such later time as the port management company may allow,

all portable appliances, materials and area used and rubbish from the servicing of the vessel, are cleaned and removed, as the case may be.

(2) The master who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(3) If the master of a vessel fails to comply with subregulation (1) the port management company may remove the appliances, materials or rubbish, as the case may be, and clean the area.

(4) Any costs incurred by the port management company in removing appliances, material or rubbish and in cleaning under subregulation (3) shall be a debt due to the port management company by the master of the vessel and may be recovered in court.

### reg 39 Tug assistance

(1) The master of a vessel shall ensure that tug assistance is used while berthing or un-berthing a vessel within a port, unless exempted by the port management company.

(2) The master who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

## PART 3 NAVIGATION HINDERANCE, MOORING AND ANCHORING [regs 40-44]

### reg 40 Notification and removal of obstruction

(1) If a vessel is sunk, stranded or abandoned within a port or where an obstruction hinders the navigation or use of the waters of a port or a fly approach to a port—

(a) the master of the vessel;

(b) the person who caused the vessel to be placed in that position; or

(c) the owner of the property which is causing the obstruction,

shall notify the port management company of the position of the vessel or the obstruction and shall exhibit on or near the vessel or the obstruction the signals and lights set out under the International Collision Regulations.

(2) The master who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(3) The following shall immediately take such steps as may be necessary to have the vessel or the obstruction removed—

(a) the master of the vessel;

(b) the person who caused the vessel to be placed in that position; or

(c) the owner of the property which is causing the obstruction referred to in subregulation (1) .

(4) The master who contravenes subregulation (3) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(5) If the person mentioned in subregulation (3) fails to comply with subregulation (3) , the port management company may cause the obstruction to be removed and the expense of so doing shall be a debt due to the port management company by such person, which may be recovered in court.

(6) For the purpose of this regulation, master when used in relation to a vessel means the master who was in charge of the vessel when the vessel was sunk, stranded or abandoned.

### reg 41 Mooring

(1) The master of a vessel which is not under way within a port shall cause the vessel to be kept properly and well moored to the satisfaction of the port management company.

(2) The master who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(3) When cyclonic weather conditions are forecasted, the port management company may direct the master of a vessel within a port to take special precautions and to maintain those precautions until the port management company informs the master that the need to take special precautions no longer exists.

(4) The master of a vessel who fails to comply with any direction given under subregulation (3) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(5) The master of a vessel shall not make fast any rope from the vessel or moor the vessel to a wharf or any part of the wharf or to any erection on a wharf other than to the bollards provided for that purpose.

(6) The master who contravenes subregulation (6) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(7) The port management company may direct the master of a vessel to moor or berth the vessel in tiers.

(8) The master of a vessel who fails to comply with a direction given to him or her under subregulation (8) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(9) The master of a vessel shall, when directed to do so by the port management company, give reasonable access for persons and goods across and over the decks of the vessel to and from the wharf and any other vessel moored or berthed in tier to the vessel.

(10) The master who contravenes subregulation (10) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 42 Mooring services

If the port management company gives assistance in mooring or unmooring a vessel within a port, the assistance is deemed to have been given subject to the conditions that—

(a) the vessel is moored or unmoored by the master and the crew, the port management company personnel being in attendance to render assistance, under instructions issued from the vessel, in taking ashore making fast or casting off hawsers and ropes; and

(b) the port management company has accepted no responsibility in carrying out the service and the port management company is not responsible for any loss or damage caused or attributed to any act or omission of its personnel.

### reg 43 Engagement of persons for mooring purposes

(1) If mooring services in respect of a vessel at a port are not given by the port management company, the master of the vessel shall engage—

(a) such number of persons to handle lines; and

(b) such tugs and boats for running lines,

as may be directed by the port management company.

(2) The master who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 44 Vessel to be anchored properly

(1) The master of a vessel anchoring in a port shall, to the satisfaction of the port management company, anchor the vessel, and keep the vessel anchored, properly and safely.

(2) The master who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(3) The port management company may designate by notice in the Gazette an area within a port where a vessel or a class of vessels shall or shall not anchor.

(4) The port management company may designate by notice in the Gazette an area within a port where a vessel or a class of vessels may anchor.

(5) The master of a vessel shall not anchor a vessel in a port in breach of the terms of a notice published in accordance with subregulation (3) and (4) .

(6) The master who anchors a vessel contrary to subregulation (3) or (4) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(7) The master of a vessel at anchor shall not permit—

(a) a boat to ride astern of or be attached to the vessel at a distance not greater than 6 metres from the vessel; or

(b) a lighter or other floating object to ride astern of or be fastened to the vessel which is likely to obstruct or damage any other vessel.

(8) The master who contravenes subregulation (7) commits an offence and is liable on conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(9) The master of a vessel at anchor is liable to pay anchorage fees levied pursuant to section 18 of the Act.

## PART 4 RESPONSIBILITY FOR DAMAGE TO PORT MANAGEMENT COMPANY’S PROPERTY [regs 45-46]

### reg 45 Damage caused by vessels

If a vessel damages any property of the port management company, the cost of repairing or making good that damage by the port management company shall be a debt due from the owner of the vessel to the port management company and may be recovered in court.

### reg 46 Damage caused by storage of goods

The cost of repairing damage caused to property belonging to the port management company by the storage of goods, shall be a debt due to the port management company from the owner of the goods, and may be recovered in court.

## PART 5 BERTHING, OVERSEAS VESSELS, MANIFESTS AND CARGOES [regs 47-54]

### reg 47 Priority

(1) If masters of 2 or more vessels wish to use a wharf at the same time, the port management company has the power to decide the order of priority.

(2) Without prejudice to subregulation (3) , the order of priority is—

(a) passenger vessels (being foreign registered vessels designed and used primarily for the carriage of passengers for reward);

(b) roll on/roll off vessels;

(c) container vessels;

(d) other cargo vessels (not liquid tankers or dry bulk vessels);

(e) liquid bulk tankers;

(f) dry bulk tankers;

(g) warships or government vessels of any flag state; and

(h) other vessels.

(3) If the port management company considers it appropriate, it may vary the order of priority having regard to—

(a) safety to the port and vessel involved;

(b) the need to handle dangerous or hazardous cargoes in a safe and fast manner;

(c) efficiency of the port;

(d) efficient operation of the port;

(e) efficient operation of all or any vessel involved; and

(f) any medical reasons relating to any person on board a vessel.

(4) If 2 vessels are of equal priority, the Port Management Company may give priority to the vessel already berthed.

(5) If 2 vessels of equal priority are yet to berth, the Port Management Company may give priority to the vessel that arrived in the stream first.

### reg 48 Supply of information prior to arrival at a port

(1) If a vessel is to arrive at a port from a place outside Fiji, the owner of the vessel shall, at least 48 hours before the expected arrival time of the vessel, give the following information to the port management company—

(a) for vessels—

(i) its quarantine status;

(ii) its overall length, breadth and arrival draught;

(iii) any operational defects apparent at the time of giving the information;

(iv) any restrictions to navigation or berthing imposed on a vessel by reason of its construction;

(v) Gross Registered Tonnage/Net Registered Tonnage/Displacement Tonnage (where there are 2 tonnages the higher tonnage shall be declared);

(vi) the IMO number of the vessel;

(vii) ISPS requirement.

(b) for cargo on vessels—

(i) the amount of cargo and the number of any cargo containers for discharge or trans-shipment; and

(ii) details of any single cargo items, other than cargo containers, in excess of 25 tonnes for discharge.

(c) for requirements of vessels—

(i) its bunkers;

(ii) its fresh water supply;

(iii) any assistance it requires in berthing;

(iv) pilotage and tug requirements;

(v) other shore services normally supplied by the port management company; and

(vi) any other shore services not provided by the port management company but by a third party with the port management company’s approval.

(2) The owner of a vessel who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 49 Cargo manifests — arrivals

(1) If a vessel is to arrive at a port to discharge or trans-ship cargo, the owner of the vessel shall, unless prior alternative arrangements have been made with the port management company, at least 24 hours before the expected arrival time of the vessel, give to the port management company a true, complete and legible copy of the manifest of the vessel in respect of all cargo intended to be discharged or trans-shipped in the port.

(2) The owner of a vessel who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 50 Cargo manifests — departures

(1) The master, owner or agent of a vessel which is about to depart from a port shall as soon as practicable before the vessel departs, unless agreed by the port management company, give to the port management company, a true, legible and complete copy of the manifest of the vessel in respect of all cargo loaded in the port.

(2) The master, owner or agent of a vessel who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 51 Copy of manifests

(1) A copy of the manifest referred to in regulation 48 or 49 shall be—

(a) in English;

(b) certified by the person giving it to be true and complete; and

(c) given to the port management company at the relevant port.

(2) The master, owner or agent of a vessel who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 52 Cargo for other ports

(1) No person shall land cargo at a wharf in a port, other than goods manifested for landing or trans-shipment at that port, except with the approval of the port management company.

(2) A person who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 53 Requirement for shipment of goods

(1) A person shall not deliver to a port for shipment goods enclosed in outside packaging unless the packaging is of sound material and of enough strength and durability to hold the goods without leakage or spilling during handling and transportation.

(2) A person shall not deliver to a port for shipment a package, container or object unless it is plainly and durably marked and denoting its gross weight.

(3) If a person delivers goods to a port in breach of subregulation (1) or (2) the port management company may direct the person delivering the goods or the owner of the goods to remove the goods from the port.

(4) A person or owner of the goods to whom a direction is given under subregulation (3) shall comply with that direction.

(5) A person or owner of the goods, who contravenes subregulation (1) , (2) or (4) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 54 Overloaded cargo containers

(1) If a person delivers a cargo container containing cargo to a wharf and the gross weight exceeds the maximum gross weight shown on the safety approval plate attached to the container, the port management company may direct that person to adjust the contents of the container so that the gross weight of that cargo and the container does not exceed the maximum gross weight shown on the safety approval plate.

(2) A person to whom a direction is given under subregulation (1) shall comply with that direction.

(3) A person who contravenes subregulation (2) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

## PART 6 OFFENCES [regs 55-75]

### reg 55 Obstruction

(1) A person shall not, unless with the prior written approval of the port management company, anchor or berth a vessel—

(a) in a fairway of a channel within a port;

(b) so as to obstruct the approach to a wharf; or

(c) in any other area within a port.

(2) A person who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(3) The port management company may give its approval under subregulation (1) subject to conditions.

(4) A person to whom approval is given under subregulation (1) who fails to comply with a condition imposed under subregulation (3) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 56 Use of raft of logs or timber

(1) A person shall not, except with the prior written approval of the port management company, tow, navigate, anchor or moor within a port a raft of logs or timber.

(2) A person who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(3) The port management company may give its approval under subregulation (1) subject to conditions.

(4) A person to whom approval is given under subregulation (1) who fails to comply with a condition imposed under subregulation (3) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 57 Use of vessels whilst intoxicated

(1) A person shall not—

(a) have control of; or

(b) operate or attempt to operate,

a vessel within a port while the person is under the influence of intoxicating liquor or drugs.

(2) A person who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 58 Depositing bulk materials

(1) A person shall not deposit bulk materials on any port management wharf except with the prior written approval of the port management company.

(2) A person who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(3) The port management company may give its approval under subregulation (1) subject to conditions.

(4) A person to whom approval is given under subregulation (1) shall comply with all conditions imposed under subregulation (3) .

(5) A person who contravenes subregulation (4) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 59 Not removing cargo handling equipment

(1) A person using equipment not belonging to the port management company in connection with the loading or unloading of a vessel at a port management company wharf shall remove the equipment from the wharf as soon as the loading or unloading of the vessel is complete.

(2) Where the user of the equipment fails to comply with subregulation (1) , the port management company may direct the user or owner of the equipment to remove the equipment from the wharf immediately or within a time specified by the port management company.

(3) A person shall comply with a direction given to him or her under subregulation (2) .

(4) A person who contravenes subregulation (3) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(5) This regulation does not apply to a person using or leaving equipment on a wharf under a licence granted under Part 7 of the Act.

### reg 60 Non cooperation in an emergency

(1) If an emergency happens within a port, the port management company may direct a person on board a vessel at hand (“first vessel”) to render such assistance and support, as are reasonable, for the protection of other neighbouring vessels and provided the assistance and support will not endanger the first vessel or the person.

(2) A person who fails to comply with a direction given to him or her under subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(3) It is a defence for an offence under this regulation that the directive given under subsection (1) was unreasonable in the circumstances.

### reg 61 Child shall be accompanied by an adult

(1) A person having the care or custody of a child shall not allow the child to go on, or remain on a wharf unless accompanied by an adult.

(2) A person who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 62 Loitering at the wharf

(1) A person shall not enter or remain on any wharf except for the purposes of legitimate business on the wharf or as approved by the port management company.

(2) The port management company may refuse to allow a person without any legitimate business on the wharf to enter or remain on the wharf.

(3) A person who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 63 Explosives prohibited

(1) No person shall, except with the prior written approval of the port management company, discharge a firearm, flare, rocket or other explosive on a vessel in a port unless the vessel is in distress or an emergency exists on the vessel.

(2) A person who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 64 Unauthorised entry in enclosed areas

(1) The port management company may designate any area of the port as an enclosed area (by means of a wall, fence, channel or other means of enclosure) where no person is allowed to enter except—

(a) officers and employees of the port management company;

(b) other persons approved in writing by authorised officers or employees of the port management company.

(2) A person who enters an enclosed area without authorisation commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 65 Entering or leaving enclosed areas

(1) No person shall enter or leave an enclosed area referred to in regulation 63 except—

(a) by embarking on to or disembarking from a vessel; or

(b) by using an entrance or exit appointed by the port management company for that purpose.

(2) A person who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 66 Interference with gate, grill or door

(1) No person shall, without reasonable excuse, in any place within a port—

(a) open;

(b) close;

(c) prevent or deny entry to; or

(d) interfere with,

any gate, grill or door which has been closed, locked or opened by the port management company or by a person acting with the authority of the port management company.

(2) A person who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 67 Prohibition on use of naked lights

(1) A person shall not use a naked light in a shed belonging to the port management company.

(2) A person who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 68 Prohibition on smoking within designated non-smoking areas

(1) The port management company may designate any area within a port where smoking is prohibited.

(2) The area designated under subregulation (1) shall be properly indicated by notice erected or posted within the vicinity of the designated area.

(3) A person who smokes in an area designated under subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 69 Not reporting fallen goods

(1) If goods fall into waters at or near a wharf or within a port, the person in-charge of the goods shall immediately give written notice of the incident to the port management company.

(2) The person-in-charge of the goods who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(3) If the goods referred to in subregulation (1) are not immediately recovered by the person referred to in subregulation (1) to the satisfaction of the port management company, it may—

(a) direct the person to recover the goods in such manner as is appropriate; or

(b) if the person fails to comply with such direction, take such action as it considers necessary to recover the goods.

(4) Any costs incurred under subregulation (3)(b) is deemed to be a debt due to the port management company from the owner of the goods and may be recovered by the port management company in court.

### reg 70 Not reporting damage

(1) Subject to subregulation (2) , a person who damages, accidentally or otherwise, property belonging to the port management company within a port shall send to the port management company—

(a) immediately after the damage occurs, a verbal report by telephone or other means; and

(b) within 48 hours of the occurrence of the damage, a detailed written report of the damage unless the port management company decides that its record of verbal report has sufficient detail.

(2) If it is unlikely for a detailed written report to be prepared under subregulation (1)(b) due to the nature and extent of the damage, the person may request the port management company to specify a time in which a full report shall be submitted.

(3) A person who contravenes subregulation (1) or (2) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 71 Public demonstrations

(1) No person shall—

(a) deliver a public address or hold a public meeting;

(b) hold or take part in a procession, parade or display,

within a port except with prior written approval of the port management company.

(2) A person who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(3) The port management company may give its approval under subregulation (1) subject to conditions.

(4) A person to whom approval has been given under subregulation (1) shall comply with all conditions given under subregulation (3) .

### reg 72 Vandalising property

(1) A person shall not write, draw upon or mark with chalk or any other substance, carve, cut, break, disfigure, damage or destroy property belonging to the port management company.

(2) A person who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 73 Improper behaviour

(1) A person shall not—

(a) while he or she is in a port, behave in a manner calculated, or likely to cause a disturbance or other breach of the peace;

(b) while he or she is in a port, interfere with the comfort of another person using the port; or

(c) enter a port whilst intoxicated and under the influence of alcohol or drugs.

(2) A person who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 74 Bringing liquor into port

(1) A person shall not bring intoxicating liquor into a port except with the prior written approval of the port management company, unless the person brings it into the port in the course of—

(a) handling the liquor as cargo of a vessel;

(b) taking the liquor, otherwise than as cargo, into a vessel;

(c) taking the liquor, otherwise than as cargo, from a vessel to a place outside the port.

(2) A person who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(3) The port management company may give its approval under subregulation (1) subject to conditions.

(4) A person to whom approval is given under subregulation (1) shall comply with any conditions imposed under subregulation (3) .

(5) Nothing in subregulation (1) shall be taken as exempting any person from obtaining Custom’s approval.

### reg 75 Unauthorised selling

(1) A person shall not except with the prior written approval of the port management company and approval of Customs—

(a) sell, offer or expose for sale any goods (including books, newspapers, magazines or other periodicals);

(b) hold a sale by auction; or

(c) carry on a retail trade,

in a port or any other property under the control of the port management company.

(2) A person who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

## PART 7 POWERS OF THE PORT MANAGEMENT COMPANY [regs 76-87]

### reg 76 Determine weight of goods

(1) The port management company may specify the maximum weight that may be imposed on the floor, roadway or platform of a port management company wharf.

(2) If the port management company has specified a maximum weight under subregulation (1) , it shall place a notice board in a conspicuous position specifying the nature and extent of the limitation and the area subject to that limitation.

(3) A person who fails to comply with the terms of a notice placed under subregulation (2) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 77 Designate free passage areas

(1) The port management company may designate any cargo handling area of a port to be a free passage way.

(2) If the port management company has designated an area under subregulation (1) , it shall place a notice board or sign in a conspicuous position specifying the area to be a free passage area.

(3) A notice board placed under subregulation (2) must specify the extent of the free passage area and the limitation in respect of obstruction in that area.

(4) A person who fails to comply with terms of a notice placed in accordance with subregulation (2) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(5) If anything is obstructing a free passage way designated under subregulation (2) , the port management company may remove the obstruction, and the cost of doing so shall be a debt due to the port management company by the owner of the obstruction which may be recovered in court.

### reg 78 Direct vessels to be moved

(1) The master of a vessel which is in a port shall, when directed by a port management company—

(a) moor the vessel;

(b) unmoor the vessel;

(c) place the vessel; or

(d) remove the vessel from its berth,

in accordance with the directions given by the port management company.

(2) The master of a vessel who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(3) The port management company may, without being liable for any damage or other claim arising from it, cause the vessel to be moored, unmoored, placed or removed in accordance with the directions if after reasonable inquiry—

(a) no person is available to whom directions can be given; or

(b) insufficient persons are on board a vessel competent to attend to the directions.

(4) Any costs incurred by the port management company under subregulation (3) shall be a debt due to the port management company by the owner of the vessel which may be recovered in court.

### reg 79 Order vessels to be moved

(1) The company may, if it is necessary to do so for the general accommodation of shipping within a port, order the master of a vessel which is at berth alongside a wharf or at anchor to move the vessel to another place in the port or to another berth.

(2) The master who fails to comply with an order given under subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 80 Directions on unloading of certain goods

(1) If the port management company is satisfied that there is on board a vessel in port goods to be unloaded which—

(a) are likely to create a nuisance;

(b) are dangerous; or

(c) are in an objectionable condition,

the company may give directions to the owner of the vessel as to the manner of unloading those goods.

(2) The directions given under subregulation (1) may contain such conditions and requirements as the port management company considers necessary to mitigate the nuisance, or the dangerous or objectionable nature of the goods.

(3) The master of a vessel who fails to comply with any directions under subregulation (1) or conditions and requirements under subregulation (2) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 81 Directions on moving and placing of goods

(1) If the port management company is satisfied that—

(a) goods are or are about to be placed in the port so that space in the port is not being properly used; or

(b) goods by reason of breaking bulk, spilling, leaking or for any other cause are likely to damage the port or create a nuisance or hinder work in the port,

the company may direct the person handling those goods—

(c) not to-place the goods except in accordance with its instructions; or

(d) to remove them in a manner, to a place and within a time specified by it.

(2) A person shall comply with a direction given to him or her by the port management company in accordance with subregulation (1) .

(3) A person who contravenes subregulation (2) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(4) If a person fails to carry out a direction given to him or her under subregulation (1) the port management company may take such action to have the goods removed as it may consider necessary to ensure that the goods do not hinder the operation of the port.

(5) Any costs incurred by the port management company in moving goods under subregulation (4) shall be deemed to be a debt from the owner of the goods and may be recovered in court.

(6) Goods shall not be handled under this regulation contrary to any direction by Customs.

### reg 82 Direct removal of goods

(1) If the port management company is satisfied that goods on a wharf are impeding the business of the wharf, the company may direct the following to remove goods from the wharf within a time specified by it—

(a) in the case of goods being inward cargo, the owner of the goods or the owner of the vessel from which the goods were discharged or both of them; and

(b) in the case of goods being outward cargo, the owner of the vessel in which the goods are intended to be shipped or the owner of the goods or both of them.

(2) A person who fails to comply with a direction given to him or her by the port management company in accordance with subregulation (j) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(3) If a person fails to carry out a direction given to him or her in accordance with subregulation (1) the port management company may take such action as it considers necessary to move the goods to ensure that they do not impede the business of the wharf.

(4) Any costs incurred by the port management company in moving goods under subregulation (4) shall be a debt due to the port management company from the person to whom the direction was given and may be recovered in court.

### reg 83 Removal of persons

(1) The port management company may direct a person—

(a) not to enter a wharf;

(b) to remove himself or herself to another part of a wharf; or

(c) to leave a wharf.

(2) A person shall comply with a direction given to him or her by the port management company under subregulation (1) .

(3) A person who contravenes subregulation (2) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(4) If a person fails to comply with a direction given to him or her under subregulation (1) such force may be used as is reasonably necessary to remove that person.

### reg 84 Closing of wharf

(1) The port management company may, after notifying port users who may be affected by the closure, temporarily close a wharf or any part of a wharf.

(2) However, the port management company may, due to unforeseen circumstance or urgency, temporarily close a wharf or part of a wharf without notifying port users who may be affected by the closure.

(3) It is sufficient to notify port users under subregulation (1) if the notice is published in a newspaper published and having wide circulation in Fiji.

(4) No person shall enter any area of a wharf closed under subregulation (1) or (2) except with the prior approval of the port management company.

(5) A person who contravenes subregulation (4) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 85 Demand production of certificate

(1) The port management company may require the master of a vessel which is not registered in Fiji to produce to the company or its authorised officer within the time specified by it—

(a) the certificate of registry of the vessel; and

(b) any other certificate relating to the vessel which the master is required by law or international convention to produce to a port authority.

(2) The master of a vessel who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 86 Removal of inward cargo

(1) The port management company may, with the prior written approval of Customs, by written notice, require all goods unloaded or about to be unloaded from a vessel to be removed from a port within a time specified in the notice.

(2) A notice (“notice”) under subregulation (1) shall—

(a) be in writing;

(b) be applicable to all goods unloaded or about to be unloaded from a vessel;

(c) state the name the vessel from which the goods have been unloaded or are about to be unloaded; and

(d) be posted at the wharf revenue office.

(3) The notice is taken to have been given on the date it is posted under subregulation (2)(d) —

(a) to the master of the vessel from which the goods have been unloaded or are about to be unloaded; and

(b) to the owner of the goods.

(4) A person to whom a notice is taken to have been given under subregulation (3) shall comply with the notice.

(5) A person who contravenes subregulation (2) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(6) A person who does not comply with subregulation (4) is deemed to have committed a separate offence under that subregulation for every 24 hours in which the person fails to comply with that subregulation.

### reg 87 Prior approval to store goods

(1) No person shall store goods within a port except with the prior written approval of the port management company.

(2) A person who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(3) The port management company may remove any goods stored contrary to subregulation (1) .

(4) Any costs incurred under subregulation (3) shall be a debt due to the port management company from the owner of the goods which may be recovered by the port management company in court.

## PART 8 ROAD TRAFFIC [regs 88-91]

### reg 88 Traffic inspectors

The port management company may, by notice in the Gazette, appoint a person to be a traffic inspector.

### reg 89 Authorisation from port management company

(1) A traffic inspector shall when exercising powers under this regulation carry a written authorisation in a form approved by the port management company.

(2) A traffic inspector shall produce the authorisation referred to in subregulation (1) when requested to do so.

### reg 90 Powers of traffic inspectors

(1) A traffic inspector may—

(a) direct a person driving a vehicle within a port for the purpose of—

(i) reducing the emission of noise or exhaust from the vehicle;

(ii) regulating the parking or movement of the vehicle; or

(iii) maintaining an orderly flow of traffic within the port;

(b) direct a person to remove from a port a vehicle that is making excessive noise or is emitting excessive exhaust gases; or

(c) remove or cause to be removed a vehicle—

(i) apparently abandoned within a port; or

(ii) interfering with parking and movement of vehicles or the flow of traffic within a port.

(2) If a vehicle is removed under subregulation (1)(c) —

(a) the port management company shall not be liable for any loss or damage suffered by the owner of the vehicle or any other person, as a consequence of the removal; and

(b) any costs incurred by the port management company in removing, storing or disposing of the vehicle shall be a debt due to the port management company from the owner of the vehicle which may be recovered in court.

(3) A person who fails to comply with a direction given to him or her by a traffic inspector acting in accordance with subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(4) A person who hinders or obstructs a traffic inspector exercising his or her power under this regulation commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 91 Designated areas

(1) The port management company may designate a roadway or any other area within a port to be—

(a) a parking area; or

(b) a limited speed area.

(2) If a roadway or other area is designated in accordance with subregulation (1) the port management company shall erect in a conspicuous place near or alongside that roadway or area a sign giving details of the designation and the limitations imposed on that roadway or area.

(3) A person shall not, within a port—

(a) drive a vehicle other than on a roadway;

(b) drive a vehicle negligently, recklessly or at a speed or in a manner dangerous to other persons;

(c) park or stand a vehicle other than on a roadway or in a parking area;

(d) contravene or fail to comply with a requirement indicated on a traffic sign;

(e) park or stand a vehicle in a parking area so as to obstruct the passage of other vehicles; or

(f) park or stand a vehicle on a roadway within a parking area other than on the left hand side of the roadway unless the traffic sign relating to that parking area;

(i) stipulates angle parking; or

(ii) allows parking on the right hand side of the roadway.

(4) A person who contravenes any provision of subregulation (3) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(5) No vehicle shall be left parked within a port for a continuous period in excess of 24 hours except with the prior written approval of the port management company.

(6) The port management company may remove any vehicle that is not parked in accordance with these Regulations and the cost to the port management company of removing the vehicle shall be a debt due from the owner of the vehicle to the port management company and may be recovered by the port management company in court.

(7) The port management company shall not be responsible for any damage howsoever caused to any vehicle within a port, whether lawfully within a port or not, or whether lawfully parked or not, nor for any damage caused by the port management company or its agents for the removal of any vehicle within the port.

(8) These Regulations shall not apply to vehicles or drivers of vehicles—

(a) whilst they are being used directly for or in connection with the maintenance and construction of roads or other services at or to the port management company;

(b) whilst conveying police officers on urgent police duty;

(c) whilst conveying Fire Brigade personnel in response to a fire alarm;

(d) being ambulances, answering an urgent call or proceeding to hospital with any sick or injured person requiring emergency treatment;

(e) being vehicles used in the service of the port management company or for the purpose of the port, when such vehicles are being used during an emergency at the port.

## PART 9 PORT USERS AND PRIVATE FACILITIES [regs 92-100]

### reg 92 Application for a licence

(1) A person who wishes to be licensed under section 23 of the Act to conduct commercial operation within a port shall apply in a form approved by the port management company and accompanied by the application fee of $100.

(2) The annual licence fee for a licence to operate a commercial operation is to be determined under section 23(6)(b) of the Act by the Board of Directors.

(3) A person who operates or permits to operate a commercial operation in a port without a licence commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(4) An application form sent to the port management company under subregulation (1) must be accompanied by such documents and other evidence as may be specified in the application form or as further required by the ports management company.

### reg 93 Conditions of licence

For the purposes of section 23(2) of the Act, the conditions to which a licence to conduct a commercial operation may include conditions relating to—

(a) the protection and maintenance of the property and of the port’s facilities;

(b) the safety and well-being of passengers and other persons using the port’s facilities;

(c) the purposes for which and the times at which the vessel, machinery or other equipment to which the licence relates may be used or operated;

(d) the payment of a consideration;

(e) the responsibilities and liabilities of the licensed port user;

(f) the identification and the conduct of the holder of the whilst engaged in the commercial operation;

(g) the determination of the charges to be made by the licensed port user for the provision of his or her services;

(h) the transferability or otherwise of the licence; and

(i) the period of validity of the licence and the manner and the circumstances in which it may be suspended, cancelled or renewed.

### reg 94 Non-compliance with provisions of licence

(1) A licensed port user shall comply with the conditions of the licence.

(2) The licensed port user who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 95 Advertisements

(1) No person shall exhibit or place a placard, notice or advertisement on a property within a port (including a wall, fence or gate enclosing a port) except with the prior written approval of the port management company.

(2) A person who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(3) The company may remove any exhibit, placard, notice or advertisement placed contrary to subregulation (1) .

### reg 96 Prior approval required for structures

(1) No person shall place or alter a structure on, under or over land (whether or not covered by water) within a port except with the prior written approval of the port management company.

(2) The approval under subregulation (1) may be given subject to conditions, and includes—

(a) the removal or modification of the structure upon being given notice by the port management company to do so;

(b) the payment of a consideration;

(c) the lodgement with the port management company of plans and specifications for the structure;

(d) the method and manner of construction of the structure; and

(e) the times and manner of the use of the structure.

(3) A person to whom approval is given under subregulation (1) shall comply with any conditions imposed under subregulation (2) .

(4) A person who contravenes subregulation (3) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(5) If a person fails to remove or modify a structure referred to in subregulation (1) when required to do so by the port management company, the port management company may remove or modify the structure.

(6) The cost of removing or modifying the structure under subregulation (5) shall be a debt due to the port management company from the owner of the structure and the cost including administration and professional costs incurred may be recoverable in court.

### reg 97 Owner of a structure prior to operation of regulations

(1) If before these Regulations came into operation, a person was the owner of a structure on, under or over land within a port, the person shall, upon receiving notice from the port management company to do so, apply for approval to retain that structure in that position.

(2) Regulation 95(2) applies to approval given under subregulation (1) .

(3) A person who fails to comply with a notice under subregulation (1) shall remove the structure.

(4) If the port management company refuses to give its approval under subregulation (1) , the owner of the structure shall remove the structure.

(5) Regulation 95(4) applies if the owner of a structure fails to remove it in accordance with subregulation (3) or (4) .

### reg 98 Annual fee

An annual fee charged by the port management company under section 18 of the Act shall be payable to the port management company for private buoys, moorings and permanent anchors.

### reg 99 Inspection of port facilities

(1) The port management company may inspect any port facility within a port to ensure that it is adequate and efficient for its purposes and that it is being properly maintained.

(2) The owner of a port facility who fails to allow the port management company to inspect the port facility commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(3) If as a result of an inspection of a port facility under subregulation (1) the port management company is satisfied that—

(a) the facility is not properly maintained;

(b) adequate or efficient life saving or fire prevention appliances and equipment are not provided; or

(c) safe working practices are not being used,

the company may direct the owner of the facility to take such action as may be necessary to restore the facility to a state in which it is adequate and efficient for its purposes.

(4) The owner of a port facility to whom a direction has been issued under subregulation (3) shall not use or operate the facility until the port management company has inspected the facility and has certified that it has no objection to its use.

(5) Nothing in this regulation applies to a marine navigational aid owned or maintained by the Government.

### reg 100 Soil investigations

(1) No person shall undertake any soil investigation within a port except with the prior written approval of port management company.

(2) An approval under subregulation (1) may be given subject to conditions, including conditions involving—

(a) provisions of a statement relating to the purpose of the investigations;

(b) restrictions on timing and use of plant or boats;

(c) lodgement of a copy of the investigation report with the port management company.

(3) A person who—

(a) undertakes soil investigations without approval under subregulation (1) ; or

(b) breaches any condition imposed under subregulation (2) ,

commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

## PART 10 CONTROL OF POLLUTION [regs 101-112]

### reg 101 Substance pollution

(1) The master of a vessel shall ensure that—

(a) oil or oily mixtures;

(b) noxious liquid substances; and

(c) harmful substances,

are not discharged from the vessel into the waters of a port.

(2) The master of a vessel who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 102 Sewage pollution

(1) The master of a vessel shall ensure that no sewage is discharged from the vessel into the waters of a port unless the vessel is equipped with an approved sewage treatment plant and the conditions set out in Annex 4 regulation 8(1)(b) of the Convention are satisfied.

(2) The master of a vessel who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(3) The master of a vessel which holds a current International Sewage Pollution Prevention Certificate (1973) in accordance with the Convention but is not equipped with a sewage treatment unit shall ensure that no sewage is discharged from the vessel except with, the prior written approval of the port management company—

(a) to port reception facilities; or

(b) if port facilities are not available, into the waters of a port with, and in accordance with, the prior written approval of the port management company.

(4) The master of a vessel who contravenes subregulation (3) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(5) The master of any vessel which is subject to the Convention but is not required to possess an International Sewage Pollution Prevention Certificate (1973) shall ensure that no sewage is discharged from the vessel into the waters of a port or an approach to a port except with, and in accordance with, the approval of the port management company.

(6) The master of a vessel who contravenes subregulation (5) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 103 Garbage

(1) The master of a vessel shall ensure that no garbage is discharged from the vessel, whether or not through a comminutor or grinder, into the waters of a port.

(2) The master of a vessel who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 104 Ballast water

(1) The master of a vessel shall ensure that ballast water discharged from the vessel into the waters of a port is not contaminated.

(2) The master of a vessel who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 105 Bathroom and laundry wastes

(1) The master of a vessel shall ensure that any oil mixture, galley, bathroom and laundry wastes discharged from the vessel into the waters of a port do not come within the definition of oil, oily mixture, noxious liquid substances or sewage.

(2) The master of a vessel who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 106 Contaminated washings

(1) Subject to subregulation (3) , the master of a vessel shall ensure that contaminated tank washings or cargo washings are not discharged from the vessel into the waters of a port.

(2) The master of a vessel who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(3) The master of a dry bulk cargo vessel may, with the prior written approval of the port management company, discharge from the vessel effluent resulting from a formal wash down of its deck or hold if reasonable efforts have first been made to remove spilled cargo from the deck or the hold.

(4) The master of a vessel shall not allow bilge matter containing oil to be discharged from the vessel into the waters of a port.

(5) The master of a vessel who contravenes subregulation (4) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 107 Incident reporting

(1) The master of a vessel involved in an incident shall send to the port management company—

(a) immediately after the accident, a verbal report by telephone or other means; and

(b) within 48 hours of the occurrence of the accident, a detailed written report of the accident.

(2) If it is unlikely for a detailed written report to be prepared under subregulation (1)(b) due to the nature and extent of the accident, the person may, request the port management company to specify a time in which a full report shall be submitted.

(3) The master of a vessel who contravenes subregulation (1) or (2) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(4) If solid ballast, ashes or any undesirable substance (“matter”) is put into or falls into the waters of a port from a vessel, the master of the vessel shall—

(a) immediately advise the port management company of the incident; and

(b) attempt to remove such matter and dispose of it to the satisfaction of the port management company.

(5) The master of a vessel who contravenes subregulation (3) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(6) If a matter is not removed under subregulation (3)(b) within such time as the port management company considers reasonable, the company may remove and dispose of such matter.

(7) Any costs incurred under subregulation (5) shall be a debt due to the port management company from the master, recoverable in court.

### reg 108 Discharge of refuse

(1) The master of a vessel shall ensure that no refuse of any kind is discharged overboard from the vessel or the scuppers of the vessel while the vessel is in a port.

(2) The master of a vessel who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(3) The master of a vessel shall ensure that while the vessel is moored to a wharf all over-side discharges and scuppers of the vessel are kept effectively screened so as to ensure that no fluid refuse falls on—

(a) the wharf;

(b) any structure, machinery or appliance on the wharf; or

(c) any vessel alongside the wharf.

(4) The master of a vessel who contravenes subregulation (3) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(5) The master of a vessel shall not, except with the written approval of the port management company, cause the vessel to depart from a wharf until the part of the wharf opposite the berth occupied by the vessel has been cleaned.

(6) The master of a vessel who contravenes subregulation (5) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(7) If the master of a vessel fails to carry out his or her obligations under subregulation (5) the port management company may take such action as it considers necessary to clean that part of the wharf.

(8) Any costs incurred under subregulation (7) shall be a debt due to the port management company from the master of the vessel that left the wharf without cleaning it and may be recovered in court.

### reg 109 Certain pollutants not to be discharged from the shore into a port

(1) The owner or occupier of a property shall ensure that no oil, oily mixture, spirit, any inflammable liquid or harmful substance is discharged from that property—

(a) into a port;

(b) into waters flowing or ebbing into a port; or

(c) on to any wharf, approach to a wharf or any road or land within a port.

(2) The owner or occupier of a property who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(3) If a person contravenes subregulation (1) , the port management company may take such action as it considers necessary to eradicate any pollution and any threat of further damage and may make good any damage caused by the discharge.

(4) Any costs incurred under subregulation (3) by port management company shall be a debt due to port management company from the owner or occupier of the premises from which the discharge was made and may be recovered by port management company in court.

### reg 110 Discharge from land into a port

(1) The owner or occupier of land shall not permit any sewer or drain on that land to discharge into the waters of a port except with the written approval of the port management company.

(2) An owner or occupier of land who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(3) The port management company may give its approval under subregulation (1) subject to conditions.

(4) The owner or occupier to whom approval is given under subregulation (3) or a subsequent owner or occupier of the land in respect of which the approval is given shall comply with any conditions subject to which it was given.

(5) The owner or occupier who fails to comply with a condition imposed under subregulation (4) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(6) The port management company may revoke any approval given by it under subregulation (3) at any time.

### reg 111 Dumping near port

(1) The owner or occupier of land shall ensure that no refuse, garbage, rubbish, corrosive substance, dead animals, fish or birds, filth or any putrefying or objectionable vegetable or animal matter is deposited or discharged on that land in a position where it may be washed by rain or otherwise into the waters of a port.

(2) An owner or occupier of land who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(3) If a person contravenes subregulation (1) the port management company may take such action as it considers necessary to eradicate any pollution or any threat of pollution and may make good any damage caused by that breach of subregulation (1) .

(4) Any cost incurred under subregulation (3) shall be deemed to be a debt due to the port management company from the occupier or owner of the premises referred to in subregulation (1) and maybe recovered by the port management company in court.

### reg 112 Material not to be dumped on foreshore etc

(1) No person shall, or cause another person to place, dump, discharge, tip or otherwise dispose of refuse, waste, rubbish, debris, soil, rock or other material on the foreshore or in the waters of a port except with the written approval of the port management company.

(2) A person who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(3) The port management company may give its approval under subregulation (1) subject to conditions.

(4) A person to whom approval is given under subregulation (1) who fails to comply with the conditions under subregulation (3) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(5) If a person contravenes subregulation (1) or subregulation (4) the port management company may take such action as it considers necessary to remove or relocate the offending refuse, waste, rubbish, debris, soil, rock or other material and restore the amenity of the foreshore and seabed.

(6) Any costs incurred under subregulation (5) shall be a debt due to the port management company from the person who breached subregulation (1) or subregulation (4) and may be recovered by the port management company in court.

## PART 11 MISCELLANEOUS [regs 113-119]

### reg 113 Water skiing

(1) The driver of a water ski boat driven within the port shall be seated behind the wheel of the boat in such a manner that the driver has instant and proper control over its course and speed.

(2) The driver of a water ski boat who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(3) A person shall not use a power ski within a port or any approach to a port unless the engine of the power ski is fitted with an automatic engine cut out, designed to put the motor in a neutral position when the hand is removed from the throttle.

(4) A person who contravenes subregulation (3) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(5) The driver of a water ski boat shall give full attention to the navigation of the vessel and the water ahead.

(6) A driver of a water ski boat who contravenes subregulation (5) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(7) A person under the age of 18 years shall not drive a water ski boat.

(8) A person who contravenes subregulation (7) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 114 Requirement for a ski observer

(1) The owner or driver of a water ski boat shall not permit the boat to be used to tow a water skier unless there is a ski observer on board the boat.

(2) The owner or driver of a water ski boat who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(3) A person shall not permit himself or herself to be towed by a water ski boat while the he or she is on a water ski unless there is a ski observer in the vessel.

(4) A person who contravenes subregulation (3) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 115 Designation of areas for clubs and associations

(1) The port management company may designate an area within a port for the use of a club or association for the purpose of racing, regattas or similar aquatic sports, subject to conditions.

(2) The port management company shall, at least 14 days before allocating an area under subregulation (1) , publish a notice setting out the details of the area to be allocated and the club or association to which the area will be allotted, in the Gazette and in a newspaper published and circulated widely in Fiji.

(3) The port management company shall if practicable, when specifying conditions under subregulation (1) , require an area allocated under subregulation (1) to be marked or patrolled so as to preclude the risk of an accident.

(4) The person having control or management of the club or association shall take such action as may be necessary to ensure that the club or association complies with those conditions.

(5) If the club or association contravenes any conditions imposed under subregulation (1) , the person having control or management of the club or association commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 116 Restriction on entering allocated areas

(1) A person shall not swim, bathe, water ski or dive, in an area allocated under subregulation 114 (1) unless the person is taking part in the function or sport for which the area was allocated.

(2) A person who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(3) The master of a vessel shall not allow the vessel to enter in an area allocated under subregulation (1) unless the person is taking part in the function or sport for which the area was allocated.

(4) The master of a vessel who contravenes subregulation (3) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 117 Requirement of approval for water sports

(1) A person shall not organise—

(a) any form of racing of vessels; or

(b) a competition in or an exhibition of any form of aquatic sports,

in a port except with and in accordance with the prior written approval by port management company.

(2) A person who contravenes subregulation (1) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

(3) The owner of a vessel shall not conduct, promote, engage in or allow the vessel be used for or in connection with, any race or competition in, or an exhibition of any form of aquatics, except in accordance with the approval given under subregulation (1) .

(4) A person who contravenes subregulation (3) commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to the penalty set out in the Schedule .

### reg 118 Schedule of penalties

(1) The maximum penalties for offences under these Regulations are set out in the Schedule .

(2) The summary of offences set out in Column 2 of the Schedule are only for references and shall not be taken as the conclusive elements of the offence set out in specific regulations.

### reg 119 Repeal and saving

(1) The Port Regulations 1990 is repealed (“repealed Regulations”).

(2) Despite subregulation (1) , any existing appointment, permit, approval or authorisation made pursuant to the repealed Regulations at the commencement of these Regulations shall continue as if were made under these Regulations until it expires, suspended, varied or revoked under these Regulations.

(3) Any liability incurred under the repealed Regulations 1990 shall continue as if it is incurred under these Regulations.

(4) Any cause, matter or proceedings initiated under the repealed Regulations shall be saved and shall continue under these Regulations.
