# ITAUKEI AFFAIRS ACT 1944

AN ACT TO PROVIDE FOR THE REGULATION OF ITAUKEI AFFAIRS

## s 1 Short title

This Act may be cited as the iTaukei Affairs Act 1944.

[s 1 subst Decree 31 of 2010 s 2, 2 July 2010]

## s 2 Interpretation

In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires—

**Board** means the iTaukei Affairs Board constituted under the provisions of this Act;

[def am Decree 31 of 2010 s 3, 2 July 2010]

**chief** means any person enrolled in the principal mataqali of a yavusa in the register of iTaukei land owners recorded under the provisions of section 8 of the iTaukei Lands Act 1905;

[def am Decree 31 of 2010 s 3, 2 July 2010]

**Council** means the Great Council of Chiefs constituted under section 3 of this Act;

[def insrt Act 25 of 2023 s 2, 1 December 2023]

**iTaukei** includes every member of an aboriginal race indigenous to Fiji and also includes every member of an aboriginal race indigenous to Melanesia, Micronesia or Polynesia living in Fiji who has elected to live in an iTaukei village;

[def am Decree 31 of 2010 s 3, 2 July 2010]

**iTaukei court** means a court constituted under the provisions of this Act;

[def am Decree 31 of 2010 s 3, 2 July 2010]

**Permanent Secretary** means the Permanent Secretary responsible for iTaukei affairs;

[def insrt Act 25 of 2023 s 2, 1 December 2023]

**province** means a province established under the provisions of this Act or By-law or custom in existence at the commencement of this Act; and

**tikina** means a tikina established under the provisions of this Act or By-law or custom in existence at the commencement of this Act.

[s 2 subst Ordinance 10 of 1966 s 2, 27 January 1967]

## s 2A Amendment of all references to “Fijian” in all written laws etc

(1) All written laws, including any Act, Proclamation, Promulgation, Decree and any subsidiary legislation, (including the titles of any written law) are amended by deleting the word “Fijian” wherever it appears and wherever it currently refers to indigenous Fijians, and replacing it with “iTaukei”, unless the context otherwise requires.

(2) All State documents, of any nature whatsoever, are consequentially amended by deleting the word “Fijian” wherever it appears and wherever it currently refers to indigenous iTaukei, and replacing it with “iTaukei”, unless the context otherwise requires.

[s 2A insrt Decree 31 of 2010 s 4, 2 July 2010]

## s 3 Great Council of Chiefs

(1) There must be in respect of the iTaukei people a council called the Great Council of Chiefs (*Bose Levu Vakaturaga*) which must consist of such persons appointed by the President on the advice of the Minister.

(2) It is the duty of the Council, in addition to any power or duty conferred on it, to submit to the President any recommendation or proposal as it may deem to be for the benefit of the iTaukei people, and to consider any question relating to the good governance and well-being of the iTaukei people as the President or the Board may submit to the Council, and to take decisions or make recommendations thereon.

[s 3 insrt Act 25 of 2023 s 3, 1 December 2023]

## s 4 iTaukei Affairs Board

(1) There shall be a Board called the iTaukei Affairs Board which shall consist of such appointed and elected persons as the Minister may by Regulations prescribe.

[subs (1) subst Decree 22 of 2012 s 5, 9 March 2012]

(2) The Board shall be a body corporate with perpetual succession and common seal and shall, under the name of the iTaukei Affairs Board, be capable of suing and, being sued and subject to the provisions of this Act, shall be capable of purchasing, holding and alienating property, real and personal, and of doing and suffering all such other acts and things as bodies corporate may by law do and suffer.

[subs (2) am Decree 31 of 2010 s 3, 2 July 2010]

(3) Subject to the approval of the Minister for Finance, it shall be lawful for the Board to raise loans, including temporary loans by way of overdraft from a bank or otherwise, for the purposes of this Act, on such terms and conditions as it may consider appropriate and it may offer all or any of its property as security for such loans.

(4) It shall be the duty of the Board, in addition to any powers or duties especially conferred upon it, to submit to the Minister such recommendations and proposals as it may deem to be for the benefit of the iTaukei people, to consider such questions relating to the good government and well-being of the iTaukei people as the Minister may from time to time submit to the Board and may refer such questions to the Council or to take decisions or submit recommendations thereon.

[subs (4) am Act 25 of 2023 s 4, 1 December 2023]

[s 4 subst Ordinance 10 of 1966 s 4, 27 January 1967am Ordinance 29 of 1967 s 2, 29 March 1968 Decree 31 of 2010 s 3, 2 July 2010 Decree 22 of 2012 s 5, 9 March 2012]

## s 5 Regulations by Minister

Without prejudice to the generality of the provisions of sections 4 and 5 , the Minister may make regulations providing for—

(a) the constitution of the Council and of the Board;

(b) the powers, duties and functions of the Council and of the Board;

(c) the holding of meetings of the Council and of the Board.

[s 5 am Ordinance 10 of 1966 s 4, 27 January 1967 Decree 22 of 2012 s 6, 9 March 2012 Act 25 of 2023 s 5, 1 December 2023]

## s 6 Regulations by Board

(1) The Board may, subject to the approval of the Minister, make regulations to be obeyed by all iTaukei, providing for the peace, order, welfare and good government of iTaukei, and, without prejudice to the generality of the foregoing, providing for—

(a) the constitution of Provincial Councils and of such other councils as the Minister or the Board may from time to time establish;

(b) the conduct of elections to Provincial Councils, provided that the provisions of the law for the time being in force relating to offences in connection with the conduct of elections to Parliament shall apply *mutatis mutandis* to elections to Provincial Councils;

(c) the imposition assessment and collection of rates and the charging of fees by Provincial Councils;

(d) the making of by-laws by Provincial Councils and of orders by other councils;

(e) the powers, duties and functions of Provincial Councils and other councils;

(f) the establishment, constitution, powers, duties and functions of committees by Provincial Councils and other councils;

(g) the holding of meetings of Provincial Councils and other councils, and committees thereof;

(h) the jurisdiction, powers and procedure of iTaukei courts and Magistrates in civil and criminal matters and the fees which may be charged in connexion with proceedings before iTaukei courts;

(i) for the recruitment, training and appointment of officers to carry out functions under the provisions of this Act;

(j) any other matter required to be prescribed.

[subs (1) subst Ordinance 29 of 1967 s 3, 29 March 1968am LN 112 of 1970 O 64, 8 October 1970 Decree 31 of 2010 s 3, 2 July 2010]

(2) Subject to the provisions of the proviso to subsection (1)(b) , regulations made under the provisions of that subsection may impose penalties for the breach thereof not exceeding a fine of $100 or imprisonment for a term not exceeding 6 months, or both such fine and imprisonment, and may make provision for extramural punishment.

[s 6 subst Ordinance 10 of 1966 s 4, 27 January 1967]

## s 7 Provincial Councils

(1) There shall be in and for each province a council called the Provincial Council which shall consist of such number of persons as may be prescribed by regulations.

(2) A Provincial Council may, subject to the approval of the Board, make such by-laws for the health, welfare and good government of, and, subject to the approval of the Minister, impose such rates or charge such fees to be paid by iTaukei residing in or being members of the community of the province as may be authorised by Regulations.

[subs (2) am Decree 31 of 2010 s 3, 2 July 2010]

(3) Any by-law made under the provisions of subsection (2) may impose penalties for the breach thereof not exceeding a fine of $40 and, in the case of a continuing offence, a further fine not exceeding $4 for each day during which the offence continues after a conviction in respect thereof. Any such by-law may also provide that, in addition to any such fine, any expense incurred by a Provincial Council in consequence of any such breach or in the execution of any work directed by any such by-law to be executed by any person and not executed by him or her shall be paid by the person committing such breach or failing to execute such work.

[s 7 subst Ordinance 10 of 1966 s 4, 27 January 1967]

## s 8 Provincial Council to be corporate body

(1) A Provincial Council shall be known by the name of the province followed by the words “Provincial Council” and shall under that name be a body corporate with perpetual succession and a common seal, and shall for the purposes and subject to the provisions of this Act be capable of purchasing, holding, disposing of and alienating real and personal property and of doing and suffering all such other acts and things as bodies corporate may by law do and suffer.

(2) It shall be lawful for a Provincial Council from time to time to raise loans, including temporary loans on overdraft from a bank or otherwise, for such purposes, in such amounts, and on such terms and conditions as shall be approved by the iTaukei Affairs Board. Such loans shall be secured on the property and revenues of the council.

[subs (2) am Decree 31 of 2010 s 3, 2 July 2010]

(3) A Provincial Council may acquire, whether by way of purchase or lease, any land, whether situated within or without the boundaries of the province, and may develop any land so acquired, and may sell or let any land which they may possess, provided that no such dealing in land shall take place without the prior approval of the Minister.

[s 8 subst Ordinance 10 of 1966 s 4, 27 January 1967]

## s 9 Other councils

(1) The Board may establish for any area in any province such other council, with authority over such area as the Board may define, which council shall consist of such persons and shall be called by such name as may be prescribed.

(2) A council created under the provisions of subsection (1) may, subject to the approval of the Provincial Council of the province within which the area of authority of such other council may be situated and, with the subsequent approval of the Minister, make orders for the good government of iTaukei residing in or being members of the community of such area as may be authorised by regulations.

[subs (2) am Decree 31 of 2010 s 3, 2 July 2010]

(3) An iTaukei shall be liable to a fine not exceeding $20 or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding 2 months, or both such fine and imprisonment, if he or she contravenes or fails to obey any order which by this Act he or she is bound to obey.

[subs (3) am Decree 31 of 2010 s 3, 2 July 2010]

## s 10 Standing orders

The Board—

(a) shall make standing orders to the satisfaction of the Minister for regulating the receipt, custody and disbursement of revenue, for accounting and audit, and for the inspection of accounts; and

(b) may make standing orders regulating the engagement, salaries, conditions of service, dismissal, superannuation and all other matters relating to the employment of persons in its service.

## s 11 Procedure with respect to Bills affecting iTaukei affairs

(1) When any proposed Bill is to be introduced into Parliament which appears to the Minister to affect in any important matter the rights and interests of iTaukei, and especially any proposed Bill imposing taxes to be paid by them or relating to their chiefs, local authorities or land, it shall first be referred to the Board for consideration, unless the Minister certifies that it is so urgent or of so secret a nature that the public interest does not so permit.

[subs (1) am LN 112 of 1970 O 64, 8 October 1970 Decree 31 of 2010 s 3, 2 July 2010 Act 25 of 2023 s 6, 1 December 2023]

(2) Where a proposed Bill is referred to the Board under subsection (1) , the Board may inform the Minister that it advises that the proposed Bill be referred to the Council and the Minister must then refer it unless the urgency of the matter is such that in his or her opinion the proposed Bill cannot be delayed.

[subs (2) insrt Act 25 of 2023 s 6, 1 December 2023]

(3) If the proposed Bill is not referred to the Council, it must be considered by the Board which must prepare a memorandum containing its comments on the proposed Bill and submit the proposed Bill with the memorandum to the Permanent Secretary.

[subs (3) insrt Act 25 of 2023 s 6, 1 December 2023]

(4) If the proposed Bill is referred to the Council, the Council must without undue delay proceed to consider it and prepare a memorandum containing its comments on the proposed Bill and submit the proposed Bill and memorandum to the Permanent Secretary.

[subs (4) insrt Act 25 of 2023 s 6, 1 December 2023]

(5) If, in the opinion of the Minister, the public interest requires or there has been undue delay, the Minister may order that the proposed Bill which has been referred to the Council be withdrawn and referred to the Board, which must then prepare a memorandum containing its comments on the proposed Bill and submit the proposed Bill with the memorandum to the Permanent Secretary.

[subs (5) insrt Act 25 of 2023 s 6, 1 December 2023]

(6) When a proposed Bill has been submitted to the Permanent Secretary with a memorandum by the Council or the Board, the proposed Bill must be presented to Cabinet.

[subs (6) insrt Act 25 of 2023 s 6, 1 December 2023]

(7) Where a proposed Bill is approved by Cabinet under subsection (6), it must be presented to Parliament with the memorandum which members of Parliament can refer to or discuss during parliamentary debate as if it were part of the Bill.

[subs (7) insrt Act 25 of 2023 s 6, 1 December 2023]

## s 12 Appointment and powers of Rokos

The Minister may, with the advice of the Board, appoint the Roko for provinces, and such Roko when so appointed shall have such powers and duties as may from time to time be conferred or imposed on them by regulation, provided that where in any province no appointment of a Roko has been made or the Roko is unable to exercise the powers or perform the duties of his or her office by reason of illness, absence on leave or other cause, then in default of the appointment of any other person to act in the office of Roko, the Commissioner of the Division within which the province is situated may exercise the powers and shall perform the duties conferred or imposed on the Roko by or under this Act.

[s 12 am Ordinance 4 of 1945 s 5, 3 March 1945]

## s 13 Appointment and powers of iTaukei Magistrates

The Minister may appoint fit and proper persons to be iTaukei Magistrates and such Magistrates shall have such jurisdiction and powers as may be conferred upon them by or under this Act.

[s 13 am Decree 31 of 2010 s 3, 2 July 2010]

## s 14 Suspension and discharge of Rokos and Magistrates

(1) If the Minister is satisfied that any Roko or iTaukei Magistrate is by reason of illness, delinquency or neglect incapable of discharging or unwilling to discharge the duties of his or her office he or she may make an order suspending him or her from office and appointing a fit and proper person to take his or her place for such time as may be prescribed in the order, and such person shall thereupon have all the powers and duties of such Roko or Magistrate.

[subs (1) am Decree 31 of 2010 s 3, 2 July 2010]

(2) If the Minister is of the opinion that there are grounds for considering the removal of a Roko or iTaukei Magistrate from office, or the imposition of a penalty by way of fine, reduction of salary or reprimand, he or she may refer the matter to the Board for inquiry, at which the Roko or Magistrate shall be entitled to be heard, and may thereafter make such order as he or she considers just.

[subs (2) am Decree 31 of 2010 s 3, 2 July 2010]

(3) If the Minister is of opinion that by reason of ill health, infirmity or physical disability a Roko or iTaukei Magistrate is unable to discharge the duties of his or her office, he or she may refer the case to a medical board and may thereafter make such order as he or she considers just.

[subs (3) am Decree 31 of 2010 s 3, 2 July 2010]

## s 15 Appointment of officers

(1) The Board may appoint such officers, accountants and servants as may be required for the efficient discharge of its duties and responsibilities and for the proper conduct and administration of iTaukei affairs.

[subs (1) am Ordinance 29 of 1967 s 4, 29 March 1968 Decree 31 of 2010 s 3, 2 July 2010]

(2) Subject to the provisions of this Act, persons so appointed shall have such powers as may be conferred by regulation, and shall perform such duties as may be imposed by regulations or as the Board may direct.

## s 16 Tikina courts

There shall be in each tikina a court, to be called a Tikina Court, which shall consist of an iTaukei Magistrate sitting alone and shall be held at such times and at such places as may be necessary.

[s 16 am Ordinance 4 of 1945 s 3, 3 March 1945 Decree 31 of 2010 s 3, 2 July 2010]

## s 17 Jurisdiction and powers of iTaukei Magistrates

(1) Subject to the provisions of this Act, iTaukei Magistrates shall have and exercise such civil jurisdiction and powers and, with respect to offences against regulations, by-laws and orders made under this Act, such criminal jurisdiction and powers, as may be prescribed by regulations, and, in so far as such regulations do not provide, shall have and exercise, with respect to such offences, jurisdiction and powers similar to those conferred on second class Magistrates by the Criminal Procedure Code (Cap. 21).

[subs (1) am Ordinance 2 of 1945 s 138am Decree 31 of 2010 s 3, 2 July 2010]

(2) No person shall be committed to prison by an iTaukei Magistrate without a written warrant, or sentenced to any punishment without a written record of the conviction.

[subs (2) am Decree 31 of 2010 s 3, 2 July 2010]

(3) An iTaukei Magistrate shall not have power to investigate an indictable offence, but, if an information or complaint discloses such an offence, he or she may issue warrants for the arrest of the offender or other preliminary process and shall, without delay, remit the case for investigation to a Magistrate holding office under the Magistrates Court Act 1944 .

[subs (3) am Ordinance 2 of 1945 s 138am Decree 31 of 2010 s 3, 2 July 2010]

## s 18 Appeal from tikina court

An appeal shall lie from a tikina court to the provincial court of the province—

(a) when the amount adjudged to be paid exceeds the sum of $2; or

(b) when a person has been adjudged as a punishment for an offence in default of payment of a fine to be imprisoned for a term exceeding 7 days; or

(c) when a person has been adjudged as a punishment for an offence to be imprisoned without the option of a fine,

provided that no appeal shall lie in the case of an accused person who has pleaded guilty and been convicted on such plea except as to the extent and legality of the sentence.

[s 18 am Ordinance 4 of 1945 s 3, 3 March 1945]

## s 19 Provincial courts

(1) There shall be in each province a court, to be called the provincial court. Such court shall be composed of 3 members of whom 2 shall be iTaukei Magistrates and the third shall be either an iTaukei Magistrate or a district officer.

[subs (1) am Ordinance 2 of 1945 s 138 Decree 31 of 2010 s 3, 2 July 2010]

(2) Subject to the provisions of this Act provincial courts shall have and exercise such civil and criminal jurisdiction as may be prescribed by regulations.

(3) Each provincial court shall sit at least once every 3 months at such times and such places as it may consider necessary.

[subs (3) am Ordinance 29 of 1967 s 5, 29 March 1968]

## s 20 Appeals from provincial court

An appeal in both civil and criminal matters shall lie from a provincial court to the High Court upon the terms and in the manner which may be from time to time prescribed by regulation and subject thereto upon the terms and in the manner prescribed by sections 308 to 322 inclusive and 329 to 340 inclusive of the Criminal Procedure Code (Cap. 21).

[s 20 insrt Ordinance 19 of 1944 s 391, 1 May 1945]

## s 21 Powers of revision of findings of iTaukei courts

(1) The legal adviser to the Board may call for and examine the record of any proceedings before an iTaukei court for the purpose of satisfying himself or herself of the correctness, legality or propriety of any finding, sentence or order recorded or passed, and as to the regularity of any proceedings of an iTaukei court.

[subs (1) am Decree 31 of 2010 s 3, 2 July 2010]

(2) If the legal adviser to the Board acting under subsection (1) considers that any finding, sentence or order of any iTaukei court is incorrect, illegal or improper or that any such proceedings are irregular, he or she may, in the case of any finding, sentence or order other than an order of acquittal, alter or reverse such finding, sentence or order, provided that no order under this subsection shall be made to the prejudice of an accused person unless he or she has had an opportunity of being heard either personally or by a barrister and solicitor in his or her own defence.

[subs (2) am Decree 31 of 2010 s 3, 2 July 2010]

(3) Subject to the provisions of subsection (2) , no party shall have any right to be heard, either personally or by barrister and solicitor, by the legal adviser to the Board when exercising his or her powers of revision, provided that the legal adviser may in his or her discretion when exercising such powers hear any party either personally or by barrister and solicitor.

## s 22 Promulgation of by-laws and orders

Notwithstanding anything contained in the Interpretation Act 1967 every by-law or order made and duly approved under this Act shall be made known to the person or persons by whom it is to be obeyed in such manner as is customary in the area of jurisdiction of the provincial or tikina council by whom the by-law or order is made, and thereupon shall be binding on the person or persons by whom it is to be obeyed.

[s 22 am Ordinance 4 of 1945 s 3, 3 March 1945]

## s 23 Personation of chief

(1) An iTaukei shall be liable to a fine not exceeding $100 or to imprisonment not exceeding one year or to both imprisonment and fine if, not being a person exercising administrative functions with the approval of the Minister or the Board, he or she shall profess to exercise administrative functions, or, not being a chief, hold himself or herself out to be a chief or assume the powers of a chief.

[subs (1) am Decree 31 of 2010 s 3, 2 July 2010]

(2) An iTaukei accused of an offence against this section may be tried summarily before any court, whether an iTaukei court or not, within the area of whose jurisdiction the offence was committed, provided always that no court shall be deemed to be authorised by this section to impose heavier penalties than such court is authorised to impose in the exercise of its ordinary criminal jurisdiction, provided further that no proceedings shall be taken for an offence against this section without the consent of the Minister.

[subs (2) am Decree 31 of 2010 s 3, 2 July 2010]

## s 24 Conflict between regulations etc made under this Act and other legislation

If the subject matter of any regulation, by-law or order made under this Act is also the subject matter of any other law for the time being in force, not being a regulation, by-law or order made under this Act, such regulation, by-law or order shall not be invalid solely by reason thereof but shall be void to the extent to which it is incompatible with such other law.

## s 25 Rotuma excepted

This Act shall not apply to the island of Rotuma.
