## PART 1 PRELIMINARY [s 1]

### s 1 Short title

(1) This Act may be cited as the Pensions Act 1983.

(2) This Act shall come into force on a date to be notified by the Minister in the Gazette.

## PART 2 CIVIL SERVICE [ss 2– 19]

### s 2 Interpretation

(1) In this Part and the regulations contained in Schedule 2 —

**house allowance** in respect of service subsequent to 31 December 1927, means the estimated value of free quarters or the allowance paid in lieu thereof to which an officer is entitled as determined in accordance with the provisions of regulation 15 in Schedule 2 or any regulation hereafter made amending or substituted for the same;

**other civil service** means civil service not under the Government of the Republic of Fiji determined by the Minister to be civil service for the purposes of this Act;

[def am Act 2 of 2016 s 20, effective 16 February 2016]

**pensionable emoluments** includes—

(a) salary;

(b) personal allowance except where such allowance has been granted subject to the condition that it shall not be pensionable;

(c) house allowance,

but does not include duty, entertainment or other allowance or any other emoluments whatsoever;

**pensionable office** means—

(a) in respect of service under the Government of the Republic of Fiji an office set out in Schedule 1;

(b) in respect of other civil service, an office which is a pensionable office under the law or regulations in force in such service;

[def am Act 2 of 2016 s 20, effective 16 February 2016]

**personal allowance** means a special addition to salary granted personally to the holder for the time being of the office, but does not include such an addition if it is granted subject to the condition that it shall not be pensionable;

**civil service** means—

(a) service in a civil capacity under the Government;

(b) any other service that the Minister determines to be civil service for the purposes of this Act; and

[def am Act 2 of 2016 s 20, effective 16 February 2016]

**salary** means the salary attached to a pensionable office or, where provision is made for taking service in a non-pensionable office into account as pensionable service, the salary attached to that office.

(2) For the avoidance of doubt it is hereby declared that, where an officer has been confirmed in a pensionable office and is thereafter appointed to another pensionable office, then, unless the terms of such appointment otherwise require, such last mentioned office is, for the purposes of this Act, an office in which he has been confirmed.

### s 3 Power to alter Schedule 1

(1) The Minister may by notice in the Gazette add any office to or delete any office from Schedule 1, provided that the deletion of an office shall not affect existing rights, and that so long as any person holding that office at the time of the deletion continues to hold it, the office shall, as respects that person, continue to be a pensionable office.

(2) The Minister may in adding an office to Schedule 1 specify that the office shall only be a pensionable office in respect of any officer or officers specified in the notice and thereupon such office shall only be a pensionable office in the said Schedule in respect of such officer or officers and, shall only be deemed to be in such Schedule in respect of such officer or officers, provided that nothing in this subsection shall prohibit the Minister thereafter adding such office to such Schedule whether generally or in respect of a further officer or further officers.

(3) An addition to or deletion from Schedule 1 made under this section shall have effect from the date specified in the notice and may be given retrospective effect.

### s 4 Pensions Regulations

(1) The Minister may from time to time make regulations amending, adding to or revoking the Regulations contained in Schedule 2. Every such regulation when made shall be laid before Parliament and shall be published in the Gazette.

(2) All regulations made under this section shall have the same force and effect as if they were contained in Schedule 2.

(3) Wherever the Minister is satisfied that it is equitable that any regulation made under this section should have retrospective effect in order to confer a benefit or remove a disability attaching to any person, that regulation may be given retrospective effect for that purpose, provided that no such regulation shall have retrospective effect unless it has received, before being made, the approval of Parliament signified by resolution.

(4) Except as may be otherwise expressly provided, a pension, gratuity or other allowance granted under this Part shall be computed in accordance with the provisions in force at the actual date of an officer's retirement.

### s 5 Grant of pensions, gratuities and allowances

Pensions, gratuities and other allowances may be granted by the Minister in accordance with the provisions of this Part and the regulations contained in Schedule 2 to officers who have been in service under the Government of the Republic of Fiji.

### s 6 Service not qualifying for pension

Save as otherwise provided in this Act, no pension, gratuity, or other allowance shall be granted to any officer in respect of any service—

(a) while on probation or agreement unless without break of service he is confirmed in a pensionable office in the civil service; or

(b) while under the age of 18 years.

[s 6 am Decree No 26 of 1988 s 4, effective 1 May 1988; Act 2 of 2016 s 20, effective 16 February 2016]

### s 7 Circumstances in which pensions may be granted

No pension, gratuity or other allowance shall be granted under this Part to any officer except on his retirement from the civil service in one of the following cases—

(a) in the case of an officer appointed to the civil service prior to 1 January 1966, on or after attaining the age of 60 years or if his retirement was approved or required by any of the Commissions mentioned in section 9(1), on or after attaining the age of 45 years;

(b) in the case of any officer appointed to the civil service on or after 1 January 1966, 50 years;

(c) in the case of transfer to other civil service, on or after attaining the age at which an officer is permitted by the law or regulations of the service in which he is last employed to retire on pension or gratuity or, if no age is prescribed by the said law or regulations, on or after attaining the age of 45 years or, in the case of any officer appointed to the civil service on or after 1 January 1966, 50 years;

(d) on the abolition of his office;

(e) on compulsory retirement for the purpose of facilitating improvement in the organisation of the department to which he belongs by which greater efficiency and economy can be effected;

(f) on medical evidence to the satisfaction of the Minister that he is incapable by reason of some infirmity of mind or body of discharging the duties of his office and that such infirmity is likely to be permanent;

(g) in the case of termination of employment in the public interest as provided in this Part,

provided that a gratuity may be granted to a female officer, in accordance with the provisions of this Part, who retires for the reason that she has married or is about to marry, notwithstanding that she is not otherwise eligible under this section for the grant of any pension, gratuity or other allowance.

[s 7 am Decree No 26 of 1988 s 5, effective 1 May 1988; Act 2 of 2016 s 20, effective 16 February 2016]

### s 8 Retirement in the public interest

Where an officer's service is terminated on the ground that, having regard to the conditions of the civil service, the usefulness of the officer thereto and all the other circumstances of the case, such termination is desirable in the public interest, and a pension, gratuity or other allowance cannot otherwise be granted to him under the provisions of this Part, the Minister may, if he thinks fit, grant such pension, gratuity or other allowance as he thinks just and proper, not exceeding in amount that for which the officer would be eligible if he retired from the civil service in the circumstances described in section 7(e).

[s 8 am Act 2 of 2016 s 20, effective 16 February 2016]

### s 9 Compulsory retirement

(1) The Judicial Services Commission or Public Service Commission, as the case may be, may require an officer to retire from service under the Government of the Republic of Fiji—

(a) at any time after he has attained the age of 55 years; or

(b) at any time after he has attained the age of 45 years or, in the case of an officer appointed to the civil service on or after 1 January 1966, after he has attained the age of 50 years, and also completed the minimum length of service needed to qualify him for a pension; or

(c) in the case of a female officer, on marriage.

[subs (1) am Decree No 26 of 1988 s 6, effective 1 May 1988; Decree No 26 of 1989 s 3, effective 1 May 1988; Act 2 of 2016 s 20, effective 16 February 2016; Act 31 of 2016 s 159, effective 1 December 2016]

(2) This section shall not apply to Judges of the High Court.

### s 15 Gratuity where an officer dies in the service or after retirement

(1)

(a) Where an officer holding a pensionable office who is not on probation or agreement, or an officer holding a non-pensionable office to which he has been transferred from a pensionable office in which he has been confirmed, dies while in service under the Government, it shall be lawful for the Minister to grant to his personal representatives a gratuity of an amount not exceeding his annual pensionable emoluments, or his commuted pension gratuity if any, whichever is the greater.

(b) For the purposes of this subsection—

(i) annual pensionable emoluments means the emoluments which would be taken for the purpose of computing any pension or gratuity granted to the officer if he had retired at the date of his death in the circumstances described in section 7(f); and

(ii) commuted pension gratuity means the gratuity, if any, which might have been granted to the officer under regulation 24(1) in Schedule 2 if his civil service had been wholly under the Government and if he or she had retired at the date of his death in the circumstances described in section 7(f) and had elected to receive a gratuity and reduced pension.

[subs (1) am Act 2 of 2016 s 20, effective 16 February 2016]

(2) Where an officer dies after retirement from service under the Government having been granted, or having become eligible for, a pension under this Part and the sums paid or payable to him at the time of his death on account of such pension (including any sum paid by way of gratuity under regulation 24 (1) in Schedule 2) are less in total than the amount of the annual pensionable emoluments enjoyed by him at the date of his retirement, the Minister may grant a gratuity equal to the deficiency to his personal representatives.

(3) The provisions of this section shall not apply in the case of the death of any officer where benefits corresponding to the benefits which may be granted under this section have been awarded in respect of other civil service under the law or regulations of that other civil service.

[subs (3) am Act 2 of 2016 s 20, effective 16 February 2016]

### s 16 Pensions where an officer dies in the service or after retirement

(1) Where an officer holding a pensionable office to which section 15(1)(a) applies dies while in the service of the Government leaving a beneficiary it shall be lawful for the Minister to grant to such beneficiary a pension equivalent to 60% of the reduced pension which the officer would have been qualified to receive if he had retired immediately before his death in the circumstances described in section 7(f) and had elected to receive a gratuity and reduced pension.

[subs (1) am Act 10 of 1995 s 2, effective 1 January 1994; Act 20 of 1998 s 2, effective 1 January 1998]

(2) Where a person who has retired from civil service and is in receipt of a pension under any of the Acts referred to in subsection (6) in respect of his service under the Government dies leaving a beneficiary, it shall be lawful for the Minister to grant to such beneficiary an amount equivalent to 60% of the pension which such person was receiving at the date of his death.

[subs (2) am Act 10 of 1995 s 2, effective 1 January 1994; Act 20 of 1998 s 2, effective 1 January 1998; Act 2 of 2016 s 20, effective 16 February 2016]

(3) A pension payable to a beneficiary under this section shall commence on 17 May 1982 or upon the date of death of the officer or person in respect of whom it is payable, whichever date is the later. The pension shall accrue daily and be payable fortnightly and shall cease upon the death or remarriage of the beneficiary.

[subs (3) subst Act 10 of 1985 s 2, effective 18 October 1985; am Act 17 of 1986 s 9, effective 1 October 1986]

(4) A beneficiary in receipt of a pension under this section shall within 30 days of the event inform the Minister in writing of his remarriage.

(5) The Minister may require such proof as he deems desirable that any person claiming to be entitled to a pension under this section is so entitled, and the payment of any pension may be refused until such proof is furnished to the satisfaction of the Minister.

(6) In this section “beneficiary” means the widow of an officer or person referred to in subsection (1) or (2), as the case may be, and the Acts referred to in subsection (2) are the Pensions Ordinance 1928, the Pensions Act 1938, the Pensions Act 1958 (all of which have ceased to have effect except as mentioned in section 48) and this Act.

### s 17 Pensions etc to dependants when an officer is killed on duty

(1) Where an officer dies as a result of injuries received—

(a) in the actual discharge of his duty; and

(b) without his own default, and

(c) on account of circumstances specifically attributable to the nature of his duty,

while in service under the Government of the Republic of Fiji, it shall be lawful for the Minister to grant, in addition to the grant, if any, made to his personal representatives under section 15 —

(i) if the deceased officer leaves a widow, a pension to her, while unmarried and of good character, at a rate not exceeding ten-sixtieths of his annual pensionable emoluments at the date of the injury or $40 a year, whichever is the greater;

(ii) if the deceased officer leaves a widow to whom a pension is granted under paragraph (i) and a child or children, a pension in respect of each child, until such child attains the age of 21 years, of an amount not exceeding one-eighth of the pension prescribed under paragraph (i);

(iii) if the deceased officer leaves a child or children, but does not leave a widow or no pension is granted to the widow, a pension in respect of each child, until such child attains the age of 21 years, of double the amount prescribed by paragraph (ii);

(iv) if the deceased officer leaves a child or children and a widow to whom a pension is granted under paragraph (i), and the widow subsequently dies, a pension in respect of each child as from the date of the death of the widow until such child attains the age of 21 years, of double the amount prescribed in paragraph (ii);

(v) if the deceased officer does not leave a widow, or if no pension is granted to his widow, and if his mother was wholly or mainly dependent on him for her support, a pension to the mother, while of good character and without adequate means of support, of an amount not exceeding the pension which might have been granted to his widow;

(vi) if the deceased officer does not leave a widow or mother, or if no pension is granted to his widow or mother, and if his father was wholly or mainly dependent on him for his support, a pension to the father, while of good character and without adequate means of support, of an amount not exceeding the pension which might have been granted to his widow;

(vii) if the deceased officer does not leave a child or children who is or are eligible for a pension under the provisions of this section, and if any brother or sister was wholly or mainly dependent on him for support, a pension in respect of any such brother or sister of the same amount and subject to the same conditions as the pension which might have been granted in respect of a child under paragraph (ii), (iii) or (iv), provided that—

(A) pension shall not be payable under this subsection at any time in respect of more than 6 children;

(B) in the case of a pension granted under paragraph (v), if the mother is a widow at the time of the grant of the pension and subsequently remarries such pension shall cease as from the date of the remarriage;

(C) a pension granted to a female child under this section shall cease upon the marriage of such child under the age of 21 years;

(D) in the case of a pension granted under paragraph (v), (vi) or (vii), if it appears to the Minister at any time that the mother or father, or any brother or sister, is adequately provided with other means of support, such pension shall cease as from such date as the Minister may determine.

(2) In the case of an officer not holding a pensionable office, the expression “pensionable emoluments” in subsection (1) shall mean the emoluments enjoyed by him which would have been pensionable emoluments if the office held by him had been a pensionable office.

(3) For the purposes of this section, the following words have, in relation to an officer, the meanings hereby respectively assigned to them—

(a) brother includes every male child of his father or of his mother;

(b) child includes—

(i) a posthumous child;

(ii) a stepchild or illegitimate child born before the date of the injury and wholly or mainly dependent upon him for support; and

(iii) an adopted child, adopted in a manner recognised by law, before the date of the injury, and dependent as aforesaid;

(c) father includes his stepfather and a male person by whom he has been adopted;

(d) mother includes his stepmother and a female person by whom he has been adopted; and

(e) sister includes every female child of his father or of his mother.

(4) This section and section 18 shall not apply in the case of death of any officer selected for appointment to service under the Government of the Republic of Fiji on or after 1 March 1941, if his dependants as defined in the Workmen's Compensation Act 1964, or any Act amending or replacing that Act, are awarded compensation under that Act.

### s 19 Application

(1) Subject to the provisions of section 48, this Part shall apply to all officers in the civil service at the commencement of this Act who are or who have been in the civil service of Fiji, or who may be appointed to the civil service of Fiji after the commencement of this Act, except—

(a) officers to whom the Pensions Ordinance 1928, applies;

(b) Provident Fund officers, subject however to regulation 16 of the Regulations contained in Schedule 2;

(c) officers in service under the Government of the Republic of Fiji at the commencement of this Act who, by writing addressed to the permanent secretary responsible for finance within 6 months of the commencement of this Act or such longer period as the Minister may in any special case allow, have elected to contribute to the Fiji National Provident Fund under the provision of section 46 and regulation 25 of the Regulations contained in Schedule 2;

(d) officers who are employed under a written contract of service except to the extent provided in regulation 16 of the Regulations contained in Schedule 2;

(e) officers who have been appointed to civil service under the Government of the Republic of Fiji as temporary officers except where such officers were appointed as temporary officers prior to 1 November 1971 due to medical reasons;

(f) officers to whom the provisions of Parts 3 and 4 apply, except to the extent provided by those Parts;

(g) officers who have been appointed to civil service under the Government of the Republic of Fiji on or after 1 November 1971.

[subs (1) am Act 10 of 1985 s 3, effective 18 October 1985; Act 2 of 2016 s 20, effective 16 February 2016]

(2) In this section “Provident Fund officer” means an officer who has under the provisions of section 3 of the Government Employees Provident Fund (Winding Up) Ordinance 1958, elected to continue to be a contributor to the Government Employees Provident Fund.

## PART 3 DISCIPLINED SERVICES [ss 20– 30]

### s 20 Interpretation

In this Part, unless the context otherwise requires—

**office** means and includes any of the offices specified in Schedule 3 and Schedule 4;

**officer** means the holder of any office;

**pay** means basic pay and includes no other allowances;

**permanent officer** means the holder of any of the offices specified in Schedule 3;

**service** means full-time service in any office, and includes any period of paid leave accruing to any officer in respect of his service in any office;

**temporary officer** means the holder of any of the offices specified in Schedule 4; and

**unbroken service** means continuous service,

provided that any break in service, or temporary suspension from office, not arising from misconduct or voluntary resignation shall be disregarded for the purpose of this definition, though the period of any such absence during the actual break in service shall not itself count as service unless it is a period during which an officer is absent—

(i) on leave without salary on the ground of public policy; or

(ii) on temporary suspension whilst investigations are carried out into his affairs and any proceedings take place which do not subsequently lead to or result either in his conviction by a competent court or in his dismissal from the service,

and in either such event the period of absence shall also count as service.

### s 21 Pensions, gratuities or bonuses may be granted by the Minister

Pensions, gratuities or bonuses may be granted by the Minister to any permanent or temporary officer at the rates and subject to the conditions hereinafter contained, provided that the Minister may approve, in such cases as he thinks fit, the grant of a reduced gratuity or the entire withholding of any gratuity.

### s 22 Power to amend Schedules

(1) The Minister may by notice in the Gazette add any office to or delete any office from Schedule 3 and Schedule 4, provided that the deletion of an office shall not affect the existing rights of any officer holding that office at the time of such deletion and while such officer continues to hold such office it shall, in respect of that officer, continue to be subject to the provisions of this Part.

(2) Any addition to or deletion from Schedule 3 and Schedule 4, made under the provisions of this section shall have effect from the date specified in the notice effecting the same and may be given retrospective effect.

### s 23 Gratuities, pensions and re-enlistment bonuses

(1) Any permanent officer who has completed 5 years’ unbroken service in any office and is re-engaged for a further period of service may be paid on re-engagement, a bonus of 2 months’ pay at his rate of pay on re-engagement.

(2) Any permanent officer who has completed 10 years’ unbroken service in any office may—

(a) if he is not immediately re-engaged for a further period of 5 years’ service, be paid on discharge a gratuity at the rate of one month's pay at his rate of pay on discharge, for each completed year of service less any amount previously withdrawn by him under the provisions of section 24; or

(b) if he is immediately re-engaged for a further period of 5 years’ service, at his option to be signified, in writing to the permanent secretary responsible for finance at the date of or prior to re-engagement—

(i) be paid on re-engagement a bonus of 2 months’ pay at his rate of pay on re-engagement; or

(ii) withdraw 60% of the gratuity which would have been payable to him under the provisions of paragraph (a), had he not been re-engaged for such further period of service.

(3) Any permanent officer who has completed 15 years’ unbroken service may—

(a) if he is not immediately re-engaged for a further period of 5 years’ service, be paid on discharge, a gratuity at the rate of one month’s pay, at his rate of pay on discharge, for each completed year of service, less any amount previously withdrawn by him under any of the provisions of this section or section 24 or under any other enactment providing for the grant of pensions or gratuities; or

(b) if he is immediately re-engaged for a further period of 5 years’ service, at his option to be signified in writing to the permanent secretary responsible for finance at the date of or prior to re-engagement—

(i) be paid, on re-engagement a bonus of 2 months’ pay at his rate of pay on re-engagement; or

(ii) withdraw 60% of the gratuity which would have been payable to him under the provisions of paragraph (a) had he not been re-engaged for such further period of service.

(4) Any permanent officer who has completed 20 years’ unbroken service may—

(a) if he is not immediately re-engaged for a further period of 5 years’ service, be paid on discharge a gratuity at the rate of one month’s pay, at his rate of pay on discharge, for each completed year of service, less any amount previously withdrawn by him under any of the provisions of this section or section 24 or under the provisions of any other enactment providing for the grant of pensions or gratuities; or

(b) if he is immediately re-engaged for a further period of 5 years’ service, at his option to be signified in writing to the permanent secretary responsible for finance at the date of or prior to re-engagement—

(i) be paid on re-engagement a bonus of 2 months’ pay at his rate of pay on re-engagement; or

(ii) withdraw 60% of the gratuity which would have been payable to him under the provisions of paragraph (a), had he not been re-engaged for such further period of service,

provided that any such officer who has completed 20 years’ unbroken service in any office in a permanent capacity may, at his option to be signified in writing to the permanent secretary responsible for finance, at any time after such officer has completed 19 years’ such service in such office and before his discharge therefrom, be paid on discharge a pension under the provisions of section 25 in which case the minimum retiring age of such officer shall be 45 years.

(5) Any permanent officer who has completed more than 20 years’ unbroken service may on completion of each subsequent period of 5 years’ unbroken service—

(a) if he is not immediately re-engaged for a further period of 5 years’ service, and has not exercised the option referred to in the proviso to subsection (4), be paid on discharge a gratuity at the rate of one month’s pay, at his rate of pay on discharge, for each completed year of service, less any amount previously withdrawn by him under any of the provisions of this section or section 24; or

(b) if he is immediately re-engaged for a further period of 5 years’ service, at his option to be signified in writing to the permanent secretary responsible for finance at the date of or prior to re-engagement—

(i) be paid on re-engagement a bonus of 2 months’ pay at his rate of pay on re-engagement; or

(ii) withdraw 60% of the gratuity which would have been payable to him under the provisions of paragraph (a) had he not been re-engaged for such further period of service.

(6) In the event of any permanent officer resigning from any office during any reengagement period of 5 years, he shall repay such proportion of any bonus paid to him in respect of that period under the provisions of this section as the Minister may determine.

(7) In the event of the discharge during any re-engagement period of 5 years of any permanent officer who has completed more than 10 years’ unbroken service, he may be paid on discharge a gratuity at the rate of one month’s pay, at his rate of pay on discharge, for each completed year of service, less any amount previously withdrawn by him under any of the foregoing provisions of this section or section 24 or under the provisions of any other enactment providing for the grant of pensions or gratuities.

### s 24 Exemptions

Notwithstanding anything contained in this Part—

(a) any permanent officer who retires from service on medical grounds or on a re-organisation or reduction of establishment may be granted on discharge a gratuity at the rate of one month’s pay, at his rate of pay on discharge, for each completed year of service in respect of which he is not otherwise eligible for any pension or gratuity under any of the provisions of any other enactment, other than those of section 28(2), less any amounts previously withdrawn by him under any of the provisions of section 23, provided that no such gratuity shall be payable under the provisions of this paragraph on retirement from service on medical grounds unless medical evidence to the satisfaction of the Minister is produced that the officer is incapable by reason of some infirmity of mind or body of discharging the duties of his office and that such infirmity is likely to be permanent;

(b) any gratuity which may be payable to a permanent officer after completion of 10 years’ unbroken service shall not be liable, nor shall any part thereof be liable, to forfeiture in consequence of any event of a disciplinary nature which has happened after his completion of 10 years’ service, provided that, if any such officer is discharged on the grounds that he has been convicted by any competent court for any offence, any such gratuity payable to him may be reduced or altogether withheld;

(c) section 23(2), (3), (4), (5) and (7), in so far as they relate to the payment of pensions or gratuities, and section 30 shall not apply to persons appointed on or after 1 November 1971, to any of the officers specified in Schedule 3 or Schedule 4.

### s 25 Pension in lieu of gratuity

(1) If a permanent officer exercises his option to be paid a pension in pursuance of the proviso to section 23(4), he shall, upon completion of 20 years’ unbroken service in any office in a permanent capacity or upon the exercise of such option whichever is the later, be treated as if he were an officer holding a pensionable office set out in Schedule 1 and his pension shall be calculated, and he shall in every other respect be deemed to be subject to the provisions of Part 2 except that sections 6, 7, 8 and 9 and regulations 5, 6, 17, 19(1), 21, 22 and Part 3 of the Regulations contained in Schedule 2, shall not apply to him, and regulation 4 of those Regulations shall only apply in so far as it provides for the rate of pension, provided that the following provisions shall have effect in his case instead of regulation 16 of those Regulations—

(a) if he has not been paid any part of any gratuity to which he has become eligible under the provisions of this Part or under the provisions of any other enactment relating to the granting of pensions or gratuities or has received any part of such gratuity but repays it in full, all his service shall be taken into account as pensionable service;

(b) if he has received any part of such gratuity and does not repay it in full, he shall forthwith be eligible to be paid the balance thereof and only that part of his service which is subsequent to the date on which he first became eligible to receive such gratuity shall be taken into account as pensionable service.

[s 25 am Decree 26 of 1988 s 7, effective 1 May 1988]

(2) If a permanent officer is promoted to any pensionable office (with the consequence that Part 2 will apply to him) the following provisions shall have effect in his case instead of regulation 16 of the Regulations contained in Schedule 2—

(a) if he has not become eligible under the provisions of section 23 or under the provisions of any other enactment relating to the granting of pensions or gratuities to be paid a gratuity, or if he has become eligible for but has not been paid any part of such gratuity, or has received any part of such gratuity, but repays it in full, all his service prior to the promotion hereinbefore mentioned shall be taken into account as pensionable service;

(b) if he has received any part of such gratuity and does not repay it in full, he shall forthwith be eligible to be paid the balance thereof and only that part of his service which is subsequent to the date on which he first became eligible to receive such gratuity shall be taken into account as pensionable service.

### s 26 Gratuity in case of death during service

In the event of any permanent officer dying during his period of service, any gratuity which would have been payable to him under the provisions of section 23 had he taken his discharge at the date of his death, and any gratuity which would have been payable to him under the provisions of section 24(a) had he retired on medical grounds, shall, subject to the provisions of section 44 be payable to his legal personal representatives.

### s 27 Retirement in the public interest

Where an officer's service is terminated on the ground that he is unlikely to become efficient or has ceased to be efficient or on the ground that, having regard to the conditions of the civil service, the usefulness of the officer thereto and all the other circumstances of the case, such termination is desirable in the public interest and a pension, gratuity or other allowance cannot otherwise be granted under the provisions of this Part, the Minister may if he thinks fit and in his absolute discretion grant such pension, gratuity or other allowance as he thinks just and proper not exceeding in amount that for which the officer would be eligible if he retired from the civil service on medical grounds.

## PART 4 REPUBLIC OF FIJI MILITARY FORCES [ss 30A– 41B]

### s 30A Application of Part 4

This Part (other than section 37 and so much of this Part as relates to section 37) does not apply to a person who joins the Forces, either as an officer or a soldier, after a date declared by the Minister by Order.

### s 31 Interpretation

(1) In this Part, and in Schedule 5 and Schedule 6, unless the context otherwise requires—

**Active List** means the Active List referred to in section 5(2) of the Republic of Fiji Military Forces Act 1949;

**active operations** means operations notified by the Minister to be active operations in accordance with—

(a) section 37(1); or

(b) section 2 of the Pensions (Royal Fiji Military Forces) (Active Operations) Act 1980,

and includes operations in which any contingent of the Forces assigned for any of the services described in Schedule 7 is or has been engaged;

**child** includes—

(a) a posthumous child;

(b) a stepchild or illegitimate child; and

(c) an adopted child adopted in a manner recognised by law;

**Commander** means the Commander of the Forces appointed under the Constitution of the Republic of Fiji;

[def am Act 31 of 2016 s 159, effective 1 December 2016]

**current pay** in respect of an officer or soldier, means the annual pay for the time being of the rank held at the material time by the officer or soldier having regard to the officer's or soldier's number of years’ service in that rank, and does not include any allowances;

**dependent child** in relation to an officer or a soldier, means a child of that officer or soldier who is or, in the case of a deceased officer or soldier, was at the time of his death, wholly or mainly dependent upon that officer or soldier, where the child—

(a) is receiving full time secondary education; or

(b) if not receiving full time secondary education, is under the age of 16 years;

**Forces** means the Republic of Fiji Military Forces established by section 3(1) of the Republic of Fiji Military Forces Act 1949;

**normal retiring age** in relation to an officer, means the age prescribed by regulations made under section 67 of the Republic of Fiji Military Forces Act 1949 for the retirement of officers of the rank held by that officer;

**officer** means a commissioned officer appointed to the Forces by the President;

**officer on the Active List** means an officer during such time as he is not posted to the Reserve of Officers or the Retired List;

**pay** in respect of an officer or a soldier, means the basic pay for the time being for the rank held at the material time by that officer or soldier having regard to his number of years service in that rank;

**qualifying service** means—

(a) in relation to an officer, service specified in section 34; and

(b) in relation to a soldier, service specified in section 36;

**rank** in relation to an officer or a soldier, means the rank held by that officer or soldier at the material time, except that where the officer or soldier held a higher temporary rank at that time it means that temporary rank;

**Regular Force** means the Regular Force established by section 4(2) of the Republic of Fiji Military Forces Act 1949;

**Reserve of Officers** means the Reserve of Officers constituted by section 4(4) of the Republic of Fiji Military Forces Act 1949;

**Retired List** means the Retired List referred to in section 5(3) of the Republic of Fiji Military Forces Act 1949;

**soldier** means a warrant officer, non-commissioned officer or person enlisted in or belonging to the Forces and includes a rating in any naval unit of the Forces; and

**standard period of qualifying service** in relation to an officer, means the period of qualifying service prescribed for the rank of that officer in the Table in regulation 1 of the Regulations set out in Schedule 5.

(2) In this Part, and in Schedule 5 and Schedule 6, a reference to retirement is a reference—

(a) in relation to an officer, to the officer ceasing to be an officer in the Regular Force; and

(b) in relation to a soldier, to his discharge from the Regular Force.

### s 32 Grant of pensions and gratuities to officers

Subject to this Act, the Minister may grant in respect of an officer's service in the Regular Force, pensions and gratuities in accordance with Part 1 of the Regulations set out in Schedule 5.

### s 33 Circumstances in which a pension may be granted to an officer

(1) Subject to subsections (2) and (3) and regulation 2 of Schedule 5, and to section 37, the Minister shall not grant a pension to an officer unless the officer has completed the standard period of qualifying service and has reached normal retiring age.

(2) Notwithstanding subsection (1), the Minister may grant a pension to an officer where the officer has not completed the standard period of qualifying service and has not reached normal retiring age in the following cases, namely—

(a) if the officer has completed 20 years or more of qualifying service and the Minister determines after consultation with the Commander that—

(i) the officer is not suitable for promotion; or

(ii) there is no further employment available to the officer in the Regular Force,

and that he should be retired;

(b) if the officer has completed 10 years or more of qualifying service and it is shown upon medical evidence to the satisfaction of the Minister that—

(i) the officer is incapable by reason of some infirmity of mind or body of discharging his duties as an officer; and

(ii) the infirmity is likely to be permanent,

and the Minister determines that he should be retired;

(c) if the office held by the officer is abolished;

(d) if the officer has completed 20 years or more qualifying service and is released from the Regular Force at his own request.

(3) Notwithstanding subsections (1) and (2), the Minister may grant a pension to an officer who has not completed the standard period of qualifying service if he is an officer who in the interests of the Forces was appointed at an age greater than that of a normal entrant and retires on or after attaining normal retiring age.

### s 34 Qualifying service in respect of an officer

(1) Subject to the remaining provisions of this section, the service to be taken into account as qualifying service by an officer is continuous commissioned service in the Regular Force.

(2) In computing an officer's qualifying service, the following periods of that service are to be excluded, namely—

(a) service by the officer whilst under the age of 18 years;

(b) service by the officer whilst temporarily posted back to the Active List from the Reserve of Officers or the Retired List;

(c) any period of leave without pay which exceeds 28 consecutive days;

(d) service which has been taken into account in computing pension, gratuity or bonus under Part 2, the Fiji National Provident Fund Act 1966 or any other written law.

(3) Service as a soldier in the Regular Force after the age of 18 years is qualifying service under subsection (1).

(4) Where an officer has had a period of service (in this section referred to as “other service”) in the Fiji Police Force, the Fiji Corrections Service or as a Forest Guard or a period of service after the age of 18 years in any other civil capacity under the Government of the Republic of Fiji, whether or not preceded by a period of service in the Regular Force, and—

(a) that period of service was immediately followed by service in the Regular Force; and

(b) he forgoes or refunds any gratuity or lump sum payable or paid to him in respect of that other service (except a war service gratuity),

that period of other service is, subject to subsection (5), qualifying service for the purposes of subsection (1).

(5) A break in service which can be disregarded under regulation 12 of the Regulations set out in Schedule 2 can likewise be disregarded for the purposes of determining under subsection (4)(a) whether a period of service was followed by another period of service.

(6) Other service which terminated in dismissal or discharge as a result of disciplinary action is not qualifying service under this section.

### s 35 Grant of pensions, gratuities and benefits to soldiers

Subject to this Act, the Minister may grant in respect of a soldier of the Regular Force pensions, gratuities and benefits in accordance with Part 2 of the Regulations set out in Schedule 5.

### s 36 Qualifying service in respect of a soldier

(1) Subject to the remaining provisions of this section, the service to be taken into account as qualifying service by a soldier is continuous full time service after the age of 18 years—

(a) in the Regular Force; or

(b) in any unit established under the Military Forces Ordinance 1923.

(2) In computing a soldier's qualifying service the following periods of that service are to be excluded, namely—

(a) any period of leave without pay;

(b) any period of absence without leave;

(c) any period of imprisonment; or

(d) any period of detention,

which exceeds 28 consecutive days.

(3) Where a soldier has had a period of service (in this section referred to as “other service”) in the Fiji Police Force, the Fiji Corrections Service or as a Forest Guard, or a period of service after the age of 18 years in any other civil capacity under the Government of the Republic of Fiji, whether or not preceded by a period of service in the Regular Force, and—

(a) that period of other service was immediately followed by service in the Regular Force; and

(b) he forgoes or refunds any gratuity or lump sum payable or paid to him in respect of that other service (except a war service gratuity), that period of other service is, subject to subsection (5), qualifying service for the purposes of subsection (1).

(4) A break in service which can be disregarded under regulation 12 of the Regulations set out in Schedule 2 can likewise be disregarded for the purposes of determining under subsection (3)(a) whether a period of service was immediately followed by another period of service.

(5) Other service which terminated in dismissal or discharge as a result of disciplinary action is not qualifying service under this section.

### s 37 Benefits in respect of injury or death whilst on active operations

(1) The Minister may, by notice in the Gazette, declare any operations in which a contingent of the Forces is or has been engaged, being operations in an area in or near which a state of war, revolution, armed conflict or serious widespread internal disturbance exists or has existed, to be active operations.

(2) Where an officer or soldier whilst engaged in active operations—

(a) is injured in circumstances specifically attributable to the nature of his service by an injury which is not wholly or mainly due to or seriously aggravated by his own serious and culpable negligence or misconduct; or

(b) contracts a disease to which he is exposed by the nature of his service, not being a disease wholly or mainly due to or seriously aggravated by his own serious and culpable negligence or misconduct,

and that officer or soldier dies as a direct result of the injury or disease, the Minister may grant to the persons specified in Part 1 of the Regulations contained in Schedule 6 pensions in accordance with and subject to those Regulations.

(3) Where an officer or soldier referred to in subsection (1) does not die but is disabled as a direct result of the injury or disease, the Minister may grant him a pension (or reduced pension and gratuity), in accordance with, and subject to Part 2 of the Regulations contained in Schedule 6 in accordance with and subject to those Regulations.

(4) A pension granted under this section is in addition to any pension, gratuity or other payment payable under this Part or any other written law except that where any such other pension, gratuity or payment is payable, a pension granted under this section shall not exceed 66.67% of the pay on which the pension under this Part is computed.

(5) This section shall not apply where in respect of an officer or soldier compensation has been paid under the Workmen's Compensation Act 1964 or the Accident Compensation Act 2017 for injury or death.

[subs (5) insrt Act 21 of 1996 s 5, effective 25 October 1996; am Act 30 of 2018 s 3, effective 1 January 2019]

### s 37A Pension where an officer dies in the Forces or after retirement

(1) Subject to subsection (3), where an officer who has completed 10 years or more of qualifying service but less than the standard period of qualifying service dies while in the Forces leaving a widow, the Minister may grant to that widow a pension of an amount equivalent to 40% of the reduced pension which the officer would have received if he had retired immediately before his death in the circumstances described in section 33(2)(b) and had opted to receive a reduced pension and gratuity.

(2) Subject to subsection (3), where an officer who has retired from the Forces and is in receipt of a pension under this Part (other than a pension granted under section 37) dies leaving a widow, the Minister may grant to that widow a pension of an amount equivalent to 40% of the pension that officer would have been entitled to receive had he continued to live.

(3) This section does not apply where an officer dies in circumstances to which section 37 applies.

### s 37B Pension where a soldier dies in the Forces or after retirement

(1) Subject to subsection (3), where a soldier who has completed 10 years or more of qualifying service but less than 21 years qualifying service dies whilst in the Forces leaving a widow, the Minister may grant to that widow a pension of an amount equivalent to l/2000th of the soldier's current pay for each complete month of the soldier's qualifying service.

(2) Subject to subsection (3), where a soldier who has retired from the Force and is in receipt of a pension under this Part (other than a pension granted under section 37) dies leaving a widow, the Minister may grant to that widow a pension of an amount equivalent to 40% of the pension that soldier would have been entitled to receive had he continued to live.

(3) This section does not apply where a soldier dies in circumstances to which section 37 applies.

### s 37C Payment of pension under sections 37A and 37B

(1) Subject to subsection (5), a pension payable to a widow under section 37A or 37B commences upon the death of the officer or soldier, as the case may be, in respect of whom it is granted.

(2) A pension granted under section 37A or 37B accrues daily, is payable fortnightly and ceases upon the death or remarriage of the widow.

(3) If a widow in receipt of a pension granted under section 37A or 37B remarries, she shall inform the Minister of the marriage within 30 days after the marriage.

(4) The Minister may require a person granted or claiming a pension under section 37A or 37B to produce to the Minister such proof as the Minister considers necessary to prove that that person is entitled to the pension, and the Minister may refuse to grant or to continue to pay the pension until he receives that proof.

(5) Sections 37A and 37B have effect in respect of an officer or a soldier, or a retired officer or a retired soldier, as the case may be, whether he died or dies before or after the commencement of this Act, but where an officer or soldier, or a retired officer or soldier, died on or before 17 May 1982, a pension payable in respect of that officer or soldier or retired officer or soldier is not payable in respect of any period prior to 17 May 1982.

### s 38 Pension etc not to pass to Trustee in Bankruptcy etc

A pension, gratuity or other allowance granted under this Part does not pass to the Trustee in Bankruptcy or other person acting on behalf of creditors on the bankruptcy of a person to whom it has been granted.

### s 39 Pension etc to cease on imprisonment

(1) Subject to subsection (2), if an officer, soldier or other person to whom a pension or other allowance has been granted under this Part is imprisoned following a conviction that pension or other allowance ceases, if the Minister so directs, from such date as the Minister determines.

(2) Where under subsection (1) the Minister may direct a pension or other allowance to cease, he may instead direct that the whole or any part of the pension or allowance be paid to or applied for the maintenance or benefit of that person to whom it was granted or to that person's wife or dependent children, or any one or more of them, in such proportion and manner, and upon such conditions as the Minister may direct, and the pension or allowance is to be paid or applied accordingly.

### s 40 Regulations

(1) The Minister may make regulations amending, adding to, or revoking a regulation contained in Schedule 5 and Schedule 6.

(2) Without prejudice to subsection (1), the Minister may make regulations for carrying the purposes of section 37 into effect and for amending, adding to or revoking the regulations contained in Schedule 6 so as to authorise, in the circumstances of any particular active operations, the making of awards which, or the amount of which, would not be authorised apart from this subsection.

### s 41 Government etc of Forces not affected

Nothing in this Part restricts the Republic of Fiji Military Forces Act 1949 in relation to the general governance, control and management of, and the enforcement of discipline in, the Forces, including, without prejudice to the generality of the foregoing, the power to dismiss any member of the Forces without compensation.

### s 41A Right of an officer or a soldier to elect to contribute to the Fiji National Provident Fund

(1) An officer or soldier may, before a date specified by the Minister by notice in the Gazette or such later date as the Minister may in any special case allow, elect to contribute to the Fiji National Provident Fund established under the Fiji National Provident Fund Act 1966.

(2) Where an officer or soldier elects under subsection (1) to contribute to the Fiji National Provident Fund, the following provisions have effect—

(a) the election of the officer or soldier is to be taken to be exercised upon receipt by the Commander of a written notice signed by the officer or soldier indicating his desire to become a contributor to the Fiji National Provident Fund;

(b) the election of the officer or soldier once exercised is irrevocable;

(c) the officer or soldier shall in the written notice of his election state which of the following options he selects, namely—

(i) that a gratuity be computed in accordance with section 41B(1) and forthwith paid to his credit in the Fiji National Provident Fund in accordance with the Fiji National Provident Fund Act 1966; or

(ii) that a pension be computed in accordance with section 41B(2) and paid to him upon his retirement.

(3) Upon receipt by the Commander of the written notice of election by an officer or soldier under this section—

(a) if the officer or soldier has selected the option mentioned in subsection (2)(c)(i) the Commander shall—

(i) compute the gratuity to be paid in accordance with section 41B(1); and

(ii) pay to the officer's or soldier's credit in the Fiji National Provident Fund the amount so computed,

and thereafter this Part and Schedule 5 and Schedule 6 (other than the provisions of section 37, so much of this Part and Schedule 5 and Schedule 6 as relate to section 37, and regulation 7 set out in Schedule 5) cease to apply to that officer or soldier;

(b) if the officer or soldier has selected the option mentioned in subsection (2)(c)(ii), the Commander shall—

(ii) record the amount of the pension computed in accordance with subparagraph (i).

(4) An officer or soldier is not entitled to receive a pension computed and recorded in accordance with subsection (3)(b) unless—

(a) in the case of an officer, he retires in any of the circumstances referred to in section 33(1), (2) or (3); or

(b) in the case of a soldier, he retires having completed 21 years of qualifying service.

(5) If an officer or soldier becomes entitled to receive the pension computed and recorded in accordance with subsection (3)(b), he shall be entitled to exercise the option referred to in regulation 3(1) or 8(2), as the case may be, of the regulations set out in Schedule 5, but, except as provided in this subsection and in subsection (6), this Part (other than the provisions of section 37, so much of this Part and Schedule 5 and Schedule 6 as relates to section 37, and regulation 7 set out in Schedule 5) cease to apply to an officer or soldier who has selected the option mentioned in subsection (2)(c)(ii) as from the exercise of his election, but the provisions of section 37A or 37B, and section 37C, shall apply, mutatis mutandis, to a pension, calculated by reference to the pension computed and recorded in accordance with subsection 3(b), awarded to the widow of an officer or soldier who dies in any circumstance specified in section 37A or 37B, as the case may be.

(6) Subject to subsection (7), section 45 applies to an officer or soldier in relation to his pension computed and recorded in accordance with subsection (3)(b) as it would have applied had the officer or soldier retired on the date of the exercise of his election and commenced to receive that pension.

(7) An officer or soldier is not entitled to the benefit of any allowance under section 45 unless and until he retires in the circumstances described in subsection (4) and receives the pension computed and recorded in accordance with subsection (3)(b).

### s 41B Computation where an officer or soldier elects to contribute to the Fiji National Provident Fund

(1) Where an officer or soldier elects under section 41A to contribute to the Fiji National Provident Fund and selects the option that a gratuity be computed under this subsection and paid to his credit in that Fund, that gratuity is to be an amount equal to 3.125 times the pension computed in accordance with subsection (2) or—

(b) in the case of a soldier who has completed a minimum period of 12 years continuous service in the Regular Force, an amount equal to one year's pay for the rank the soldier held at the date of his election,

whichever is the greater.

## PART 5 MISCELLANEOUS [ss 42– 51]

### s 42 Pensions, gratuities and allowances to be charged on Consolidated Fund

There shall be charged on and paid out of the Consolidated Fund all such sums of money as may from time to time be granted by way of pension, gratuity or other allowance under this Act.

### s 43 Pensions etc not of right

(1) No person shall have an absolute right to compensation for past services or to pension, gratuity or other allowance under this Act, nor shall anything contained in this Act affect the right of the State to dismiss any public officer without compensation.

(2) Where it is established to the satisfaction of the Minister that any public officer to whom a pension, gratuity or other allowance may be granted under Part 2 has been guilty of negligence, irregularity or misconduct, the pension, gratuity or other allowance may, if the Minister so directs, be reduced or altogether withheld.

### s 44 Pensions etc not assignable

A pension, gratuity, or other allowance granted under this Act shall not be assignable or transferable except for the purpose of satisfying—

(a) a debt due to the Government; or

(b) an order of any competent court for the payment of periodic sums of money towards the maintenance of the wife or former wife or minor child of the person to whom the pension, gratuity or other allowance has been granted,

and shall not be liable to be attached, sequestered or levied upon for or in respect of any debt or claim whatsoever except a debt due to the Government.

### s 45 Allowances to meet inflation

Notwithstanding anything contained in the foregoing provisions of this Act, the Minister may pay to any person or class of persons in receipt of a pension pursuant to Part 2 or Part 4 of this Act such further allowances as the Minister may from time to time deem fit having regard to general inflation in the cost of goods and services in Fiji, but such further allowances shall not form part of the pension paid to such person or class of persons unless the Minister so directs and if he does not so direct an allowance may be withdrawn or varied by him at any time.

### SCHEDULE 2 REGULATIONS FOR THE GRANTING OF PENSIONS, GRATUITIES AND OTHER ALLOWANCES TO OFFICERS

#### PART 1 PRELIMINARY [ss 1, 2]

##### cl 1 Short title

These Regulations may be cited as the Pensions (Civil Service) Regulations.

##### cl 2 Interpretation

In these Regulations, unless the context otherwise requires—

**pensionable service** means service which may be taken into account in computing pension under these Regulations; and

**qualifying service** means service which may be taken into account in determining whether an officer is eligible by length of service for pension, gratuity or other allowance.

#### PART 2 OFFICERS WITHOUT OTHER CIVIL SERVICE [ss 3–6]

##### cl 3 Application of Part 2

Save when the Minister in any special case otherwise directs, this Part shall not apply in the case of any officer transferred to or from service under the Government of the Republic of Fiji from or to other civil service except for the purpose of determining whether such officer would have been eligible for pension or gratuity, and the amount of pension or gratuity, for which the officer would have been eligible, if the service of the officer had been wholly under the Government of the Republic of Fiji.

##### cl 4 Pensions, to whom and at what rates to be granted

Subject to the provisions of the Act and of these Regulations, every officer holding a pensionable office in Fiji who has been in service under the Government of the Republic of Fiji in a civil capacity for 10 years or more may be granted on retirement a pension at the annual rate of 1/600th of his pensionable emoluments for each complete month of pensionable service.

##### cl 5 Gratuities where length of service does not qualify for pension

Every officer, otherwise qualified for a pension, who has not been in service under the Government of the Republic of Fiji in a civil capacity for 10 years, may be granted on retirement a gratuity not exceeding 5 times the annual amount of the pension which, if there had been no qualifying period, might have been granted to him under regulation 4.

##### cl 6 Marriage gratuities

Where a female officer having held a pensionable office or pensionable offices under the Government of the Republic of Fiji for not less than 5 years and having been confirmed in a pensionable office, retires from service under the Government of the Republic of Fiji for the reason that she has married or is about to marry, and is not eligible for the grant of any pension or gratuity under these Regulations, she may be granted, on production within 6 months after her retirement or such longer period as the Minister may in any particular case allow, of satisfactory evidence of her marriage, a gratuity not exceeding one-twelfth of a month's pensionable emoluments for each completed month of pensionable service under the Government of the Republic of Fiji or one year's pensionable emoluments, whichever shall be the less.

#### PART 3 SPECIAL REGULATIONS FOR TRANSFERRED OFFICERS [ss 7–10]

##### cl 7 Application of Part 3

This Part shall apply only in the case of an officer transferred to or from service under the Government of the Republic of Fiji from or to other civil service.

##### cl 8 Pension where there is other civil service

(1) Where the aggregate civil service of the officer would have qualified him had it been wholly under the Government of the Republic of Fiji for a pension under these Regulations, he may, on his retirement from the civil service in circumstances in which he is permitted by the law or regulations of the service in which he is last employed to retire on a pension or gratuity, be granted in respect of his service under the Government of the Republic of Fiji a pension at the rate of 1/600th of his pensionable emoluments at the date of his retirement or transfer from Fiji as the case may be for each complete month of his pensionable service under the Government of the Republic of Fiji.

(2) Where the officer is not in service under the Government of the Republic of Fiji at the time of such retirement, his pensionable emoluments for the purposes of paragraph (1) shall be those which would have been taken for the purpose of computing his pension if he had retired from the civil service and been granted a pension at the date of his last transfer from service under the Government of the Republic of Fiji.

##### cl 9 Gratuities where length of service does not qualify for pension

Where an officer has been transferred to or from service under the Government of the Republic of Fiji from or to other civil service, and retires from the civil service in circumstances in which he is permitted by the law or regulations of the service in which he is last employed to retire on pension or gratuity, but has not completed in the aggregate the minimum period of 10 years’ service qualifying him for a pension, he may be granted from the funds of Fiji a gratuity not exceeding 5 times the annual amount of the pension which, if there had been no qualifying period, might have been granted to him under regulation 8.

##### cl 10 Marriage gratuities

Where a female officer has been transferred to or from service under the Government of the Republic of Fiji from or to other civil service and retires from the civil service for the reason that she has married, or is about to marry, and in consequence—

(a) would if the whole of her civil service had been under the Government of the Republic of Fiji have been eligible for a gratuity under regulation 6; and

(b) at the date of her retirement from other civil service, is eligible for a gratuity under the provisions corresponding to that regulation in the law or regulations of the civil service in which she is last employed,

she may, if she is not eligible for the grant of any pension or otherwise eligible for gratuity under these Regulations, be granted a gratuity which bears to the gratuity for which she would be eligible if the whole of her civil service had been in the service in which she is last employed, the proportion which her service under the Government of the Republic of Fiji bears to her total pensionable service, provided that for the purpose of computing such an officer's total pensionable service under this paragraph no regard shall be had to any service under a Government which does not grant a gratuity to her in consequence of her retirement.

#### PART 4 GENERAL [ss 11–17]

##### cl 11 General rules as to qualifying service and pensionable service

(1) Subject to the provisions of these Regulations, qualifying service shall be the inclusive period between the date on which an officer begins to draw salary in respect of civil service and the date of his leaving the civil service, without deduction of any period during which he has been absent on leave.

(2) No period which is not qualifying service by virtue of paragraph (1) shall be taken into account as pensionable service.

(3) No period during which the officer was not in civil service shall be taken into account as qualifying service or as pensionable service.

##### cl 12 Continuity of service

(1) Except as otherwise provided in these Regulations, only continuous service shall be taken into account as qualifying service or as pensionable service, provided that any break in service caused by temporary suspension of employment not arising from misconduct or voluntary resignation shall be disregarded for the purposes of this paragraph.

(2) An officer—

(a) whose pension has been suspended under section 11 of the Act or under a corresponding provision in any law or regulation relating to the grant of pensions in respect of civil service; or

(b) who has retired from the civil service without pension on account of ill-health, abolition of office, or reorganisation designed to effect greater efficiency or economy, and has subsequently been re-employed in the civil service,

may, if the Minister thinks fit, be granted the pension or gratuity for which he would have been eligible if any break in his civil service immediately prior to such suspension, re-employment or employment had not occurred, such pension to be in lieu of—

(i) any pension previously granted to him from the funds of Fiji, and

(ii) any gratuity so granted which is required to be refunded as a condition of the application to the officer of this regulation,

but additional to any gratuity so granted which is not required to be refunded as aforesaid.

##### cl 13 Leave without salary

No period during which an officer shall have been absent from duty on leave without salary shall be taken into account as pensionable service unless such leave shall have been granted on grounds of public policy.

##### cl 14 Service in Her Majesty’s Forces

Where an officer, during some period of his service, has been on the active list of the Royal Navy, the Army or the Royal Air Force, and pension contributions have been paid in respect of that period from the funds of Fiji and have not been refunded, such period shall not be taken into account as pensionable service.

##### cl 15 Emoluments to be taken for computing pension or gratuity

(1) For the purpose of computing the amount of the pension or gratuity of an officer who has had a period of not less than 3 years’ pensionable service before his retirement—

(a) in the case of an officer who has held the same office for a period of 3 years immediately preceding the date of his retirement, the full annual pensionable emoluments enjoyed by him at that date in respect of that office shall be taken;

(b) in the case of an officer who at any time during such period of 3 years has been transferred from one office to another, but whose pensionable emoluments have not been changed by reason of such transfer or transfers, otherwise than by the grant of any scale increment, the full annual pensionable emoluments enjoyed by him at the date of his retirement in respect of the office then held by him shall be taken;

(c) in other cases one-third of the aggregate pensionable emoluments enjoyed by the officer in respect of his service during the 3 years of his service immediately preceding the date of his retirement shall be taken, provided that—

(i) if such one-third is less than the highest annual pensionable emoluments enjoyed by him at the date of any transfer within such period of 3 years those annual pensionable emoluments shall be taken; and

(ii) if such one-third is less than the annual pensionable emoluments which would have been enjoyed by him at the date of his retirement, if he had continued to hold any office from which he has been transferred at any time during such period of 3 years, and had received all scale increments which, in the opinion of the Minister, would have been granted to him, the annual pensionable emoluments which would have been so enjoyed shall be taken.

(2) For the purpose of determining under paragraph (1) the pensionable emoluments that an officer has enjoyed or would have enjoyed, as the case may be, he shall be deemed—

(a) to have been on duty on full pensionable emoluments throughout the period of 3 years immediately preceding the date of his retirement; and

(b) to have enjoyed the benefit of any increase due to a general revision of salaries in the pensionable emoluments of any office held by him as if such increase had been payable throughout such period of 3 years,

provided that if he has been transferred from an office which has been abolished and for this reason the pensionable emoluments of that office have not been considered in a general revision of pensionable emoluments, then, for the purposes of this regulation, the pensionable emoluments attached to the abolished office shall be equated with those of such office or offices as are certified by the Minister in his discretion as having enjoyed the same pensionable emoluments as the abolished office at the time of the said revision.

(3) For the purpose of computing the amount of the pension or gratuity of an officer who has had a period of less than 3 years’ pensionable service before his retirement—

(a) the average annual pensionable emoluments enjoyed by him or her during such period shall be taken;

(b) he or she shall be deemed to have been on duty on full pensionable emoluments throughout such period; and

(c) he shall be deemed to have enjoyed the benefit of any increase due to a revision in the pensionable emoluments of any office held by him as if such increase had been payable throughout such period.

(4) The estimated value of free quarters in respect of service under the Government of the Republic of Fiji shall be taken as 15% of the initial salary of the officer's appointment, or of the actual salary if such salary is non-incremental, provided that—

(a) the point in any scale of salary at which an efficiency bar occurs shall for this purpose be regarded as the initial salary of the appointment in the case of any officer who has passed such efficiency bar;

(b) the estimated value of free quarters shall in no case be reckoned at more than $400 per annum.

##### cl 16 Non-pensionable service followed by pensionable service

(1) Subject to the provisions of these Regulations only service in a pensionable office shall be taken into account as pensionable service, provided that, subject to paragraph (9) —

(a) where a period of service in a civil capacity otherwise than in a pensionable office and (subject to the succeeding provisions of this regulation) not being service in an office in respect of which contributions are payable under the Government Employees Provident Fund Ordinance 1958—

(i) is immediately followed by service in a pensionable office and the officer is confirmed therein, the whole of such service shall be taken into account as pensionable service;

(ii) is subsequently but not immediately followed by service in a pensionable office and the officer is confirmed therein, the whole, or such proportion of such service may be taken into account as pensionable but not qualifying service as the Minister shall consider to be equitable in the circumstances of any particular case, having regard to any increased remuneration which the officer may have received by reason of the assumption that such service was not pensionable and to any gratuity which may have been paid to such officer in respect of such service;

(b) service in Fiji as a teacher in a non-Government school not being service in respect of which contributions are payable under the Government Employees Provident Fund Act 1958, or the Fiji National Provident Fund Act, shall, whatever his date of retirement was or is being taken into account as pensionable but not as qualifying service in the case of a teacher who joined or joins the service of the Government of the Republic of Fiji as a teacher immediately following such other service as a teacher and remains in the service of the Government until the date of his retirement, to the same extent and in such proportion as if such other service had been service under the Government of the Republic of Fiji in an equivalent office specified in Schedule 1, as may be appropriate. In the case of a teacher in a non-government school who first joined or joins the service of the Government of the Republic of Fiji as a teacher and who was or is subsequently and immediately following his service as a Government teacher required, solely in the interests of the Government and not of his own choice or at his own request nor arising out of or in consequence of any act, neglect or misconduct on his part, to transfer to a post in the civil service other than as a teacher, his service in any other such post and in any further subsequent posts in the civil service shall for the purposes of this paragraph be deemed to be service as a Government teacher; and

(c) any break in service which may be disregarded under the provisions of regulation 12 may likewise be disregarded in determining for the purposes of paragraphs (a) and (b) whether one period of service immediately follows another period of service.

(2) The civil service of a person who was a depositor in the Government Employees Provident Fund (in this regulation referred to as “the Fund”) immediately before the commencement of these Regulations shall be deemed to have been and to be service in a pensionable office in Schedule 1 to the Act, and—

(a) the provisions of the Act and of these Regulations shall accordingly apply to his civil service from the date of his appointment thereto; and

(b) the bonus standing at his credit in the Fund and the interest credited thereon pursuant to the provisions of the Government Employees Provident Fund Ordinance 1958 and the Government Employees Provident Fund (Winding-up) Ordinance 1958 (in this regulation referred to as “the Ordinances”) shall forthwith be paid to the Consolidated Fund to be applied as general revenue and his own deposits in the Fund and the interest credited thereon pursuant to the provisions of the Ordinances shall be paid to him, and thereafter the provisions of the Ordinances shall cease to apply to him.

(3) The civil service of a person who was a depositor in the Fund on 31 December 1957, and who—

(a) was appointed to a pensionable office in Schedule 1 to the Pensions Act 1958; and

(b) retired from civil service on or before 17 May 1982,

shall be deemed, to have been service in a pensionable office in Schedule 1 to the Pensions Act 1958 from the date of his appointment to the civil service until the date of his retirement, and—

(i) in the case of such a person who exercised the option to be paid a reduced pension and gratuity, his reduced pension, but not the gratuity, shall be recalculated accordingly; and

(ii) in the case of such a person who did not exercise the option to be paid a reduced pension and gratuity, his pension shall be recalculated accordingly,

and in either case the difference between the reduced pension or pension as so recalculated and the pension which he had actually received shall, subject to paragraph (5), be paid to him.

(4) The civil service of a person who was a depositor in the Fund on 31 December 1957, and who—

(a) was appointed to a pensionable office in Schedule 1 to the Pensions Act (Cap 77 — 1978 Rev Ed); and

(b) retired from the civil service after 17 May 1982, but before the commencement of these Regulations,

shall be deemed to have been service in a pensionable office, in Schedule 1 to the Pensions Act (Cap 77 — 1978 Rev Ed) from the date of his appointment to the civil service to the date of his retirement, and his or her pension, or his reduced pension and gratuity if he exercised the option to be paid a reduced pension and gratuity, shall be recalculated in accordance with the provisions of the Act and of these Regulations and the difference shall, subject to paragraph (5) be paid to him accordingly.

(5) There shall be deducted from the amount payable to any person pursuant to the recalculation under paragraphs (3) and (4) of his pension, reduced pension or reduced pension and gratuity as the case may be, the amount of the bonus which was paid to him from the Fund under the provisions of the Ordinances and the interest which was credited thereon in respect of the period of his civil service from the date of his appointment thereto to 31 December 1957.

(6) The civil service of an officer who was a depositor in the Fund on 31 December 1957, and who—

(a) was appointed to a pensionable office in Schedule 1 to the Pensions Act 1958; and

(b) is serving in the civil service at the date of commencement of these Regulations,

shall be deemed to be and to have been service in a pensionable office in Schedule 1 to the Act.

(7) Upon the retirement of an officer to whom paragraph (6) applies in circumstances in which he is eligible to receive a pension or gratuity under the provisions of the Act and of these Regulations, there shall be deducted in such manner as the Minister may direct from the pension or gratuity payable to him the amount of the bonus which was paid to him from the Fund under the provisions of the Ordinances and the interest which was credited thereon in respect of the period of his civil service from the date of his appointment thereto to 31 December 1957, provided that if such an officer retires from the civil service in circumstances in which he is not eligible to receive, and does not receive, a pension or gratuity the amount of such bonus and interest shall be paid by him to the Chief Accountant upon his retirement unless the Minister otherwise directs.

(8) Where any officer has been transferred to service under the Government of the Republic of Fiji from service under the Civil Aviation Authority of Fiji at Nadi Airport, his service under that Authority shall for the purpose of these Regulations be deemed to have been service under the Government of the Republic of Fiji otherwise than in a pensionable office.

(9) Where—

(a) an officer has had a period of service in respect of which contributions are payable under the Fiji National Provident Fund Act 1966; and

(b) the Government of the Republic of Fiji has paid such contributions; and

(c) the period of such service is immediately followed by service in a pensionable office in which the officer is confirmed; and

(d) the officer commences, after such confirmation, to refund, at such rate and in such manner as the Minister may require, an amount equal to the difference between the contributions paid as aforesaid and the amount recovered in respect thereof by the Government of the Republic of Fiji from his wages,

that period may be taken into account as pensionable service in an office in Schedule 1 to the Act.

##### cl 17 Acting service

Any period during which an officer has performed only acting service in an office may be taken into account as pensionable service (subject, if the office is a non-pensionable office, to the provisions of regulation 16) if the period of such acting service—

(a) is not taken into account as part of his pensionable service in other civil service; and

(b) is immediately preceded or followed by service in a substantive capacity in a pensionable office under the same government or authority.

#### PART 5 SUPPLEMENTARY [ss 18–25]

##### cl 18 Abolition of office and re-organisation

If an officer holding a pensionable office retires from the civil service in consequence of the abolition of his office or for the purpose of facilitating improvements in the organisation of the Department to which he belongs, by which greater efficiency or economy may be effected,

(a) he may, if he has been in the civil service for less than 10 years, be granted in lieu of any gratuity under regulation 5 or regulation 9, a pension under regulation 4, as if the words “for 10 years or more” were omitted from regulation 4;

(b) he may, if he retires from service under the Government of the Republic of Fiji, be granted an additional pension at the annual rate of one-sixtieth of his pensionable emoluments for each complete period of 3 years’ pensionable service, provided that—

(a) the addition shall not exceed ten sixtieths; and

(b) the addition together with the remainder of the officer's pension shall not exceed the pension for which he would have been eligible if he had continued to hold the office held by him at the date of his retirement, and retired on reaching the age of 60 years, having received all increments for which he would have been eligible by that date.

##### cl 19 Service in Republic of Fiji Military Forces or Fiji Royal Naval Volunteer Reserve

(1) Where an officer—

(a) has had a period of full time service in the Republic of Fiji Military Forces or on the permanent staff of the Fiji Royal Naval Volunteer Reserve; and

(b) that period was immediately followed by service in a civil capacity (whether or not in a pensionable office) culminating in the officer's appointment to a pensionable office in which he was confirmed; and

(c) he forgoes or refunds any gratuity or other lump sum payable or paid to him in respect of that period and the whole or such part, as the Minister thinks equitable, of any compensation for discharge from the Republic of Fiji Military Forces or the Fiji Royal Naval Volunteer Reserve which he would otherwise be eligible to receive,

the whole of the officer's service in the Republic of Fiji Military Forces or on the permanent staff of the Fiji Royal Naval Volunteer Reserve shall be taken into account as pensionable service in an office in Schedule 1 to the Act.

(2) Any break in service which may be disregarded under the provisions of regulation 12 may likewise be disregarded in determining for the purposes of this regulation whether an officer's period of service in the Republic of Fiji Military Forces or the Fiji Royal Naval Volunteer Reserve was immediately followed by service in a civil capacity.

(3) Where an officer has had a period of full time service in the Republic of Fiji Military Forces or on the permanent staff of the Fiji Royal Naval Volunteer Reserve and that period was subsequently, but not immediately, followed by service in a civil capacity (whether or not in a pensionable office) culminating in the officer's appointment to a pensionable office in which he was confirmed, the whole, or such proportion of the officer's service in a civil capacity may be taken into account as pensionable, but not as qualifying service as the Minister shall consider to be equitable, in the circumstances of any particular case.

##### cl 20 Officers retiring on account of injuries

(1) If an officer holding a pensionable office in which he has been confirmed is permanently injured—

(a) in the actual discharge of his duty; and

(b) without his own default; and

(c) on account of circumstances specifically attributable to the nature of his duty—

(i) he may, if his retirement is thereby necessitated or materially accelerated and he has been in the civil service for less than 10 years, be granted, in lieu of any gratuity under regulation 5 or regulation 9, a pension under regulation 4, as if the words “for 10 years or more” were omitted from the said regulation 4;

(ii) he may, if so injured while in service under the Government of the Republic of Fiji, be granted on retirement an additional pension at the annual rate of the proportion of his actual pensionable emoluments at the date of his injury appropriate to his case as shown in the following table—

When his capacity to contribute to his own support is—

slightly impaired, five-sixtieths;

impaired, ten-sixtieths;

materially impaired, fifteen-sixtieths;

totally destroyed, twenty-sixtieths,

provided that the amount of the additional pension may be reduced to such an extent as the Minister shall think reasonable where the injury is not the cause or the sole cause of retirement.

(2) An officer so injured while in service under the Government of the Republic of Fiji, not holding a pensionable office, or holding a pensionable office in which he has not been confirmed, may be granted on retirement a pension of the same amount as the additional pension which might be granted to him under paragraph (1) if his office were a pensionable office and he had been confirmed therein. The provisions of regulation 24 shall not apply to a pension granted under this paragraph.

(3) If an officer proceeding by a route approved by the Minister to or from Fiji at the commencement or termination of his service therein, or of a period of leave therefrom is permanently injured as a result of damage to the vessel or aircraft or vehicle in which he is travelling, or of any act of violence directed against such vessel or aircraft or vehicle, and the Minister is satisfied that such damage or act is attributable to circumstances arising out of war in which Fiji may be engaged, such officer shall be deemed for the purposes of this regulation to have been injured in the circumstances described in paragraph (1).

(4) An officer who is permanently injured while travelling by air in pursuance of official instructions shall be deemed to have been injured in the circumstances detailed in (a) and (c) of paragraph (1), provided that in such a case and if (b) is also satisfied the rates of pension prescribed in that paragraph shall be seven and a half sixtieths, fifteen sixtieths, twenty-two and a half sixtieths and thirty sixtieths respectively.

(5) This regulation shall not apply in the case of an officer selected for appointment to service under the Government of the Republic of Fiji on or after 1 March 1941, who, in consequence of his injury, is awarded compensation under the Workmen's Compensation Act 1964, or any Act amending or replacing that Act.

##### cl 21 Officers who have served in a non-pensionable office

(1) Subject to the provisions of paragraph (4), an officer, not having been a contributor to the Provident Fund established by the Government Employees Provident Fund Ordinance 1958, holding a non-pensionable office who is not eligible for a gratuity or a pension (other than a pension under regulation 20(2) or an allowance or capital sum under regulation 22) from the funds of Fiji—

(a) if he has been in the civil service for not less than 7 years and is removed in consequence of the abolition of his office or for the purpose of facilitating improvements in the organisation of the department to which he belongs, by which greater efficiency and economy can be effected; or

(b) if he has been in the civil service for not less than 15 years and he retires in the circumstances mentioned in section 7(a), (b) or (e)of the Act,

may, if the Minister thinks fit, be granted a compassionate gratuity not exceeding $2 or one week's pay, whichever is the greater, for each completed year of his service which would be pensionable service if he were eligible for a pension under these Regulations.

(2) If an officer, who is qualified for a compassionate gratuity under paragraph (1), dies within 6 months of the date of his retirement from the civil service and before the Minister has approved such a compassionate gratuity the Minister may, if he thinks fit, grant to the officer's personal representatives a gratuity of an amount not exceeding the amount of the gratuity which might have been granted under paragraph (1) if he had not died before the Minister had approved the payment of a compassionate gratuity to him.

(3) If an officer, otherwise qualified for a compassionate gratuity under paragraph (1), dies while still in the service of Fiji, the Minister may, if he thinks fit, grant to the officer's personal representatives a gratuity of an amount not exceeding the amount of the gratuity which might have been granted to the officer under paragraph (1) if he had retired at the date of his death in any of the circumstances mentioned in that paragraph.

(4) Where, on or after 1 August 1966, an officer, who was in the service of the Government of the Republic of Fiji on that date is qualified for a compassionate gratuity under the provisions of paragraph (1), the Minister may, if he thinks fit, grant to such officer or his personal representative, as the case may be, a gratuity under the provisions of this regulation calculated on the basis of the pay of such officer at the date of his retirement from the civil service or the date of his death while still in the service of the Government of the Republic of Fiji, as the case may be, but relating only to the service of such officer prior to the said 1 August 1966.

##### cl 22 Officers in non-pensionable offices serving on 1 January 1938

(1) An officer who is eligible for a compassionate gratuity under regulation 21 and who was serving under the Government of the Republic of Fiji on 1 January 1938, may, if he retires in any of the circumstances mentioned in section 7(a) to (e) of the Act, and if the Minister thinks fit, be granted instead of a compassionate gratuity under regulation 21 —

(a) an allowance payable monthly at a rate not exceeding three-quarters of the pension for which he would have been eligible had he been employed in a pensionable office in Schedule 1; or

(b) unless he retires in the circumstances mentioned in section 7(e) of the Act, a capital sum equal to 3 of such monthly payments for each completed year of his service in Fiji which would be pensionable service if he were eligible for a pension under these regulations.

(2) If an officer who is qualified for the grant of a capital sum under paragraph (1) dies within 6 months of the date of his retirement from the civil service and before the Minister has approved the grant of such a capital sum the Minister may, if he thinks fit, grant to the officer's personal representatives a capital sum of an amount not exceeding the amount of the capital sum which might have been granted under paragraph (1)(b), if he had not died before the Minister had approved the grant of a capital sum to him or her.

(3) If an officer, otherwise qualified for the grant of a capital sum under paragraph (1), dies while still in the service of Fiji, the Minister may, if he thinks fit, grant to the officer's personal representatives a capital sum of an amount not exceeding the amount of the capital sum which might have been granted to the officer under paragraph (1)(b) if he had retired at the date of his death in any of the circumstances mentioned in that paragraph.

##### cl 23 Definition and special provisions

For the purposes of regulations 21 and 22 —

(a) “pay” includes any allowance which the Minister may think fit to include;

(b) regulation 12 may be disregarded, but directions may be given by the Minister, either generally or in any particular case, for disregarding periods of service prior to breaks of 3 years or more, to dismissal for misconduct or to voluntary resignation, or periods during which an officer has served less than the number of days prescribed in the directions, and such directions shall have effect as if they were contained in this regulation;

(c) an officer who, having held a non-pensionable office, is serving on probation in a pensionable office, shall be deemed to hold a non-pensionable office and his pay shall be deemed to be the pensionable emoluments of the pensionable office or the pay last received by him in respect of his service in a non-pensionable office, whichever shall be the greater;

(d) in the case of a daily paid officer, no monthly period in which it is shown that such officer has been in receipt of pay for less than 16 days shall be taken into account in calculating the length of qualifying and pensionable service, but save as aforesaid, every month in which any such officer was in Government employment shall be reckoned as a complete month of qualifying and pensionable service, irrespective of the number of days upon which such officer was absent from work;

(e) in the case of an officer who was in the service of the Government of the Republic of Fiji immediately prior to 1 August 1966, and has, by agreement between the Government of the Republic of Fiji and the Fiji Electricity Authority been transferred to the service of that Authority with effect from that date, all such officer's continuous service with that Authority on or after that date shall be deemed to be service under the Government of the Republic of Fiji otherwise than in a pensionable office, provided that—

(i) the break in such officer's service caused by such transfer, and any break in his service with the Fiji Electricity Authority caused by temporary suspension of his employment, not arising from misconduct or voluntary resignation, shall be disregarded for the purpose of calculating the length of such officer's period of continuous service;

(ii) any such continuous service with the Fiji Electricity Authority in respect of which such officer is liable to contribute to the Fiji National Provident Fund shall be taken into account as qualifying service but not as pensionable service and the compassionate gratuity for which he or his personal representative is eligible shall be calculated on the basis of the pay of such officer at the date of his retirement from the service of that Authority or, in the case of such officer dying while still in the service of that Authority, at the date of his death; and

(iii) in the case of such an officer being promoted or transferred to any office of employment in the service of that Authority, the conditions of his employment in which office are considered by the Minister to be equivalent to those applicable to an office in Schedule 1, the compassionate gratuity, allowance or capital sum for which such officer or his personal representative is eligible under the provisions of these Regulations shall be calculated on the basis of the pay of such officer at the date immediately prior to such promotion or transfer, and in the case of an allowance or capital sum for which such officer or his personal representative is eligible under the provisions of the last preceding regulation, shall relate only to the service of such officer prior to the date of such promotion or transfer.

##### cl 24 Gratuity and reduced pension

(1) Any officer to whom a pension is granted under the Act shall, if he has exercised his option as hereinafter provided, but not otherwise be paid in lieu of such pension, a pension at the rate of three-fourths of such pension, together with a gratuity equal to twelve and a half times the amount of the annual reduction so made in the pension.

(2) The option referred to in paragraph (1) shall be exercisable, and if it has been exercised may be revoked not later than the day immediately preceding the date of such officer's retirement, provided that the Minister may, if it appears to him equitable in all the circumstances so to do, allow him to exercise the option or revoke an option previously exercised at any time between that date and the actual date of award of pension under the Act.

(3) Subject to the provisions of paragraph (2), if an officer has exercised the option, his decision shall be irrevocable so far as concerns any pension to be granted to him under the Act.

(4) If an officer who has not exercised the option dies after he has finally retired but before a pension has been awarded under the Act, it shall be lawful for the Minister to grant a gratuity and a reduced pension as provided in paragraph (1), as if the officer before his death had exercised the option.

(5) The date of the exercise of the option by an officer shall be deemed to be the date of the receipt of his written notification addressed to the permanent secretary responsible for finance.

(6) A re-employed officer or pensioner shall be deemed to have exercised, or not to have exercised in respect of his service subsequent to re-employment, the said option, according as he exercised, or did not exercise, such option in respect of his previous service, unless the option was not available to him.

##### cl 25 Computations where officer elects to contribute to Fiji National Provident Fund

(1) Where a public officer has elected under section 46 of the Act to contribute to the Fiji National Provident Fund and has selected the option that a gratuity be computed under this regulation and paid to his credit in that Fund, the gratuity shall be computed in the manner specified in regulation 24(1) on the amount of pension he would have received had he selected the option to receive a pension computed in accordance with paragraph (2).

(2) Where the public officer has selected the option that a pension be computed in accordance with this regulation and paid to him upon his retirement from civil service in the circumstances described in section 46(2)(c)(ii) of the Act, the pension shall be computed in accordance with the provisions of regulation 8(1) as if the date of the exercise of the officer's election (as specified in section 46(2)(a) of the Act) were the date of his transfer from Fiji to other civil service.

##### cl 2 Early retirement

The Minister may grant an officer who retires in the circumstances specified in section 33(2) or (3) of the Act, a pension computed in accordance with regulation 1 reduced by l/600th for every month that his service falls short of the standard period of qualifying service for his rank.

##### cl 3 Gratuity and reduced pension

(1) An officer who is granted a pension under these Regulations may opt to receive, in lieu of that pension, a pension at the rate of 0.75 of that pension and a gratuity equal to 12.5 times the amount of the annual reduction so made in the pension.

(2) Subject to paragraph (3), the option referred to in paragraph (1) is exercisable by the officer and, if exercised, is revocable by the officer not later than the day immediately preceding the date of his retirement.

(3) The Minister may allow an officer to exercise the option referred to in paragraph (1) or revoke an option previously exercised at any time between the date referred to in paragraph (2) and the commencement of payment of the pension to the officer.

(4) If an officer who has not exercised his option under paragraph (1) dies (otherwise than in circumstances to which section 37 of the Act applies)—

(a) whilst he is still serving but has qualified by service for the grant of a pension; or

(b) after he has retired but before the commencement of payment of the pension,

the Minister may grant a gratuity and reduced pension as provided for in paragraph (1) to his widow, or if there is no widow to his personal representatives for the benefit of any dependent child or children, as if he had exercised the option immediately before his death.

##### cl 4 Gratuity

The Minister may grant to an officer who retires having completed 5 years or more qualifying service for his rank a gratuity not exceeding one month's pay for each complete year of his service.

##### cl 5 Gratuity where officer dies

When an officer dies whilst serving in the Regular Force (otherwise than in circumstances to which section 37 of the Act applies) the Minister may grant to the personal representatives of the officer a gratuity in respect of any period of leave accrued to but not taken by that officer of an amount equal to the amount the officer would have received during that period at the current rate of pay for the rank the officer held at the time of his death.

#### PART 2 SOLDIERS [ss 6–10]

##### cl 6 Gratuities to soldiers

(1) Subject to paragraph (2), the Minister may grant to a soldier who retires having completed a minimum period of 12 years’ continuous service in the Regular Force a gratuity equal to one year's pay for the rank the soldier held at the time of his retirement.

(2) Service counted towards a pension or benefit under this Part or Part 1 of these Regulations does not count as service towards a gratuity under paragraph (1).

##### cl 7 Re-engagement benefits

(1) Subject to paragraphs (2) and (3), the Minister may grant to a soldier who has completed a minimum period of 12 years’ continuous service in the Regular Force, and is without break re-engaged for a further period of such service, on each successive re-engagement, benefit at the rate of one month's pay for the rank he held immediately prior to his re-engagement for each complete year of such intended re-engagement.

(2) If a soldier who has accepted a re-engagement benefit under paragraph (1) retires from the Regular Force prior to the completion of his current engagement, he shall refund the whole or such portion of that re-engagement benefit as the Minister determines.

(3) A break in continuous service which can be disregarded under regulation 12 of the regulations contained in Schedule 2 to the Act may likewise be disregarded for the purpose of paragraph (1).

##### cl 8 Gratuity and reduced pension

(1) The Minister may grant to a soldier who retires having completed a minimum period of 21 years qualifying service a pension at the annual rate of 1/600th of the current pay for the rank the soldier held at the time of his retirement for each complete month of service.

(2) A soldier who is granted a pension under paragraph (1) may opt to receive, in lieu of that pension, a pension at the rate of 0.75 of that pension and a gratuity equal to 12.5 times the amount of the annual reduction so made in the pension.

(3) Subject to paragraph (4), the option referred to in paragraph (2) is exercisable and, if exercised by the soldier, is revocable by the soldier, not later than the day immediately preceding the date of his retirement.

(4) The Minister may allow a soldier to exercise the option, referred to in paragraph (2) or revoke an option previously exercised at any time between the date referred to in paragraph (3) and the commencement of payment of the pension to the soldier.

(5) If a soldier who has not exercised his option under paragraph (2) dies (otherwise than in circumstances to which section 37 of the Act applies)—

(a) whilst he is still serving in the Regular Force but has qualified by service for the grant of a pension; or

(b) after he has retired but before the commencement of payment of the pension,

the Minister may grant a gratuity and reduced pension as provided in paragraph (2) to the soldier's widow, or if there is no widow, to his personal representatives for the benefit of any dependent child or children, as if he had exercised the option immediately before his death.

##### cl 9 Gratuity in case of death whilst serving

(1) Where a soldier who has completed 12 or more years of qualifying service dies whilst serving as a soldier (otherwise than in the circumstances to which section 37 of the Act applies), the Minister may grant to his personal representatives a gratuity of an amount equal to one year's pay for the rank he held at the time of his death.

(2) Where a soldier who has not completed 12 years qualifying service dies whilst serving as a soldier (otherwise than in the circumstances to which section 37 of the Act applies), the Minister may grant to his personal representatives a gratuity not exceeding such number of months pay, at the rate thereof at the date of his death, as he had completed years of continuous regular service.

##### cl 10 Gratuity in case of death after discharge

Where a soldier dies (otherwise than by reason of injury sustained or disease contracted in circumstances to which section 37 of the Act applies) after having been granted or having become eligible for a pension or a pension and gratuity under regulation (8), and the total of the sums paid to him between his retirement and his death is less than the amount of the gratuity provided for by regulation 9(1), the Minister may grant to his personal representatives a gratuity equal to the difference.

### SCHEDULE 6 PENSIONS (REPUBLIC OF FIJI MILITARY FORCES) (ACTIVE OPERATIONS) REGULATIONS

#### PART 1 DEATH [ss 1, 2]

##### cl 1 Pensions on death of an officer or soldier

Where an officer or soldier dies as a direct result of injury sustained or disease contracted in the circumstances specified in section 37 of the Act, the Minister may grant—

(a) if the deceased officer or soldier leaves a widow but no dependent child or children, a pension to the widow for his lifetime but if he remarries until his marriage of an amount equal to 33.33 of the officer's or soldier's current pay at the time of his death;

(b) if the deceased officer or soldier leaves a widow and a dependent child or children who are in the widow's custody, a further pension to the widow in respect of each such child of amount equal to—

(i) 6% of the officer's or soldier's current pay at the time of his death in respect of each child up to a maximum of 3; and

(ii) 3% of the officer's or soldier's current pay at the time of his death in respect of each additional child;

(c) if the deceased officer or soldier leaves a dependent child or children but does not leave a widow, or if survived by a widow he does not have the custody of such child or children, a pension at the rates specified in paragraph (b) payable to the person having the custody of such child or children;

(d) if the deceased officer or soldier leaves a parent or parents who were wholly or mainly dependent upon him for support at the time of his death — a pension to each such surviving parent of an amount equal to 12.5% of the current pay for the rank held by the officer or soldier at the time of his death.

##### cl 2 Reduction or cessation of pension

A pension granted under regulation 1(b) or (c) in respect of a child, ceases if that child dies before ceasing to be a dependent child.

#### PART 2 DISABILITY [ss 3–5]

##### cl 3 Pension on disability of officer or soldier

(1) Where an officer or soldier is disabled as a direct result of injury sustained or disease contracted in the circumstances specified in section 37 of the Act, the Minister may grant to him on or after his retirement, a pension at the annual rate appropriate to his case as provided in this regulation.

(2) In the case of an unmarried officer or soldier whose capacity to contribute to his own support is totally impaired, the Minister may grant him a pension (hereinafter referred to as a “total impairment pension”) of an amount equal to 40% of the current pay for the rank he held at the time when he sustained such injury or contracted such disease.

(3) In the case of an unmarried officer or soldier whose capacity to contribute to his own support is not totally impaired but is impaired to the extent (expressed as a percentage) shown in column 1 of the following Table, he may be granted a pension at the annual rate of the percentage of his total impairment pension shown in column 2 of that Table—

| | | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TABLE | | |
| Column 1 | | Column 2 |
| 99% to 75% | | 75% |
| 74% to 50% | | 50% |
| 49% to 20% | | 25% |

except that an officer or soldier can not be granted a pension under this paragraph if the impairment of his or her capacity is less than 20%.

(4) In the case of an officer or soldier who is married and whose wife lives with him, the pension appropriate to his case under paragraph (2) or (3) may be increased by 31.25% of that pension.

(5) In the case of an officer or soldier who has a dependent child or children, the pension appropriate to his case under paragraph (2) or (3) may be increased (in addition to any increase under paragraph (4)) by 15% of that pension in respect of each such dependent child up to a maximum of 3 and by 7.5% of that pension in respect of each additional dependent child.

(6) In the case of an officer or soldier who has a parent or parents wholly or mainly dependent upon him, the pension appropriate to his case under paragraphs (2) or (3) may be increased (in addition to any increases under paragraph (4) or (5)) by 31.25% of that pension in respect of each such parent.

##### cl 4 Conditions on the grant of pension

The Minister may impose such conditions as he may think fit in respect of a pension granted by him under regulation 3 including, but not limited to, a condition that the officer or soldier whilst in receipt of the pension is to undergo medical examination in such manner and at such times as the Minister specifies.

##### cl 5 Variation of amount of pension

(1) Where upon medical examination pursuant to regulation 4, it is determined that the degree of impairment of the officer's or soldier's capacity to contribute to his own support is different from that on which the amount of the pension was computed, the amount of the pension shall be varied by the Minister as appropriate to the case as from the date of that determination, but the officer or soldier is not liable to repay or entitled to receive any sum in respect of pension paid prior to that determination in consequence of the determination.

(2) Where—

(a) the wife of an officer or soldier in respect of whom an increase of pension was granted to him under regulation 3(4) dies; or

(b) any dependent child in respect of whom an increase in pension was granted to him dies before ceasing to be a dependent child; or

(c) a parent of an officer or soldier in respect of whom an increase of pension was granted to him under regulation 3(6) dies,

the pension of that officer or soldier may be reduced in consequence thereof by such amount as the Minister may determine, not exceeding the amount by which the pension was originally increased.
