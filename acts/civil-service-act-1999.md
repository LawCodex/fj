## PART 2 CIVIL SERVICE [ss 4– 10]

### s 4 Civil Service Values

In addition to the values set out in section 123 of the Constitution, the civil service shall abide by the following values—

(a) the civil service respects the values, policies, rights and freedoms set out in the Constitution and Human Rights and Anti-Discrimination Commission Act 2009;

(b) employment decisions in the civil service are made without patronage, favouritism or political influence, and appointments and promotions are made on the basis of merit after an open, competitive selection process;

(c) the civil service provides adequate and equal opportunities for training and advancement in the civil service;

(d) the civil service carries out the Government’s policies and programmes effectively and efficiently and with due economy;

(e) the civil service provides a working environment that is free from discrimination;

(f) the civil service is apolitical, performing its functions in a neutral, impartial and professional way;

(g) the civil service is fully accountable, within the framework of the Constitution and the Financial Management Act 2004, to the Government, the Parliament and the people of Fiji;

(h) the civil service is responsible to the Government for providing frank, honest, comprehensive, accurate and timely advice and implementing the Government’s policies and programmes;

(i) the civil service has the highest ethical standards, particularly for integrity and honesty;

(j) the civil service delivers services fairly, effectively and courteously;

(k) the civil service develops and maintains leadership of the highest quality;

(l) the civil service provides a fair, flexible and rewarding workplace; and

(m) the civil service focuses on achieving results and managing performance.

[s 4 subst Act 31 of 2016 s 32, effective 1 December 2016]

### s 5 Regulations and directions about the Civil Service Values

(1) The Commission may make regulations, or give written directions—

(a) for the purpose of ensuring that all permanent secretaries uphold the Civil Service Values; and

(b) as to the scope or application of the Civil Service Values.

[subs (1) am Act 2 of 2016 ss 5, 19 and 20, effective 16 February 2016]

(1A) All permanent secretaries must comply with any regulations prescribed or directions issued by the Commission under subsection (1).

[subs (1) insrt Act 2 of 2016 s 5, effective 16 February 2016]

(2) The Civil Service Values have effect subject to any regulations made or directions given under subsection (1), including any regulations or directions restricting the scope or application of the Civil Service Values or resolving potential inconsistencies between them.

[s 5 am Act 2 of 2016 s 20, effective 16 February 2016]

### s 6 Civil Service Code of Conduct

(1) An employee must behave honestly and with integrity in the course of employment in the civil service.

[subs (1) am Act 2 of 2016 s 20, effective 16 February 2016]

(2) An employee must act with care and diligence in the course of employment in the civil service.

[subs (2) am Act 2 of 2016 s 20, effective 16 February 2016]

(3) An employee, when acting in the course of employment in the civil service, must treat everyone with respect and courtesy, and without coercion or harassment of any kind.

(4) An employee, when acting in the course of employment in the civil service, must comply with all applicable Acts and subordinate legislation.

[subs (4) am Act 2 of 2016 s 20, effective 16 February 2016]

(4A)

[subs (4A) rep Act 4 of 2015 s 18, effective 11 September 2015]

(5) An employee must comply with all lawful and reasonable directions given by persons in authority in the employee’s ministry, department or parliamentary body.

[subs (5) am Act 17 of 2004 Sch 1, effective 1 January 2005]

(6) An employee must maintain appropriate confidentiality about dealings that the employee has with any Minister or any member of the staff of a Minister.

(7) An employee must disclose, and take reasonable steps to avoid, any conflict of interest (real or apparent) in connection with employment in the civil service.

[subs (7) am Act 2 of 2016 s 20, effective 16 February 2016]

(8) An employee must use Government resources and assets in a proper way.

(9) A person must not, in the course of or in connection with employment in the civil service, provide false or misleading information in response to a request for information that is made for official purposes.

[subs (9) am Act 2 of 2016 s 20, effective 16 February 2016]

(10) An employee must not make improper use of official information or of the employee's duties, status, power or authority in order to gain, or seek to gain, a benefit or advantage for the employee or for anyone else.

(11) An employee must not, except in the course of his or her duties as an employee, or with the express authority of the permanent secretary of his or her ministry, department or parliamentary body, give or disclose, directly or indirectly, any information about public business or anything of which the employee has official knowledge.

[subs (11) am Act 17 of 2004 Sch 1, effective 1 January 2005; Act 31 of 2016 s 32, effective 1 December 2016]

(12) An employee must at all times behave in a way that upholds the Civil Service Values and the integrity and good reputation of the civil service.

[subs (12) am Act 2 of 2016 s 20, effective 16 February 2016]

(13) An employee on duty overseas must at all times behave in a way that upholds the good reputation of the State.

(14) An employee must comply with any other conduct requirement prescribed by regulations, specified in directions or required of the employee by his or her permanent secretary.

[subs (14) am Act 31 of 2016 s 32, effective 1 December 2016]

(15) In this section “employee” includes a wage earner.

### s 7 Ground for discipline

A breach of the Civil Service Code of Conduct, the provisions of this Act or any regulations or written directions issued under this Act, is a ground for disciplinary action or removal.

[s 7 subst Act 31 of 2016 s 32, effective 1 December 2016]

### s 8 Responsibilities of permanent secretaries

(1) A permanent secretary must uphold and promote the Civil Service Values and is bound by the Civil Service Code of Conduct.

[subs (1) am Act 17 of 2004 Sch 1, effective 1 January 2005; Act 2 of 2016 s 20, effective 16 February 2016; Act 31 of 2016 s 32, effective 1 December 2016]

(2) The permanent secretary of a ministry or department is responsible to the relevant Minister or Ministers concerned for the efficient, effective and economical management of the ministry or department.

[subs (2) am Act 17 of 2004 Sch 1, effective 1 January 2005; Act 31 of 2016 s 32, effective 1 December 2016]

(3) Permanent secretaries are collectively responsible for providing leadership of the highest quality for the civil service.

[subs (3) am Act 17 of 2004 Sch 1, effective 1 January 2005; Act 2 of 2016 s 20, effective 16 February 2016; Act 31 of 2016 s 32, effective 1 December 2016]

(4) In recognition of their collective responsibility, permanent secretaries must work together to—

(a) provide direction on the future of the civil service;

(b) motivate employees to reach their full potential; and

(c) initiate good management practice.

[subs (4) am Act 17 of 2004 Sch 1, effective 1 January 2005; Act 2 of 2016 s 20, effective 16 February 2016; Act 31 of 2016 s 32, effective 1 December 2016]

## PART 3 PUBLIC SERVICE COMMISSION [ss 11– 17]

### s 11 Functions of the Commission

In addition to its functions under section 126 of the Constitution, the Commission must perform the following functions—

(a) manage the performance of the permanent secretaries;

(b) consider and determine appeals against decisions made by a permanent secretary in accordance with guidelines, directions, policies and other rules or regulations issued by the Commission;

(c) manage the remuneration of permanent secretaries;

(d) provide a written report to the Minister on any relevant matter or issue, in accordance with the Minister’s request; and

(e) do all other things necessary for the performance of its functions and the implementation of this Act.

[s 11 subst Act 2 of 2016 s 7, effective 16 February 2016]

### s 13 Inquiries by Commission

(1) The Commission may, on its own initiative, conduct an inquiry into an alleged breach of the Civil Service Code of Conduct and Civil Service Values by any permanent secretary.

[subs (1) subst Act 2 of 2016 ss 9 and 20, effective 16 February 2016]

(2) For the purpose of an inquiry, the Commission, or a person authorised by the Commission, may—

(a) enter the premises of any department;

(b) require production of, examine and copy any document in the possession of a department;

(c) interview any employee; or

(d) interview anyone else who can provide information relevant to the inquiry.

[subs (2) am Act 2 of 2016 s 19, effective 16 February 2016]

(3) Permanent secretaries and other employees must give the Commission any assistance it reasonably requires to conduct an inquiry.

[subs (3) am Act 17 of 2004 Sch 1, effective 1 January 2005; Act 2 of 2016 s 19, effective 16 February 2016]

(4) Without limiting subsection (3), the Commission may require an employee to answer any question relevant to the inquiry.

[subs (4) am Act 2 of 2016 s 19, effective 16 February 2016]

(5) Subsection (4) does not prevent an employee from asserting a claim of privilege against self-incrimination in accordance with law.

(6) For the purpose of an inquiry under this section the Commission has the same powers and authority to summon witnesses and to admit and receive evidence as are given to the commissioners of a commission of inquiry by section 9 of the Commissions of Inquiry Act 1946, and sections 14 and 17 of that Act apply, with necessary changes, in relation to the powers and authority given to the Commission by this subsection.

### s 21 Powers of the permanent secretary

In accordance with section 127(7) and (8) of the Constitution, a permanent secretary must exercise all of his or her powers in relation to the employment, recruitment, discipline and removal of any staff in accordance with guidelines, directions, policies and other rules or regulations issued by the Commission.

[s 21 subst Act 2 of 2016 s 14, effective 16 February 2016]

### s 22 Improperly influencing the Commission

Any person who seeks to, directly or indirectly, improperly influence the Commission or a member, employee or agent of the Commission commits an offence and is liable upon conviction to a fine not exceeding $20,000.

[s 22 subst Act 2 of 2016 s 15, effective 16 February 2016]

### s 23 Protection of members of the Commission

A member, employee or agent of the Commission will not be liable for any act done or omitted to be done by that person in good faith, in relation to the performance or purported performance of a function or the exercise of a power, of the Commission.

[s 23 subst Act 2 of 2016 s 16, effective 16 February 2016]
