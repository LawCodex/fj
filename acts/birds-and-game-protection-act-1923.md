## PART 1 PRELIMINARY [ss 1, 2]

### s 1 Short title

This Act may be cited as the Birds and Game Protection Act 1923.

### s 2 Interpretation

In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires—

**close season** in relation to any particular kind of game means the period stated in Schedule 3 to be the close season for that kind of game;

[def am Ordinance 18 of 1953 s 2, effective 10 July 1953; Ordinance 26 of 1966 s 2, effective 4 August 1966]

**game** means any bird or animal specified in Schedule 2; and

**protected bird** means any bird other than those specified in Schedule 1 but does not include any tame or domestic bird or any bird defined as game in this Act.

## PART 2 PROTECTED BIRDS [ss 3– 5]

### s 3 Protection of birds, nests and eggs

Any person who—

(a) wilfully kills, wounds or takes any protected bird;

(b) takes, removes, injures or destroys any nest or egg of such protected bird;

(c) exposes for sale or knowingly has in his or her possession any such protected bird or any part of such protected bird killed, wounded or taken in Fiji subsequent to 27 December 1923, or the nest or eggs of any such protected bird taken in Fiji;

(d) exports or attempts to export the skin or plumage of any such protected bird killed, wounded or taken, or the nest or eggs of any such protected bird taken in Fiji,

shall be guilty of an offence against this Act.

### s 4 Onus of proof on person in possession

When any person is proceeded against for exposing or offering for sale or having in his or her possession or exporting or attempting to export any protected bird or any part of any protected bird or the nest or eggs of any protected bird, the proof that such protected bird was killed, wounded or taken, or the nest or eggs taken, without the limits of Fiji shall be on such person.

### s 5 Authority for scientific purposes

Notwithstanding anything contained in this Act, the Minister may by writing under his or her hand authorise any person for such time and subject to such conditions as he or she thinks fit for scientific or other purposes to kill, take or keep any protected bird or to take or remove the nest or eggs of any protected bird, and he or she may also authorise any such person to export the skins or any part of such protected bird.

[s 5 am LN 112 of 1970 O 102, effective 8 October 1970; Act 10 of 1978 s 2, effective 6 October 1978]

## PART 3 GAME [ss 6– 11]

### s 6 Game not to be killed etc without licence

Every person commits an offence against this Act who kills, wounds or takes, or attempts to kill, wound or take, any game, or who uses any gun, net or other instrument whatsoever for such purpose, without having previously obtained a licence as hereinafter provided.

### s 7 Close season for game

(1) No person shall, save with the permission in writing of the Minister, during the close season for any particular kind of game kill, wound or take game of that kind or purchase game of that kind or expose or offer for sale game of that kind.

[subs (1) am LN 112 of 1970 O 102, effective 8 October 1970]

(2) Any person contravening the provisions of subsection (1) shall be guilty of an offence against this Act.

[s 7 subst Ordinance 18 of 1953 s 3, effective 10 July 1953]

### s 8 Onus of proof

When any person is prosecuted for an offence against the provisions of section 7, the proof that the game in respect of which the offence is charged was killed, wounded or taken otherwise than during the close season shall lie on such person.

### s 9 Licences

(1) Licences to kill or take game shall be in force for one year only and may be issued in the form contained in Schedule 4 by a licensing officer appointed by the Chief Accountant by notice in the Gazette on payment of such fee therefore as the Minister may by notice in the Gazette prescribe. All such licence fees shall be paid into the Consolidation Fund.

[subs (1) am Ordinance 2 of 1945 s 67, effective 1 May 1945; Ordinance 27 of 1959 s 2, effective 9 October 1959; LN 112 of 1970 O 102, effective 8 October 1970]

(2) A person holding a licence to kill or take game issued under subsection (1) shall not in any one day take or kill more than such number of birds as may for the purposes of this subsection be specified by the Minister from time to time by notice in the Gazette. Any person contravening the provisions of this subsection shall be guilty of an offence.

[subs (2) subst Ordinance 18 of 1953 s 4, effective 10 July 1953; am LN 112 of 1970 O 102, effective 8 October 1970]

(3) Every person holding a licence to possess, use or carry arms and ammunition issued under the provisions of the Arms and Ammunition Act 2003 and a licence to kill or take game issued under the provisions of subsection (1), shall carry the same on his or her person at all times whilst engaged in killing or taking game, and shall produce the same on demand by a game ranger. Any person failing to comply with the provisions of this subsection shall be guilty of an offence against this Act.

[subs (3) insrt Ordinance 15 of 1939 s 3, effective 20 September 1939]

### s 10 Game rangers

The Minister may appoint any person or persons to act as game rangers in any Division or Divisions, and may prescribe the duties and fix the remuneration of such persons. Any person interfering with, obstructing or resisting a game ranger in the execution of his or her duty shall be guilty of an offence against this Act.

[s 10 am LN 112 of 1970 O 102, effective 8 October 1970]

### s 11 Definition of reserved area

(1) Notwithstanding that any person may have been licensed to kill or take game, the Minister may, by order, define an area or areas, to be specifically described in such order, within which it shall be unlawful for any person without the written permission of the Minister to shoot, capture, take or destroy any particular kind of game, or to take or destroy the nest or eggs of any such game, provided that the areas defined in Schedule 5 shall, until revoked or varied by order by the Minister, be reserved areas within the meaning of this section in respect of the particular kinds of game specified therein.

[subs (1) am LN 112 of 1970 O 102, effective 8 October 1970]

(2) Any person unlawfully shooting, capturing, taking or destroying, or attempting to shoot, capture, take or destroy, any game, or taking or destroying the nest or eggs of any game, within any such area, shall be guilty of an offence against this Act.

## PART 4 GENERAL [ss 12– 18]

### s 12 Offender must give name etc

In the case of an offence against this Act any person may require the offender to give his or her name, description and place of abode, and if such offender does not truly give his or her name, description and place of abode, he or she shall be guilty of an offence against this Act in addition to that which he or she shall have been found committing.

### s 13 Amendment of Schedules

The Minister may from time to time by order replace, add to, revoke, amend or vary any of the Schedules.

[s 13 am Ordinance 18 of 1953 s 5, effective 10 July 1953; LN 112 of 1970 O 102, effective 8 October 1970]

### s 14 Offences committed at sea — how heard

An offence committed against this Act at sea shall be deemed to have been committed on the sea coast adjoining the sea, or to have been committed in any place where the offender is found, and may be heard and determined accordingly.

### s 15 General penalty

Every person who is guilty of an offence against this Act shall be liable to a fine not exceeding $50 and in default of payment of such fine to imprisonment for any period not exceeding 3 months.

[s 15 am Ordinance 2 of 1945 s 67, effective 1 May 1945; Ordinance 26 of 1966 s 4, effective 4 August 1966]

### s 16 Forfeiture

Any protected bird or game, or any part of any protected bird or game, or the nest or eggs of any protected bird or game, in respect of which a conviction takes place shall be forfeited, and any gun, net or other instrument used for the purpose of the offence may also be forfeited.

### s 17 Accused must prove licence

In any prosecution under this Act charging any person with killing or taking game without a licence, the charge shall be held to be proved if it is shown to the satisfaction of the court that the accused did actually kill or take game, unless the accused can produce his or her licence in court or can otherwise show to the satisfaction of the court that such licence was duly taken out.

### s 18 Powers of rangers

(1) Whenever any game ranger thinks it necessary for the purpose of verifying the licence or registration of any person, or suspects upon reasonable grounds that any person has committed an offence against this Act, he or she may inspect and search any baggage, package, vessel or vehicle belonging to such person, and if there is found as a consequence of such search any protected bird or game, or any portion thereof, or the nest or eggs of any protected bird or game, appearing to have been obtained in contravention of this Act the same may be seized and taken before a court.

(2) Any game ranger may enter upon any land for the purpose of carrying out the provisions of this Act or of preventing or detecting offences thereunder.

(3) The production by a game ranger of his or her letter of appointment shall be sufficient evidence of such appointment.

[subs (3) insrt Ordinance 15 of 1939 s 5, effective 20 September 1939]
