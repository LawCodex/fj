# PATENTS ACT 1879

AN ACT TO MAKE PROVISION FOR THE ISSUE OF LETTERS PATENT FOR INVENTIONS

## s 1 Short title

This Act may be cited as the Patents Act 1879.

## s 2 Interpretation

In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires—

**invention** means any manner of new manufacture and every new process of manufacture and every new method of application of known processes and improvements in any known process; and

**inventor** includes the heirs, executors, administrators or assigns of an inventor.

## s 3 Exclusive right to invention

Every inventor shall be entitled under the conditions and restrictions hereinafter enacted to the sole exclusive right of and in his or her invention.

## s 3A Exceptions to infringement of patent rights

Notwithstanding section 3, it is not an infringement of patents in pharmaceuticals for any person to make, construct, use or sell the patented invention in respect of a pharmaceutical product or substance, solely for uses reasonably related to the development and submission of information required under any law of Fiji or of another country that regulates the manufacture, construction, use or sale of such pharmaceutical product or substance.

[s 3A insrt Act 21 of 2002 s 2, effective 1 January 2003]

## s 4 Form of letters patent and privileges conferred

The right and privilege granted to inventors shall be conferred by letters patent under the seal of Fiji in the prescribed form whereby the inventor shall be entitled to the sole and exclusive privilege of using, selling or making his or her said invention in Fiji and of authorising others so to do for the term of 14 years from the date of the letters patent.

[s 4 am Ordinance 26 of 1967 s 2, effective 1 July 1968]

## s 5 Letters patent not to be granted in certain cases

No person shall be entitled to letters patent—

(a) if the invention is of no utility;

(b) if the invention at the time of presenting the petition was not a new invention;

(c) if the petitioner is not the true and first inventor thereof; or

(d) if the petition or any specification contains a wilfully false statement,

and any person interested may make an application to the High Court on any such grounds for the cancellation or revocation of any letters patent.

[s 5 am Ordinance 30 of 1959 s 2, effective 9 October 1959 ]

## s 6 Application for letters patent

Every person desirous of obtaining letters patent under this Act shall file in the office of the Solicitor-General a petition in the prescribed form signed by him or her or his or her agent or attorney setting forth the residence of the inventor and the title of the invention. To the petition shall be annexed a specification (or instrument in writing) particularly describing and ascertaining the nature of the said invention and the manner in which the same is to be produced or performed, and with such specification or instrument in writing shall be filed the drawings necessary to elucidate the same, and such petition and all documents and drawings accompanying the same shall be in duplicate.

[s 6 am Ordinance 30 of 1959 s 3, effective 9 October 1959 ; Ordinance 26 of 1967 s 3, effective 1 July 1968; Decree 55 of 2012 s 3, effective 9 July 2012]

## s 7 Declaration

Every petition and specification particularly describing the nature of an invention shall be accompanied by a declaration in writing signed by the petitioner or his or her agent or attorney for him or her in the prescribed form.

[s 7 am Ordinance 26 of 1967 s 4, effective 1 July 1968]

## s 8 Petition, specification etc, to be recorded

The Solicitor-General shall cause to be filed every petition and specification with accompanying drawings, if any, explaining the nature of any invention and also any certificate granted by the Attorney-General and every letters patent issued and a memorandum of any order relating thereto.

[s 8 am Ordinance 30 of 1959 s 4, effective 9 October 1959 ; Decree 55 of 2012 s 3, effective 9 July 2012]

## s 9 Records may be inspected

Such records shall be open to the inspection of all persons at any reasonable hour upon the payment of a fee of $1, and any person may have a copy of any document recorded on payment for the same at the rate of 10 cents for every folio of 72 words.

[s 9 am Ordinance 30 of 1959 s 5, effective 9 October 1959 ]

## s 10 Certified copies to be received as evidence

Copies of any documents so recorded certified as correct by the Solicitor-General shall be received in evidence in any court of law in Fiji.

[s 10 am Ordinance 30 of 1959 s 6, effective 9 October 1959 ; Decree 55 of 2012 s 3, effective 9 July 2012]

## s 11 Specifications may be amended

A specification may be amended on application to the Solicitor-General or, if the Solicitor-General refuses to allow such amendment, the same may be amended by leave of the Attorney-General, and an amended specification shall, except as to suits and proceedings pending at the date of amendment, have the same effect as though it had been the specification originally filed, provided that no extension or enlargement of any exclusive privilege before acquired shall be affected by such amendment.

[s 11 subst Ordinance 7 of 1882 s 1, effective 5 August 1882 ; am Ordinance 30 of 1959 s 7, effective 9 October 1959 ; LN 112 of 1970 O 153, effective 8 October 1970; Decree 55 of 2012 s 3, effective 9 July 2012]

## s 12 Provisional certificate

When any applicant has filed his or her petition for letters patent the Solicitor-General shall refer the same to the Attorney-General with one of the duplicates of such petition and the specification and all other accompanying documents and drawings and a certificate of the filing of the petition, and the Attorney-General shall, if he or she deems the invention as prima facie entitled to protection, issue a certificate to that effect as in the prescribed form, and the inventor shall, subject to the provisions of this Act be protected for a period of 6 months in like manner as by letters patent, provided nevertheless that it shall be competent to the Attorney-General to extend the period of such protection pending the decision as to any opposition made to the granting of any letters patent as to him or her shall seem necessary.

[s 12 am Ordinance 30 of 1959 s 8, effective 9 October 1959 ; Ordinance 26 of 1967 s 5, effective 1 July 1968; Decree 55 of 2012 s 3, effective 9 July 2012]

## s 13 Appeal from Attorney-General

If in any case the Attorney-General refuses to issue such certificate as hereinbefore provided an appeal shall lie from the decision of the Attorney-General so refusing such certificate to the High Court who may direct the same to be issued by the Solicitor-General in such manner and subject to such conditions and restrictions as shall seem fit, provided that—

(a) such application shall be made to the High Court within one month from the time of the decision of the Attorney-General refusing a certificate being made known to the applicant for letters patent or to his or her agent or attorney;

(b) any certificate to be issued by direction of the High Court shall be issued within 3 months from the date of such appeal and upon the issue of such certificate like proceedings shall be had and taken as if such certificate had been issued by the Attorney-General.

[s 13 am LN 112 of 1970 O 153, effective 8 October 1970; Decree 55 of 2012 s 3, effective 9 July 2012]

## s 14 Procedure where no opposition is made to application

Within 2 months of the issue of the certificate of the Attorney-General or, where the Attorney-General has refused to issue such certificate, from the date of issue of the same by the Solicitor-General as provided in section 13, the applicant for letters patent shall give notice in the prescribed form twice in the Gazette and twice in one other newspaper published in Fiji and, if no notice of opposition to the application for letters patent be sent to the Solicitor-General within 3 months of the date of publication of the last of such notices, the Solicitor-General shall report such fact to the Attorney-General and Attorney-General shall, within 3 months from the date of such report, cause letters patent to be issued with such reservations, provisos and conditions as may be deemed fit.

[s 14 am Ordinance 7 of 1882 s 2, effective 5 August 1882 ; Ordinance 30 of 1959 s 9, effective 9 October 1959 ; Ordinance 26 of 1967 s 6, effective 1 July 1968; LN 112 of 1970 O 153, effective 8 October 1970; Decree 55 of 2012 s 3, effective 9 July 2012]

## s 15 Where application is opposed. Holder of certificate may appeal

Any person desiring to oppose such application shall within 3 months of the date of the first notice of the application for letters patent provided for in section 14 give notice in writing of his or her opposition to such application and of the grounds thereof to the Solicitor-General who shall, after hearing the parties to and against such application and such witnesses as he or she may deem necessary, decide the same and intimate his or her decision to the Attorney-General who shall within 3 months from the date of such intimation, if the same be favourable to the person holding a provisional certificate, direct the issue of letters patent to such person with such reservations, provisos and conditions as may be meet. In case the decision be adverse to the party holding the provisional certificate he or she may appeal against such decision to the High Court which shall within 3 months from the date of such appeal either direct the issue of letters patent to the appellant subject to such reservations, provisos and conditions as it may deem fit or make such other order as may be meet.

[s 15 subst Ordinance 7 of 1882 s 3, effective 5 August 1882 ; am Ordinance 30 of 1959 s 10, effective 9 October 1959 ; Ordinance 26 of 1967 s 7, effective 1 July 1968 ; LN 112 of 1970 O 153, effective 8 October 1970; Decree 55 of 2012 s 3, effective 9 July 2012]

## s 16 Where letters patent have been granted for inventions already patented outside Fiji

Where upon application made under this Act letters patent are granted in Fiji in respect of any invention first invented in any other country or place and a patent or like privilege for the exclusive use of such invention has been granted in any such other country or place before the grant of letters patent in Fiji, all rights and privileges under such letters patent last mentioned shall (notwithstanding any term therein limited) cease and be void immediately upon the expiration or other determination of such patent or like privilege granted in such other country or place or, where more than one such patent or like privilege is obtained, then upon the expiration or other determination of the term which shall first expire or be determined of such several patents or like privileges, provided always that no letters patent shall be of any validity if issued for or in respect of any invention for which any such patent or like privilege as aforesaid shall have been obtained in any such other country or place and which shall be granted in Fiji after the expiration of the term for which such patent or privilege was granted or was in force.

[s 16 subst Ordinance 7 of 1882 s 4, effective 5 August 1882 ]

## s 17 Letters patent to be registered

All letters patent and assignments of letters patent shall be registered in the office of the Solicitor-General on payment to him or her of the prescribed fees.

[s 17 subst Ordinance 37 of 1966 s 62, effective 13 January 1967; am Decree 55 of 2012 s 3, effective 9 July 2012]

## s 18 Attorney-General may annul letters patent

Letters patent may be annulled by the Attorney-General if the same be proved to be prejudicial to the public interests or if the special conditions on which the same have been granted are not observed. Notice of any letters patent being annulled and the cause thereof shall be inserted in the Gazette.

[s 18 am Ordinance 30 of 1959 s 12, effective 9 October 1959 ; Ordinance 26 of 1967 s 8, effective 1 July 1968; LN 112 of 1970 O 153, effective 8 October 1970]

## s 19 Letters patent to be for one invention only

No letters patent shall include several distinct and separate inventions but where one invention is applicable to the improvement of several manufactures or where several inventions are applicable to the improvement of one and the same manufacture the whole may be included in the same letters patent.

## s 20 Notice of proceeding under section 5 to be published

Any person intending to apply to the High Court under the provisions of section 5 for cancellation or revocation of letters patent shall give at least one month's notice of his or her intention to proceed under section 5 by notice in the Gazette and such notice shall be published in not less than 3 issues of the Gazette and the period of one month before mentioned shall commence from the date of the publication of the last of such notices and such publication shall be in lieu of service of any writ or process required by the rules of the High Court.

[s 20 am Ordinance 30 of 1959 s 13, effective 9 October 1959]

## s 21 Copy of order of High Court forwarded to Solicitor-General

If upon any of the grounds set out in section 5, the High Court shall order any letters patent to be cancelled or revoked, a copy of such order shall be forthwith forwarded by the Chief Registrar of the High Court to the Solicitor-General who shall record the same, and all rights under any letters patent so cancelled or revoked shall cease.

[ s 21 am Decree 55 of 2012 s 3, effective 9 July 2012]

## s 22 High Court may order amendments

The Court may order any specification or petition to be amended and may reserve its decision for a time sufficient to allow of such amendment to be effected and thereafter give judgment on such terms as to costs and otherwise as may seem fit.

## UNITED KINGDOM PATENTS [ss 23– 32]

### s 23 Patent granted in United Kingdom may be registered in Fiji

Any person being the grantee of a patent in the United Kingdom or any person deriving his or her right from such grantee by assignment, transmission or other operation of law may apply to the Solicitor-General within 3 years from the date of issue of the patent to have such patent registered in Fiji. Where any partial assignment or transmission has been made all proper parties shall be joined in the application for registration.

[s 23 insrt Ordinance 12 of 1924 s 3, effective 27 November 1924 ; am Ordinance 30 of 1959 s 14, effective 9 October 1959 ; Decree 55 of 2012 s 3, effective 9 July 2012]

### s 24 Copy of specifications to be submitted with a certificate

Every application under the provisions of section 23 shall be accompanied by a certified copy of the specification or specifications, including drawings, if any, of the United Kingdom patent and a certificate of the Comptroller-General of the United Kingdom Patent Office giving full particulars of the issue of the patent on such specification or specifications.

[s 24 insrt Ordinance 12 of 1924 s 3, effective 27 November 1924 ]

### s 25 Opposition

(1) Upon such application being received together with the documents mentioned in section 24 the Solicitor-General shall advertise the application in such manner as may be prescribed and any person may at any time within 2 months from the date of such advertisement give notice to the Solicitor-General of opposition to the issue of a certificate of registration as though such notice were a notice under section 15 for opposition to the grant of letters patent.

[subs (1) am Decree 55 of 2012 s 3, effective 9 July 2012]

(2) Where notice of opposition is given within the prescribed time the Solicitor-General shall transmit a copy of the notice to the applicant and, after hearing both parties if desirous of being heard, shall decide on the case.

[subs (2) am Decree 55 of 2012 s 3, effective 9 July 2012]

(3) If there is no opposition or, in the case of opposition, if the decision of the Solicitor-General is in favour of the issue of the certificate of registration, the Solicitor-General shall issue a certificate of registration.

[s 25 insrt Ordinance 12 of 1924 s 3, effective 27 November 1924 ; am Ordinance 30 of 1959 s 15, effective 9 October 1959 ; Decree 55 of 2012 s 3, effective 9 July 2012]

### s 26 Effect of certificate of registration

Such certificate of registration shall confer on the applicant privileges and rights subject to all conditions established by the law of Fiji as though the patent had been issued in the United Kingdom with an extension to Fiji.

[s 26 insrt Ordinance 12 of 1924 s 3, effective 27 November 1924 ]

### s 27 Commencement and duration of privileges and rights

Privileges and rights so granted shall date from the date of the patent in the United Kingdom and shall continue in force only so long as the patent remains in force in the United Kingdom, provided that no action for infringement shall be entertained in respect of any manufacture, use or sale of the invention prior to the date of the issue of the certificate of registration in Fiji.

[s 27 insrt Ordinance 12 of 1924 s 3, effective 27 November 1924 ]

### s 28 Court may annul

The court shall have power, upon the application of any person who alleges that his or her interests have been prejudicially affected by the issue of a certificate of registration, to declare that the exclusive privileges and rights conferred by such certificate of registration have not be acquired on any of the grounds upon which the United Kingdom patent might be revoked under the law for the time being in force in the United Kingdom. Such grounds shall be deemed to include the manufacture, use or sale of the invention in Fiji before the priority date applicable to the patent in the United Kingdom, but not to include the manufacture, use or sale of the invention in Fiji by some person or persons after the priority date applicable to the patent in the United Kingdom and before the date of the issue of the certificate of registration under section 25.

[s 28 insrt Ordinance 12 of 1924 s 3, effective 27 November 1924 ; am Ordinance 13 of 1933 s 2, effective 25 July 1933 ; Ordinance 4 of 1956 s 2, effective 13 June 1956]

### s 29 Amendment of specification

Whenever the specifications or drawings of a United Kingdom patent registered in Fiji have been amended by way of disclaimer, correction or explanation according to the law of the United Kingdom, a request accompanied by a copy of the specifications and drawings, if any, as amended duly certified by the Comptroller-General of the United Kingdom Patent Office may be made to the Solicitor-General to substitute a copy of the specifications and drawings as amended for the specifications and drawings originally filed.

[s 29 insrt Ordinance 12 of 1924 s 3, effective 27 November 1924 ; am Decree 55 of 2012 s 3, effective 9 July 2012]

### s 30 Where rights have been assigned, transmitted etc

Where a person becomes entitled by assignment, transmission or other operation of law to the privileges and rights conferred by a certificate of registration or to any interest therein he or she may make application in the prescribed manner to the Solicitor-General for the entry on the register of such assignment, transmission or other instrument affecting the title or giving an interest therein.

[s 30 insrt Ordinance 12 of 1924 s 3, effective 27 November 1924 ; am Decree 55 of 2012 s 3, effective 9 July 2012]

### s 31 Regulations

The Attorney-General may make such general regulations and do such things as he or she may think expedient subject to the provisions of this Act for regulating procedure under this Act, and for prescribing the forms to be used and the fees to be paid in respect of proceedings under this Act.

[s 31 insrt Ordinance 12 of 1924 s 3, effective 27 November 1924 ; am Ordinance 30 of 1959 s 16, effective 9 October 1959 ; Ordinance 26 of 1967 s 9, effective 1 July 1968; LN 112 of 1970 O 153, effective 8 October 1970]

### s 32 Power to nominate

The Solicitor-General may nominate a person to act on his or her behalf for the purposes of exercising his or her powers conferred by or duties prescribed under this Act.

[s 32 insrt Decree 55 of 2012 s 4, effective 9 July 2012]
