# PUBLIC SAFETY ACT 1920

AN ACT TO CONFER ON THE PRESIDENT THE POWER TO MAKE BETTER PROVISION FOR THE PUBLIC SAFETY DURING TIMES OF CIVIL COMMOTION

## s 1 Short title

This Act may be cited as the Public Safety Act 1920.

## s 2 President may make regulations in emergency

Whenever the President is satisfied that a state of civil commotion which threatens the public safety exists or is likely to arise in Fiji or any part thereof, or that an occasion has arisen or is threatened of such a nature as to be calculated, by interfering with the supply or distribution of food, water, fuel or light, or the maintenance of health or health services to deprive the community or any substantial portion thereof of the essentials of life, he or she may, by order under his or her hand, make regulations for all or any of the following matters, that is to say—

(a) the regulation, restriction or prohibition of the entry of all or certain persons or certain classes of persons into, the movement of such persons within, and the exit of such persons from, such area or areas as may from time to time be prescribed by the President;

(b) the regulation or restriction of the movement of means of transport and the supply of animals or articles which give motive power to the means of transport;

(c) the regulation or restriction of the supply or possession of intoxicating liquor and the prohibition or restriction of the opening or closing of premises or any part of premises in which intoxicating liquor is sold or supplied or of the opening or closing of any kava saloon, hop beer saloon or restaurant;

(d) the regulation of the storage, possession or use by all persons or by certain classes of persons or by persons in particular areas or by persons following particular occupations of firearms, ammunition or explosives and all or any offensive weapons or instruments of whatsoever description and the seizure and forfeiture thereof and the limitation or variation of the conditions of any licence, certificate or permit possessed by any persons under any law relating to arms, ammunition or explosives;

(e) the regulation, restriction or prohibition of gatherings of persons in any place whatsoever and the prohibition of the holding of meetings in any place whatsoever without the permission of the Commissioner of Police, and any gathering of persons or meeting held in contravention of any of the provisions of any regulations made under this paragraph shall be deemed to be an unlawful assembly and may be dealt with accordingly;

(f) the prohibition and prevention of any description of intimidation or acts or threats of violence or any other disorderly conduct of whatsoever description; and

(g) generally for all such matters as in the opinion of the President will better provide for the public safety.

[s 2 am Ordinance 2 of 1945 s 57, opn 1 May 1945 ; Ordinance 17 of 1960 s 2, effective 7 October 1960; Ordinance 37 of 1966 s 24, effective 13 January 1967; Act 28 of 2020 s 4 and Sch, effective 1 August 2020]

## s 3 Penalty for contravening the regulations

Any person who infringes or contravenes any of the provisions of any of the regulations made by the President under this Act shall be guilty of an offence and shall be liable to a fine not exceeding $1,000 or to imprisonment for any term not exceeding 2 years or to both such fine and imprisonment.

[s 3 am Ordinance 2 of 1945 s 57, opn 1 May 1945 ; Ordinance 37 of 1966 s 24, effective 13 January 1967; Ordinance 16 of 1969 s 2, effective 29 May 1969]

## s 4 Defacing the regulations etc

Any person who without lawful authority (the proof whereof shall be upon him or her) defaces, obliterates, covers over, removes or destroys any copy of any of the regulations made by the President under this Act affixed to any building or place shall be guilty of an offence and shall be liable to a fine not exceeding $40 or to imprisonment for any term not exceeding 2 months.

[s 4 am Ordinance 2 of 1945 s 57, opn 1 May 1945 ; Ordinance 37 of 1966 s 24, effective 13 January 1967]

## s 5 Powers of arrest and search

(1) Any magistrate, police officer, special constable or member of the Republic of Fiji Military Forces may arrest without warrant any person who so acts as to endanger the public safety or who is guilty or whom he or she suspects on reasonable grounds of being guilty, of an offence against any regulations made under this Act.

(2) Any police officer, special constable or member of the Republic of Fiji Military Forces who has effected an arrest under this section shall as soon as practicable report such arrest to a Magistrate and, unless the arrested person has in the meantime been released, produce such person before a magistrate.

(3) Any police officer of or above the rank of inspector may without warrant and with or without assistance, and using force if necessary—

(a) enter and search any premises;

(b) stop and search any vessel, vehicle or individual, whether in a public place or not,

if he or she suspects on reasonable grounds that any evidence of the commission of an offence against such regulations is likely to be found on such premises, vessel, vehicle or individual, and may seize any evidence so found.

(4) Whenever it is necessary under such regulations to cause a woman to be searched, the search shall be made by another woman and strict regard shall be had to decency.

(5) For avoidance of doubt it is declared that the powers conferred under this section shall be in addition to and not in substitution for any powers of arrest or search conferred by any other law.

## s 6 Promulgation of regulations, orders etc and judicial notice

(1) When any regulation is made under this Act or any order is made or direction given under regulations made under this Act, such regulation, order or direction shall be promulgated in such manner as the President or other authority making or giving the same thinks necessary for bringing it to the notice of persons who in his or her opinion ought to have notice of it, and such regulations, orders or directions shall, notwithstanding anything contained in section 21 of the Interpretation Act 1967, have effect as soon as notice as aforesaid has been given, without publication in the Gazette. Such regulations, orders or directions shall however be published in the Gazette as soon as circumstances permit.

(2) Without prejudice to the provisions contained in the said section as to judicial notice, any copy of any such regulation, order or direction as aforesaid, which purports—

(a) to be printed by the Government Printer; or

(b) to be certified to be a true copy of the original by the permanent secretary responsible for the ministry,

shall be judicially noticed, and shall in all courts of law be conclusive evidence of the making and publication of such regulation, order or direction and of the fulfilment of all the conditions precedent to the making thereof.
